3. The Bull List: Stage 2 breakout screen

A Stage 2 screen looks for names above their 50- and 200-day averages with price, momentum and volume behaving like an advancing trend. Four Information Technology names and one Energy name are exceptions to weak or weakening sector backdrops. A pick is a screen result, not a purchase recommendation; the qualifier matters because only a subset clears the volume hurdle.

The screen checks price trend and volume. Stage 2 means price sits above key moving averages. That can mark a strong trend. It does not mean a stock is cheap. It does not mean price will keep rising. The breakout list holds names with that trend. Each size group has ten names. The qualifier shows whether volume backs the move. Confirmed means volume met the screen rule. Other rows lack that added proof. They stay on the list because trend and strength still pass. Use the breakout level as a point to watch. Do not treat it as an order. The playbook adds the entry rule. A name must also sit near support. It must pass the market risk rules. This keeps the screen from turning into a chase list.

Market leaders can still fail. A breakout may reverse on the next close. A stop can slip during a gap. Large firms often trade with more liquidity. Small firms can move more in one day. The size bands help compare like with like. They do not set a safe risk level. New positions still need the same support and volume rules.

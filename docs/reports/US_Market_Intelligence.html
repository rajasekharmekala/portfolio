Ten ideas carry the whole report. A moving average is the average closing price over the last N days, like a trend line drawn through a noisy week. The 50-day average is the mood of the last ten weeks, and the 200-day average is the mood of the last ten months. A Stage is the season a stock is in. Stage 1 is a winter base where the price drifts sideways, Stage 2 is the spring and summer climb above rising averages, Stage 3 is autumn when the climb stalls, and Stage 4 is the winter decline under falling averages. RSI, the relative strength index, is a speedometer from 0 to 100: above 70 means the price has run unusually fast, and below 30 means it has fallen unusually fast. ATR, the average true range, is the normal daily wobble of a stock in dollars. It sets how far away a stop should sit, so ordinary noise does not shake you out.

Volume is how many shares changed hands. A move on heavy volume is a vote with a large turnout, and a move on thin volume is easier to reverse. The put/call ratio divides bearish option contracts by bullish ones and works as a fear gauge: readings near 0.7 are calm, and readings above 1.0 mean traders are paying for protection. Option walls are the strikes with the most open contracts. The dealers who sold those contracts hedge by trading the underlying fund, so a wall behaves like a ceiling or a floor that slows a move. A stop-loss is an exit price chosen before you buy, so a mistake costs a known amount. Reward-to-risk divides what you could gain by what you could lose, and this report asks for 3.5 to 1 before a setup counts. Position size is the dollars you commit, and the sizing rule exists so that no single stock can hurt you badly.

1.2 Options Gamma Wall & Volatility Architecture

How to read this table. An option wall is the strike price with the most open contracts. When traders buy options from dealers, the dealers hedge by buying or selling the underlying fund, and that hedging pulls the fund toward the strikes where the most contracts sit. The call wall is the strike above today's price with the most call contracts, and it tends to act as a ceiling. The put wall is the strike below today's price with the most put contracts, and it tends to act as a floor. The volatility trigger, also called the gamma flip, is the price at which the dealers' net hedging changes from damping moves to amplifying them. Take SPY: it closed at 766.48, the put wall is 750, the call wall is 790 and the trigger is 774.14. 2. US 11-Group Sector & Thematic Rotation Matrix

Money does not leave the stock market all at once. It moves between sectors the way water moves between rooms of a house: one room fills while another drains, and the total stays roughly the same until someone opens a door to the outside. Sector rotation is the study of which rooms are filling. It matters to a beginner because a stock in a filling room has a tailwind that a stock in a draining room does not, and because most of the damage in a correction lands on the sectors that were most crowded on the way up. The pieces that show it are: a close above the 50-day average on volume of at least 1.5 times normal, an average that has stopped falling, and a market that no longer punishes the rally the next day. 5. Smart Money Radar & Pre-Earnings Defense Calendar

Insiders are the officers, directors and large owners of a company, and they know its business better than any analyst. When one of them buys shares on the open market with their own money, it is a vote of confidence that cost them something. The Securities and Exchange Commission requires a Form 4 filing within two business days of such a trade, so the data is public and fast. An insider buy is a stronger signal than an insider sale, because insiders sell for many reasons, such as taxes and diversification, and buy for essentially one. The signal is strongest when two or more insiders buy within a short period, which is called a cluster, and when the dollar amount is large relative to what they already own.

The limits matter as much as the strength. Most clusters are in small and obscure companies, many in closed-end funds, and a purchase can reflect a belief that is early by months. The filing may describe a trade that happened weeks before the filing date, so the information is already priced in by the time it is read. A buy is also not a reason to ignore the chart: insiders are often early buyers of a stock that is still falling. This report therefore asks one question of the Form 4 data: does it corroborate or contradict what the charts in Sections 3 and 4 show? The answer today is that it mostly does neither, because the largest clusters are in names outside the two lists, with three connections: Fox, H.B. Fuller and Lennar.

The second half of the section is defensive. An earnings date is the single most dangerous day for a stock, because the price can gap through any stop overnight and the stop becomes an order that fills far below its level. The section lists every date that matters for the lists, the Section 6 setups and the book, and applies one rule to each. Glossary

ATR (average true range)
The average daily range of a stock over 14 sessions, in dollars. A stop two ATRs away is outside the stock's normal noise.
Breadth
The share of stocks above their 50-day average. Low breadth means a rising index depends on a few large names.
Call wall and put wall
The strikes with the most open call and put contracts. Dealer hedging makes them behave like a ceiling and a floor.
Confirmed
A name that passes every hurdle, including volume of at least 1.5 times normal on a day the stock moved in the trend's direction.
Distribution day
A day an index falls on higher volume than the day before. Six of them in 25 sessions is a warning.
Gamma flip (volatility trigger)
The index level at which dealer hedging changes from damping moves to amplifying them. This report estimates it from the option chain.
Moving average (DMA)
The average closing price over the last N days. The 50-day line is the ten-week mood, and the 200-day line is the ten-month mood.
Quadrant
Leading, Improving, Weakening or Lagging: a sector's place by its one-month return against the S&P 500 and this week's momentum.
Regime
Uptrend, Correction or Downtrend, set by where the S&P 500 closes against its 50-day and 200-day averages.
Reward-to-risk
The gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop. This report requires 3.5 to 1.
RSI (relative strength index)
A speedometer from 0 to 100. Above 70 is fast and below 30 is a hard fall.
Stage 1 to 4
The four seasons of a stock: base, advance, top and decline.
Stop-loss
A pre-set exit price. A gap can fill it below the stated level.
Setup candidate and Watch only
The two labels in the Bull List tables: a name that passes the ratio and the distance rule, and one that needs a pullback first.
Volume multiple
Today's shares traded divided by the 20-day average. A multiple of 1.5 or more is heavy.

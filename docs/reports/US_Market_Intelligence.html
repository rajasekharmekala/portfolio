Ten ideas carry the whole report. A moving average is the average closing price over the last N days, like a trend line drawn through a noisy week. The 50-day average is the mood of the last ten weeks, and the 200-day average is the mood of the last ten months. A Stage is the season a stock is in: Stage 1 is a winter base where the price drifts sideways, Stage 2 is the spring and summer climb above rising averages, Stage 3 is autumn when the climb stalls, and Stage 4 is the winter decline under falling averages. RSI, the relative strength index, is a speedometer from 0 to 100. Above 70 means the price has run unusually fast, and below 30 means it has fallen unusually fast. ATR, the average true range, is the normal daily wobble of a stock in dollars, and it sets how far away a stop should sit so that ordinary noise does not shake you out.

Volume is how many shares changed hands. A move on heavy volume is a vote with a large turnout; a move on thin volume is easier to reverse. The put/call ratio divides bearish option contracts by bullish ones and acts as a fear gauge: readings near 0.7 are calm and readings above 1.0 mean traders are paying for protection. Option walls are the strikes with the most open contracts. The dealers who sold those contracts hedge by trading the underlying fund, so a wall behaves like a ceiling or a floor that slows a move.

A stop-loss is an exit price chosen before you buy, so that a mistake costs a known amount. Reward-to-risk divides what you could gain by what you could lose, and this report asks for 3.5 to 1 before a setup counts. Position size is the dollars you commit; no single stock should be able to hurt you badly.

1.2 Options Gamma Wall & Volatility Architecture

Options add a second layer of forces beneath the price chart. When traders buy or sell options, the dealers on the other side hedge by trading the underlying fund, and the strikes with the most open contracts become magnets, ceilings and floors. A call wall is the strike above the price with the most open call contracts; it tends to cap rallies because dealers sell into it. A put wall is the strike below the price with the most open puts; it tends to hold declines until it breaks, after which hedging can speed the fall. The volatility trigger, also called the gamma flip, is the price where dealer hedging changes from calming moves to amplifying them.

How to read the table. Each row is one fund. Call Wall and Put Wall give the strike and the number of open contracts at it, so a bigger count means a stronger barrier. Volatility Trigger is the flip price, and the Gamma Regime column says which side of it the fund sits on today. Read the SPY row aloud: calls pile up at 800 with 134,331 contracts, puts at 745 with 157,386, and the flip is at 777, above the 763.25 price, so dealers are short gamma and tend to amplify moves in either direction. 5. Smart Money Radar & Pre-Earnings Defense Calendar

Corporate insiders know their companies better than any analyst, and the law makes them report their trades to the Securities and Exchange Commission on a document called Form 4 within two business days. Glossary

  • 50-day and 200-day average (DMA): the average closing price over the last 50 or 200 trading days; the 50-day shows the intermediate trend and the 200-day the long trend.
  • ATR (average true range): the usual daily price range of a stock over 14 days, in dollars; used to place stops and to measure how far a price sits from support.
  • Breadth: the share of stocks trading above a given average; 34.3% above the 50-day average means most stocks are weaker than the index.
  • Call wall and put wall: the strikes with the most open call or put contracts, which act as a ceiling or a floor as dealers hedge.
  • Distribution day: a session in which an index falls on heavier volume than the day before; seven in 25 sessions signals persistent selling.
  • Form 4: the filing in which an insider reports a trade in their own company's stock, due within two business days.
  • Gamma flip (volatility trigger): the price at which dealer hedging changes from calming moves to amplifying them.
  • Limit order: an order to buy at a chosen price or lower, which fills only if the market reaches that price.
  • Measured move: a target found by adding the height of a base to its breakout level.
  • Put/call ratio: put contracts traded divided by call contracts traded; near 0.7 is calm and above 1.0 shows demand for protection.
  • Quadrant (Leading, Improving, Weakening, Lagging): a sector's one-month and one-week return compared with the S&P 500.
  • Regime: the ledger's one-phrase label for the S&P 500's position against its 50-day and 200-day averages.
  • Reward-to-risk: the gain from entry to target divided by the loss from entry to stop.
  • RSI (relative strength index): a 0 to 100 gauge of how fast a price has moved; above 70 is stretched and below 30 is oversold.
  • Stage 1 to 4: the four seasons of a stock: base, markup, topping and decline.
  • Stop: an exit price set in advance; a gap through the stop fills at the next available price.

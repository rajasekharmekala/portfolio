Nine ideas carry the whole report. A moving average is the average closing price over a number of days, drawn as a line; think of it as the trend line through a noisy road. Volume counts how many people voted with their money; a move on twice the usual volume is a louder vote than the same move on half of it.

The put/call ratio is a fear gauge: puts are bets on a fall, calls are bets on a rise, and the ratio is puts divided by calls. Option walls are the strikes where option sellers hold the most contracts; because those sellers hedge by trading the underlying fund, the strikes act like a ceiling (the call wall) and a floor (the put wall). A stop-loss is an exit price decided before you buy. Risk/reward is what you can reasonably win divided by what you accept losing; this report asks for at least 2.5 to 1 before it calls anything a setup. Position size is how much of your money sits in one idea, and the point of sizing is that no single idea should hurt you badly.

1.2 Options Gamma Wall & Volatility Architecture

An option wall is a strike price where a large number of option contracts are open. The call wall is the strike with the most open calls above today's price and acts like a ceiling; the put wall is the strike with the most open puts below it and acts like a floor. The volatility trigger, also called the gamma flip, is the price where option sellers switch from calming the market to amplifying it: above it, their hedging trades dampen moves, and below it, they add to moves. Worked example: what a break of the 50-day line would cost on $10,000. Suppose you hold $10,000 of an S&P 500 fund. The index closed at 7,720.39. 5. Smart Money Radar & Pre-Earnings Defense Calendar

Insiders, meaning the executives and directors of a company, know more about it than any outside analyst. When several of them spend their own cash on shares in the open market, the purchase is a statement of confidence that costs them something. That is why the Securities and Exchange Commission requires a Form 4 filing within two business days of a trade, and why this report looks for clusters: two or more insiders buying for more than $250,000 in total, with the 14 days counted from the filing date. The signal has limits. Insiders buy for many reasons, they are early as often as they are right, and a purchase says nothing about the next three weeks. Institutional investors file a second kind of report, Form 13F, once a quarter and up to 45 days after the quarter ends, so the third-quarter filings are not available today and no 13F claim is made here.

The second half of the chapter is a calendar. A company's results date is the biggest single source of risk for a stock you own, because a stock can open 10% to 20% away from where it closed, past any stop you placed. The report applies one rule to named holdings: a position larger than its risk ceiling is cut to the ceiling before the scheduled report, and a position at or under its ceiling keeps its stop but gets no new money until the report is out. Glossary

Moving average
The average closing price over a set number of days; the 20-day, 50-day and 200-day lines show short, medium and long trends.
Stage 1 to 4
Four phases of a stock: base, uptrend, top, decline.
RSI
Relative strength index, a 0 to 100 speedometer; above 70 is fast, below 30 is oversold.
ATR
Average true range, the normal daily move in dollars; stops sit a fraction of it away from support.
Volume multiple
Today's trading volume divided by its 20-day average.
Regime
The market's overall condition, set by the S&P 500's position against its averages and by breadth.
Breadth
The share of stocks above a moving average; here, above the 50-day average.
Distribution day
A session in which an index falls on higher volume than the day before.
Put/call ratio
Put option volume divided by call option volume; a fear gauge.
Call wall and put wall
Strikes with the most open call or put contracts; they act as a ceiling and a floor.
Gamma flip (volatility trigger)
The price where option dealers' hedging changes from calming moves to amplifying them.
Stop-loss
An exit price chosen before buying.
Reward-to-risk (R:R)
Possible gain divided by possible loss, measured from the entry.
Form 4
The SEC filing that insiders submit within two business days of trading their own company's stock.
Form 13F
A quarterly filing of large institutions' holdings, due up to 45 days after quarter end.
Limit order
An order to buy or sell at a set price or better, never worse.
Stage 4 breakdown
A stock under falling 50-day and 200-day averages, with selling on heavy volume.
Quadrant
One of four boxes (Leading, Improving, Weakening, Lagging) that classify a sector by relative strength and its direction.

1. The decision at a glance

The table gives each verdict under the repository's three-layer rules. Layer 1 tests the financial floors and the type of business (the archetype), using insurer equivalents where the standard floors do not fit, because free cash flow and return on invested capital are not meaningful for an insurer that holds customers' premiums before paying claims. Layer 2 scores three to five operating measures that run the business. Layer 3 turns the chart into an entry rule and a position size. Reward-to-risk is the gain from the entry price to the base target divided by the loss from the entry price to the stop. The floor is 2.0 to 1 for Core Alpha names, so a name that clears the floor only at a lower price needs a limit order, not a market purchase.

What each decision rests on

Bowhead. American Family has agreed to buy the company for $34.00 in cash, with the stockholder vote on 2026-11-13 and third-quarter results on 2026-11-03. The business is healthy (written premium up 28.2 percent in the second quarter, return on equity 13.9 percent in the first half), but the contract caps the upside and the shares already trade 13 cents above the offer. We weigh a 90 percent chance the deal closes, 3 percent that a higher bid appears and 7 percent that it fails, which gives a probability-weighted value of $33.80, 1.0 percent below the close. The $29.40 failure case is a reference value, not a floor: at 11 times 2027 earnings it would be $26.95. The work reopens only if the merger agreement ends, the vote fails or an approval is refused.

Selective. The stock rose 18.8 percent in a month on a third-quarter operating profit of $2.37 a share against $1.91 expected. The dossier finds that about $0.18 to $0.25 of the beat will not repeat, and that the move is mostly a rise in the multiple (price to 2027 earnings from 9.5 to 11.1 times, price to book value from 1.52 to 1.81 times) because 2027 consensus earnings rose only 2.4 percent. Net premiums written fell 7 percent, which scores as the one FAIL on Layer 2, so the score sits exactly on the 3.0 line and sizing is TIER_4 (a 2.0 percent cap). The order waits for a pullback to the cluster of its 20, 50 and 200-day averages.

Hanover. Third-quarter operating earnings were $5.15 a share with a 92.3 percent combined ratio and a return on equity of 18.7 percent. At 11.4 times consensus earnings and 2.36 times book value, the probability-weighted value of $237.25 is only 0.9 percent above the close. The dossier also finds the FY2026 consensus still contains the old third-quarter estimate, so it understates the year by about $0.50 a share. Reward-to-risk clears 2.0 only at $222.00 or lower, and a one-point worse 2027 combined ratio would cut it to 0.74:1 at that price, so any order is conditional on refreshed numbers at the time it fills.

AXIS Capital. The seed figures check out in the 2026-10-28 release (operating return 15.7 percent, combined ratio 94.0 percent, book value per share $79.06, gross premiums up 11 percent), but the trend is weaker than the seed implies: the combined ratio went 89.8, 93.1 and 94.0 percent over the first three quarters and operating income fell 10 percent year on year. The price already assumes earnings fall by roughly 3 to 7 percent a year on our own reverse DCF. The zone top of $95.90 is the highest price that still clears 2.0:1 under both the central and the lower book-value multiple, and it holds only by about four cents on the stress case.

Ledger gaps. Each dossier shows its own cross-check from cited filings in Chapter 5. The Hanover ledger table row shows a street target of $237.12 where the source page says $237.13, and the dossier discloses the one-cent difference.

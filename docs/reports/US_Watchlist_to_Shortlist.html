Start here: this is a four-name underwriting batch, not a promise that a rising chart deserves capital. Underwriting means testing a company as an owner would test a business before buying it. A hard gate is like a building inspection: one failed structural check stops the purchase even when the paint looks good. ROIC asks whether operations earn enough on the money tied up in the business. Free cash flow is the cash left after capital spending, so it is closer to the money an owner can actually use than reported earnings alone. Net debt to EBITDA compares debt with a rough annual operating earning power. Stock-based compensation, or SBC, is pay issued in shares; it can conserve cash while increasing the share count held by everyone else. The momentum gate checks price against moving averages, relative strength and a base. ATR is a volatility ruler: five ATRs above support means a large pullback can occur without the business changing. The decision rule is deliberately repetitive in logic but not in the final call: a screen identifies candidates, an underwriting pass tests evidence, and a portfolio book decides whether the risk deserves a slot. The report therefore distinguishes a company that is interesting from a company that is investable today. An investor can agree with a company story and still reject the current price. A DCF or measured move is an estimate, so its discount rate, fade path and target must be visible. A support band is a rule for execution, not evidence that the business is sound. Glossary

ROIC is return on invested capital, the profit earned from the money tied up in operations. FCF is free cash flow after capital spending. SBC is stock-based compensation issued in shares. ATR is average true range, a volatility ruler used to compare distance from support. A Stage 2 trend is a sustained markup phase above moving averages; it can still be overextended and can coexist with a failed financial gate. DCF is discounted cash flow, a valuation method whose output changes with its assumptions.

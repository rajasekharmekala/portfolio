1. The decision at a glance

The table gives each verdict under the repository's three-layer rules. Layer 1 tests the financial floors and the type of business (the archetype), using insurer or pipeline equivalents where the standard floors do not fit. Layer 2 scores three to five operating measures that run that business. Layer 3 turns the chart into an entry rule and a position size. Reward-to-risk is the gain from the entry price to the base target divided by the loss from the entry price to the stop. The floor is 2.0 to 1 for Core Alpha names and 2.5 to 1 for Tactical Momentum and turnaround names, so a name that clears the floor only at a lower price needs a limit order, not a market purchase.

What each decision rests on

Loews. The parts are sound: an insurer (CNA), a gas pipeline (Boardwalk), hotels and a net-cash parent. They sum to about $124 a share in our estimate, 10 percent above the close. But return on equity is 8.9 percent against the 12 percent floor, the price is 1.18 times book value against a three-year median of 1.03, and CNA's underlying combined ratio has risen to 94.2 percent. A Delaware court sent a lawsuit against Boardwalk back to the lower court, and the CEO says a worst-case award would exceed $1.2 billion, with no accrual. The central value is $115, so the stock is priced for most of the discount. Both Loews and CNA report on 2026-11-02, and the dossier lists the results that would reopen the work.

Everest. The third-quarter combined ratio was 107.1 percent (Core 105.8 percent) after a $268 million net reserve charge, the fourth quarter in the last eight with net adverse development above $100 million. The stock still rose 3.54 percent on 4.95 times normal volume after results. Book value per share was $381.04, so the price is 1.05 times book. The base value of $470 rests on end-2027 book value near $447 times 1.05. Layer 2 sits exactly at its floor, with one FAIL on reserve development, and all three rating agencies hold negative outlooks. That is why sizing is TIER_3 and the order waits inside $368.00 to $382.00.

Kinder Morgan. A record $14.4 billion backlog and 3.5 times leverage make it a steady toll road, but the dossier finds 2027 looks like a pause year as 2026 commodity gains and $225 million of one-time items fade. At 21.4 times adjusted earnings the price equals its three-year median, so there is no discount. The ledger's reverse DCF (27.2 percent implied growth) rests on a free cash flow base the dossier could not reproduce from filings, and its own cross-checks give 0 to 12.5 percent. The order waits for the 2027 budget in early December.

ONEOK. The chart is a Stage 2 leader, but the central target is only 1.8 percent above the close, and 88 percent of nine-month EBITDA growth (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization) came from optimization and marketing gains management expects to narrow. Apollo's new $9.1 billion Class B stake takes 15 percent of cash flow ahead of shareholders. The red team's funding test (cash from operations less capex, dividends and Apollo's full share) leaves a gap of $672 million, 10.3 percent of cash flow, so the funding measure scores FAIL and sizing fell from TIER_3 to TIER_4. No order should be placed before the first report that shows a full quarter of Apollo distributions.

This is research cycle 2 for four leveraged funds in the US watchlist queue: ProShares UltraPro QQQ (TQQQ), ProShares UltraPro Dow30 (UDOW), Direxion Daily S&P 500 Bull 3X Shares (SPXL) and GraniteShares 2x Long NVDA Daily ETF (NVDL), each re-underwritten from fresh filings on the 2026-10-28 close. A leveraged fund is a fund that tries to deliver two or three times one day's move of an index or stock, so it decays in choppy markets and the reports judge the index, the trend and the decay rather than a business. You hold TQQQ, UDOW and SPXL; the reports cut all three (sell 17 of 35.0806 TQQQ shares, sell 5 of 10 UDOW shares, sell all 6.6209 SPXL shares) and NVDL, which you do not hold, gets limit orders only. None of the four earns new money at the close, and the shortlist does not change. These conclusions would be wrong if the October 29 and November 2 reports from the Nasdaq-100 and Dow giants, or the NVIDIA report listed for 2026-11-17, move the index levels the dossiers rest on by more than the levels each dossier names.

1. The decision at a glance

The table gives each verdict under the repository's three-layer rules. Layer 1 tests the financial floors and the type of business (the archetype), Layer 2 scores three to five operating measures, and Layer 3 turns the chart into an entry rule and a position size. Reward-to-risk is the gain from the entry price to the base target divided by the loss from the entry price to the stop. The floor is 2.5 to 1 for tactical and turnaround names, so a name that clears the floor only at a lower price needs a limit order, not a market purchase. Where the rulebook has no exact label for a decision, the dossier says which label is nearest and where it does not fit.

What each decision rests on

TQQQ. The fund rose 9.16 percent in three sessions, from $66.29 to $72.36, so its distance from support went from 0.26 to 2.09 average true range (ATR, the typical daily swing) and left the band where the rules allow buying. The ladder band that now applies calls for trimming a held name by 30 to 50 percent, and the weighted value of $68.80 sits 4.9 percent below the close. The remaining 18.0806 shares stay under a closing stop at $63.70 with dated exits on 2027-01-22 and 2027-04-23.

UDOW. The first draft kept all 10 shares under the label KEEP_COMPOUNDING. The red team showed that the plan's own half-sale at $70.33 made it worth $68.13 against $68.46 for selling today, so the dossier modelled every exit path against a cash sale and changed the verdict. A plain hold is about equal to cash; selling 5 shares is a risk-size decision, not a gain, and the report says so. An owner who wants the best expected return should sell all 10.

SPXL. The S&P 500 fund now passes the momentum test for a stock already in an uptrend, and its stop rose from $241.83 to $266.70. Even so, holding beats a cash sale by $21.09 only on the report's own endpoint model, and only $7.90 over cash earning interest, with a break-even bear probability of 23.2 percent against the report's 20 percent. The red team called that edge too thin to justify keeping the stake, so the default is a full exit and the dossier gives a limit zone of $270.60 to $274.30 for a later re-entry that must pass a fresh underwriting run.

NVDL. The fund is a 2x daily-reset bet on NVIDIA, and the new GraniteShares prospectus dated 2026-10-28 shows a 1.64 percent net expense ratio including 0.60 percent interest, against 1.05 percent on the old basis. NVIDIA itself passes the quality floors (return on invested capital above 80 percent, free cash flow margin 41.9 percent, net cash), but the fund's reward-to-risk clears the floor only at the limit zone, and the position is cut to 30 shares before NVIDIA's report on 2026-11-17.

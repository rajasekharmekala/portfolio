What the company sells and what drives the economics

AvePoint's product families cover distinct steps in the cloud-data lifecycle. Cloud Backup and Microsoft 365 Backup preserve content and provide restore workflows. Fly maps users and files, moves them between cloud systems, and checks the migration. Opus helps records teams classify, retain, archive, or delete information. Confidence Platform reviews permissions, sharing, policy gaps, and AI-related data risk. Elements lets service partners manage repeated work across many customer tenants. These descriptions are based on vendor product pages; there is no product-level revenue disclosure, so the product map cannot establish which line contributes most to growth.

The company reported Q2 2026 revenue of $124.5 million, up 22% year over year; SaaS revenue was $98.5 million, up 27%, and ARR reached $465.1 million, up 27%. Gross retention was 89% and net retention was 111%, making retention a central check on the recurring-revenue thesis. Software gross margin was 83%, while total non-GAAP gross margin was 73.7%, reflecting the importance of services and mix. Net new ARR of $29.9 million was a record. AvePoint said it served more than 28,000 organizations through 6,000 partners, and 59% of ARR came through the channel. These are management-reported metrics from the Q2 earnings release and call transcript.

Q2 ARR grew 27% to $465.1 million, but gross retention was 89% and net retention was 111%. If Q3 ARR growth stays in the mid-20% range and retention holds near those levels, recurring demand remains credible; if gross retention falls or net retention approaches 100%, expansion may no longer offset customer losses. Two-thirds of incremental ARR came through the channel, according to management, which can extend distribution but also makes partner productivity and economics important. The next report should clarify whether large-account growth and SaaS mix continue to support margins.

The subscription model can scale when a client adds users, workloads, backup coverage, or policy controls. It can also create support and service costs when systems change or recovery tests fail. Microsoft competes as both the owner of the leading productivity platform and a provider of native backup and resiliency services. Veeam and Rubrik market alternatives. Their websites describe product capabilities, not comparative independent tests; a buyer should compare restore scope, retention, recovery time, access controls, and total cost in its own environment. See the primary product references for Microsoft 365 Backup, Veeam, and Rubrik.

With the buying tasks and service burden in view, the next check is whether management has delivered the growth and margin profile it has promised.

Management delivery and financial guardrails

The walk-the-talk record is mixed but currently supportive. FY2024 revenue was $330.5 million, above the initial $308.6–$316.6 million range; FY2025 revenue of $419.5 million also beat its initial range. FY2025 non-GAAP operating margin was 18.9%, while operating cash flow was $85.3 million compared with $88.9 million in FY2024. For the first half of 2026, operating cash flow was $40.2 million versus $20.8 million a year earlier. Q2 cash was $417.3 million and the company repurchased $110.3 million of shares. These figures indicate cash generation and prior revenue outperformance, but repurchases should be weighed against share-based compensation and the investment needed to maintain product quality.

Management's Q3 2026 revenue guide was $128.2–$130.2 million and non-GAAP operating income guide was $21–$22 million. FY2026 guidance called for revenue of $508.5–$512.5 million, ARR of $522.1–$528.1 million, and non-GAAP operating income of $86.4–$88.4 million. Guidance is forward-looking and the fiscal year is not complete. Historical guide beats do not ensure another beat. Foreign exchange was cited by the CFO as an offset to the ARR increase and Q2 outperformance; product mix, channel contribution, retention, and operating expense growth remain measurable checks at the next release. The company has not provided enough public detail in the materials reviewed to verify customer concentration, product-level margins, or a full dilution bridge.

The CEO described the demand context this way: “AI agents are being created faster than organizations can manage them.” The CFO said, “FX headwinds offset the ARR raise and the Q2 outperformance.” Both statements come from the Q2 call transcript. The first frames a possible need for governance and data controls, but AvePoint did not report AI-specific revenue, so it remains a thesis to test rather than a booked growth driver. The second is a useful reminder that ARR momentum and reported revenue can diverge when foreign exchange changes. Management also reported that two-thirds of incremental ARR came through partners; investors should follow channel mix alongside gross retention and SaaS growth rather than infer that partner reach automatically produces durable economics.

That delivery record informs the range of outcomes, but the share price still needs a valuation test against weaker and stronger growth paths.

3. Business quality, operating tests and management record

Intuitive sells systems, procedure-specific instruments and accessories, and service. Placements add to the installed base; procedure growth and utilization show whether hospitals use that base; repeat sales indicate whether use turns into durable revenue. Q3 included 546 da Vinci placements, of which 315 were da Vinci 5, and the company reported 12,094 da Vinci systems in use. Ion’s installed base reached 1,152. Combined procedure growth was 15%, while reported average utilization rose 2% year over year [1][2].

The recurring model is strong but does not remove price risk. Management said 84% of Q3 revenue was recurring, which gives the company a broad stream tied to procedures and service rather than only new system sales. The same Q3 call described future price reductions of 3%–5% per da Vinci case at constant mix. A lower price may attract more cases; whether that offsets lower revenue per case is not yet known. The next reports should pair procedure growth, utilization, I&A revenue per procedure and gross margin [1][3].

The guidance review is conditional. In July, management framed 2026 da Vinci procedure growth at 13.5%–15.5%; by October, the range was 14%–15%, with management nearer the midpoint. Q3 procedures grew 14%, but the year was not complete at the cutoff, so the guide remains a forecast. In 2025, management raised its procedure-growth range late in the year; the annual filing showed US growth of about 15% and growth outside the US of about 23%. Those regional rates indicate demand across markets but do not produce the consolidated actual needed for a clean forecast score [1][3][5][8][27].

There is no single public customer-retention rate or verified total addressable procedure denominator in the evidence used here. The report does not invent either one. Instead, it follows what the company reports each quarter and separates adoption from monetization. This is the core business hinge behind the conditional rating: the company can keep placing systems and still disappoint if case growth fails to replace lower dollars per case. Conversely, more use on a larger installed base could support the premium if gross margin holds [1][2][3]. The next section weighs that potential against reported cash flow and the price already attached to it.

5. Competitive and clinical context

Intuitive’s incumbent position benefits from system familiarity, trained teams, service and a broad instrument ecosystem. Medtronic’s Hugo, Johnson & Johnson’s OTTAVA and CMR’s Versius Plus show that alternatives are advancing through US regulatory pathways. Their clearances differ by indication and do not prove commercial adoption. The dossier’s peer matrix compares the systems and the evidence gap, rather than treating announcements as market-share data [19][20][21][22].

Clinical evidence needs the same restraint. The 2026 Benign COMPARE review reported that robot-assisted procedures were associated with 54% lower odds of conversion to open surgery across 13 benign procedures, with an odds ratio of 0.46. Heterogeneity was high at 88%, and two authors were Intuitive employees. That result may support adoption in some settings, but it is not causal proof across all surgeries. Long-term economics still depend on procedure choice, operating time, hospital cost, patient outcomes and room throughput [17][18].

Several comparator links and official product pages are included in the dossier for direct reading. Four Intuitive product pages could not be parsed by the fetcher; their links are not treated as evidence in this report.

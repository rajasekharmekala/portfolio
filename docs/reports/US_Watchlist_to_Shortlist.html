What this batch did

The watchlist workflow takes the next pending names, researches each one, and writes one dossier per name. Only three names had a pending status this session, so the batch is short. The rule is to process the eligible names and say so, not to widen the list to fill the batch.

Every price in this report comes from one frozen snapshot dated 9 October 2026. The ledger labels that session's market "Uptrend (narrow breadth)", which means the index rose while only 28.9 percent of stocks sat above their 50-day average [1]. None of the three names is held in any account, so no holding was at risk from the verdicts.

Two terms recur in the results. Free cash flow means cash from operating activities minus capital expenditure, which is money spent on equipment and other long-lived assets; it is the cash left after the company pays for the equipment it needs. Net debt is total borrowing minus cash on hand, and the leverage test compares it with EBITDA, which stands for earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. ATR (average true range) is the typical daily price move, and a base is a sideways period in which a stock builds a floor.

What this means for you: the watchlist has three fewer pending names, and the shortlist is unchanged. No purchase can be drawn from this batch. The next chapter lists the results.

What would change these verdicts

Each verdict has a trigger: the filed result that would change it. The financial triggers change only with quarterly and annual filings, so a verdict is unlikely to change between now and the next Form 10-Q. The price test can change in weeks, but a price move alone cannot overturn a financial floor.

For Perrigo, a verdict change needs all four of these in a later filing: a return on invested capital of at least 12 percent, a free cash flow margin of at least 12 percent, positive EBITDA, and a momentum pass. For Coca-Cola Consolidated, it needs free cash flow of about $867 million on a sales base near the 2025 level, a published stock-based compensation figure below 5 percent of sales, and a momentum pass. For Cleveland-Cliffs, it needs operating income of about $1.6 billion, free cash flow of about $2.2 billion, EBITDA of about $2.4 billion, a rising 200-day average, and a momentum pass.

Estimate: the gap to each cash floor

This estimate measures how far each name's cash margin sits from the 12 percent floor. It matters because the cash test is the easiest one for an owner to check, and it is the one that decides the most cases here.

The number

Coca-Cola Consolidated needs a gap of about $248 million of free cash flow. Perrigo needs about $365 million. Cleveland-Cliffs needs about $3.3 billion.

How it was calculated

Twelve percent of fiscal 2025 net sales, less actual free cash flow. Coca-Cola Consolidated: 12 percent of $7,228.1 million is $867.4 million, against $619.6 million. Perrigo: 12 percent of $4,253.1 million is $510.4 million, against $145.1 million. Cleveland-Cliffs: 12 percent of $18,610 million is $2,233.2 million, against a negative $1,023 million.

The inputs

Net sales, operating cash flow and capital expenditure for fiscal 2025 come from each company's filings [3][4][5] and the structured figures behind them.

The range

The gap is the same arithmetic in every case, so the range comes from the cash flow itself. Coca-Cola Consolidated's first-half 2026 free cash flow was $273.3 million, a margin of 7.0 percent, which is below the 2025 figure [12]. Half-year figures are seasonal, so this is a reading, not a forecast.

If the number is off

The verdicts do not turn on a small error here. Coca-Cola Consolidated is the closest name, and even a margin of 10 percent would still fail the floor. Its verdict is the one a reader should watch in the next filing.

Next review dates: each archived name is due for review on 2027-01-07, which is 90 days from the 2026-10-09 session, or earlier on a material filing.

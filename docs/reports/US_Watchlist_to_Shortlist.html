What each decision rests on

Microsoft. Return on invested capital is about 30.9%, net debt to EBITDA is 0.16x and stock-based pay is 3.7% of revenue. Free cash flow margin is 20.2% on management's definition and 12.8% after finance leases, down from 30.2% in fiscal 2024. The ledger's Reverse DCF (39.55% a year on $16.5 billion of cash flow) could not be traced to the filings and was replaced with 26.03% a year on $42.4 billion. The $640 central target against a $423.24 stop gives 1.05 to 1 at $529.05. A fall toward $477 or a better earnings base is a reason to re-underwrite, not to buy. Next catalyst: first-quarter fiscal 2027 results on 28 October 2026.

Apple. Return on invested capital is 66.7% (equity is small because of buybacks), free cash flow margin is 29.3% and the company holds $62.2 billion of net cash. The ledger's Reverse DCF was replaced (13.54% a year instead of 16.75%) because its cash-flow figure did not match the filings. The $324 central target sits below today's price, so reward to risk is negative; the Layer 2 scorecard is 3 of 5, below the 4 of 5 minimum. A close at or below $295 starts a fresh review. Next catalyst: fourth-quarter fiscal 2026 results and the first call under the new chief executive on 2 November 2026.

Capital One. A bank, so free cash flow, net debt to EBITDA and the stock-pay floor do not apply; the dossier tests return on tangible equity, capital, charge-offs and efficiency instead. The ledger's cash-flow Reverse DCF is not meaningful for a bank and was replaced with an earnings-based one (-0.45% a year). The stock is Stage 1, below falling 50-day and flat 200-day averages, and its 3.12 to 1 reward to risk clears the 3.0 core floor but not the 3.5 turnaround floor. Next catalyst: third-quarter results on 20 October 2026.

TSMC. Return on invested capital is 47.4%, free cash flow margin 26.3% and the company holds net cash. The ledger divided Taiwan-dollar cash flow by a US-dollar market value; the dossier recomputes the Reverse DCF in dollars (16.58% a year). The verdict is a chart verdict: the stock is 5% below its rising 50-day average and 12.7% off its high in two weeks. A close above $434.36 with non-negative relative strength clears the Stage 2 test, but at a $437 entry reward to risk falls to about 1.4, so both the chart and the price must work together. Next catalyst: monthly revenue around 10 November 2026 and the January 2027 report.

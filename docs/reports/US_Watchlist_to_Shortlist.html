Business quality and current evidence

Nasdaq runs market access, issuer services, financial software and trading systems. It reports three segments. CAP and FinTech grew faster than Market Services in Q3. Market Services depends more on trading volume, order flow and fees. Gross revenue includes transaction costs; net revenue removes them. Q3 gross revenue of $2.393B less $912M in transaction costs left $1.481B in net revenue. We compare segments on net revenue [1][2][3].

CAP links issuers, investors and asset managers through listings, corporate tools, market data and indexes. FinTech sells software for fraud cases, regulatory reports, treasury, risk and post-trade work. These tools connect to client data and regulated tasks. That can raise the cost of a switch. Market Services runs venues and related systems. Nasdaq said it still planned 23/5 trading for December 6, 2026; it also closed LeveL Markets on October 15. Neither event proves new revenue or returns yet [1][2][6][10][12][17][19][20][22][26].

Risks, invalidation and next review

Nasdaq still runs an exchange business with cyclical volumes. Investors also pay a premium for its software growth. That growth must offset integration risk. At September 30, goodwill and net intangibles were $20.353B. Debt was $8.740B, while cash was $365M. Q3 free cash flow was strong, but the large acquired asset base makes returns more sensitive to slower growth or added costs [2][3][6].

Debt service and acquisition costs compete with cash for integration, buybacks and debt paydown. Q3 free cash flow and 98% trailing-twelve-month conversion show current coverage. They do not prove that this rate will hold. Interest, restructuring and acquisition costs can reduce future cash flow. If cash falls while debt stays high, balance-sheet risk rises and per-share returns weaken [2][3][6].

FinTech implementations can take longer than expected. Cloud migrations can raise costs. Clients can renegotiate, data providers compete, and rules may limit longer trading or tokenization. The $100M AI-efficiency and cross-sell targets remain future goals. The key test is whether Nasdaq can turn regulated workflows, issuer links and data into recurring growth and free cash flow without lowering returns on acquired assets. Q3 supports demand, but later reports must prove that the trend lasts [6][7][10][17][19][26].

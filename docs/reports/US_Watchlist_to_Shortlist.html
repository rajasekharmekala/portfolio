1. The decision at a glance

The table gives each verdict under the repository's three-layer rules. Layer 1 tests the financial floors and the type of business (the archetype), Layer 2 scores three to five operating measures that run that business, and Layer 3 turns the chart into an entry rule and a position size. Reward-to-risk is the gain from the entry price to the base target divided by the loss from the entry price to the stop. The floor is 2.0 to 1 for Core Alpha names and 2.5 to 1 for tactical and turnaround names, so a name that clears the floor only at a lower price needs a limit order, not a market purchase.

What each decision rests on

Talen. The nuclear-and-gas power producer has multiplied its cash flow through acquisitions and settled capacity prices in the PJM grid auction ($333.44 and $325.00 per megawatt-day), but it has tripled its debt since the end of 2024. Net debt is 5.95 times adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) on the last twelve months and 4.45 times on 2026 guidance. The hard red line (4.0 times with interest cover under 2.0 times) is not tripped on the adjusted figure used in the credit agreement, but it is tripped on filed GAAP numbers, so the gate is conditional. The dossier's own reverse DCF gives 4.0 percent a year on cash flow with a one-time $495 million payment added back and 13.8 percent a year without that adjustment; the ledger has none. The probability-weighted value of $351.15 is only 1.0 percent above the close, so the idea works only at the zone top.

Vistra. The producer passes the hard red lines and scores 4.0 of 5.0 on operating measures (nuclear capacity factor, hedge cover and 3,809 megawatts of 20-year contracts pass; debt and retail earnings are on watch). Return on invested capital of 11.0 percent and free cash flow margin of 11.7 percent miss the 12 percent floors narrowly. Net debt is 3.08 times adjusted EBITDA and is expected to reach about 3.36 times after the Cogentrix purchase, above the 3.2 times line the prior record set. The dossier's reverse DCF is 6.4 percent a year against the ledger's -0.64 percent, which uses a different cash flow base; both are shown. The verdict and levels are unchanged, but Vistra loses its Core Alpha slot (see chapter 2).

GE Aerospace. The engine maker passes every Layer 1 floor (return on invested capital 32.2 percent, free cash flow margin 15.2 percent, net debt 0.89 times EBITDA), holds a $212.1 billion backlog and raised guidance in the 2026-10-20 report. The price already pays for it: the reverse DCF is 14.3 percent a year of free cash flow growth for ten years, and the dossier's fade-case value of $232 to $265 sits below the whole buy zone. The ledger labels the stock Stage 4, but the hard red line needs a falling 200-day average and GE's is rising, so the stock is only in a timing wait. Expected return from the zone midpoint is 5.0 percent, half of the 10 percent hurdle, which is why the size is the smaller tier and the order is optional.

Spotify. The subscription audio platform (306 million subscribers, 33.0 percent gross margin, net cash of EUR 9.4 billion) passes every quality level and sits only 0.45 ATR (average true range, a typical day's price swing) above its 50-day average after falling 7.8 percent since the 2026-10-23 run. Reward-to-risk is still only 0.64 to 1 because the structural stop is 16.5 percent below the close, and the buy zone lies mostly below a falling 200-day average. The dossier's own reverse DCF is 10.24 percent a year in euros; mixing euro cash flow with a dollar market value would wrongly give 13.02 percent.

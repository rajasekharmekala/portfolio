1. The decision at a glance

The table gives each verdict under the repository's three-layer rules. Layer 1 tests the financial floors and the type of business (the archetype), Layer 2 scores 3 to 5 operating measures that run that business, and Layer 3 turns the chart into an entry rule and a position size. Reward-to-risk is the gain from the entry price to the base target divided by the loss from the entry price to the stop. The floor is 2.0 to 1 for Core Alpha names and 2.5 to 1 for tactical and turnaround names, so a name that clears the floor only at a lower price needs a limit order, not a market purchase. The label TRIM_PROFIT_BOOKING is the nearest label in the rulebook for a cut made to respect the loss budget; each dossier says where the label does not fit exactly.

What each decision rests on

Arista Networks. The AI and cloud Ethernet switch maker has no debt, a free cash flow margin of 48.9 percent over the last twelve months and stock compensation of 4.8 percent of revenue, so it passes every Layer 1 floor as a compounder. The price is the issue: the 12-month expected price is $207.04 (+10.7 percent) if the valuation multiple recovers to 40.86 times 2027 earnings and $193.84 (+3.7 percent) if it stays at 35.8 times, so the dossier calls the price fair at best. The loss at the $155.76 stop on all 25.73 shares is 0.94 percent of net worth against a 0.75 percent budget, which is why about 5.2 shares go. The call is sold because, with the shares, it behaves like 14.9 percent of net worth against a 6.5 percent ceiling. The reverse DCF, which asks what yearly growth today's price assumes for ten years, is 15.70 percent on filing cash flow of $5,155.3 million, not the ledger's 19.44 percent on $3.892 billion, which the filings do not support.

DigitalOcean. The developer and AI cloud is growing fast (annual recurring revenue $1,125 million, up 29 percent; AI customer revenue up 212 percent; $894 million of contracted backlog) but it funds that growth with equity and financed servers: gross margin fell to 55.0 percent, stock compensation is 9.4 percent of revenue, diluted shares rose 25.8 percent in a year and return on invested capital is 7.6 percent. The $106.39 price already assumes about $5.6 billion of 2036 revenue at a 25 percent cash margin. The expected value is $95.0, or 10.7 percent below the price, and the position is 3.4 times its 2.0 percent size ceiling. The loss at the stop on the 12.56 shares kept is 0.33 percent of net worth against a 0.35 percent budget. The reverse DCF is 22.5 percent a year on reported adjusted free cash flow of $174.6 million, not the ledger's 18.2 percent on a $232 million base that no filing contains.

Advanced Micro Devices. Data Center revenue rose 107 percent to $6.7 billion, server unit share is 34.5 percent, the free cash flow margin is 18.7 percent and net cash is $9.885 billion. The chart moved from a Stage 3 pullback to a Stage 2 leader sitting 0.61 ATR above its 50-day average, so the momentum test now passes. The price test does not: at $560.26 the stock pays the five-year mean multiple on consensus 2027 earnings, the probability-weighted value is $541 (bull $716, base $610, bear $291), and at the mean multiple it falls to $518.30. That is why the top of the buy zone was cut from $523.50 to $509.30, the highest price that still gives 2.5 to 1 on the mean multiple. Options imply a move of about 8.9 percent either way on 2026-11-03. The reverse DCF is 29.21 percent a year on continuing-operations free cash flow of $7.736 billion, not the ledger's 27.37 percent on $8.841 billion, which includes about $667 million from a divested business.

Bloom Energy. The fuel cell maker reported third-quarter revenue of $1,235.8 million (up 138 percent) and a GAAP gross margin of 33.8 percent, but free cash flow was negative $180.6 million, the twelve-month free cash flow margin of 11.4 percent misses the 12 percent floor, and two counterparties are 43 percent and 26 percent of revenue. Layer 2 scores 2.5 of 5.0 with one FAIL on cash conversion. The probability-weighted value of $201.65 is 7.7 percent below the price, and the edge is about zero even when the bear case stops at the stop. The residual holding is sized from the 50 basis point loss budget: 10.8 shares at a $39.59 loss each to the $178.91 stop. Adds reopen only if a fresh Layer 2 score of 3.0 or more, a qualifying Layer 1, a passing momentum test and a refreshed 2.5 to 1 all hold together. The reverse DCF is 32.52 percent a year on filing cash flow of $436.7 million, not the ledger's 30.65 percent on 75 percent of operating cash flow.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run completed with the status PUBLISHED_WITH_GATE_EXCEPTIONS and BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT. No dossier received a reviewer APPROVE in either round; unresolved or post-round-2 items are listed in each dossier's own run notes.

Stale source dates. The citation checker ran with its stale as-of allowance because the latest periodic filings (the quarter ended 2026-06-27 or 2026-06-30) are older than 60 days and the third-quarter reports are due on 2026-11-03, and because five-year tables describe closed periods by design. The flagged record counts are 135 for ANET, 69 for DOCN, 54 for AMD and 69 for BE (Bloom's third-quarter report is already out, so its exceptions are mostly annual and earlier-2026 records). No as-of date was edited, and each dossier lists every exception line with its cure.

Ledger fundamentals. The ledger's reverse DCF could not be reproduced from filings for any of the four names: Arista 19.44 percent became 15.70, DigitalOcean 18.2 became 22.5, AMD 27.37 became 29.21 and Bloom 30.65 became 32.52. The Arista render used a run-local copy of the ledger with only its reverse DCF replaced, so its key-figures strip shows the corrected value; the DigitalOcean and AMD strips still show the ledger's value, and the Bloom strip is annotated with the filings value. All four dossiers explain the difference in Chapter 5 and in their run notes. The ledger's AMD market value rested on a larger share count than the 10-Q cover, so $914.6 billion was used instead of $933.78 billion.

Renderer workaround. The repository's assembler skips the first-use glossary definition for text that sits directly inside a paragraph, which makes the standard dossier builder fail the readability check. The four dossiers were rendered with run-local wrappers that apply a corrected glossary pass and, in three cases, plain-English lead-in paragraphs before table-led sections. No repository script was modified. The assembler bug should be fixed at its source before the next run.

Data dates and sources. Several consensus pages, vendor pages and the Arista call option quote (bid $17.85, ask $19.50, 2026-10-28 midday) were fetched after the 2026-10-27 cutoff; the dossiers label them. Sources that returned an access error (Gartner and an investor article for DigitalOcean; S&P Global ratings, Seeking Alpha and Macrotrends for AMD) are shown as not available, and the unfindable Bloom items (third-quarter dollar backlog, 2028 consensus, the identity of the 43 percent customer, a primary source for the Brookfield expansion) are marked as such. The two options on DigitalOcean and Arista have no verified marks in the book, so their values are N/A or model-based and disclosed.

Structural validator. The repository's structural validator has no dossier mode, so the readability check, the citation check and the review packet checks stood in for it.

Overrides and email. The earlier dossiers for these four names were generated on the 2026-10-23 session; this run replaces them in place with a later session date and a later generation time, as the override rules allow. Email is permanently disabled for watchlist-to-shortlist reports, so no email was sent.

1. The decision at a glance

The table gives each verdict under the repository's three-layer rules. Layer 1 tests the financial floors and the type of business (the archetype), Layer 2 scores three to five operating measures that run that business, and Layer 3 turns the chart into an entry rule and a position size. Reward-to-risk is the gain from the entry price to the base target divided by the loss from the entry price to the stop. The floor is 2.0 to 1 for Core Alpha names and 2.5 to 1 for tactical and turnaround names, so a name that clears the floor only at a lower price needs a limit order, not a market purchase. Where the rulebook has no exact label for a decision, the dossier says which label is nearest and where it does not fit.

What each decision rests on

MercadoLibre. Latin America's largest online marketplace grows gross merchandise volume strongly, but its operating margin is 6.7 percent against 12.2 percent a year ago and the stock has fallen about 7.6 percent since the cycle-1 report after Brazil's election. All three momentum tests fail, and reward-to-risk is 1.96 to 1 at the close, just under the 2.0 floor, so the rule is to wait for a lower price and for the Q3 operating margin. The ledger has no reverse DCF for MELI, so the dossier shows its own (16.4 percent a year) and labels it as its own. The company-adjusted free cash flow margin of 3.2 percent is below the 12 percent floor and the dossier carries it as a documented exception.

ASE Technology. ASE leads in advanced chip packaging, but return on invested capital (10.4 percent) and free cash flow margin (minus 5.3 percent) miss the static floors, so it cannot be a compounder, and reward-to-risk is 0.66 to 1 at the close. It reports Q3 on 2026-10-29 before the US open. The dossier therefore places no sale on 2026-10-28 and sets the sale by the ATM gross margin (the share of packaging and testing revenue left after direct costs): below 28.0 percent sell 42 of 82 shares, 28.0 to 28.9 percent sell 25 at a $44.50 limit, and 29.0 percent or more sell none and re-run the underwriting. The ledger's reverse DCF (minus 35 percent a year) divides a Taiwan-dollar cash flow by a US-dollar market value; the dossier is rendered with a one-currency recalculation (plus 8.5 percent a year).

Qualcomm. The quality floors pass (return on invested capital 25.7 percent, free cash flow margin 23.6 percent, net debt to EBITDA 0.59 times), but the central 12-month value of $184.00 is about the price, so reward-to-risk is 0.05 to 1, and the position is 178 percent of its 3.5 percent ceiling. The dossier sells 13 of 30 shares, keeps 17 into the 2026-11-04 report, and says plainly that the stop is a trigger and not a cap on a gap loss (about 1.5 percent of net worth at the bear value). New since cycle 1: the Huawei licence, the Arm jury verdict and the job-cut memo.

American Express. The business passes every level that applies to a lender (return on equity 33.9 percent, expense growth on watch) and Layer 2 scores 4.5 of 5.0, but the price is $317.94 and reward-to-risk is only 1.62 to 1 at the close. At the $305.00 order price the dossier gets 4.01 to 1 on its central target and 2.03 to 1 on a lower 2023 multiple. Because a fill near $305 sits below the 20-day average, the recovery test would then fail on one condition and the chart would read as a Stage 4 structure, so the dossier makes the buy conditional on a passing closing test instead of leaving a resting order.

Dated triggers: ASX results on 2026-10-29 before the US open; MELI Q3 results provisionally 2026-11-04 (not confirmed by a company notice); QCOM Q4 fiscal 2026 results and fiscal 2027 outlook on 2026-11-04 after the close; AXP Q4 results in late January 2027 (date not announced), with the KBW conference on 2026-11-11 and the Goldman Sachs conference on 2026-12-08 as smaller events.

Run notes and gate exceptions

Stale as-of dates. The citation checker ran with its stale as-of allowance for all four dossiers, because the latest periodic filings (MELI's Q2 10-Q filed 2026-08-06, QCOM's 10-Q filed 2026-07-29, ASE's Q2 results of 2026-07-30 and AXP's most recent 10-Q) are more than 60 days old with no newer filing yet, and because five-year tables and earlier-period records describe closed periods by design. The flagged record counts at the final check are 106 of 177 for MELI, 142 of 205 for ASX, 81 of 106 for QCOM and 35 for AXP. No as-of date was edited, every other check passed (all cited pages are in the fetch cache and every value is present in its cached text), and each dossier lists its exception groups with the cure for each. The cure for the latest-quarter records is the next report: ASX 2026-10-29, MELI provisionally 2026-11-04, QCOM 2026-11-04.

Ledger reverse DCF. The ledger's reverse DCF for ASX (minus 35.0 percent a year, free cash flow yield minus 108 percent) divides a Taiwan-dollar cash flow by a US-dollar market capitalisation. The ASX dossier was rendered from a copy of the ledger in which only the ASX reverse-DCF entry was replaced by the one-currency recalculation (plus 8.47 percent a year at NT$32.54 per US$), and its review packet used the same copy; every other ledger field is unchanged and the dossier discloses the replacement. The ledger has no reverse DCF or consensus-revision entry for MELI at all; the MELI dossier shows N/A in those rows and a separately labelled working of its own (16.4 percent a year).

Builder display limits and validator. The dossier builder has no slot for text between an H3 heading and its table, so several lead-in paragraphs sit at the end of the paragraph above the heading, and it prints the scoring file's raw stop-rule text in the Layer 3 table, which the dossiers explain in a paragraph afterwards. The packet's ticker check reads "MA" in that text (a moving average) as Mastercard in every dossier, a false positive the reviewers cleared. The shared structural validator supports only market reports and portfolio audits, so it was not run on the dossiers (readability, citation and the Research Desk layout check were). No repository script was modified.

Post-cap edits. After round 2 the AXP entry plan was changed (conditional buy, no standing orders) and the ASX Chapter 7 lost one unsourced after-hours price. Neither edit was re-reviewed. The readability, citation and layout checks were rerun on all four dossiers after the last edit.

Source gaps. MELI: Zacks, Seeking Alpha and MarketScreener revision pages were blocked, so the price-target trend replaces 90-day consensus revisions, and the BBB- rating is cited through a Cbonds summary of S&P's action. ASX: the ASE and Amkor investor sites returned 403 (the ASE calendar and the Amkor release were read through other copies), JCET margins are N/A, and the non-LEAP outlook is 20 percent on the filed slide against 30 percent in the transcript. QCOM: FY2028 consensus EPS, the Huawei licence terms and the job-cut count are not published, and the $11.50 FY2028 EPS is the dossier's own estimate. AXP: Visa's 2026-10-27 release could not be fetched, ROTCE is not published and FY2028 EPS is not in free sources. None of these figures was filled from memory.

Holdings. The holdings transaction log is treated as accurate under the repository rule (the ledger was built with the stale-holdings allowance).

Business and financial read-through

RNR earns through underwriting, investment income and fees from owned and managed capital. FY2025 was an unusually strong year: operating ROE was 18.2%, adjusted combined ratio 85.4%, underwriting income $1.3 billion, net investment income $1.7 billion, fee income $328.9 million and tangible book value per share plus accumulated dividends grew 30.8%. These are historical figures and should not be projected as a normalized run rate.[4]

The Q3 earnings release reports 18.9% annualized operating ROE and $879.8 million of mark-to-market losses, mainly tied to fixed-maturity investments.[1] Q3 2026 showed both sides of the model: reported combined ratio was 79.7% and operating income was $512.6 million, while GAAP earnings were a $161.4 million loss. The Casualty & Specialty adjusted combined ratio was 109.5%, including 10.5 points from large loss events. The CEO said year-to-date tangible book value plus accumulated dividends had risen almost 6% despite a 1.9% quarterly decline; the CFO forecast about $325M retained investment income in Q4, a guide that was not yet an actual result at the session cutoff.[1][3]

Renewal pricing is an additional pressure. A secondary market report described a 16% global property-catastrophe rate-on-line decrease by July 2026; RNR’s Q3 call also discussed softer property pricing. This is broad market context, not a measured RNR renewal result. The peer releases show that quarter-level ratios can diverge substantially with business mix, catastrophe activity and reserve development, so peer figures are not directly ranked.[21][22][23][24]

RNR has three main sources of earnings: risk premiums, bond income and fees. Each can help or hurt. A storm can drive up claims. Higher bond yields can lift future income. Those same rate moves can lower current book value. Long claims can take years to settle. This is why one strong quarter is not enough. Investors need to see steady book-value growth across good and bad periods. The next section turns these trade-offs into a capital decision.

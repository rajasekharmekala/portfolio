1. The decision at a glance

The table gives each verdict under the repository's three-layer rules. Layer 1 tests the financial floors and the type of business (the archetype), Layer 2 scores three to five operating measures that run that business, and Layer 3 turns the chart into an entry rule and a position size. Reward-to-risk is the gain from the entry price to the base target divided by the loss from the entry price to the stop. The floor is 2.0 to 1 for Core Alpha names and 2.5 to 1 for tactical and turnaround names, so a name that clears the floor only at a lower price needs a limit order, not a market purchase. Where the rulebook has no exact label for a decision, the dossier says which label is nearest and where it does not fit.

What each decision rests on

Astera Labs. The chip maker sells the connectivity silicon that links AI processors: revenue grew 104 percent in the June quarter, the free cash flow margin is 23.0 percent and net cash is $1,253.0 million. It fails the allocation test for three reasons. Stock-based compensation is 16.2 percent of revenue against the 15 percent cap for tactical names; one customer is more than 70 percent of 2025 sales, which scores a FAIL on the customer KPI; and the $256.40 base target (40 times consensus 2027 earnings of $6.41) sits below the $286.69 close. The chart is a Stage 3 pullback above a rising 200-day average, 1.62 average true range (ATR, the typical daily swing) above support, against 0.16 in the October 23 report, so the stock ran up 11 percent without the business changing. The reverse DCF, which asks what yearly growth today's price assumes for ten years, is 34.9 percent on filed cash flow. The ledger carried no reverse-DCF or consensus block for ALAB this run, so the dossier states that figure as its own rebuild.

D.R. Horton. The homebuilder has low debt (net debt to EBITDA of 1.15 times, or 0.79 times without its mortgage arm) and a cash flow that implies no growth at all in the price: the reverse DCF is about 0.0 percent a year. The base target fell from $150.75 to $132.57 because the dossier anchors to the three-year median trailing multiple of 11.89 times (its own calculation from daily closes, since no source publishes one) instead of the 13.52-times average used on October 23. At the one-year median of 13.59 times the base target is $151.53 and the weighted value is $137.27, still 6.6 percent below the close. Return on invested capital of 10.41 percent and a free cash flow margin of 9.60 percent miss the 12 percent floors. The five calls are worth about $350 at a delayed midpoint of $0.70, none of the three price scenarios reaches the $190 strike, and the dossier says to confirm a live quote before selling.

Strategy. The company is the largest corporate holder of bitcoin (848,517 coins), so the usual floors do not apply and the dossier adapts them: senior claims are 29.3 percent of bitcoin value and the dollar reserve covers 3.2 years of preferred dividends and interest. Layer 2 now scores 2.5 of 5.0 and fails, down from 3.5, because the bitcoin yield KPI (growth in bitcoin per share) is down about 3.9 percent for 2026 by the dossier's own arithmetic. That reading is provisional: the company's own figure arrives with its 2026-10-29 report, and a positive figure restores PASS_WITH_WATCH. The enterprise-value multiple to bitcoin (mNAV) is 1.09 times, the price implies bitcoin near $88,110 against $80,805 at the close, and the weighted value of $150.80 depends on a judgmental bull weight (moving ten points from bull to bear gives $132.64). The sale of 11 shares brings the position to 1.72 percent of net worth, under the 2.00 percent ceiling. You also hold MSTX, a 2x MSTR fund with its own dossier; counted at twice its weight the combined MSTR exposure is about 9.7 percent of net worth.

Vertiv. The maker of data-center power and cooling gear passes Layer 1 as a compounder (return on invested capital of 22.9 percent, trailing adjusted free cash flow margin of 25.5 percent, net cash at 2026-06-30). The ledger labels the chart Stage 4, but the exact red line is not met because the 200-day average is still rising ($269.49 against $245.71 sixty-three sessions earlier). Run notes and gate exceptions

Stale source dates. The citation checker ran with its stale as-of allowance for all four dossiers, because the latest periodic filings (the quarters ended 2026-06-30 or 2026-07-26) are more than 60 days old and the next reports are not yet filed, and because five-year tables describe closed periods by design. The flagged record counts are 91 for ALAB, 103 for DHI, 36 for MSTR and 74 for VRT. No as-of date was edited, and each dossier lists its exception groups with the cure for each.

Ledger copies and fundamentals. The 2026-10-27 ledger held no reverse-DCF or consensus data for ALAB and DHI. For VRT its valuation fields were fetched after the 2026-10-28 price move (they match a price near $243, not the $208.56 close). The ALAB and VRT dossiers were rendered from run-local copies of the ledger in which only those blocks were replaced by each dossier's own rebuild, and both say so in their run notes; the original ledger is untouched. DHI and MSTR were rendered from the original ledger and show N/A where it is empty, with the rebuilt figures in the rows below. Other symbols' valuation fields in the same ledger may carry the same problem.

Session date. The run started at 15:32 Eastern on 2026-10-28, before the close and its 30-minute publication allowance, so 2026-10-27 was the latest completed session. By the time the dossiers were finished the 2026-10-28 session had closed. Every price and technical figure stays on the 2026-10-27 bar, and 2026-10-28 facts (the Vertiv results, the MSTR daily-dividend vote, the DHI and MSTR closes, the Federal Reserve decision, a delayed DHI option quote) appear only in labelled post-cutoff passages.

Renderer. The repository assembler skips the first-use glossary definition for text sitting directly inside a paragraph, so the writers wrapped those first uses by hand in their specs. No repository script was modified. The assembler bug should be fixed at its source before the next run. The structural validator has no dossier mode, so the readability check, the citation check and the review-packet checks stood in for it.

Sources that failed. Where a primary page returned an access error (for example, Strategy's own site returned HTTP 403, so the company's mNAV and diluted share count were rebuilt rather than read) the dossier shows the figure as rebuilt or not published. Vertiv does not publish customer concentration or liquid-cooling revenue, and DHI's fiscal 2028 consensus is not published on the free pages checked.

Overrides and email. The earlier dossiers for these four names were generated on the 2026-10-23 session; this run replaces them in place with a later session date and a later generation time, as the override rules allow. Email is permanently disabled for watchlist-to-shortlist reports, so no email was sent.

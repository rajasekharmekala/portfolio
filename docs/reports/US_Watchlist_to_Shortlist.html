1. The decision at a glance

The table gives each verdict under the repository's three-layer rules. Layer 1 tests the financial floors and the type of business (the archetype), Layer 2 scores 3 to 5 operating measures that run that business, and Layer 3 turns the chart into an entry rule. Reward-to-risk is the gain from the entry price to the base target divided by the loss from the entry price to the stop. The floor is 2.0 to 1 for Core Alpha names and 2.5 to 1 for turnaround names, so a name that clears the floor only at a lower price needs a limit order, not a market purchase.

What each decision rests on

Adobe. The creative and document software maker passes every Layer 1 floor: return on invested capital of 40.1 percent, free cash flow margin of 40.8 percent, net debt of 0.07 times earnings and stock compensation of 7.97 percent of revenue, which passes the 8 percent line by only 0.03 points. The hinge is net new annual recurring revenue (ARR), the yearly added subscription base: $0.40 billion in the third quarter, down 36 to 37 percent, and the fourth quarter needs $0.78 billion to hit the 10.2 percent target. A new chief executive starts on 2026-12-01 and results are expected about 2026-12-09 (an estimate, not confirmed). The chart improved from Stage 3 to Stage 1 (basing) and is 0.23 ATR above its 20-day average, but reward-to-risk is 1.66 to 1 at the close and passes 2.0 only at $244.50 or lower.

UnitedHealth. The managed-care group qualifies as a turnaround, not a compounder: its free cash flow margin of 5.08 percent misses the 12 percent floor, which for an insurer is structural because most of its revenue passes through as premiums. Three improving signals hold the turnaround case: the medical care ratio fell to 88.4 percent from 89.9 percent (89.1 percent against 90.0 percent excluding a $610 million reserve release), debt fell, and consensus estimates rose 3.9 percent in 90 days. Membership shrank 3.6 percent and Justice Department matters remain open. The close is 4.33 ATR above support, so reward-to-risk is only 0.71 to 1 today and passes the 2.5 floor at $375.85 only if the multiple stays near 19.4 times.

NVIDIA. The AI chip leader has Data Center revenue of $89.0 billion (up 117 percent), a 75.0 percent gross margin and a 41.9 percent free cash flow margin. Offsetting risks are $279 billion of supply commitments, up to $108.5 billion of customer guarantees and five customers holding 70 percent of receivables. Reward-to-risk is a thin 2.11 to 1 at the close and 3.10 to 1 at the zone top, but the chart is a Stage 3 pullback with the momentum gate at NO_MOMENTUM. Cash of $2,780.20 is already under the 5 percent policy floor, so the half-size starter waits for a funding decision.

Microsoft. The software and cloud group has Azure growth of 43 percent, a $678 billion commercial backlog and an operating margin of 46.8 percent. Capital spending takes 34.9 percent of revenue, so free cash flow margin is 20.2 percent as reported and 12.8 percent after finance leases. The price is at support (0.21 ATR), but reward-to-risk is 1.89 to 1 at the close, and the report after the close on 2026-10-28 can gap the stock through the $499.24 stop: after-hours moves were minus 7 percent in January and plus 8 percent in July. No new capital goes in before the result; the plan is rescored on 2026-10-29.

Builder wording. A few table cells and headings are emitted by the dossier builder and cannot be edited in a run: the Layer 3 stop-formula and shorthand cells, the 3.5 percent tier ceiling beside smaller actual first purchases (UNH 2.1 percent, MSFT 3.0 percent cap), and some headings that open straight into a table. The dossiers explain these in the surrounding text, and the reviewers accepted that as a builder limit.

Structural validator. The repository's structural validator has no dossier mode, so the readability check, the citation check and the review packet checks stood in for it.

Sources and positions. Some aggregator pages were read intraday on 2026-10-28, after the ledger close, and are labelled as such; the Adobe datasheet PDF could not be read, so the net new ARR decline rests on a statement in the third-quarter call.

Start here: how to use this report

This report is a dated map of market conditions, not a promise about tomorrow and not a personalized order list. It uses the 2026-10-05 closing ledger for equity prices and technical measures. Its central message is “Uptrend (narrow breadth)”: a few leaders are strong, while many shares remain weak. Read the opening and the tactical section in ten minutes if you need the decision first. Read Sections 1 through 5 when you want to understand why the decision follows from the evidence. Section 7 explains which conditional ideas enter research, not which securities should be bought today.

A moving average is the average closing price over a set number of sessions; imagine a rolling path behind a hiker. Price above a rising path suggests buyers have recently had control, but the path is not a floor. Stage 1 means a stock is building a base, Stage 2 means it is advancing, Stage 3 means the advance is tiring, and Stage 4 means it is declining. These are four market seasons, not predictions. RSI is a speedometer from 0 to 100: it describes the pace of recent price moves, not the quality of the business. ATR estimates the stock's normal daily wobble; it helps set a stop far enough away to avoid ordinary noise, but it cannot prevent a gap through that stop.

Volume is the number of shares traded, like the number of votes behind a move. A 1.5× relative-volume threshold means today's volume is at least one and a half times its recent average; below that, a breakout has less confirmation. A stop-loss is a pre-decided exit level, like a fire door: it limits planned loss but does not guarantee the execution price. Risk/reward divides the possible gain from entry to target by the planned loss from entry to stop. A 3.5:1 result means the chart offers $3.50 of modeled upside for each $1 of modeled downside; it does not make a losing trade impossible. Position size translates that planned loss into a share count so one idea cannot dominate the portfolio's risk.

The put/call ratio compares option trading in puts and calls and is a rough sentiment gauge, not a direct forecast. Open-interest shelves are strikes where many option contracts remain open; they may attract attention, but they are not verified dealer gamma walls. This report labels them as maximum-open-interest strikes because a true gamma flip requires positions, dealer direction, and option sensitivities that the saved chain does not provide. A regime label summarizes index trend and breadth. In “Uptrend (narrow breadth),” the policy is normal deployment into leaders, no chasing extended prices, and cash at the investor's chosen policy target. That is more restrained than a broad uptrend because only 26.3% of the universe was above its 50-day average.

Verdicts in this report are signal labels, not portfolio-specific instructions. “KEEP” or “HOLD” means the stated trend rule remains intact; “TRIM” means exposure or risk should be reduced under the relevant mandate; “EXIT” means the pre-agreed invalidation condition has occurred; “ACCUMULATE” requires a supported entry rather than enthusiasm. “Core” means a long-horizon fundamental holding and “Tactical” means a smaller, time-sensitive position. This market-intelligence report does not set tax lots, personal limits, or options suitability. A 10-minute reader should take away three rules: do not confuse a rising index with broad participation, do not chase a price far above support, and do not risk money until the setup's trigger and stop are both explicit.

Glossary

ATR: Average True Range, a measure of typical daily price movement. 50-DMA / 200-DMA: average closing price over 50 / 200 sessions. Breadth: the share of a tracked universe above a chosen moving average. Distribution day: a market session meeting the ledger's price-and-volume selling-pressure rule. RSI: Relative Strength Index, a momentum measure. Stage 2: advancing trend; Stage 4: declining trend. Relative volume: current volume compared with a recent average. Open interest: outstanding option contracts. R:R: modeled reward divided by modeled risk. Trading stop: a planned exit level that may execute worse after a gap. Conditional setup: an idea awaiting price, volume, event, and fundamental confirmation.

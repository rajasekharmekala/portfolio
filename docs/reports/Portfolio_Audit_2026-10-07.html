This audit is a decision aid for the Zerodha share portfolio recorded in the copied holdings file. It values each holding at the fresh market-facts ledger close for 7 October 2026. That one-session delay matters: prices and technical levels can move before an order is placed. The first practical step after reading is therefore to refresh every price and stop before acting.

Three separate analyses inform the decision. The market specialist reads the broad market and sector backdrop; that is like checking the weather before choosing a route. The holdings auditor looks at each position, its account weight, price structure and stop distance. The greenfield allocator ignores the holdings and asks what it would buy if the entire net worth were in cash; this removes the temptation to defend a position simply because it is already owned. The chief investment officer (CIO), the person responsible for reconciling the independent views, then checks them against the written rules. Independent reviews reduce one kind of bias, but they do not make missing facts appear or turn a judgment call into a mechanical rule.

A moving average is the average closing price over a fixed number of sessions, like the average speed on a long drive: it smooths daily noise and helps show direction. A day moving average (DMA) is that same average with its window counted in market days; for example, 20-DMA is the average closing price over the latest 20 trading sessions. Stage 1 through Stage 4 are the seasons of a share price: base, advance, topping, and decline. The relative strength index (RSI) is a speedometer for recent price movement; a low reading says the fall has been fast, not that a rebound is certain. Average true range (ATR) is the stock’s typical daily wobble; it helps place a stop far enough away to avoid ordinary noise. Volume counts shares traded, a rough measure of how many investors took part. Relative strength compares a stock’s performance with the index; it is not the same as RSI. A stop is a pre-decided exit level, while money at risk is the distance from the close to that stop multiplied by shares. Gaps and poor liquidity can make the actual loss larger.

HOLD / HARD STOP means keep the shares for now but exit or reassess if the stated stop is reached. HOLD / TRAIL means keep them while a rising technical anchor remains intact and move the stop as that anchor rises. TRIM means reduce a position only when a rule or approved target weight specifies how much. EXIT means sell the whole position after verifying the quote and the stated reason. NO TRADE / WATCH means a candidate does not qualify for a purchase today. The single-stock cap is 20% of net worth, except that a winning Stage 2 holding above its rising 20-day average and 21-day EMA (an exponential moving average that weights recent prices more heavily), with relative strength above 80 and RSI below 82, is protected from trimming into strength; instead, its stop is ratcheted to the 21-day EMA.

Core Alpha is intended for durable businesses held over years; Tactical Momentum is for shorter Stage 2 advances with stronger technical confirmation. Their normal target weights do not override a Downtrend-specific cash instruction. Risk/reward compares a possible gain with a possible loss, like comparing a prize with the amount at stake. Asymmetry is the size of that possible reward relative to the possible loss after estimated taxes and trading friction. A 3-condition swap needs a triggered exit or trim, a fully qualified challenger at support, and challenger asymmetry at least 1.5 times the holding’s after-tax asymmetry. Chapter 3: Greenfield Shortlist Allocation Under a Downtrend

A greenfield allocation asks a deliberately simple question: if the entire account were available as cash today, which screened companies would earn a new position, and how much money would each receive? The exercise is kept separate from what is already owned so that familiarity, a low purchase price, or a paper gain cannot make an existing holding look more attractive than a fresh alternative. Here the independent allocator received a sanitized candidate list and the account capital base, while remaining blind to positions, cost bases, and profit or loss. Its job was to judge the candidates as if the portfolio started in cash. That separation is useful even when the answer is no purchase, because it makes clear that the decision came from the market and candidate rules rather than loyalty to an incumbent.

The valuation anchor is the latest completed session represented in the supplied ledger, 7 October 2026. The ledger classifies the broad market as a Downtrend. Its breadth reading says 23.3% of the 497-stock pool stood above the 50-day average and 38.0% above the 200-day average; it recorded ten distribution days over the measured 25-session window. These are not predictions of the next session. They describe weak participation at the audit date: fewer stocks and sectors were positioned above their intermediate trend references. The allocator therefore follows the explicit Downtrend instruction to keep cash at its maximum and start no new momentum longs.

That decision creates a visible tension with the normal strategy-bucket bands. The portfolio-audit workflow’s normal Indian target bands are Core Alpha at 30%–40% of net worth, Tactical Momentum at 50%–65%, and cash at 5%–10%. A mechanical attempt to meet those ranges would put new money into risk precisely when breadth and sector participation are weak. The allocator resolves the conflict by applying the regime constraint first. Chapter 4: Shortlist Funnel, Gate Review and Displacement Test

The shortlist funnel is a sequence of questions designed to stop a persuasive company story from skipping the evidence needed for a purchase. First comes the financial screen: does the supplied record indicate acceptable operating quality and balance-sheet or cash-flow health, or does it mark a failed financial gate? Second comes the technical screen: does the 7 October ledger support the stated trend and price posture, including the recorded stage and relationship to the 20-, 50-, and 200-day averages? Third comes the asymmetry screen: is the supplied potential reward large enough relative to the stated risk to pass the strategy’s minimum? A candidate needs more than a single green label to advance. This chapter reports those supplied statuses, but it does not turn screening inputs into independently verified research about each company.

The supplied gate-status mix makes that distinction important. Among the 51 candidates, 39 carry the exact status PASS, five say PASSED, and three say ALL_GATES_PASSED. Four candidates carry explicit failure labels: one fails asymmetry, one fails debt and cash flow, one fails financial quality, and one fails both financial quality and asymmetry. The three wording variants among the apparent passes are preserved rather than normalized, because changing the underlying source label could imply a more consistent review than the files establish. A status of PASS means only that the supplied screen says the relevant gate passed. It is not a guarantee against a price decline, a substitute for reading filings, or permission to buy in a regime that restricts new exposure.

The market facts also show why passing a company-level screen is not enough today. Of the candidate rows with stage labels, 19 are in Stage 2 Markup, 21 are Stage 3 Topping or Pullback, five are Stage 4 Markdown, four are Stage 1 Basing, one has insufficient technical history, and one lacks an instrument row in the supplied ledger. Stage labels describe the pattern in the price record, not the quality of the underlying business. They are also distinct from entry posture: a name can be near a proposed support area while its broader chart is still in a topping phase, or a Stage 2 label can coexist with an extended entry. In this review, all the candidate postures are evaluated under a Downtrend market label. That macro state blocks new momentum longs and directs maximum cash, even when the candidate has strong relative setup fields.

Asymmetry is the third gate, but the figures in the shortlist are supplied screen values. They are not independently recalculated here because the complete underlying derivation, including all entry, stop, target, and friction assumptions, is not part of the approved inputs for this assignment. Where the source marks a failure, the table says so; where a ratio appears, it is retained as supplied and should be treated as a screening value, not an audited forecast. Under the standard rules, a tactical idea needs at least 3.5 to 1 and a core idea at least 3 to 1, with a challenger also needing to be strictly at support before it can displace a triggered holding. The present allocation has no new buys, so an attractive ratio cannot become a trade by itself.

The displacement test matters because replacement trades have costs and risks. A challenger should replace a current holding only if the holding has first triggered its own exit or trim condition, the challenger clears every required gate and is at support within the stated tolerance, and the challenger’s asymmetry exceeds the holding’s by at least 1.5 times after taxes and trading friction. These are separate conditions; one cannot compensate for another. For example, a candidate that passes financial checks but remains 0.8 ATR from its support zone does not satisfy the support condition. A high ratio also cannot justify replacing an incumbent that has not met an exit or trim rule. In this chapter we cannot name a replacement trade because no new allocation is authorized, and we do not import holding-level facts into this candidate-only funnel.

The dated research record separately contains 20 NSE security-wise delivery observations for the market-intelligence Bull and Bear screen picks. Those figures describe deliverable shares as a percentage of traded shares on 7 October; they are trading-activity context, not a fundamental-quality measure and not a standalone purchase trigger. Because the delivery screen and this 51-name shortlist are different evidence sets, those delivery readings do not repair a failed gate or alter the greenfield allocation. The source is the NSE 7 October security-wise delivery file; this chapter uses it only to keep those data categories separate.

The same discipline applies to cases where a company’s supplied data appear attractive. The shortlist can include clean leverage, improving cash conversion, a large order book, a product moat, or an appealing catalyst, but this task’s approved source fields do not independently verify those narratives. The table therefore stays close to observed facts: the gate code, strategy bucket, entry posture, dated ledger snapshot, and supplied asymmetry value. This prevents a polished company description from being mistaken for proof. It also ensures that missing technical coverage is visible rather than silently filled with a guess. In particular, MEDANTA has no corresponding row in the supplied facts ledger and must not receive an invented current close, moving-average comparison, or RSI.

Other candidates may deserve renewed attention after the market regime improves, but their current entry condition and ledger data would need to be refreshed at that time. The funnel’s output is thus “no displacement, no new buy, retain maximum cash,” with a later review conditional on the explicit triggers below.

Chapter 7: Rulebook and monitoring

The audit’s rules are meant to keep decisions consistent when prices and emotions move quickly. They do not replace judgment; they tell you where judgment is required. The first rule is to keep each reported figure tied to one session date and refresh it before trading. The second is to apply the stop formula by strategy bucket, not to move a stop farther away merely because a holding is down. For Core Alpha, the stop is the lower of recent swing low, 50-day average and 200-day average, minus one ATR. For Tactical Momentum, it is the lower of swing low, 20-day average and 50-day average, minus half an ATR. If an average is unavailable, it is omitted and the gap is disclosed.

The third rule is the 20% concentration ceiling with its narrow exception. A winner that remains in Stage 2, above a rising 20-day average and 21-day EMA, has relative strength above 80 and RSI below 82 should not be cut just to satisfy the cap; its stop is ratcheted to the rising 21-day EMA. That exception currently prevents a definitive RNITAI trim because the facts ledger does not include current RS or 21-EMA slope. The fourth rule is the three-condition swap: the current holding must trigger an exit or trim, the challenger must pass all gates and sit within half an ATR of verified support, and its after-tax risk/reward must exceed the holding by at least 1.5 times. No candidate passes the independent greenfield allocator’s Downtrend cash veto, so none is a replacement today.

The fifth rule is to keep market posture and normal bucket targets separate. The usual 5%–10% cash target is not appropriate as an automatic minimum when the same-date market report says Downtrend and recommends 60%–80% cash. Yet a strategic target must not be converted into arbitrary sales where the rules do not specify which lots to liquidate. The current bridge gets only to 13.63% cash through the three proposed exits, or 17.26% if RNITAI’s exception fails and the excess is trimmed. That gap requires a new explicit portfolio-wide reduction plan, a tax-lot review, and current-session valuations. Glossary

A 20-day, 50-day or 200-day moving average is the mean closing price across that number of trading sessions. A 21-day EMA is an exponential moving average that gives more weight to recent prices. ATR, or average true range, estimates normal daily movement; it is not a forecast of maximum loss. RSI, or relative strength index, measures the pace of recent gains and losses on a 0–100 scale. Relative strength (RS) compares a stock’s performance with a benchmark and is a different measure from RSI. Stage 2 describes an advancing trend; Stage 3 a topping or transition phase; Stage 4 a declining trend. A cash waterfall reconciles proceeds and spending step by step. A pledge is shares offered as collateral by a promoter; it can create forced-sale risk if collateral terms are breached. A result calendar lists expected board meetings or financial disclosures. “Not found” means the search did not verify a claim, not that the event or risk does not exist.

Glossary of Institutional Investment & Forensics Terms

To demystify complex Wall Street jargon, the following glossary provides clear, plain-English definitions accompanied by intuitive everyday analogies:

Return on Invested Capital (ROIC)
A percentage measuring how efficiently a company allocates capital to generate profits. Analogy: If you invest $100 in an espresso machine for your cafe and it generates $30 in pure annual profit after all expenses, your ROIC is 30%. High ROIC is the primary engine of long-term stock compounding.
Free Cash Flow (FCF) Conversion
The ratio of actual cash generated from operations to reported accounting net income (CFO / NI). Analogy: If an employee earns an official salary of $100,000 on paper but only takes home $50,000 in cash due to withholdings and non-cash perks, their cash conversion is weak. In companies, we demand a ratio above 0.70x to ensure reported earnings are authentic cash.
Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) Dilution
The practice of paying executive and engineering salaries in newly minted shares of stock rather than cash. Analogy: Imagine owning a pizza sliced into 8 pieces. If management bakes no new pizza but cuts the pie into 12 smaller slices to give 4 away to employees, your original slice is smaller. High SBC quietly dilutes existing shareholders.
Weinstein Stage Analysis (Stage 2 Markup vs. Stage 4 Markdown)
A classic technical framework classifying stock trends into four distinct cycles: Stage 1 (Basing), Stage 2 (Advancing/Markup), Stage 3 (Distribution/Topping), and Stage 4 (Declining/Markdown). Analogy: Stage 2 is like sailing with a strong tailwind pushing your boat smoothly forward. Stage 4 is sailing directly into a hurricane where every wave knocks you backward.
Moving Averages (20-DMA, 50-DMA, 200-DMA)
The average closing price of a stock over the past 20, 50, or 200 trading days, creating a smoothed trendline. Analogy: Like a moving radar beacon that smooths out daily sea waves to show the true ocean tide. A rising moving average indicates institutional accumulation.
Average True Range (ATR)
A volatility metric measuring the average dollar distance a stock moves from high to low over the past 14 trading sessions. Analogy: Like measuring a person's average stride length. Knowing a stock's ATR allows us to place stops far enough away that normal daily breathing room does not trigger an accidental exit.
Daily-Reset Compounding Decay (Volatility Drag)
The mathematical loss suffered by leveraged ETFs when an asset fluctuates up and down in a choppy, sideways market. Analogy: If you start with $100, gain 10% on Monday (reaching $110), and lose 10% on Tuesday, you end at $99—not $100. When leveraged 2x or 3x, this daily math quietly consumes your capital like a hidden leak in a water bucket.
Net Gamma Exposure (GEX) & Gamma Walls
A quantitative options metric measuring how options market makers must hedge their books as the underlying stock price moves. Analogy: A massive strike with huge open call interest acts like a giant magnetic wall. As the stock approaches it, market makers must sell stock to stay hedged, creating strong price resistance.
CBOE Put/Call Ratio
The total volume of bearish put options traded divided by bullish call options traded. Analogy: Like checking a stadium crowd's betting slips. When the equity put/call ratio drops below 0.60, it indicates extreme complacency and bullish euphoria, warning of potential short-term pullbacks.
LEAPS Options (Long-Term Equity Anticipation Securities)
Standardized call or put option contracts with expiration dates extending more than nine months into the future (up to 2.5 years). Analogy: Like buying a long-term architectural option to purchase a prime piece of real estate at today's price anytime over the next two years, requiring only a fraction of the full property cost upfront.
Theta (Time Decay)
The rate at which an option contract loses value each day as it approaches its expiration date. Analogy: Like an ice cube melting on a warm table. Theta decay is gentle when the ice cube is large (far from expiration), but melts with terrifying speed in the final 30 days.
Reward-to-Risk Asymmetry Ratio
The ratio comparing the potential dollar profit to a realistic target against the potential dollar loss if the stop loss is triggered. Analogy: Flipping a coin where heads wins you $3.50 and tails loses you only $1.00. By demanding at least a 3.5:1 ratio, you can be wrong half the time and still achieve massive long-term wealth.
Good 'Til Canceled (GTC) & Good 'Til Triggered (GTT)
Brokerage order instructions specifying that an order remains active across multiple trading days until it either executes or is manually canceled. Analogy: Leaving a standing instruction with your assistant to buy groceries if the store drops prices by 20%, rather than asking them fresh every single morning.
Trailing Stop-Loss Order
An automated sell order that automatically ratchets upward as a stock's price rises, maintaining a fixed dollar or percentage distance behind the peak price. Analogy: A climbing ratchet on a mountain harness. It allows you to climb as high as you want, but the second you slip, it locks firmly in place to prevent a fall.
Greenfield Unconstrained Allocation
An investment modeling exercise where a portfolio manager pretends they hold 100% cash and re-underwrites the optimal asset mix from scratch without any bias toward existing holdings. Analogy: Renovating a home by clearing the entire floor plan to design the optimal dream layout, rather than awkwardly arranging furniture around an old, broken chair.

How the business earns and where growth comes from

Private Wealth is the main engine. Relationship managers work with high-net-worth families, while centralized research, product and portfolio teams help build and monitor portfolios. Client assets stay in the client’s accounts. Anand Rathi earns distribution and service fees, so revenue grows when assets rise, clients add money and the product mix generates recurring trails. Trail revenue increased from ₹267 crore in FY24 to ₹494 crore in FY26. That recurring stream improves visibility, but it still depends on market levels, client allocation and retention.[6]

Two channels extend the reach of the model. Digital Wealth serves a broader customer base through a business-to-business-to-consumer route. Q2 FY27 digital AUM was ₹2,531 crore, up 14%, and clients numbered 7,584, up 15%. Omni Financial Advisor (OFA) sells software and service tools to independent advisers. At FY26 year-end it reported 6,906 subscribers, 23.52 lakh platform clients and ₹1,47,000+ crore of platform assets. Those assets belong to the advisers’ clients and must not be added to group AUM. Separate profit and retention data for these channels remain limited.[2][6]

New money and client retention are the key operating tests. Q2 FY27 net flows reached ₹4,186 crore, up 39% year over year; equity mutual-fund flows were ₹2,867 crore, also up 39%. The company added 1,500 net new families over the trailing year and reported AUM attrition of 0.17% for Q2 and 0.26% for H1. These measures point to demand and retention, but they are company-reported and do not show cohort-level client longevity, flow mix by channel or profit per new family. Future filings should keep the same definitions and add cohort and adviser-productivity measures.[3]

The business has a people constraint as well as a capital-market constraint. Relationship managers carry trust and service quality, and a larger network needs training and oversight. OFA and digital tools may extend reach without matching headcount growth, but the company does not yet report channel-level margins that prove this operating leverage. Investors should track family growth, AUM attrition, trail revenue, RM productivity and the paid scale of OFA together rather than treating asset growth alone as proof of better economics.

Listed peers offer useful models but imperfect financial comparisons. 360 ONE combines wealth, family-office and estate services. Nuvama spans mass-affluent distribution, private wealth and capital markets. Motilal Oswal operates wealth beside broking, investment banking, asset management, private equity and home finance. Anand Rathi’s narrower focus may support clearer wealth metrics, while leaving results more exposed to client flows and RM retention. Group multiples are not directly comparable without same-cutoff segment data. The next check is whether execution supports the premium valuation.[12][13][14]

Management delivery and issues to monitor

The company met and exceeded its previous FY26 plan. The earlier targets were ₹1,175 crore of revenue and ₹375 crore of PAT. Actual adjusted revenue was about ₹1,198 crore and adjusted PAT was ₹386 crore. FY27 targets are ₹1,415 crore of revenue and ₹460 crore of PAT. H1 delivery is 49% of revenue and 52% of PAT goals. The comparison base matters: ₹460 crore implies about 19% growth over FY26 adjusted PAT, but about 23% against the earlier ₹375 crore guide. The next filing should show reported and adjusted profit together and explain the bridge.[6][8][9]

Q2 adjusted PAT rose to ₹121.9 crore from ₹99.8 crore, while statutory reported PAT fell to ₹89.3 crore from ₹99.8 crore. The gap is not a minor presentation detail at this valuation. FY26 reported PAT was ₹397.17 crore and adjusted PAT was ₹385.73 crore, illustrating that accounting and ESOP effects can alter the comparison in different periods. Reported and adjusted figures should stay side by side, with the exact adjustments named and held consistent across future quarters.[1][6][7]

Product outcomes create a trust test. Management said 1,190 consecutive structured products had met their expected returns until a run ended in 2026; some recent products returned 6–7% against an expected 14–15%. Management also said none of its products had lost money at maturity since issuance began in 2012. These are management statements rather than an independent product-by-product record. A return shortfall can still weaken client confidence, even when principal is returned. Net flows, client attrition and future product maturities will show whether that risk stays contained.[3]

The FY27 mutual-fund licence application was described as in preparation, with filing expected by late October. No confirmation was recorded by the cutoff. Treat the licence as an option, not a current earnings driver, until an exchange filing or regulator record confirms the status and operating plan. UK and GIFT City initiatives are also excluded from the scenario math. This keeps the valuation tied to reported assets, flows and earnings rather than unverified future channels.[3][17]

Balance-sheet risk looks manageable on the audited FY26 figures, although client assets sit outside the company balance sheet and should not be confused with corporate liquidity. Audited consolidated operating cash flow was ₹280.97 crore, year-end cash and equivalents were ₹240.94 crore, and borrowings totaled ₹20.96 crore before separately reported lease liabilities. These figures do not remove the main investment risk: a premium multiple can contract much faster than operating cash flow changes. The valuation review shows what that means for entry.[6][7]

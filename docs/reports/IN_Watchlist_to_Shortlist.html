APLAPOLLO — Stage 2 business, wait for market confirmation

On October 30, APL Apollo closed at ₹2,165.10, just above its rising 50-day average of ₹2,161.21 and above its 20-day average of ₹2,128.27 and 200-day average of ₹2,010.67. The market-facts ledger classifies the stock as Stage 2. The Nifty 50, by contrast, closed at 23,053.10 below its 50-day average of 23,254.75 and 200-day average of 24,041.15; only 19.0% of the screened pool stood above its 50-day average. The stock setup is constructive, but the market gate fails [4].

Q2 FY27 sales volume was 963,143 tonnes, up 13% year over year and 29% quarter over quarter. EBITDA was ₹5.8 billion, EBITDA per tonne ₹5,999 (+15% year over year), and net profit ₹3.8 billion (+27%). The H1 comparison is more measured: volume was 1,707,966 tonnes, +4%, while revenue grew 22%, EBITDA 21%, and profit after tax 20%. H1 EBITDA per tonne was ₹5,791. The gap between a strong quarter and modest half-year volume is why the next quarter matters [1][2][3].

Mix supports the margin story but also exposes regional risk. H1 APL Apollo Brand volume was 1,343,442 tonnes, roofing 226,771, SG Premium 85,596 and UAE operations 52,156. UAE volume was 52,156 tonnes, down from 106,093 tonnes in H1 FY26 [2]. CEO Sanjay Gupta maintained 15% growth guidance for profitable branded and roofing products, while saying the company could not commit volume growth in lower-margin categories amid Dubai disruption and domestic steel shortage. The 15% guidance applies to those profitable categories; it is not a promise for total company volume [2][3][5].

Operating quality is the strongest part of the case. The company’s Q2 presentation reports zero net working-capital days, ₹16.3 billion of net cash, annualized H1 ROCE of 39.7% and ROE of 27.7%. H1 growth in earnings and EBITDA per tonne exceeded volume growth. These are company-presented figures, and the return ratios are annualized from H1 rather than full-year outcomes. They should be checked again after H2 cash flow and capex [1][4].

Capital allocation is the next proof point. Existing capacity is reported at 5 million tonnes, with an 8 million tonne target by FY28 that combines 2 million tonnes of greenfield/brownfield additions and 1 million tonne of debottlenecking. The plan is not yet productive capacity. Evidence should include commissioning dates, utilization, incremental return on capital and cash after capex. If capacity expands faster than demand, operating leverage can reverse and net cash can erode [4].

Management statements are kept separate by speaker and status. The CEO’s 15% profitable-product volume target is forward guidance. Chief Strategy Officer Anubhav Gupta said the company was on track for 20% absolute EBITDA growth by March 2027; that is also a target, not a result or CFO confirmation. Chetan Khandelwal is listed as CFO in the latest call transcript, but the transcript contains no CFO speaking turn. The CEO also acknowledged that power cost remains ₹6.2–₹6.3 per unit against a ₹5 target and shifted the target to the next year. That is a disclosed delay, and the next check is delivered cost [5].

The valuation screen has a meaningful limitation. APL closed at ₹2,165.10 against audited FY26 diluted EPS of ₹43.34, or about 50 times that annual EPS. This is a simple analyst ratio, not a current trailing multiple; it uses a March 31 earnings denominator because the Q2 release does not provide fresh EPS. We omit current EV/Sales, synchronized peer P/E and cutoff-matched consumer metrics. Scenario values therefore remain sensitivities: the base case assumes 15% EPS growth and 50x, producing roughly ₹2,500; the bear assumes a 10% decline and 37.2x, near ₹1,450; the bull assumes 25% growth and 52.5x, near ₹2,850. Growth rates and multiples are analyst assumptions, not consensus [6]. A one-factor check keeps base EPS at ₹49.84 and uses the bear 37.2x multiple. That gives about ₹1,854, or 14.4% below the close. Only the multiple changes; this is not fair value.

At the lower support edge, the base target against the ₹1,960 structural reference gives about 2.21:1 before costs. At the upper edge, the ratio is about 1.68:1, and at the current close about 1.63:1. Even the proposed zone does not authorize an order by itself. The Nifty trend, support hold, fresh EPS and a reward-to-risk ratio of at least 2.0:1 must all pass.

The ₹1,960 stop is a screen reference. The October 30 20-day swing low was ₹1,994.10 and ATR14 was ₹65.73. Subtracting half the ATR gives ₹1,961.24, rounded to ₹1,960. It is not a live sell order.

Operating proof: order visibility exceeds organic proof

Q2 has two different signals. The 23 October Q2 FY27 release reported ₹61,708m revenue, up 53.6% in rupees and 40.1% in dollars. New orders were $1.04bn. The executable book for the next year was $2.38bn, up 45% from a year earlier. Eight large deals closed. This book supports demand, but it is work to deliver, not revenue already earned. On the Q2 earnings call, management reported 10.9% sequential constant-currency growth overall and 3.8% without acquired businesses. That gap shows how much Encora added to the headline.

The order book and client engagement measures justify continued research, not a straight-line revenue forecast. The Q2 call says 86% of revenue sits in AI-led engineering, data and cloud categories, but that is a service classification rather than audited AI revenue. Coforge’s banking, insurance, travel, public-sector and healthcare pages describe workflow delivery and vertical assets; those company pages explain how the work is sold, but they are marketing material. The next test is whether organic growth and utilization convert the order book without diluting margins. That delivery proof feeds the financial quality test next.

Banking clients buy payment, lending and core-system work. Insurers buy claims and policy systems; NEXA is company-marketed. Travel work supports airline and hotel platforms. Coforge also sells government systems and healthcare and life-sciences services. Each vertical requires skilled teams to learn the client’s systems and meet its service targets. Coforge’s descriptions are available on its official banking, insurance, travel, public-sector and healthcare pages. The practical economics depend on teams ramping quickly, renewing work and expanding within accounts; the top-ten concentration and utilization figures make execution central to the thesis.

The CEO said firms get the most AI value when they redesign and automate workflows. The CFO said savings in fixed-price work should start appearing in Q4 margin. Those claims can be tested. Coforge has not split out realised savings by AI program. The Investor Day sessions, the quarterly filings and the annual reports give the earlier statements and results for comparison. Until savings appear in reported margins, AI activity counts are leading indicators, not profit proof.

Financial quality, integration and valuation

FY26 grew rapidly before the full Encora combination entered the reported base. The FY26 annual report records revenue of ₹164,027m, EBIT of ₹23,645m and PAT of ₹15,557m; FY25 values were ₹120,733m, ₹12,942m and ₹8,121m. EBIT margin rose from 10.7% to 14.4%. Those audited figures are now more than seven months old and predate Encora consolidation from 1 May 2026. FY26 free cash flow rose to $135.4m from $80.4m, while DSO increased from 60 to 69 days, so cash generation improved but collection time lengthened.

Encora adds scale and execution risk together. The April closing announcement disclosed a $550m three-year loan at 4.6%, consolidation from 1 May and a 20–25% management target for general-and-administrative synergies. The Cigniti closing notice adds another integration stream. These targets are not realized savings. Reported Q2 EBIT margin was 16.0%, and FY27 guidance rose to 16%; management’s Q4 exit-rate expectation above 17% is an ambition to verify. The Investor Day CFO presentation gives earlier margin context, but a post-close balance sheet and cash-flow bridge are still needed.

The valuation model uses FY26 diluted EPS of ₹45.90 and explicit assumptions. Base EPS growth of 16.6% to ₹53.52 multiplied by 45x gives about ₹2,408, rounded to ₹2,400. A 45x base multiple is not a margin of safety; the entry band improves the risk/reward, but even at its top the base case returns 1.17 units for each unit risked.

PENNARIND: the leverage test

Pennar Industries makes pre-engineered buildings, precision tubes and hydraulic parts. Its borrowings rose from 812 crore to 1,162 crore in a year, while shareholders' money stood at 1,163 crore. That gives debt-to-equity of 1.00, far above the limit of below 0.70. The verdict is REJECTED because this is a hard floor, and the rule is that one failed floor ends the evaluation. Return on capital employed clears its floor, but only by 0.2 points, and the report says so. Reopening requires borrowings of about 814 crore or less, against the same net worth, together with a passing momentum test. The full reasoning is in the PENNARIND dossier.

KAYNES: the cash and return tests

Kaynes Technology is an electronics manufacturer that is moving into semiconductor packaging. Over three years it converted negative cash from operations against a positive profit of 840 crore, a cash conversion of -0.71, against a floor of 0.70. Its return on capital employed, 12.7 percent, is below the 15.0 floor as well. The working-capital build behind the cash figure is large, and the dossier shows its size. Debt-to-equity of 0.19 passes. The verdict is REJECTED, and reopening requires both the return and the cash tests to pass, together with a momentum pass.

CAMS: a sound business in a falling trend

CAMS keeps the official records of mutual fund unit holders. Its financial tests pass comfortably: return on capital employed of 47.0 percent, debt-to-equity of 0.05, and cash conversion of 1.14. Its share price, however, has been falling, and its 50-day and 200-day averages are both falling, which is the definition of Stage 4, the phase of sustained decline. The verdict is REJECTED because the technical floor fails. The archive is not a judgment on the business. It records that the gate for buying does not yet open, and the reopen level is a close above 738.32 with a rising 50-day average.

ENDURANCE: the reversal of an earlier view

Endurance Technologies makes aluminium castings, suspensions and braking parts for two-wheelers and cars, with a large European business. Its accounts pass: return on capital employed of 17.8 percent, debt-to-equity of 0.19, and cash conversion of 1.80. The earlier watchlist note read the stock as resting on its 200-day average near 2,595 and recommended accumulating on dips. The 9 October close of 2,407.50 is about 7 percent below that average, and the 50-day average has fallen. The earlier view is therefore withdrawn, and the verdict is REJECTED on the technical floor.

Shortlist changes and email

No company newly passed the three gates, so no company newly joined the shortlist. Displacement applies only when a shortlisted candidate newly beats a holding that you own, and no shortlisted candidate changed in this batch. So there is no displacement to report.

What would change this view: if any of the four names closes above its reopen level and its tests then pass in a re-run, it would return to the watchlist for a fresh underwriting. Reopen levels are in each dossier, and each reopen requires every failed test to pass together.

The email policy for this workflow sends a message only when a company newly qualifies for the shortlist or a shortlisted candidate newly passes the displacement gates. Neither happened here, so no email was sent. The publication itself was not affected by this decision.

What the batch found

Each row gives the verdict, the close used, the entry zone (the price range where we would place a buy order), the stop, our central target and the reward-to-risk ratio. That ratio is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop. A stop is the price at which the idea is wrong. The scoring file asks for at least 2.0 for a core name and 2.5 for a tactical name. Read the ratio at the close together with the ratio at the top of the entry zone. The first is what a buyer gets today. The second is what a limit order would get after a pullback.

KARURVYSYA: a high-return bank whose price asks for patience

Karur Vysya earns a return on equity of 20.47 percent (annualised from the September quarter), a return on assets of 2.10 percent, gross bad loans of 0.69 percent and a capital ratio of 17.97 percent. Debt-to-equity, cash conversion and return on capital employed are not meaningful for a bank, so the dossier uses return on equity, asset quality and capital ratios in their place. Its operating score is 4.0 of 5.0, with three passes (margin, asset quality, return and cost) and two watch rows (credit cost and the gap between loan and deposit growth). Profit in the September quarter rose 37.24 percent, but half of the gain came from lower provisions. The share price is in Stage 2 and passes the recovery test (Test C), yet it is 2.77 ATR (average true range, the typical daily price swing) above its swing low and volume is only 0.48 times the 20-day average. Our base value is ₹400 and the reward-to-risk ratio is 1.09 to 1 at ₹350.20. It reaches 2.53 to 1 only at ₹331.50 or lower. No order rests until a closing retest confirms support, a refreshed ledger confirms at least 2.5 to 1, a price-to-book check at 1.8 times passes, the bank's expected-credit-loss answer needs no charge to profit and the final insurance-distribution rules cost no more than assumed. A close below ₹304.39 ends the idea.

GLENMARK: a debt-free pharma whose profit is inflated by one licensing deal

Glenmark has no gross bank borrowing and interest cover of 21.4 times, and the Screener headline return on capital of 39.8 percent is not decision-grade, because FY26 profit includes about US$560 million of one-time AbbVie licensing income. Our own FY26 figure is 20.4 percent. Three-year cash conversion of 2.41 passes the written rule, but it depends on the US$700 million upfront payment. Its operating score is 3.5 of 5.0 with two passes (India growth and debt) and three watch rows (US plant compliance, including an open warning letter at its Indore plant, the margin path, and licensing-income quality). The price is 2.41 ATR above support, reward-to-risk is 0.19 to 1 at ₹2,265.70, and consensus estimates are falling (FY27 earnings per share down 2.76 percent in 90 days). The plan is observation only until the 5 November results show an operating margin of at least 21 percent with zero borrowings, receivables no higher than March, a refreshed price file without a Stage 4 reading and a refreshed target that still gives 2.0 to 1. A close below ₹2,101.60 cancels it.

SUPRAJIT: a cable and electronics maker with thin static levels

Suprajit passes every red line, but narrowly: return on capital is 15.5 percent, debt-to-equity is 0.69 including leases (0.55 without), and about ₹13 crore of extra borrowing would push the ratio past 0.70. Its operating score is 3.5 of 5.0 with two passes (operating margin against the guided band, and customer concentration) and three watch rows (growth against the Indian auto market, working capital, and overseas subsidiary profit). Estimates are rising (FY27 earnings per share up 11.17 percent in 90 days), but the price already assumes about 13.3 percent annual growth for ten years. Reward-to-risk is 0.84 to 1 at ₹476.65 and 2.07 to 1 at ₹456 on a 22 times multiple, and only 0.52 to 1 at ₹456 on a 20 times multiple. The Q2 results come on 7 November. No order is armed before them, and after them only if reward-to-risk at the actual fill is at least 2.0 on a refreshed target. A close below ₹425.05 ends the idea.

SUNDRMFAST: a low-debt fastener maker whose margin and cash are slipping

Sundram Fasteners passes every red line (debt-to-equity 0.19 at September 2026, no pledge, three-year cash conversion 1.11) with a return on capital of 17.6 percent. Its operating score is exactly 3.0 of 5.0, with two passes (export mix and customer concentration, and new business that does not depend on fuel type), two watch rows (operating margin trend, capacity and capex) and one fail on working-capital days (86 days). Q2 FY27 results, filed after the close on 28 October, showed sales up 30 percent, profit up 13 percent and an operating margin of 14.9 percent against 16.6 percent a year earlier. The ledger bar is the 27 October close of ₹1,059.90, and the share fell on 29 October to an intraday low of ₹993.25, below the ₹1,009.80 support. So the zone and stop must be refreshed before any order. Reward-to-risk is 2.41 to 1 at the close on the central target, but a stressed value of ₹1,176 gives 1.33 to 1 at the close and 2.02 to 1 at ₹1,040, so the zone top is held at ₹1,040. A second failing row, for instance a quarterly operating margin below 14.0 percent, would turn the verdict to REJECTED.

Run notes and gate exceptions

The cure is the Q2 FY27 filings in the first half of November 2026.

Second, prices. The ledger bars for Glenmark, Suprajit and Sundram Fasteners are the 27 October close, one session behind the verified benchmark session, so every price and chart level for those three is labelled as the prior-session bar. Sundram Fasteners also traded below its support on 29 October, after the ledger, so its zone and stop need a refreshed bar. The page builder prints the ledger date beside the price in each dossier's key-figures strip, so the three writers relabelled that cell in a post-render step without editing the repository's scripts.

Third, the data feeds. The ledger's Yahoo reverse DCF and market value were on a different price basis for Glenmark and were replaced by figures recomputed at the close, and for Karur Vysya the ledger's trailing-profit basis (negative 0.47 percent growth) was replaced by a bank-adjusted reading (5.24 percent). Both ledger and own figures are shown in Chapter 5 of the dossiers. Several exchange pages were blocked or scanned images, so affected figures come from locally extracted text or aggregator pages and are labelled in the dossiers.

Fourth, the reviewer cycle. All four dossiers returned REVISE from the reviewer and the red team in round 1. The writers fixed the findings (arithmetic, unsupported claims, internal workflow wording, entry conditions and stress ratios), and in round 2 the reviewer returned APPROVE and the red team returned verdict_holds for all four. Round 2 also left minor NOTES, which were fixed after the final round without a third review: a level consistency in Glenmark, a sizing wording in Karur Vysya, a 12.0 percent margin boundary and a head label in Suprajit, and a condition count in Sundram Fasteners. Those post-review edits were re-checked with the readability and citation gates only.

Fifth, tooling. The shared structural validator has no dossier mode, so the packet's code checks, the readability check, the citation check and the layout check were used instead. The first-use glossary tagging in the page builder skips text that sits directly inside a paragraph, so some writers applied the same rules in a post-render pass. Four writers worked in parallel on one shared worktree and fetch cache; one writer's scripts were overwritten by another's and were rebuilt in a separate folder.

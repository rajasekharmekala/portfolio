What the batch found

Each row gives the verdict, the 28 October close, the entry zone, the stop, our central target and the reward-to-risk ratio, which is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop. A stop is the price at which the idea is wrong. The scoring file asks for at least 2.0 for a core name and 2.5 for a tactical or turnaround name. Read the ratio at the close together with the ratio at the top of the entry zone, because the first is what a buyer gets today and the second is what a limit order would get after a pullback.

The other three are in Stage 3, a pullback above a rising 200-day average, which the scoring rules route to a wait rather than a rejection. The second rule is the operating test, scored out of 5.0, where a name needs at least 3.0 and may have at most one failing row. AWL scores 2.5 and fails it, and ENGINERSIN and CEMPRO sit exactly on the line. The third rule is the reward-to-risk floor: ENGINERSIN, CEMPRO and KPIL clear it only inside a lower price zone, and AWL clears it only at a zone it cannot use while the chart red line stands.

ENGINERSIN: a debt-free consultant whose last year was flattered by one-off gains

Engineers India is a government-owned engineering consultant to the oil and gas sector, with a ₹14,424 crore order book, borrowings near zero, no pledge, a CARE AAA rating and a return on capital employed of 30.4 percent. It passes every hard red line, but its three-year cash conversion is 0.38, below the 0.70 compounder standard, so it is a conditional tactical breakout idea and not a compounder. The operating test scores 3.0 of 5.0 with four rows on watch, because order inflow was only 34 percent of the ₹8,000 crore target by mid-August. FY26 profit held about ₹248 crore of one-off gains, so the 18.3 times earnings multiple is nearer 27 to 29 times on clean profit. The close of ₹256.35 is 1.64 average daily ranges above support, outside the buyable bands, and reward-to-risk is 1.51 against the 2.5 floor. A limit order is allowed only at ₹234.00 to ₹240.00 and only after the 30 October 2026 results show a clean operating margin and positive half-year cash flow. The verdict changes on a close below ₹230.77, which cancels the setup, or below ₹220.10, which rejects it.

CEMPRO: a record order book at 47 times normalised earnings

Cemindia builds marine structures, underground metro, airports and data centres, and the Adani Group is the controlling shareholder. The order book is ₹31,307 crore, 3.07 times sales, the return on capital employed is 32.8 percent, debt to equity is 0.42 and there is no pledge. The operating test is exactly at the 3.0 line with one failing row: FY26 revenue grew 8.8 percent against a 25 percent forecast. Adani Group work is 52.53 percent of the order book, which is a concentration risk, and working capital days have risen. At the close the reward-to-risk is 0.31 to 1, and the price is about 47 times our normalised FY26 profit. The red team asked for a stricter verdict in round 1, so the limit order at ₹1,070 to ₹1,100 is conditional: no fill unless refreshed earnings and multiple inputs give at least 2.0 to 1 on the fill day and the full momentum gate is rerun. A 10.0 percent FY28 margin alone cuts the entry ratio to 1.77 to 1. The 5 November 2026 results, with the half-year cash flow, are the first test.

KPIL: the cleanest of the three, held back by chart position and a pledge near its red line

Kalpataru Projects is an EPC contractor with a ₹66,607 crore order book (44 percent power transmission), net debt of ₹917 crore, a three-year cash conversion of 1.56 and a return on capital employed of 18.3 percent. The operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0 with no failing row. Two cautions stand out. The promoter pledge is 24.56 percent of promoter shares, 0.44 points under the 25 percent red line, although it is static and down from 31.51 percent in March 2024. The close of ₹1,324.70 is 1.58 average daily ranges above support, so reward-to-risk is 1.98 to 1 at the close, just under the 2.0 core floor, and 2.97 to 1 at ₹1,295. The weighted value of ₹1,484 is 12.0 percent above the close. If margins stay flat the entry ratio falls to 0.32 to 1, so the order is re-checked on the Q2 FY27 numbers before it is armed. The older archive row for KALPATPOWR is the same company and is kept as history only.

AWL: a large staples franchise whose return on capital has fallen to 10 percent

AWL sells Fortune edible oils and packaged foods and is controlled by Wilmar at 56.94 percent, with no pledge and the Adani exit completed in November 2025. The close of ₹165.49 fell 6.16 percent on 28 October on 4.95 times average volume, after Q2 FY27 results released on 27 October. Profit after tax was ₹239 crore against ₹245 crore, stock rose by about ₹6,000 crore and first-half operating cash flow turned negative. The chart is Stage 4 with the recovery test failing on two conditions (below the 20-day average and a 14-day RSI of 31.2). The operating test scores 2.5 of 5.0 because the company's own return on capital employed is 10 percent against the 18.3 percent shown on Screener. The rejection does not lift on a price level or a cash result alone: the recovery test has to pass first, and then asymmetry, the operating score and the archetype are retested.

Before you read

This report sums up one batch of four India consumer and pharma names, each with a full research dossier. Marico (MARICO), a hair-oil and foods maker, passes every safety test and earns the one buy-on-a-dip plan in the batch, but no order may be placed until its 2 November results are out. Emcure Pharmaceuticals (EMCURE) also passes the safety tests, yet it has fewer passing operating rows than the weakest shortlisted names, so it waits on the watchlist. ITC and Dabur are rejected because each share price is in Stage 4, a long fall below the 50-day and 200-day average prices, and the recovery test fails. Each dossier states the price or event that would change its verdict, and nothing here is a buy today.

What the batch found

Each row gives the verdict, the 28 October close, the entry zone (the price range where we would place a buy order), the stop, our central target and the reward-to-risk ratio. That ratio is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop. A stop is the price at which the idea is wrong. The scoring file asks for at least 2.0 for a core name. Read the ratio at the close together with the ratio at the top of the entry zone. The first is what a buyer gets today. The second is what a limit order would get after a pullback.

The four decisions, one at a time

Each paragraph below says what the dossier concluded, why, and the level or event that would change it. The dossiers carry the full working, including five-year tables, estimate sections and a numbered source list.

MARICO: a high-return staples franchise that has to prove its multiple

Marico earns a 47.0 percent return on capital employed, carries a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.13, has three-year cash conversion of 0.97 and has 2.03 percent of promoter shares pledged, so every red line passes. India volume grew 11 percent in the June quarter and Parachute rigid packs hold 59 percent volume share. The operating score is 4.0 of 5.0 with three passes and two watch rows (the operating margin path and the push into foods and premium brands). The share price is a Stage 1 base, 0.8 ATR (average true range, the typical daily price swing) above its ₹756.85 support, and all three momentum tests fail, so the dossier gives a limit-order plan rather than a market buy. The base target is ₹937.50, which is 50 times our FY28 earnings estimate of ₹18.75 a share. That is about today's trailing multiple held steady, not a discount, so the multiple is as important as the earnings. At 42 times, the same earnings give ₹787.50 and a 0.82 to 1 ratio at the top of the zone. No order is placed before the 2 November results. After them we recheck support, earnings and the ratio at 44 times, and place the order only if the tests pass. A close above ₹806.41 cancels the plan for a fresh review, and a close below ₹740.87 ends the idea.

EMCURE: a branded-pharma compounder with a thin operating score

Emcure passes every red line (debt-to-equity 0.31, interest cover 9.9 times, 0.04 percent pledge, three-year cash conversion 1.33) and earns a 24.0 percent return on capital employed. Its operating score is 3.5 of 5.0 with two passes and three watch rows: India growth that only matches its market, international growth that is partly currency, and a gross margin down from 61.8 to 58.4 percent. The chart is a Stage 3 pullback with Test C passing, so the rule sends it to WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT and not to a rejection. The plan is a limit order at ₹1,735 to ₹1,790, activated only after the 3 November results and a fresh recheck of target, ratio and support. A close below ₹1,716 before purchase rejects the pullback idea, and ₹1,654 is the stop after purchase. A combined stress of flat margin and the lowest multiple since listing gives about ₹1,904 and a ratio of 0.84 to 1 at the top of the zone, which is why the order waits for the results.

ITC: a cash-rich cigarette group whose profit is still resetting

ITC passes every financial red line (debt-to-equity 0.03, return on capital employed 38.9 percent, no promoter and no pledge, adjusted three-year cash conversion 0.85) and trades at about 16.7 times earnings. But cigarette profit fell 35 percent after the 1 February 2026 tax rise, consensus earnings for FY27 have fallen 9.3 percent in 90 days, and the chart is a literal Stage 4 with Test C failing on one condition. The ratio is 1.01 to 1 at the close. The verdict is REJECTED. It reopens only when a fresh bar passes Test C, the ratio is at least 2.0 on that bar (about 17.05 of FY28 earnings a share would do it at today's price), and the 2 November quarter shows cigarette profit at or above ₹3,341 crore.

DABUR: a sound business in a sector-wide downtrend

Dabur passes every financial red line (return on capital employed 20.3 percent, debt-to-equity 0.11, cash conversion 1.21, no pledge), its operating score is 3.5 of 5.0, and sales growth picked up to 10.6 percent in June. Yet the share price is 33 percent below its high in a Stage 4 downtrend, and Test C fails. The verdict is REJECTED on timing, not on the business. A multiple-only stress to 26 times on FY28 earnings gives ₹341.64, so the headline ratio is not a safe margin. It reopens when Test C passes or the price closes above its falling 50-day average of ₹383.69, after the 4 November results, with the target and stop recomputed on the new bar.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run published with gate exceptions. A gate that the evidence could not fully meet is listed here instead of being hidden.

First, the stale-date exception. Each citation check ended PASS WITH EXCEPTIONS because many research records are older than 60 days: annual reports, rating letters, earlier management calls used to audit what management promised, and the June-quarter filings. Q2 FY27 results are not yet filed for any of the four (MARICO on 2 November, ITC on 2 November, EMCURE on 3 November, DABUR on 4 November). The counts of stale lines are 28 for MARICO, 76 for ITC, 42 for DABUR and 82 for EMCURE. Every as-of date is true, and each dossier lists its own stale lines. The cure is the Q2 FY27 filings in the first week of November 2026.

Second, the data feeds. The ledger's Yahoo reverse DCF and consensus fields were empty or in the wrong unit for some names, so the writers solved the reverse DCF from fetched filings (the working is in each dossier's Chapter 5) and show consensus as not published where no source was available. Screener quoted a few prices after the frozen session; each dossier keeps the 28 October ledger close and labels the difference.

Third, the reviewer cycle. DABUR and ITC returned REVISE from the reviewer in round 1 and APPROVE in round 2, with their red teams holding the verdict in round 1. EMCURE and MARICO returned REVISE from the reviewer in round 2 (one remaining source wording item for EMCURE, two consistency items for MARICO) with their red teams approving in round 2. Those findings were fixed after the cap without a third review, so both are marked BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT and neither is described as reviewer APPROVE. After the cap, the displacement comparison in both dossiers was also corrected to the house convention that compares passing-row counts, and the EMCURE and MARICO pages were re-rendered with those edits.

Fourth, tooling. The shared structural validator has no dossier mode, so the packet's code checks, the readability check, the citation check and the layout check were used instead. The first-use glossary tagging in the page builder skips text that sits directly inside a paragraph, so the writers applied the same rules in a post-render pass without editing the repository's scripts. Four writers worked in parallel on one shared fetch cache, and some exchange pages were blocked or scanned images, so affected figures come from locally extracted text or aggregator pages and are labelled in the dossiers.

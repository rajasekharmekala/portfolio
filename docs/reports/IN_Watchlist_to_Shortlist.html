Before you read

This report sums up the twenty-third batch of India names: four companies, each with a full research dossier. Carysil (quartz kitchen sinks) passes every safety rule but sits a little above support, so it joins the shortlist with no order today. Westlife (McDonald's restaurants) earns too little on its capital after rent, Aster DM (hospitals) has two thirds of its promoter shares pledged, and KEC International (power lines) has not turned profit into cash. Those three are rejected. Nothing here is a buy today.

Three rules decided these outcomes. The first is a set of hard red lines. They cover debt, share pledge, cash conversion and a chart test called Stage 4. In Stage 4 the price has fallen for a long time: the close sits below a falling 50-day average, and that average sits below a falling 200-day average. Only KEC meets that test, and it also fails the cash conversion line (0.21 against a floor of 0.25) and sits just over the debt limit once trade acceptances are counted. Aster DM fails the pledge line: 66.99 percent of the promoters' shares are pledged against a 20 percent limit.

The second rule is the operating test. It is scored out of 5.0. A name needs at least 3.0 and may have one failing row. Carysil scores 4.0 and Aster DM scores 3.5. Westlife scores 2.5 because its return on capital after rent is 7.9 percent, under the 8 percent floor. KEC scores 2.0 with two failing rows. The third rule is the reward-to-risk floor, which Aster DM misses even without the pledge.

The four decisions, one at a time

Each paragraph gives the rule behind the verdict, the number that triggered it and what would change it. The full working is in the linked dossier.

Carysil: shortlisted core challenger, no order

Carysil makes quartz and steel kitchen sinks, faucets and built-in appliances, and exports about 81 percent of its sales. Every hard red line passes: debt to equity is 0.46, three-year cash over profit is 1.00, there is no pledge and the chart is Stage 1, not Stage 4. Return on capital employed is 18.0 percent and the operating test scores 4.0 of 5.0 with no failing row. Cash conversion is the soft spot: 60.6 percent of operating profit in FY26 after tax, with inventory still rising. The reward-to-risk ratio clears the 2.0 core floor at the close (3.13 to 1) and at the zone top (4.04 to 1). The price sits 1.13 average-true-range units above support and the momentum test says there is no momentum, so the verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT. The dossier sets two limit orders (orders that fill only at a chosen price or better) at ₹975 and ₹955, a half-size first purchase, and only after the 4 November 2026 result shows quartz volume of at least 217,000 units and no reversal of the US price discounts. The seed's "duopoly" claim was wrong: Carysil is one of four firms with the German quartz technology.

Westlife Foodworld: rejected

Westlife is the McDonald's master franchisee for west and south India, not a supplier as the watchlist note said. The business has turned: September same-store sales grew 10.1 percent and the promoter group bought 22.7 lakh shares. It is rejected on the operating test alone. Half-year operating profit after depreciation, doubled and divided by equity plus bank debt, gives 7.9 percent against an 8 percent floor, so one KPI fails and the score is 2.5 of 5.0, under the 3.0 line. On the reported basis the balance sheet also fails the debt and interest-cover red lines (debt to equity 2.92 and cover 0.98 times); they pass only once lease liabilities are removed, which the dossier shows with both bases. The stock trades near 42.6 times last-twelve-month operating profit before the leasing standard, our central value is ₹589 against a ₹600.70 close, and the reverse DCF needs about 25 percent a year of cash flow growth. The idea reopens if half-year annualised return on capital reaches 8 percent with the Q3 FY27 results in early February 2027.

Aster DM Quality Care: rejected

Aster DM merged with Quality Care India on 1 July 2026 and now runs a large hospital chain in India with a 64 percent bed occupancy and a margin near 22 percent. The operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0 with no failing row, and debt, cover, cash conversion and the chart all pass. But 66.99 percent of the promoters' holding is pledged (it was 18.17 percent in July), most of it for holding-company loans, about 36 percent of all shares. That fails the 20 percent red line on its own. Even without it, our value of ₹714 gives only 1.09 to 1 at the close and 1.51 to 1 at the zone top. Reopening needs an exchange filing showing the pledge below 20 percent, and then a fresh model that gives at least 2.5 to 1 at the actual entry, which under today's assumptions means ₹643.93 or lower.

KEC International: rejected

KEC builds power transmission lines and civil projects, with a record order book of ₹37,697 crore. The stock is down about 62 percent from its high, roughly four fifths from a lower multiple and one fifth from lower earnings. It is in Stage 4 with the recovery test failing, three-year cash over profit is 0.21 (FY26 operating cash was minus ₹414 crore), debt to equity is 1.22 once trade acceptances are counted, and management missed six of its own targets on margin and working capital (the money tied up in unpaid bills and stock). The operating test scores 2.0 with two failing rows. The reward-to-risk ratio of 3.26 to 1 is measured to a stop that a gap could carry the price through, and falls to 0.79 to 1 against the bear value, so it is not relied on. Reopening needs the recovery test to pass for five sessions and the 2 November half-year cash flow to show positive operating cash, net debt (borrowings minus cash) near ₹6,200 crore or lower and working capital days at or below 125.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run published with gate exceptions. That means a check could not be fully met by the evidence available, and the report says so instead of hiding it.

First, the stale-date exception. The newest periodic filings for all four companies are the June 2026 quarter and the FY26 annual reports, more than 60 days old, because the September-quarter results are not out yet (Carysil 4 November, KEC 2 November, Aster DM by 14 November, Westlife in early February for the December quarter). The citation check was run with its stale-date allowance and ended PASS WITH EXCEPTIONS for every dossier. Every as-of date in the records is true. The cure is the next quarterly results.

Second, the reviewer cycle. Each dossier went through two rounds with the configured reviewer and red team. Carysil, Aster DM and KEC were approved by the reviewer in round 2. Westlife received REVISE from the reviewer in round 2 on three small points (a claim about when a measure was defined, a table that did not match the text, and an unsupported time of day); all three were fixed afterwards, with no third review, so it carries the status BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT. The Westlife red team approved the rejection in round 2.

Third, data limits. Westlife's lease cash cost was derived because the audited line was an image. Aster DM's merged cash flow is not published, so its reverse DCF uses a proxy. Carysil's three-year median P/E is approximate because price history was blocked. KEC's consensus history is a single broker. In every dossier the gate table still shows the builder's older rule wording and raw field names, and the paragraphs around it give the thresholds that were actually applied. The price source, the dossiers and the data files all use the 27 October 2026 session.

Its job is to answer one question: which of the names waiting on the watchlist are now strong enough to join the shortlist, the list of companies we would be prepared to buy at the right price? The process has three layers, and each name must pass them in order. Layer 1 is a set of hard gates: the business must earn a return on capital employed (ROCE) of at least 15 percent, keep its borrowings below 0.70 times its equity, using the debt-to-equity ratio, which compares borrowings with shareholders' equity, and turn at least 70 percent of its profit into operating cash. The price must also be in an acceptable trend, and the asymmetry test must pass. In plain terms, asymmetry is the reward-to-risk ratio: the upside to a verified target must be at least 3.0 times the downside to the stop-loss for a core name. A name that fails any Layer 1 gate is not scored further. Layer 2 scores three to five business-specific operating measures. Layer 3 asks whether the price is close enough to a support level to buy now. A name that fails Layer 1 is moved to the archive sheet of the watchlist, and its re-entry trigger is written down so that it can come back when the evidence changes.

The reading guide for the rest of the report is simple. Chapter 1 gives the batch result. Chapter 2 sets out the market backdrop the names were judged against. Chapter 3 explains each name. Chapter 4 shows what changed in the watchlist and the shortlist. Chapter 5 lists what was flagged for the user to decide. The run notes at the end list every gate exception.

2. The market behind the decisions

A stock is judged against the market it trades in, and this session the market was weak. The Nifty 50 closed at 22,520.45 on 9 October 2026. That is below its 50-day average of 23,726.47 and below its 200-day average of 24,271.24, and the 50-day average is falling. The ledger therefore labels the regime Downtrend. Only 17.7 percent of the stocks in the screen were above their 50-day averages, and there were 10 distribution days in the last 25 sessions, which means heavy selling on days when the index fell.

This matters for the batch in a specific way. In a Downtrend the process gives little weight to new buying, even for a good business. The momentum gate applies the same test to every name in the screen, and at this session it returned NO_MOMENTUM for all 576 names it could test. The gate's thresholds are marked as needing calibration in the scoring file, so we treat its result as a warning rather than a verdict on any one company. The result still matters, because it means no name in the screen would have been allocated new money under the current rules.

The regime label is the single most important number in this chapter. "Downtrend" is a market-level verdict, and it is taken from the ledger, not from any news story. Breadth is the share of stocks above their 50-day average, and distribution days are sessions where the index fell on heavier volume than the day before. Together they describe how many stocks are participating in the market's direction.

The backdrop is consistent with the four outcomes. Every name failed on its own price test, and the market-level trend explains why several names that are sound businesses failed for the same reason. The trigger that would change the process's stance on new buying is the Nifty 50 closing back above its 50-day average and that average turning up for several sessions, as set out in the ledger.

If the Nifty 50 closes above its 50-day average and that average turns up for several sessions, the regime moves out of Downtrend, and the names above should be re-run through the gates in order.

The business figures pass, but the price test that decides whether the name can be bought now does not. What would change this verdict is a price test that passes, not a better story. The stock was previously on the shortlist as a Core Alpha challenger. That status is withdrawn in this run, which is the correct result of a Layer 1 failure and not a judgment that the business has become worse.

If AJANTPHARM closes above 3,544.04 on at least the normal volume with the 50-day average still rising, re-run its Layer 1 gates. Its verdict is REJECTED until then.

It does not mean it must rise, and here the trend has not turned.

The dossier also records a question about which set of accounts the cash-flow test should use, the consolidated group or the parent company alone. The verdict does not depend on that choice, because the technical gate and the missing target both fail on their own. The verdict is REJECTED. Re-entry needs a price close above the 50-day average on heavier volume, with that average rising, and a verified target that gives at least 3.0 times the downside. The dossier gives the exact levels.

If BAJAJ-AUTO closes above 11,430.95 on at least the normal volume with a rising 50-day average, and a verified target gives at least 3.0 times the downside to the stop, re-run it. Its verdict is REJECTED until then.

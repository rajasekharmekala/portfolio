2. Underwriting conclusion and operating evidence

Sai Life Sciences recorded FY26 revenue of ₹2,192 crore, including ₹775 crore in discovery CRO and ₹1,417 crore in CDMO services. Revenue grew about 29% for the year; EBITDA was ₹661 crore at a 30.1% margin, PAT was ₹349 crore and operating cash flow was ₹509 crore. The annual report counted 34 commercial molecules, 11 Phase III or pre-registration molecules and around 155 earlier-stage programs. These are promising operating inputs, but molecule counts do not state expected launch dates, contract value or future contribution. [1,2]

The company has met a prior disclosed ambition to exceed 25 commercial molecules by 2025: FY26 reporting counted 34. Its long-term guide is 15–20% revenue growth and a 28–30% EBITDA margin. Q1 FY27 revenue growth was 12% and the presentation showed a 27% margin. The CEO said clients were engaging “across the full spectrum” of services; the CFO said returning customers accounted for over 90% of FY25 and FY26 revenue. That statistic measures revenue from returning customers, not account retention. [1,5–7,29]

SAILIFE’s operating case rests on integrating discovery, process development and manufacture. A client program can begin in CRO work and later require process scale-up or commercial API supply. FY26 revenue split was ₹775 crore CRO and ₹1,417 crore CDMO; the report does not assume every CRO program converts to CDMO. The strongest evidence is a 34-molecule commercial portfolio, 11 late-stage programs and more than 90% revenue from returning customers, alongside strong FY26 cash flow. The counterweight is a 49% sales contribution from large pharma, 27% Q1 margin, higher working-capital days on Screener and a ₹1,100–1,300 crore FY27 capex plan. The verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT, not a buy at the current quote. If the 5 November results show weaker conversion or margins below 28%, keep zero new allocation; otherwise reassess pipeline conversion, cash generation and fresh support. [1,5–8,36]

The four decisions, one at a time

Each paragraph gives the rule behind the verdict, the number that drove it and what would change it. The full working is in the linked dossier.

SYNGENE: a rebound driven by manufacturing batches, with research revenue still falling

Syngene rose 12.19 percent on 28 October, on 43.5 times its usual 20-day volume, after it filed September-quarter results on 27 October and held its call on 28 October. Revenue grew 13 percent from the June quarter and a first agreement was signed for its Bayview biologics site, but research revenue, about 65 percent of sales, fell, and a hedge loss of ₹61 crore weighed on profit. The balance sheet is sound: net cash, debt to equity 0.10, no pledge and three-year cash conversion of 2.36. The return on capital is the problem. It is 9.99 percent against the 15 percent compounder floor, and the turnaround route needs two improving signals while only one, the quarterly margin recovery, clearly counts. Estimates fell 19.6 percent in 90 days. Our FY28 earnings per share is ₹10.14 and our central target ₹426 (42 times), so the weighted value of ₹420.70 is only 2.4 percent above the close. The verdict rests on one disclosed judgment: the debtor-days improvement is scored mixed and not counted, and counting it would move the name to WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT at ₹345 to ₹355. The name reopens on a pullback into that zone together with a second improving signal, such as research revenue growing in both the December and March quarters.

LUPIN: a cash-rich drug maker at a price that returns 1.7 percent

Lupin has debt to equity of 0.29, interest cover of 20.3 times, three-year cash conversion of 1.32 and no promoter pledge. Its profit peak rests on US products with limited exclusivity, and the ledger labels the chart Stage 4: the ₹1,972.70 close sits below the 50-day and 200-day averages, and the recovery test misses by ₹1.45 against the 20-day average of ₹1,974.15. The dossier states the strict reading, because the 200-day average is not falling, and notes that the verdict does not depend on it. The return does: our weighted 12-month value is ₹1,988.75, a 1.7 percent total return against a 12 percent hurdle, and a 12 percent return needs a price near ₹1,790. Reward-to-risk is 0.81 to 1 at the close. September-quarter results are due on 3 November with the call on 4 November. A close at or above ₹1,974.15 would pass the recovery test, and US sales of at least US$280 million with a margin above 26 percent would lift our estimates.

ERIS: cheap-looking earnings, rising pledge and a thin reward

Eris sells chronic-therapy medicines (heart and diabetes) and grew 12.6 percent in the September quarter, filed 26 October, but reported profit was lifted by tax one-offs; company-adjusted profit was ₹158 crore. The Swiss injectables unit is in remediation and management has missed or moved several promises (net debt ₹2,255 crore against a ₹1,800 crore guide). The promoter pledge rose to 21.25 percent of promoter shares from 16.85 percent in June. The scoring file treats 20 percent or more combined with a Stage 4 chart as a red line. The ledger's recovery test passes, so the Stage 4 rule alone would not fire, and the dossier says the pledge reading is a judgment call. A second, independent failure stands either way: reward-to-risk is 1.19 to 1 at the close and 1.44 to 1 at the ₹1,160 zone top, against floors of 2.0 and 2.5, and the weighted return is 7.9 percent. The name reopens if the pledge falls below 20 percent and the third-quarter margin reaches 36 percent or more.

ELECTCAST: a low-debt pipe maker with weak volumes and a downtrend

Electrosteel Castings has debt to equity of 0.26, a pledge of 9.69 percent and three-year cash conversion of 1.51, and promoters bought stock in late September while foreign funds cut their holding from 15.73 percent to 8.57 percent. Volumes fell 27 percent in the June quarter and management cut its FY27 guide from 7 to 5.75 lakh tonnes. The ledger labels the chart Stage 4 with the recovery test failing on one condition (the close is below the 20-day average). The operating score is 2.5 of 5.0, below the 3.0 minimum, and the weighted return is 7.2 percent. The dossier shows that reward-to-risk is 2.39 at the close on the unrounded ₹75.50 target and 2.53 only if the target is rounded to ₹76, so the tactical floor of 2.5 is missed at the close; the base target is anchored on 0.77 times book value, and on the earnings-multiple anchor of ₹63.12 the return would be about zero. September-quarter results are not yet filed.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run published with gate exceptions. A gate that the evidence could not fully meet is listed here instead of being hidden.

First, the stale-date exception. Each citation check ended PASS WITH EXCEPTIONS because many research records are older than 60 days: audited annual figures, rating letters, earlier management calls used to audit what management promised, and June-quarter filings. The counts of stale lines are 47 for SYNGENE, 121 for LUPIN, 46 for ERIS and 100 for ELECTCAST. SYNGENE and ERIS used their September-quarter results for current figures; LUPIN reports on 3 November 2026 and ELECTCAST has not yet filed, so their earnings figures are for the June quarter. Every as-of date is true, and each dossier lists the stale records with the filing that cures them.

Second, the reviewer cycle. ERIS and ELECTCAST were approved by the reviewer in round 2 after round 1 REVISE with every finding fixed. LUPIN and SYNGENE did not reach approval: each reviewer returned REVISE in round 1 and again in round 2 with one finding (an unsupported sourcing clause for LUPIN, a backwards sentence for SYNGENE). Both were corrected after round 2 and no third review was run, so both are marked BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT. The red team held the verdict in round 1 for ERIS, LUPIN and SYNGENE. For ELECTCAST it asked for a correction of the rounded 2.53 reward-to-risk figure in round 1 and approved the fix in round 2. The configured red-team model (ao-foundry-lc/gpt-6.1-sol) was blocked by the provider on every attempt, so every red-team pass ran on the runner's default agent, a substitution recorded in each dossier and in the manifest.

Third, tooling and data. The shared structural validator has no dossier mode, so the packet's code checks, the readability check, the citation check and the layout check were used instead. The assembler's automatic glossary step skips terms written directly inside a paragraph; the LUPIN writer rendered through a runtime copy of that step so first-use definitions appear, and the repository script was not changed. The ledger's consensus, price targets and reverse DCF are cited to the frozen ledger with no independent source, and the reverse DCF uses profit as a stand-in for cash flow. Several pledge figures come from Trendlyne because the exchange pages could not be read by script. Some filings and transcripts could not be fetched, and several call transcripts are third-party or preliminary versions; each dossier lists them. Four writers worked in parallel on one shared fetch cache and scratch folder, and no cache entry was edited by hand.

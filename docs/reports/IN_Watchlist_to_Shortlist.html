Before you read

The four decisions, one at a time

Each paragraph gives the rule behind the verdict, the number that drove it and what would change it. The full working is in the linked dossier.

CENTUM: a defence and electronics maker whose price already assumes a decade of fast growth

Centum Electronics builds electronics for defence, space and aerospace customers, plus contract manufacturing. Its order book is ₹1,797 crore, 1.81 times trailing sales, and the overseas units that distorted its accounts were liquidated and left the group on 4 June 2026. No hard red line fails: debt to equity is 0.28, nothing is pledged, and three-year cash conversion is 0.55, which passes only through the working-capital exemption for fast-growing order-book businesses. The operating test scores exactly 3.0 of 5.0, with one failing row, because two customer groups gave 43.6 percent of FY26 revenue. The ledger's 368 times earnings and its ₹16 crore cash-flow figure mix the exited units with the continuing business, so the dossier uses its own bases and finds that the price needs 21 to 25 percent profit growth for ten years. At the ₹4,000 close the reward-to-risk is -0.32 to 1. The ₹3,150 to ₹3,240 zone clears the 12 percent return hurdle only if the market keeps paying 50 times FY28 profit (about +6.6 percent at 45 times and +0.9 percent at 40 times). The red team asked for a stricter verdict, so the zone is provisional: no order before the 4 November 2026 results and a refreshed chart, KPI and return check, and the first position is cut to 2.0 percent. A second customer above 10 percent of revenue, or Q2 margin at or above 13.5 percent with cash flow at least equal to profit, would lift the case; a Q2 margin below 11.5 percent rejects it.

GREENLAM: a turnaround in wood panels that works only if the new plants stop losing money

Greenlam makes decorative laminates, plywood and chipboard. About ₹1,300 crore of new plants has pulled the return on capital employed down to 8.1 percent, so it fails the compounder standard and is judged as a turnaround. It passes every hard red line (debt to equity 0.98 against 1.20, no pledge, three-year cash conversion 2.66), although interest cover is the one soft spot: 2.50 times over the last twelve months and 1.93 times on the strictest FY26 reading. The operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0 with no failing row, helped by chipboard turning profitable in Q1 FY27. The chart is Stage 4, but the recovery test passes, so the rule routes it to WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT instead of rejecting it. At the ₹213.24 close the reward-to-risk is 1.71 to 1 and the expected return is 8.2 percent, both short of the bar. They clear it at or below about ₹206, so the limit order is ₹203.50 to ₹205.50 after the Q2 results, and only if a refreshed value is at least 12 percent above the order price. The seed label of core alpha was changed to tactical momentum under the turnaround archetype.

HDFCAMC: a first-class business at a price that leaves no return

HDFC Asset Management runs one of India's largest mutual fund houses and carries no debt, no pledge, a return on equity of 32.9 percent and cash conversion of 0.86. The operating test scores 4.0 of 5.0. It is rejected for two independent reasons. The chart is in Stage 4 and the recovery test fails on one condition (the close of ₹2,300.00 is just below its 20-day average of ₹2,303.64, with negative relative strength). The price also leaves little return: our probability-weighted value is ₹2,300, an expected 2.5 percent including the ₹58 dividend against the 12 percent hurdle, with a reward-to-risk of 0.47 to 1. Consensus estimates are falling and the market share of average assets slipped from 11.4 to 11.1 percent. Re-opening needs three things on one ledger rerun: the recovery test passes with the ₹2,215.60 floor intact, a refreshed FY28 profit per share of at least ₹86, and a limit price that gives both 2.0 reward-to-risk and a 12 percent expected return.

NAM-INDIA: a growing fund house that fell 20 percent in a month and still needs a lower price

Nippon Life India Asset Management holds 9.02 percent of industry assets, 21 percent of ETF assets and 38.4 percent of all unique fund investors, with a return on equity of 35.3 percent, almost no debt and a payout near 90 percent. The operating test scores 4.0 of 5.0 with no failing row. The 20 percent fall in a month followed Q2 profit down 35.6 percent from June (a ₹96.76 crore SEBI settlement charge and lower other income), a lower equity fee rate of 52 basis points, a flat systematic investment plan book and a weak market. The stock is Stage 1 (a basing stage), not Stage 4, but the recovery test fails on two conditions and the RSI (relative strength index, a 0 to 100 gauge of recent selling pressure) is 28.3. At the ₹922.50 close the reward-to-risk is 0.54 to 1 and the expected return is 4.7 percent, so nothing is bought today. Both tests pass at ₹862 or lower under a 30 times central multiple; at the 25.55 times long-run average they fail, which is why the order of ₹850 to ₹862 is allowed only after a refreshed check at order time. It means waiting for a dip through or just under the ₹881.60 support, not a bounce off it.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run published with gate exceptions. A gate that the evidence could not fully meet is listed here instead of being hidden.

First, the stale-date exception. Each citation check ended PASS WITH EXCEPTIONS because many research records are older than 60 days: annual reports, rating letters, earlier management calls used to audit what management promised, and the Q1 FY27 filings. Q2 FY27 results are not filed for CENTUM (4 November) or GREENLAM (date not announced), so their earnings figures are before Q2. HDFCAMC reported on 15 October and NAM-INDIA on 22 October, so theirs are after Q2. The counts are 132 for CENTUM, 131 for GREENLAM, 61 for HDFCAMC and about 51 for NAM-INDIA. Every as-of date is true, and each dossier lists its own stale lines and why they cannot be cured now. The cure is the Q2 FY27 filings and the FY27 annual accounts.

Second, the reviewer cycle. For CENTUM, GREENLAM and HDFCAMC the reviewer returned REVISE in round 1 and APPROVE in round 2 after the writers fixed every finding. The CENTUM red team asked for a stricter verdict in round 1 (the zone was made provisional and the starting size cut) and held the verdict in round 2; the GREENLAM and HDFCAMC red teams held the verdict in round 1. A few reviewer notes from round 2 were fixed afterwards without a further review. NAM-INDIA did not reach approval: the reviewer returned REVISE in round 1 and again in round 2, where one finding remained (a sentence attributed an about 20 percent fall to a news site that the research record does not support). That sentence and three other statements without a recorded source were corrected after round 2, each now backed by a record, and no third review was run, so NAM-INDIA is marked BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT. Its red team asked for changes in round 1 (the recovery-test wording) and held the verdict in round 2.

Third, tooling. The shared structural validator has no dossier mode, so the packet's code checks, the readability check, the citation check and the layout check were used instead. The shared renderer does not add first-use glossary definitions when a term sits directly inside a paragraph, so the writers added them in a post-render pass in the run folder. The ledger's reverse DCF is a poor proxy for CENTUM and GREENLAM because their trailing profit is distorted, so each dossier shows its own calculation next to it, and the auto-rendered CENTUM key-figures strip still shows the ledger's 47.2 percent, which its run notes flag. Four writers worked in parallel on one shared fetch cache and one scratch folder; a few cache entries were overwritten by another writer's copy, and the affected records were checked against the cache again before the final citation runs. Three-year median price-to-earnings ratios (the share price divided by yearly profit per share) were not published or were blocked for several names, so those are recorded as not found and stress multiples are labelled as assumptions. Some filings were scanned or PDF-only, which the fetch tool cannot read, so affected figures come from locally extracted text and are labelled in the dossiers.

EPL: deferred candidate, not shortlisted

EPL makes laminated tubes for toothpaste and cosmetics, with a return on capital near 19 percent and low debt. All four red lines clear and the reward-to-risk is 7.32 to 1 on our central value. The name is still deferred for four reasons the dossier lists: the share-swap merger with Indovida is unresolved, the cash left after capex, interest and leases is only 0.43 of three-year profit, the largest customer's share of the parent group is unpublished, and no momentum test passes. The merger needs filed Indovida profit of about ₹280 crore on FY28 numbers to leave existing holders no worse off, against management's unverified ₹410 crore. The reopening list has six checks and the merger must be settled first.

Lumax Auto Technologies: deferred candidate, not shortlisted

Lumax supplies parts to car and two-wheeler makers, with revenue up 34 percent in FY26 and an order book of ₹1,879 crore. The dossier is built on filings available by 23 October only; the results filed on 24 October sit in one labelled overlay paragraph. No red line is breached on any debt reading, the highest being 1.017 against the 1.20 line. The routing rule's literal result is PASSED_CONDITIONAL, and not shortlisting is the dossier's own judgment: the results filed after the session are unpriced, the price cushion over the floor is under 5 percent, and the chosen archetype depends on how debt is counted. Mahindra is about 30 percent of recent revenue.

Bharti Airtel: deferred pending a debt-to-equity ruling

Airtel is India's second-largest mobile operator and a large African operator, with a mobile revenue per user of ₹264. The status rests on how debt is counted. Borrowings plus leases over owners' equity give 1.31 at March 2026 and 1.25 at June 2026, which breach 1.20 and would mean REJECTED. The same debt over total equity gives 1.00. The rule text does not choose, and the dossier shows all twenty readings. The reward-to-risk is 2.68 to 1 on our own multiple series and 2.499 on Screener's, so the archetype qualifies narrowly on one series only. The routing rule's positive clause is not met literally, so no order is enabled.

Pennar Industries: rejected, conditionally

Pennar makes engineered steel products. Debt to equity is 1.32 against the 1.20 line if ₹394.80 crore of trade letter-of-credit acceptances count as debt, and 0.98 if not. Interest cover is under 3.0 on every formula, so the rule's exemption for acceptances is not available, and the dossier reads them as debt. If they did not count, no red line would breach on the company's own cover formula and the group pledge reading, and the name would be a deferred candidate. Other readings still reject it. The June 2026 pledge rise was the managing director's own 31,73,000 shares. Receivables grew 26 percent against revenue growth of 12 percent. The paper reward-to-risk of 13.81 to 1 does not cure a red line, and a joint stress gives ₹105.55, a fall of 30 percent.

What this means for you is no purchase today. The next filings that could change these views are the September-quarter results, and no result date is confirmed in these dossiers.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run published with gate exceptions. That means some checks could not be fully met with the evidence available. Each exception below names what failed, why it cannot be cured now, how it lowers confidence and what would cure it. The status of every dossier is PUBLISHED_WITH_GATE_EXCEPTIONS.

Data date. The run took place on Sunday 25 October 2026. The benchmark's 23 October bar was present and every batch name had a 23 October bar, so every price and technical level uses that session. Results filed after that session are used in no gate or calculation. The Lumax dossier was first drafted with its 24 October results in the gates and was rebuilt on filings available by 23 October after review.

Stale periodic filings. The latest periodic filing for each company is the quarter to 30 June 2026, about 117 days old, so the citation check lists many records as EXCEPTION AS-OF lines. Each dossier lists each group with its age and cure. The cure is to rerun the research once the September-quarter results are filed and priced.

Ledger fields. The ledger has no reverse DCF or consensus revisions for these names, so each dossier states its own recompute beside the ledger's value and labels it a price-implied diagnostic. Consensus figures are a dated overlay and enter no gate. Several figures rest on secondary readings, such as aggregator ratios and news summaries of pledge filings, and are labelled where used. The seed figures for Bharti Airtel (5.46 times cash conversion and 1.55 times net debt over EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization), which is operating profit before depreciation) could not be reproduced and are replaced.

Validator mismatch. The shared structural validator has only market-report and portfolio-audit modes. Run on a single-company dossier, it fails a check built for those formats, which asks for case notes for all holdings, and it prints one to four template bridge warnings. The review packet code skips it for dossiers, and no script was modified. The readability check also looks for estimate parts and a Chapter 7 that this summary does not carry, because the dossiers do. The cure is a dossier mode in the validator and a summary mode in the readability check.

Reviewer gate. EPL was approved by both the reviewer and the red team in round 2. Pennar was approved by the reviewer in round 2, and the red team's three requested changes were made afterwards without a third review. Bharti Airtel and Lumax reached the strict two-round limit with the reviewer asking for changes in round 2 (one corrected discount figure for Bharti Airtel, and a growth rate, a status paragraph and a day count for Lumax), and the red team approved both with notes. The fixes were made afterwards without a third review, so those two close as BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT. This summary report was not reviewed separately.

Status changes during review. EPL was first written as a shortlisted challenger candidate. Both the reviewer and the red team said that claim was unsupported, so it was lowered to a deferred candidate. Bharti Airtel and Lumax were reviewed with the strict readings in view. Bharti Airtel's red team judged that rejecting the country's largest telecom on the most punitive reading was not supported when the rule text is silent, so it stays a deferral pending a ruling. Judgment calls. The Pennar rejection rests on counting trade letter-of-credit acceptances as debt, and the Bharti Airtel deferral rests on counting debt against total equity. The Lumax archetype rests on how debt is counted. If the repository owner confirms a different reading, those labels would change with the same reopening steps.

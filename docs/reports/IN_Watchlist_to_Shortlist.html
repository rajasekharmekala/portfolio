Terms used in this report

The framework uses a small set of market terms. ROCE is return on capital employed, which measures the profit a business earns on the money tied up in it. A moving average is the average closing price over a set number of sessions, like a smoothed trend line; the 50-day and 200-day averages are the two the rules use. Stage is the Weinstein picture of a stock's season: Stage 1 is a flat period after a fall, Stage 2 is a rising markup, Stage 3 is a top or pullback where the rise has stalled, and Stage 4 is a decline. A base is a period of sideways trading that comes before a new move, and a pivot is the price level above which a base is considered to have broken out. A breakout is a close through that level on enough volume to be meaningful.

RSI, the relative strength index, is a 14-day reading of how fast a price has risen or fallen. ATR, the average true range, is the typical daily swing of the stock over 14 sessions, and the rules use it as a ruler for distances. Asymmetry is the ratio of the possible gain to the possible loss, measured against a verified target and a stop. An operating margin is operating profit as a share of sales, before interest and tax.

The momentum gate is a code check that runs on the same price file for every name. Test A asks whether the stock is in an upward trend now, and Test B asks whether a new uptrend is starting from a base. A name that fails both gets no allocation, however good the business.

Before you read

This report sums up one batch of four India names, each with a full research dossier. Kirloskar Ferrous (KIRLFER) passes the safety tests but is a narrow case, so it gets a limit order plan, which is an order to buy only at or below a set price, allowed only after its 30 October results and a refreshed check. JTL Industries is rejected because its operating cash flow was negative in each of the last three years while it reported profits. Lemon Tree Hotels and S Chand are sound businesses whose charts are in Stage 4, a long fall below the 50-day and 200-day averages, with the recovery test failing, so both are rejected for now. Each dossier states the price or event that would change its verdict, and nothing here is a buy today.

What the batch found

Each row gives the verdict, the 28 October close, the entry zone (the price range where we would place a buy order), the stop, our central target and the reward-to-risk ratio. That ratio is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop. A stop is the price at which the idea is wrong. The scoring file asks for at least 2.0 for a core name and 2.5 for a tactical or turnaround name. Read the ratio at the close together with the ratio at the top of the entry zone. The first is what a buyer gets today. The second is what a limit order would get after a pullback.

The four decisions, one at a time

Each paragraph gives the rule behind the verdict, the number that drove it and what would change it. The full working is in the linked dossier.

JTLIND: a fast-growing steel tube maker whose cash has not followed its profit

JTL Industries makes steel tubes and structural pipes. Debt to equity is 0.16, interest cover is 11.9 times and the promoter pledge is 5.0 percent of the promoters' shares, so solvency is not the problem. Cash is. Over the last three years operating cash flow was about minus ₹335 crore against about ₹315 crore of profit, a three-year ratio of minus 1.06 against a floor of 0.25. The working-capital exemption does not apply, because the order book is about 0.26 times a year of sales against the 1.5 times needed and the half-year cash flow is not yet filed. The operating test scores 2.5 of 5.0 with one failing row, and the return on capital employed is 9.61 percent. At the ₹74.11 close the reward-to-risk is 0.71 to 1. The rejection lifts only if the half-year operating cash flow, filed on 2 November 2026, is positive and half-year profit is at least 50 percent above the same half a year earlier; even then the name would only move to a conditional watch, because the ratio at the top of the zone is 0.99 to 1.

KIRLFER: a low-debt iron and castings maker whose margin has to turn

Kirloskar Ferrous makes pig iron, tractor and engine castings, alloy steel and seamless tubes. It passes every hard red line: debt to equity 0.27, interest cover 5.0 times, no pledge and a three-year cash conversion of 1.97. It misses the compounder standard on return on capital employed (13.4 percent against 15 percent) and qualifies narrowly as a turnaround on two positive signals (cash conversion and year-end debt reduction) against two negative ones (margin and falling estimates). The operating test scores exactly 3.0 of 5.0, with seamless tubes as the one failing row. The chart has only 136 trading days, so no 200-day average and no stage can be computed, and the rule leaves it as a conditional wait. At the ₹443.40 close the reward-to-risk is 3.00 to 1 and 4.00 to 1 at the ₹437 zone top, but our own FY28 margin one point lower gives 1.86 to 1, and adding a lower multiple gives a value about 1.9 percent below today's price. The red team asked for a stricter verdict, so orders are allowed only after the 30 October 2026 results, if the September margin is at least 12.0 percent, gross debt is no higher than ₹1,156 crore and a refreshed reward-to-risk is at least 2.5 at ₹437.

LEMONTREE: a hotel owner and manager whose chart is in a Stage 4 decline

Lemon Tree owns, leases and manages mid-priced and upscale hotels, with half of its rooms under management contracts that earn fees. It passes the debt, cash and pledge tests (debt to equity 0.96 with leases counted, interest cover 3.12 times, three-year cash conversion 2.17), although debt to equity would breach the 1.20 line on one narrower measure that the dossier discloses. The operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0 with no failing row. The chart is Stage 4 and the recovery test fails on one condition, so the chart rule rejects it. At the ₹107.15 close the reward-to-risk is 1.12 to 1, and the probability-weighted value of ₹109.90 is only 2.6 percent above the price. A close above ₹109.61 with positive four-week relative strength would pass the recovery test, and the dossier says it would then be a trigger to rerun the work, not a buy signal. The next results are on 6 November 2026.

SCHAND: a cheap school-book publisher with no turn in sight

S Chand publishes school books and sells content licences. It trades at 0.43 times book value and about 6.3 times trailing earnings, carries more cash than borrowings for most of the year and passes every balance-sheet line (debt to equity 0.13, no pledge, three-year cash conversion 1.61). It is rejected because the chart is a Stage 4 breakdown with the recovery test failing, and because no archetype qualifies: the return on capital employed is 9.97 percent and none of the four turnaround signals is positive. The operating test scores 3.0 of 4.0 with no failing row. There is no analyst consensus, so the 3.39 to 1 reward-to-risk at the close rests on our own model, and the dossier says so. A good half-year cash result on 5 November 2026 would count as one of four turnaround signals, so a second signal from margin or debt would also be needed.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run published with gate exceptions. A gate that the evidence could not fully meet is listed here instead of being hidden.

First, the stale-date exception. Each citation check ended PASS WITH EXCEPTIONS because many research records are older than 60 days: annual reports, rating letters, earlier management calls used to audit what management promised, and the Q1 FY27 filings. Q2 FY27 results are not yet filed for any of the four: JTLIND reports on 2 November, KIRLFER on 30 October, SCHAND on 5 November and LEMONTREE on 6 November, so every earnings figure is before Q2. The counts of stale lines are 40 for JTLIND, 101 for KIRLFER, 75 for LEMONTREE and 95 for SCHAND. Every as-of date is true, and each dossier lists its own stale lines and why they cannot be cured now. The cure is the Q2 FY27 filings in the first week of November 2026.

Second, the reviewer cycle. KIRLFER and LEMONTREE returned REVISE from the reviewer in round 1 and APPROVE in round 2 after the writers fixed every finding. The KIRLFER red team asked for a stricter verdict in round 1 (the dossier now states how fragile the reward-to-risk is and makes every order conditional on a refreshed check) and held the verdict in round 2; the LEMONTREE red team held the verdict in round 1. JTLIND and SCHAND returned REVISE in both rounds; their writers fixed the remaining findings after round 2 without a third review, so both are marked BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT. Their red teams held the verdict in round 1. Two small LEMONTREE notes from round 2 were fixed afterwards without a further review. One reviewer request could not be met as written: the renderer places three tables directly under their headings, so the plain-English lead-ins sit in the paragraph before each heading instead.

Third, tooling. The shared structural validator has no dossier mode, so the packet's code checks, the readability check, the citation check and the layout check were used instead; its remaining findings are market-report rules that do not apply to a company dossier, plus one false hit on rule text that the renderer prints. Four writers worked in parallel on one shared fetch cache and one scratch folder; one writer briefly ran another writer's script and regenerated that writer's research file, and the citation checks were rerun on every final dossier. The web search tool returned no results in this run, so the writers used the built-in search and fetch tools. Several exchange pages returned errors or were scanned images, so affected figures come from locally extracted text or press summaries and are labelled in the dossiers. Three-year median price-to-earnings ratios (the share price divided by yearly profit per share) were not published for several names and are recorded as not found.

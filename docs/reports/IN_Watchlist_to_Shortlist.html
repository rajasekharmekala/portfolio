Before you read

This report sums up one batch of four India names, each with a full research dossier. Granules India and Usha Martin are good businesses that are priced a little too high for a safe purchase today, so each gets a limit-order plan and no order now. GRM Overseas fails the three-year cash conversion test and Apollo Micro Systems fails the promoter pledge test, so both are rejected. Each dossier states the price or event that would change its verdict, and nothing here is a buy today.

What the batch found

Each row gives the verdict, the 28 October close, the entry zone, the stop, our central target and the reward-to-risk ratio, which is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop. A stop is the price at which the idea is wrong. The scoring file asks for at least 2.0 for a core name and 2.5 for a tactical or turnaround name. Read the ratio at the close together with the ratio at the top of the entry zone, because the first is what a buyer gets today and the second is what a limit order would get after a pullback.

GRMOVER: a basmati exporter whose cash has not followed its profit

GRM Overseas sells basmati and other rice at home and abroad. Its debt to equity is 0.61, interest cover is 5.58 times and the promoter pledge is zero, so solvency is not the problem. Cash is. Over the last three years the business produced ₹55.9 crore of operating cash against ₹197.9 crore of profit, a ratio of 0.28, under the 0.40 line for a mature business with no order book. The working-capital exemption does not apply, because there is no order book, FY26 operating cash flow was negative and FY26 revenue growth was 31 percent against the 35 percent needed. Receivables are the visible symptom: 25 percent of the ₹577 crore owed is more than a year past due, up from 9 percent a year earlier, with no provision taken. The operating test scores 3.0 of 5.0 with one failing row. At the ₹87.39 close the reward-to-risk is -0.11 to 1. The rejection lifts only if the half-year operating cash flow on 13 November 2026 is above about ₹40 crore and FY27 operating cash reaches about ₹80 crore in the May 2027 results.

GRANULES: a strong margin story that waits on one FDA outcome

Granules India makes paracetamol, metformin, ibuprofen and ADHD medicines, with 72 percent of sales in North America. It passes every hard red line: debt to equity 0.30, interest cover 7.9 times, no pledge and a three-year cash conversion of 1.40. It qualifies narrowly as a compounder with a return on capital employed of 15.5 percent, and the seed label of tactical momentum does not hold. The operating test scores 3.0 of 5.0 with one failing row, because 68 percent of tablet capacity sits at the Gagillapur plant, which has had a US FDA Warning Letter since 26 February 2025. The reinspection began on 19 October 2026 and has no published result. At the ₹840.45 close the reward-to-risk is 1.42 to 1, below the 2.0 core floor, and the weighted value of ₹894.9 is only 6.5 percent above the close. After round 2 of the red team the order rule is strict: no order before the 2 November 2026 results, and then only if a refreshed scenario gives at least a 12 percent return at the order price, which caps the order at about ₹799 on today's numbers.

USHAMART: a net-cash franchise at a fair price

Usha Martin makes specialty steel wire ropes and strands, with 57 percent of sales abroad. It holds more cash than debt (net cash ₹465 crore), has no pledge, a return on capital employed of 19.5 percent and a three-year cash conversion of 1.17. The operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0 with one failing row: tonnes shipped have been flat against a promise of 10 to 12 percent growth. The chart is a Stage 1 base with the momentum gate showing no momentum. At the ₹464.10 close the reward-to-risk is 1.90 to 1, just under the 2.0 core floor, and it is 2.58 to 1 at ₹458. The red team asked for a stricter first order, so the limit order at ₹449 to ₹458 is allowed only after the Q2 FY27 results, a refreshed target and stop, and a ratio of at least 2.0 at the order price. With the stop at ₹432, a refreshed target below ₹510 rules out ₹458. The Q2 date is not yet announced; past years fell between 3 and 8 November.

APOLLO: a growing defence supplier that fails the pledge rule

Apollo Micro Systems makes electronics and now whole weapons for state defence agencies. First-half FY27 sales are up 78 percent and the order book is ₹1,765 crore. It is rejected because 35.56 percent of the promoters' shares were pledged to lenders on 30 September 2026, against a 25 percent limit, and the pledge rose 5.06 points in the quarter. Cash is also thin: three-year operating cash over profit is -1.02, and the planned purchase of Premier Explosives could cost ₹2,536 crore against a net worth of ₹1,519 crore. The working-capital exemption applies, but only just. The price assumes about 30 percent yearly profit growth for ten years on our own calculation, and the reward-to-risk is 0.35 to 1 at the ₹414.90 close. A lower price alone would not lift the rejection; the December 2026 filing would have to show the pledge below 25 percent and debt to equity below 0.70 after the Premier closing.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run published with gate exceptions. A gate that the evidence could not fully meet is listed here instead of being hidden.

First, the stale-date exception. Each citation check ended PASS WITH EXCEPTIONS because many research records are older than 60 days: annual reports, rating letters, earlier management calls used to audit what management promised, and the Q1 FY27 filings. Q2 FY27 results are not filed for GRANULES (2 November), USHAMART (date not announced) or GRMOVER (13 November); APOLLO filed Q2 on 24 October and uses it. The counts are 116 for GRMOVER, 98 for GRANULES, 79 for USHAMART and 60 for APOLLO. Every as-of date is true, and each dossier lists its own stale lines and why they cannot be cured now. The cure is the Q2 FY27 filings and the FY27 annual accounts.

Second, the reviewer cycle. GRMOVER was approved by the reviewer in round 1 and its red team held the verdict, so it needed no second round. GRANULES, USHAMART and APOLLO returned REVISE in round 1 and the writers fixed every finding; the reviewer approved all three in round 2. The red team asked for a stricter verdict on GRANULES and USHAMART in round 1 (the entry orders were made conditional on a refreshed return test) and held the verdicts in round 2; the APOLLO red team held the verdict in round 1. No dossier carries BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT. Some reviewer notes were left as written because the renderer fixes them: the key-figures strip shows the tier cap (3.5 percent) as sizing while the first order is half of it, and three table headings in the APOLLO dossier open straight into a table, which the renderer emits, so a bridge sentence and a caption were added instead.

Third, tooling. The shared structural validator has no dossier mode, so the packet's code checks, the readability check, the citation check and the layout check were used instead. The shared renderer does not add first-use glossary definitions when a term sits directly inside a paragraph, so the GRANULES dossier received a post-render glossary pass in the run folder. The ledger carries no consensus coverage for GRMOVER, and for the other three the reverse DCF on the ledger basis is a poor proxy, so each dossier shows its own calculation next to it. Some filings were scanned images, which the fetch tool cannot read, so the affected figures come from locally extracted text and are labelled in the dossiers. Original BSE and NSE pages were blocked for several names, so a few promoter and warrant facts rest on press summaries.

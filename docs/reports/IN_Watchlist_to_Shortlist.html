Before you read

This report sums up the sixteenth batch of the watchlist to shortlist process for India: four companies you do not own, each with a full research dossier. Sansera Engineering and CDSL are deferred candidates, which means no purchase today and a list of checks that must pass before the name is looked at again. Kernex Microsystems is rejected because its operations have consumed cash over three years, and Banco Products is rejected because its share price is in a Stage 4 markdown, the falling phase of a stock's life cycle. Nothing here is a buy, and none of the four joins the shortlist. These calls are wrong if the September-quarter results, due between 31 October and about 14 November, change the facts behind them.

What the batch found

Each row below gives the verdict, the 23 October close, the inactive stop, our central target and the reward-to-risk ratio. A stop is the price at which the idea is wrong. Reward-to-risk is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop, and the scoring file asks for at least 2.0 for a core name and 2.5 for a turnaround. Read the ratio together with the status column, because a good ratio on paper does not override a failed red line.

Sansera Engineering: deferred candidate, not shortlisted

Sansera forges and machines engine and drivetrain parts for two-wheelers, cars and trucks, and is adding aerospace, defence and semiconductor-equipment work. No hard red line is breached: debt to equity is 0.19, interest cover 12.9 times, three-year cash over profit 1.55, no shares are pledged, and the Stage 4 selector is not met. The gate reads PARTIAL, our own label, because return on capital is 14.5 percent on Screener's basis against the 15 percent standard (the company's own basis gives 16.72 percent) and because the floor is met only at or below ₹3,098.50. At the ₹3,978.00 close the stock trades at 66.7 times profit against a three-year median of 40.2, so our central value of ₹3,457.68 is 13 percent below the price. The one-year median of 53.2 times would lift the value to ₹4,575.84, which is still only about 0.56 to 1 on the adopted stop. What changes the call is a price at or below ₹3,098.50 that holds above ₹2,918.92, or a rise in our FY28 profit per share to about ₹152 (about ₹115 if the market keeps paying 53.2 times). The reviewer approved the dossier in round 2.

CDSL: deferred candidate, not shortlisted

CDSL is one of India's two depositories and holds about 80 percent of 18.59 crore demat accounts. Every hard red line passes: debt to equity is 0.001, three-year cash over profit is 1.00 and the promoter's 15.00 percent holding is not pledged. The gate reads PARTIAL, our own label, because profit has stalled (a 4.1 percent yearly rise over FY24 to FY26 against 18.7 percent for sales), the price is 52.7 times trailing profit, and the reward-to-risk floor holds only if the multiple stays at or above 47.1 times our FY28 profit per share. The chart is the other reason to wait: it fails all three momentum tests, and the 50-day average, ₹1,328.67, is about ten sessions from crossing below the 200-day average, ₹1,309.66, which on a flat price would happen near 6 November and meet the Stage 4 selector. Results on 31 October are the next test. A re-underwrite is allowed only after those results, two closes at or above ₹1,173.60, RSI back above 32 and a short-term turn, and any adverse trigger vetoes it. The reviewer approved the dossier in round 2; one calculation requested by the red team was added afterwards.

Kernex Microsystems: rejected

Kernex makes railway Kavach and signalling electronics, and its order book of ₹3,641 crore is about 8.5 times FY26 income. It is rejected on the hard red line for three-year cash conversion: cash from operations was -₹156.31 crore against profit of ₹111.58 crore, a ratio of -1.40 against a floor of 0.25, and FY26 operating cash alone was -₹99.74 crore as borrowings rose from ₹40 crore to ₹162 crore. The scoring file lets a company escape that line with working-capital or order-book turnaround evidence and sets no numeric bar. The dossier reads the clause as needing evidence that cash is turning, which is our own judgment, and shows that under the looser reading no archetype qualifies either, because reward-to-risk is 1.62 to 1 against 2.5. What would change it is the September half-year cash flow statement, due by about 14 November, showing about ₹100 crore of positive operating cash with receivables well below ₹316 crore. That would start a fresh underwriting, not an automatic reversal. The reviewer still asked for changes in round 2, and the fixes were made without a third review.

Banco Products: rejected

Banco makes engine cooling parts, gaskets and heat shields, and owns a Dutch aftermarket business, NRF. The business is sound on the numbers: return on capital is 30.9 percent, debt to equity 0.36, three-year cash over profit 0.88 and the operating score 3.5 of 5.0, with one failing row for inventory that takes 263 days to sell. The verdict rests on the chart alone. The ₹558.40 close is below a falling 50-day average of ₹608.26, which is below a falling 200-day average of ₹621.91, and the recovery test fails on two conditions, so the Stage 4 hard red line is met. The central value of ₹701.12 and the 4.87 to 1 ratio are inactive for that reason. What would change it is a close above ₹594.21 with RSI at or above 32 and a positive two-week return, and the 6 November results showing cash from operations at least equal to half-year profit. The reviewer approved the dossier in round 2.

What this means for you is no purchase today. The next filings that could change these views are the September-quarter results, and the dossiers name dates only where a company notice gave one.

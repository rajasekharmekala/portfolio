Company case

IHCL owns and operates hotels, leases properties and manages or franchises hotels for other owners. Taj anchors the luxury portfolio. SeleQtions, Vivanta, Gateway, Ginger, Tree of Life, ama Stays & Trails, Brij, Atmantan and Qmin serve a wider set of guests. TajSATS adds air and institutional catering. At a hotel, teams coordinate rates, bookings, arrivals, housekeeping, food, maintenance, events and guest service. Room revenue varies with occupancy and price, while payroll and property costs keep operating risk high. At the October portfolio update IHCL reported 670 hotels, including 390 open properties and 280 in pipeline. H1 FY2027 had 47 signings and 20 openings. The count includes projects that do not yet earn room revenue. Q2 management-fee income and domestic same-store RevPAR each rose 25%. Trailing EPS is ₹15.53, so ₹811.65 implies 52.26x. If the price reaches ₹737.39–₹756.22, then check volume, the rising 50-day average and the Nifty market gate before reassessing. If fees, RevPAR or openings weaken for several quarters, then downgrade the thesis.

IHCL’s strength comes from a known luxury name, operating experience and partner reach. The hotel pipeline can support fee growth, but signed projects are not current revenue. The core case needs new properties to open, meet service standards and earn returns above the cost of leases and renovation.

Quality, growth and delivery

FY2026 revenue was ₹9,971 crore, EBITDA ₹3,477 crore and PAT ₹2,084 crore. IHCL reported a 19% revenue CAGR and 21% EBITDA CAGR from FY2023 to FY2026. FY2025 PAT of ₹1,908 crore included exceptional items; before them PAT was ₹1,603 crore. In Q2 FY2027, consolidated revenue was ₹2,566 crore, EBITDA ₹788 crore and PAT ₹349 crore. Operating revenue grew 22%, operating EBITDA 24%, domestic same-store RevPAR 25% and management-fee income 25%.

IHCL has achieved earlier scale and margin goals. The FY2024 release said it reached more than 300 hotels and a 33.7% EBITDA margin under Ahvaan 2025 ahead of schedule. FY2025 revenue grew 23% to ₹8,565 crore and EBITDA margin was 35.0%. FY2026 revenue grew 16%. The current Accelerate 2030 revenue and ROCE targets remain open. FY2026 pre-tax ROCE was 17% against a 20% goal.

Management raised its FY2027 consolidated revenue-growth guide from 12–14% to 15–17%; hotel-segment revenue is expected to grow 17–19%. That is a positive revision, not delivery. H1 growth and the 25% same-store RevPAR increase support the outlook, while event mix and renovated-property benefits may not repeat at the same rate.

The catering segment is a counterweight. Q2 revenue was ₹283.03 crore versus ₹285.56 crore a year earlier. Segment result fell to ₹33.06 crore from ₹52.10 crore. Lease liabilities were about ₹3,355 crore at 30 September, with gross cash of ₹4,369 crore. Liquidity is ample, but lease payments and renovation needs remain fixed commitments. Funded borrowings of about ₹116 crore are separate from leases.

Three listed competitors define the field. EIH focuses on Oberoi and Trident luxury; Lemon Tree leans toward mid-scale; ITC Hotels spans luxury and upscale. The cutoff ledger maps EIHOTEL to Stage 4, LEMONTREE to Stage 1, ITCHOTELS (ITC Hotels) to Stage 3 and INDHOTEL to Stage 2. The separate symbol ITC refers to ITC Limited, not the hotel peer. Trend is not a business or value ranking. Peer structures differ, and cutoff-aligned valuation multiples were not available.

Recent growth supports the company’s quality score. The market price still demands sustained delivery, and the 20% ROCE target is not yet achieved. The next section shows what earnings growth and a lower entry price would need to look like.

Before you read

This report sums up one batch of four India names, each with a full research dossier. Bhansali Engineering Polymers (BEPL) is a debt-free plastics maker that passes every safety test, but it is a pullback and not a buy at the market: it gets a limit order plan, which is an order to buy only at or below a set price, and that plan is valid only while its central target stays above about ₹124. Supreme Petrochem (SPLPETRO) is rejected because its price already equals our central value, which leaves no reward for the risk. NBCC and Transrail Lighting (TRANSRAILL) are rejected because their charts are in Stage 4, a long fall below the 50-day and 200-day averages, with the recovery test failing. Each dossier states the price or event that would change its verdict, and nothing here is a buy today.

What the batch found

Each row gives the verdict, the close, the entry zone (the price range where we would place a buy order), the stop, our central target and the reward-to-risk ratio. That ratio is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop. A stop is the price at which the idea is wrong. The scoring file asks for at least 2.5 for a tactical name. Read the ratio at the close together with the ratio at the top of the entry zone. The first is what a buyer gets today. The second is what a limit order would get after a pullback.

Three rules decided these outcomes. The first is a set of hard red lines on debt, share pledge, cash conversion and the Stage 4 chart test. A name is in Stage 4 only when the price is under a falling 50-day average that is itself under a falling 200-day average and the recovery test fails. NBCC and TRANSRAILL meet that line. SPLPETRO and BEPL do not: both sit above a rising 200-day average, so the written rule routes them to a pullback verdict and not to a chart rejection. The second rule is the operating test, scored out of 5.0, where a name needs at least 3.0 and may have at most one failing row. The third rule is the reward-to-risk floor of 2.5 for a tactical name. SPLPETRO fails that floor because its central value of ₹706 is below its ₹713.70 price. NBCC and TRANSRAILL clear it on paper only because their stops are close, which the dossiers say does not outweigh the chart rule.

The four decisions, one at a time

Each paragraph gives the rule behind the verdict, the number that drove it and what would change it. The full working is in the linked dossier.

BEPL: a debt-free ABS maker whose price already assumes little

Bhansali Engineering Polymers makes ABS, a hard plastic used in appliances, dashboards and kitchenware. It has no debt, no promoter pledge, a three-year cash conversion of 0.94, a return on capital employed of 23.8 percent on the Screener basis and a price-to-earnings ratio of 12.3 against its own three-year median of 15.3. It is classed as a COMPOUNDER on the static tests and scores 3.5 of 5.0 on the operating test, with two passes, three watch rows and no failure. The ledger's reverse DCF, which asks what growth the price already assumes, gives 2.0 percent a year, and the dossier's own rework on audited free cash flow gives about 4.2 percent. The chart is a Stage 3 pullback above a rising 200-day average, and all three momentum tests fail, so the verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT with a limit order in ₹105.00 to ₹108.40. Two cautions came out of the review. A margin-only stress, with the operating margin at 14 percent and everything else central, cuts the target to about ₹113 and the ratio at the zone top to 0.81 to 1, so the order is valid only while the central target stays above about ₹124. Unsecured loans to outside companies rose from ₹175.9 crore to ₹306.6 crore in six months and the borrowers are not named. Size is TIER_4, 2.0 percent of net worth, and a bounce above ₹120.85 is a trigger for a fresh review and not a buy.

NBCC: a government project manager in a Stage 4 fall

NBCC has no debt, no pledge, a return on capital employed of 29.3 percent and an order book of ₹1,26,964 crore, about ten times its trailing revenue. Its three-year cash conversion is 0.64, above the 0.25 and 0.40 red lines but under the 0.70 compounder floor, and no archetype fits. The operating test scores 3.0 of 5.0 with one pass, four watch rows and no failure. It is rejected on the chart: the ₹69.84 close is under a falling 50-day average of ₹80.87 that is under a falling 200-day average of ₹92.54, and the recovery test fails on two conditions, the close under the 20-day average of ₹74.26 and a 14-day relative strength index of 31.52 against a floor of 32. Quantified contingent liabilities are ₹3,557 crore, 111 percent of equity. The case reopens only when the recovery test passes on a fresh run and the 5 November results show revenue of at least ₹3,200 crore, a margin near 6 percent and operating cash flow of at least 0.7 times profit.

SPLPETRO: India's polystyrene leader, priced at its central value

Supreme Petrochem holds over half of the Indian polystyrene market, has almost no debt, a three-year cash conversion of 1.08 and no pledge, and no red line trips. The operating test scores 3.0 of 5.0 with one failing row, the ABS plant that has run at about 65 percent of design since March 2026. Profit for the last twelve months carries a one-off spread windfall in the June quarter that management called an aberration. On normalised profit per share of ₹21.4 for FY28 at 33 times, our central value is ₹706, so the reward-to-risk is -0.18 to 1 at the ₹713.70 close and -0.03 to 1 at the ₹707 zone top, against the 2.5 floor. The probability-weighted value is ₹690. The case reopens if operating profit per tonne holds at ₹18,000 or more for two quarters with the ABS plant at 85 percent of design (about ₹840 and 3.8 to 1), or on a close near ₹630 that builds a higher low. A price move alone does not reopen it.

TRANSRAILL: a solvent transmission contractor in a confirmed markdown

Transrail builds power transmission lines and makes the towers and conductors for them. Its order book of ₹16,035 crore is 2.3 years of sales, debt to equity is 0.29 (0.81 if about ₹1,200 crore of letter-of-credit acceptances are counted, which misses the 0.70 standard but stays under the 1.20 red line) and audited three-year cash flow equals profit. The operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0 with no failure. It is rejected on the chart: the close is under a falling 50-day average of ₹449.01 that is under a falling 200-day average of ₹499.57, and the recovery test fails on one condition, a close under the 20-day average of ₹459.71. The weighted twelve-month value is ₹499, a gain of 16 percent, and the ratio is 6.19 to 1 only at today's close. At ₹447.40 it falls to the 2.5 floor, so no order goes above that price. Cash conversion rests on ₹1,487 crore of supplier credit, and an Income Tax search in March 2026 and a planned ₹600 crore share sale are open. The name reopens only when a fresh momentum-gate run passes the recovery test after a close above the 20-day average. Strong 3 November results support an order but do not lift the rejection alone.

What changed in the shortlist and the watchlist

The tactical book already held 10 autowritten challengers, which is its cap, so a new name must beat the weakest incumbent on quality, not just fit. The precedence is operating-test pass count, then position against support, then catalyst timing, then reward-to-risk. BEPL and GROWW both have two passes of five. BEPL sits 0.90 average daily swings above support against 1.39 for GROWW, its reward-to-risk is 2.81 at the close and 3.84 at the zone top against 0.26 and 2.64, and its catalyst, delivery of the new reactor in November 2026, is nearer than the 7 December 2026 settlement change at GROWW. Run notes and gate exceptions

This run published with gate exceptions. A gate that the evidence could not fully meet is listed here instead of being hidden.

First, the stale-date exception. Each citation check ended PASS WITH EXCEPTIONS because many research records are older than 60 days: audited annual reports, rating letters, earlier management calls used to audit what management promised, and the Q1 FY27 filings. Q2 FY27 results are not yet filed for any of the four: TRANSRAILL reports on 3 November and NBCC on 5 November, while the September-quarter figures for BEPL are published and the SPLPETRO call transcript was not yet filed. The counts of stale lines are 44 for BEPL, 104 for SPLPETRO, 117 of 146 for NBCC and 149 of 180 for TRANSRAILL. Every as-of date is true, and each dossier lists its own stale lines and why they cannot be cured now. The cure is the Q2 FY27 filings in the first week of November 2026.

Second, the reviewer cycle. BEPL and TRANSRAILL returned REVISE from the reviewer in round 1 and APPROVE in round 2 after the writers fixed every finding, and their red teams approved in round 2 (BEPL asked for a stricter verdict in round 1 and the writer cut its size to TIER_4 and removed a rebound buy trigger that broke the 2.5 floor). SPLPETRO and NBCC returned REVISE from the reviewer in round 1 and again in round 2, each with one new finding that the writer fixed after round 2 without a third review, so both are marked BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT and neither is described as reviewer APPROVE. Their red teams held the verdict in round 1. The red team ran on its configured model.

Third, tooling and data. The shared structural validator has no dossier mode, so the packet's code checks, the readability check, the citation check and the layout check were used instead. The nifty500 screen does not enrich watchlist-only names, so the orchestrator added the reverse DCF and consensus fields to the four instruments in the frozen ledger by calling the ledger script's own function, and the ledger's free-cash-flow base for BEPL was reworked in its dossier because it exceeded operating cash flow. SPLPETRO's last ledger bar is the 27 October close. Annual reports, transcripts and presentations that were PDFs were converted to text locally and cached under their own addresses because the fetch cache reads only text and HTML, and several exchange and news pages returned errors or were scanned images, so affected figures are labelled in the dossiers. Four writers worked in parallel on one shared fetch cache.

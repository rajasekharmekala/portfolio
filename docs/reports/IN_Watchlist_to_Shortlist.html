How the verdicts were reached

Each company went through three layers in order. Layer 1 is a set of hard gates: financial health (return on capital, debt to equity and three-year cash conversion), a technical floor (where the price sits against its moving averages), and an asymmetry floor (the ratio of the verified upside to the downside at the stop). A name that fails any Layer 1 gate is rejected, and the later layers are not run. Layer 2 is a short scorecard of operating measures, and Layer 3 is the entry posture, which measures how far the price sits above its nearest support in units of ATR (average true range), the typical daily price swing averaged over 14 sessions.

On top of the three layers, a separate momentum test is computed in code from the frozen price snapshot. It asks whether a stock is already in a confirmed uptrend, or has just broken out of a base (a sideways stretch in which a stock builds a floor). A name with no momentum receives no allocation, however good its business. The thresholds are marked as needing calibration, and this batch treated them as they are written.

The asymmetry rule has a further consequence in this batch. If no target can be verified from a forward earnings estimate multiplied by a three-year median P/E, or from a measured move of the base, the gate fails. An analyst's single target is not accepted as the anchor. That rule explains why all four names fail on the asymmetry test, even where the business looks sound.

Terms used on this page

The asymmetry ratio is the verified upside to the target divided by the distance from the price down to the stop. A ratio of 3.0 means the target is three times as far away as the stop. The ATR (average true range) is the typical daily price swing over 14 sessions, so a distance in ATR units shows how far a price has moved relative to its normal daily noise. A base is a sideways stretch in which a stock builds a floor, and a pivot is the price level a breakout must clear. Stage 1 to Stage 4 are the four phases of a stock's trend: basing, markup, topping and markdown. The core bucket is the book for durable, high-return businesses held for years. The displacement test asks whether a challenger beats the weakest incumbent in a full book. A distribution day is a session when the index falls on higher volume than the day before. The three-year median P/E is the middle value of the price-to-earnings ratio over three years, which is used to set a fair multiple for a target. CFO/PAT is three-year operating cash flow divided by three-year profit after tax, which shows whether reported profit turns into cash. ROCE (return on capital employed) measures the operating return on the capital a business uses.

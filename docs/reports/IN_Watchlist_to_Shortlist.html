Before you read

This report sums up the twentieth batch of the watchlist to shortlist process for India: four companies, each with a full research dossier. Mankind Pharma passes every safety rule and the operating test, and Marine Electricals clears them only narrowly, but both prices are still weak, so each joins the shortlist as a challenger with no order today and a list of conditions to meet first. Indo Tech Transformers is rejected because its parent has pledged 82.41 percent of its shares as security for its own debt, and AIA Engineering is rejected because the gain to our target is far smaller than the loss to our stop. Nothing here is a buy today. These calls are wrong if the September-quarter results, due from 3 November, change the facts behind them.

Three rules decided these outcomes. The first is a set of hard red lines: debt, share pledge, cash conversion and a chart test called Stage 4, which is a long fall in which the close sits below a falling 50-day average that itself sits below a falling 200-day average, with no turn forming. Indo Tech Transformers fails the pledge line. No name in this batch meets the Stage 4 test on the written rule: the 200-day average is still rising for all four, even though the price file labels AIA Engineering Stage 4. The second rule is the operating test. It is scored out of 5.0, needs at least 3.0 and allows one failing row. Mankind Pharma scores 4.0, Indo Tech Transformers 4.0, AIA Engineering 3.5 and Marine Electricals exactly 3.0 with one failing row. The third rule is the reward-to-risk floor, which AIA Engineering fails by a wide margin and which Marine Electricals meets only if its price multiple holds.

The four decisions, one at a time

Each paragraph gives the rule behind the verdict, the number that triggered it and what would change it. The full working is in the linked dossier.

Indo Tech Transformers: rejected

Indo Tech Transformers makes power and distribution transformers for utilities and industry. The factory numbers are strong: FY26 sales of ₹782.08 crore, profit of ₹92.77 crore, return on capital employed of 33.0 percent on the audited figures, debt of 0.01 times equity and an order book of 1.71 times sales, and the operating test scores 4.0 of 5.0. The company is rejected on a governance red line. Its parent, Shirdi Sai Electricals, has pledged 82.41 percent of its 70.29 percent holding (61,52,236 shares) as security for the parent's own debentures, against a limit of 20 percent, and sold 5 lakh shares in June 2026 for about ₹157 crore. The chart is a Stage 3 pullback, not a Stage 4, with the close of ₹2,648.30 on the 23 October bar above a rising 200-day average of ₹2,494.45. The seed was right on the operating ratios and wrong on the claim that it passes all gates and on a link to a 765 kV grid programme. The file reopens only if pledged shares fall below 14,93,000 and fresh tests pass. The September-quarter results are due by 14 November.

Marine Electricals: shortlisted tactical challenger, no order

Marine Electricals builds switchboards and automation for ships and the navy and now power distribution units for data centres. June 2026 quarter sales grew 55 percent and the backlog is ₹2,073 crore. It clears the hard red lines only narrowly: three-year cash over profit is 0.26 against a floor of 0.25 and FY26 operating cash flow was negative, so the cash-conversion row fails and the operating test scores exactly 3.0 of 5.0. The momentum test fails all three parts, so the verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT with zero live size. Orders arm only after the half-year results on 4 November show cash from operations at or above zero, September sales of at least ₹240 crore and two closes at or above ₹384.15, and the maximum fill price is recomputed so reward-to-risk stays at 2.5 or better. The stop is ₹371.89 and the central target ₹440. The seed was partly wrong: the SBI purchase was matched by a 3 percent promoter-group sale, and the Goa plant has an 800-unit target for March 2027, not orders.

AIA Engineering: rejected

AIA Engineering is a debt-free maker of high-chrome grinding media and mill liners for mining and cement, with an operating margin near 28 percent, return on capital employed near 21 percent, no promoter pledge and three-year cash over profit of 0.77. It is rejected on price, not on quality. Our FY28 profit per share of ₹137.6 at 26.5 times gives a central target of ₹3,645 against a stop of ₹3,405.93, which is 0.38 to 1 where a core name needs 2.0, and the probability-weighted value of ₹3,574 equals the close of ₹3,579.20, so there is no margin of safety. Mining volume is still 13 percent below its FY24 peak and management gives no volume target. The written Stage 4 test is not met because the 200-day average is rising. The idea reopens after the 3 November results if mining volume reaches 42,000 tonnes with a margin of 27 percent or more, or if management shows conversions that add about 27,000 tonnes a year, and the technical tests are refreshed.

Mankind Pharma: shortlisted core challenger, no order

Mankind Pharma is the fourth-ranked Indian drug company by domestic sales, with a large field force, a growing chronic-therapy share and the Bharat Serums and Vaccines business it acquired. Every hard red line passes: debt is 0.38 times equity, interest cover is 4.3 times, the pledge is nil and three-year cash over profit is 1.30. Reported return on capital employed is 13.5 percent, below the 15 percent floor, but 17.8 percent before goodwill, a basis the scoring file allows, and the operating test scores 4.0 of 5.0. Reward-to-risk is 3.23 to 1 at the close and 4.04 to 1 at the top of the ₹2,196.10 to ₹2,281.95 zone. The close is 1.62 average daily ranges above support, just outside the buy band, so the verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT with a limit order in the zone. The reward-to-risk depends on holding a 40.9 times multiple: at 33 times it falls to 0.46 to 1. The key risk is that one quarter of faster-than-market growth does not last.

Before you read

This report sums up one batch of four India names, each with a full research dossier. Sun TV, Hero MotoCorp and Eicher Motors are sound businesses that are priced too high for the reward they offer today, so each gets a limit-order plan that only switches on after a pullback or after new results. VST Industries, a cigarette maker, is rejected because its chart is in a Stage 4 downtrend and its profit is still falling after a tax rise. None of the three passing names beats the weakest name already on the core shortlist, so none is added to it. Each dossier states the price or event that would change its verdict. Nothing here is a buy today.

What the batch found

Read the ratio at the close together with the ratio at the top of the entry zone. The first is what a buyer gets today. The second is what a limit order would get after a pullback.

Three rules decided these outcomes. The first is a set of hard red lines on debt, share pledge, cash conversion and the Stage 4 chart test (a falling price below a falling 50-day average that is itself below a falling 200-day average, with no sign of a recovery). Sun TV, Hero MotoCorp and Eicher Motors pass every money-based red line and are not in Stage 4, so for them the question is price and timing. VST Industries passes every money-based red line, including cash conversion of 0.65 against a 0.40 line for a mature business, and fails the chart test. The second rule is the operating test, scored out of 5.0, where a name needs at least 3.0 and may have at most one failing row. VST Industries misses it at 2.5. The third rule is the reward-to-risk floor of 2.0 for a core name, which VST Industries fails outright and the other three fail at the close but pass at the top of their entry zone.

The table shows that the three passing names have a business that works and a price that is not yet low enough. Hero MotoCorp and Eicher Motors clear the floor only at the upper half of their zones, and both fall short of it if the multiple does not hold. Sun TV clears it only after the Q2 results. The system code REJECTED_BELOW_HURDLE for VST Industries records that a hard rule was not met; the dossier says plainly which rules failed and which tests would reopen the name.

The four decisions, one at a time

Each paragraph below says what the dossier concluded, why, and the level or event that would change it. The dossiers carry the full working, including five-year tables, estimate sections and a numbered source list.

SUNTV: a debt-free broadcaster with a big cash pile whose profit is no longer growing

Sun TV owns the leading regional channels in Tamil, Telugu, Kannada and Malayalam, an Indian Premier League cricket franchise and about ₹7,395 crore of cash and investments, which is 30 percent of its ₹24,393 crore market value. Return on capital employed is 16.4 percent, debt-to-equity is 0.01, three-year cash conversion is 1.11 and the promoters have pledged nothing, so every red line passes. The operating score is 3.5 of 5.0 with two passes (cricket economics and cash conversion) and three watch rows (advertising, subscription, and content cost and margin). Earnings per share fell from ₹48.85 in FY24 to ₹36.53 in FY26, 79 percent of the first-quarter profit rise came from cricket, and companies linked to the promoters pay 56 percent of subscription income. The share is in Stage 3, 2.24 ATR (average true range, the typical daily price swing) above support, which is outside the 1.6 ATR limit. Our base value is ₹615 against bull ₹790 and bear ₹470, a probability-weighted ₹606.5 that sits below the ₹619.90 close. The dossier arms no order today. After the Q2 FY27 results on 30 October 2026, four checks must pass: advertising of at least ₹270 crore and domestic subscription of at least ₹470 crore, a rebuilt earnings bridge, reward-to-risk of at least 2.0 on the base, stressed and 13-times tests, and a fresh support and stop check. If they do, half goes in at ₹520 and half at ₹512 for at most 2.0 percent of net worth. A close below ₹504.37 ends the idea.

HEROMOTOCO: a debt-free market leader with margin and share both slipping

Hero MotoCorp is the largest two-wheeler maker in India by volume, rated AAA, with return on capital employed of 35.2 percent, debt-to-equity of 0.06, three-year cash conversion of 1.26 and a 3.6 percent dividend yield, at 16.8 times earnings. The operating score is 3.5 of 5.0 with two passes (price and mix, and cash conversion) and three watch rows (domestic share against the industry, the electric VIDA ramp and its losses, and margin pass-through). The September-quarter standalone profit rose only 4.5 percent and missed estimates; the 41 percent rise in consolidated profit is mostly a ₹397 crore gain on its Ather stake. The share is in Stage 1, 1.75 ATR above support at ₹4,825, just beyond the 1.6 limit. Our base value is ₹5,670 against bull ₹6,940 and bear ₹4,035, and the probability-weighted value is ₹5,579. Half goes in at ₹4,825 and half at ₹4,950 for at most 3.5 percent of net worth. The upper half is conditional: if refreshed FY28 earnings per share fall below ₹307, it is repriced to where the ratio returns to 2.0, and cancelled below ₹4,825. A close below ₹4,665 ends the idea.

EICHERMOT: an excellent motorcycle franchise at a price that needs patience

Eicher Motors makes Royal Enfield motorcycles and owns 54.4 percent of the VECV commercial-vehicle venture. Return on capital employed is 30.5 percent, debt-to-equity is 0.02, and the company holds ₹19,091 crore of cash and investments, but the price-to-earnings multiple of 34.2 is 4 percent above its three-year median. Three-year cash conversion is 0.88. The ledger tags the chart as a turnaround inflection, but the business is not a turnaround, so the dossier classifies it as a compounder. The operating score is 3.5 of 5.0 with two passes and three watch rows, and gross margin fell 2.7 points in the June quarter. The share is in Stage 1, 0.79 ATR above support, and only Test C of the momentum gate passes. Our base value is ₹8,260 against bull ₹9,870 and bear ₹5,600, with a probability-weighted ₹7,998, 10.5 percent above the close. A 22.7 percent margin would cut reward-to-risk at ₹7,113 to 1.55, so the zone top is lowered to ₹7,038, where the ratio is 2.85 on the base and 2.00 on that margin stress. The ₹7,038 and ₹6,900 orders stay inactive until the Q2 FY27 results (deadline 14 November 2026) are read. A close below ₹6,609.11 ends the idea.

VSTIND: a cigarette maker whose profit has not finished resetting

VST Industries has no borrowings, a CRISIL AA+ rating and ₹615 crore of liquid assets. The February 2026 tax rise cut its September-quarter volume by 19.1 percent to 562 million sticks a month, net revenue after excise by 21.4 percent and profit by 30.7 percent. The headline price-to-earnings multiple of 12.4 and the 6.34 percent dividend yield rest on trailing profit that includes a ₹116.7 crore March 2026 quarter. Run-rate earnings per share are about ₹8 to ₹10, which is a multiple of 19 to 23, and the ₹12 dividend was 244 percent of first-half profit. The watchlist seed's price-to-earnings of 52.4 could not be reproduced from any filing. The share is in Stage 4 at its 52-week low with an RSI of 16.5, and Test C fails on its higher-low floor, its short-term turn and its momentum floor. Our base value is ₹168 against bull ₹234 and bear ₹94, a probability-weighted ₹155 against the ₹189.41 close. The name reopens only if all four tests hold: a refreshed bar passes Test C; Q3 FY27 volume reaches at least 600 million sticks a month with at least ₹55 crore of EBITDA and 30 paise per stick; the operating score recomputes to at least 3.0; and reward-to-risk recomputed is at least 2.0. A read-across from ITC's results on 2 November 2026 is the first test of whether the volume loss is VST's own.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run published with gate exceptions. A gate that the evidence could not fully meet is listed here instead of being hidden.

First, the stale-date exception. Each citation check ended PASS WITH EXCEPTIONS because many research records are older than 60 days: annual reports, earlier management calls used to audit what management promised, and June-quarter filings. The stale lines are 82 for Sun TV, 45 for Hero MotoCorp, 84 for Eicher Motors and 68 for VST Industries. For Sun TV, Q2 FY27 is due on 30 October 2026, one day after the as-of date. For Eicher Motors the newest periodic filing is 92 days old and Q2 is due by 14 November. For VST Industries the Q2 FY27 data is fresh and the stale lines are annual reports, tax notifications and a rating letter. Every as-of date is true, and each dossier lists its own stale groups with ages and cure dates. The cure is the Q2 FY27 filings in the first half of November 2026.

Second, the data. The ledger for 29 October was built once, and its reverse-DCF and consensus-revision fields are empty for these four names because they were not added as extra symbols at build time. A rebuild with them added failed on the same afternoon: the data vendor was rate limiting and then returned no 29 October bar for the Nifty 50, which fails the freshness check, so nothing was written and the original ledger was kept unchanged. The Chapter 5 tables therefore show placeholders for the ledger rows, and each dossier supplies its own reverse DCF at the house 12 percent discount rate, 20 times terminal multiple and ten years, with the working shown. Consensus earnings revisions are not available for any of the four. Several sources were blocked or returned PDF files as binary, so the writers extracted PDF text locally and stored it through the cache's text-file option; blocked sources are shown as not found. Hero MotoCorp could not obtain the full Q2 call transcript or a three-year median multiple, and VST Industries has no call transcripts, investor presentations or analyst coverage.

Third, the reviewer cycle. All four dossiers returned REVISE from the reviewer in round 1, with 18 findings for Sun TV, 10 for Hero MotoCorp, 9 for Eicher Motors and 14 for VST Industries. The red team returned a stricter verdict for Sun TV and Eicher Motors, which led to a tighter entry zone and a conditional activation rule for each, and verdict_holds for Hero MotoCorp and VST Industries. In round 2 the reviewer returned APPROVE for Eicher Motors and VST Industries and REVISE for Sun TV (one judgment finding) and Hero MotoCorp (two findings). The Eicher Motors red team asked for one more change in round 2: that the orders stay inactive until the Q2 results are read. Those changes were made after the final round without a third review, so Sun TV, Hero MotoCorp and Eicher Motors carry BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT and did not receive a clean reviewer approval from every reviewer. The Eicher Motors red team did not confirm that the verdict holds. VST Industries received one mechanical note in round 2, fixed afterwards with no figure changed.

Fourth, tooling. The shared structural validator has no dossier mode, so the packet's code checks, the readability check, the citation check and the layout check were used instead. The four writers shared one worktree and fetch cache, and each kept its working files in its own folder. Email is intentionally skipped for this workflow.

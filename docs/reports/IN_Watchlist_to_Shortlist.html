The four decisions, one at a time

Each paragraph states the rule behind the verdict, the number that triggered it and what would change it. The full working is in the linked dossier, with five-year tables, a peer comparison, growth scenarios and sources.

Grindwell Norton: deferred candidate, not shortlisted

Grindwell makes abrasives and industrial ceramics and is controlled by Saint-Gobain. It has no debt, about ₹1,097 crore of net liquid cash and a return on capital of 21 percent. No hard red line is breached, the Stage 4 test is not met, and the reward-to-risk floor is cleared on our central value. The routing rule itself gives the gate result PASSED_CONDITIONAL and a wait-for-support route. Deferring is the dossier's own judgment, because the cushion over the floor is thin, no momentum test passes, and results due on 29 October have an unknown outcome. The central value is only 6.6 percent above the level that gives 2.0 to 1, and it needs the market to keep paying about 47 times profit.

Supreme Industries: deferred candidate, not shortlisted

Supreme makes plastic pipes and other plastic products, with no debt, a return on capital of 20.7 percent and no pledge in the exchange filings. This is the batch's momentum-gate name: only one of the three momentum tests passes. The gate result is PARTIAL because the routing rule's positive clause is not met literally. The close is under a falling 200-day average and the stock is not in Stage 2 (the rising phase of a price trend, with the close above a rising 200-day average). The reward-to-risk floor is missed at the close (1.62) and met at the zone top (2.26), by a thin margin. A quarter of group profit came from an associate company, and results are due on 26 October.

SKF India: rejected

SKF India makes bearings for vehicles. In October 2025 it went through a demerger (a split into two companies) that moved the industrial business out. The chart fails the Stage 4 test on a price series adjusted for that split, and that test alone decides the verdict. Debt, cash conversion and pledge pass, and the operating test scores 3.5. The central value of ₹1,756.34 is well above the close, but profit margin fell from 19.2 percent to 12.5 percent in the retained business, and the price history before the split cannot be compared directly. Reopening needs the chart to clear first.

Affordable Robotic and Automation: rejected

Affordable is a small Pune maker of welding robots and parking systems, with a market value near ₹189 crore and thin trading. The verdict has two independent grounds. The chart fails the Stage 4 test, and the operating score of 2.5 is under the 3.0 minimum, mainly because customers take about 179 days to pay. The three-year cash rule is contested and does not carry the verdict. Operating cash of ₹7.18 crore in FY26 is propped up by a promoter loan and borrowing, and falls to about −₹35.5 crore without them, on our reading of the statement.

What this means for you is no purchase today. The next filings that could change these views are the September-quarter results, and the dossiers name dates only where a company notice gave one.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run published with gate exceptions. That means some checks could not be fully met with the evidence available. Each exception below names what failed, why it cannot be cured now, how it lowers confidence and what would cure it. The status of every dossier is PUBLISHED_WITH_GATE_EXCEPTIONS.

Data date. The run took place on Sunday 25 October 2026. The benchmark's 23 October bar was present and every batch name had a 23 October bar, so every price and technical level uses that session. Results filed after that session, and filings whose release time before the close could not be proven, are used in no gate or calculation, and each dossier keeps them in a labelled overlay or leaves them out.

Stale periodic filings. The citation check lists many records as EXCEPTION AS-OF lines because the latest periodic filing used for several names is the quarter to 30 June 2026, about 117 days old. Each dossier lists each group with its age and cure. The cure is to rerun the research once the next results are filed and priced.

Ledger fields. The ledger has no reverse DCF or consensus revisions for these names, so each dossier states its own recompute beside the ledger's value and labels it a price-implied diagnostic. Consensus figures are a dated overlay and enter no gate. Several figures rest on secondary readings, such as aggregator ratios and pledge summaries, and are labelled where used. The seed figures for all four names were stale and are replaced.

Validator mismatch. The shared structural validator has only market-report and portfolio-audit modes. Run on a single-company dossier, it fails a check built for those formats, which asks for case notes for all holdings, and it prints a few template bridge warnings. The review packet code skips it for dossiers, and no script was modified. The readability check also looks for estimate parts and a Chapter 7 that this summary does not carry, because the dossiers do. The cure is a dossier mode in the validator and a summary mode in the readability check.

Reviewer gate. Grindwell was approved by the reviewer in round 2, and the red team's one correction, a stress claim that its own royalty case contradicted, was made afterwards without a third review. SKF India, Supreme and Affordable reached the strict two-round limit with the reviewer asking for changes in round 2 (an unrendered table entry and wording fixes for SKF India, unrendered template code and a label bar defined two ways for Supreme, and an inference label, an earnings explanation and a status sentence for Affordable). Those fixes were made afterwards without a third review, so those three close as BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT. This summary report was not reviewed separately.

Status changes during review. Affordable first called the chart alone its single hinge, and the reviewers showed that its operating score is an independent second ground. Supreme first blamed the missed floor for its PARTIAL gate, but the floor is cleared at the zone top, so the cause is the unmet routing clause. SKF India's operating test row on industry growth printed unrendered template text and was rebuilt from the record, and Grindwell's margin over the floor was corrected from 6.2 to 6.6 percent.

Judgment calls. The SKF India rejection applies the Stage 4 selector to a price series that we scaled for the demerger, which the dossier labels a sensitivity assumption. The Affordable rejection does not depend on the contested cash rule. The Supreme deferral depends on how the routing rule and the scoring file's current-multiple wording are read, and Grindwell's deferral rather than a shortlist label is the dossier's own judgment. If the repository owner confirms a different reading, those labels would change with the same reopening steps.

Source quality. One company agent used a private page cache because the shared cache is off limits, and no agent disabled certificate checks. Some company-hosted documents were image-only or unreadable, so those figures rest on cross-checked aggregator readings and are labelled. Pledge evidence is stated as no pledge observed in the readable evidence unless a primary filing confirms it.

Holding labels. The verdict list has no hold-and-wait label, so the routing rule's WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT is applied to pipeline names and printed with its plain meaning. The orchestrating agent could not start nested subagents, so the four company agents and the reviewers ran as separate sessions, one per company.

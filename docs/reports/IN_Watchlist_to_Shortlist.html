Before you read

This report sums up the eighteenth batch of the watchlist to shortlist process for India: four companies, each with a full research dossier. Uno Minda, Action Construction Equipment and Dixon Technologies all pass the safety rules and the operating test, but their prices are falling and show no sign of a turn, so each joins the shortlist as a challenger with no order today and a list of conditions to meet first. Nothing here is a buy today. These calls are wrong if the September-quarter results, due from 28 October to about 14 November, change the facts behind them.

Three rules decided these outcomes. The first is a set of hard red lines: debt, share pledge, cash conversion and a chart test called Stage 4, which is a long fall below both the 50-day and the 200-day average. Only Macfos breaks a line. Over three years its cash from operations was negative while it reported profit, so cash over profit is −0.49 against a floor of 0.25. The second rule is the operating test. It is scored out of 5.0, needs at least 3.0 and allows one failing row. Uno Minda, Action Construction Equipment and Dixon each score 3.5, and Macfos scores 2.5. The third rule is the reward-to-risk floor. A ratio on paper is not enough. Dixon's 2.07 to 1 holds only at the very top of its buy zone, and its 1.13 to 1 at the close is far below the floor.

Uno Minda: shortlisted challenger, no order

Uno Minda makes switches, lighting, alloy wheels, seating and electric-vehicle parts for cars and two-wheelers. Every hard red line passes: borrowings are 0.40 times equity, interest cover is 9.6 times, three-year cash over profit is 1.17 and the promoter pledge is 0.0 percent. Return on capital employed is 19.6 percent against the 15 percent standard, the operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0, and the business fits the compounder profile. The chart is the problem: the price is in Stage 1, a base (a flat resting phase after a fall), with the momentum test failing, and the formal Stage 4 crossover is 15 to 45 sessions away depending on which pace of the 50-day average is used. The verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT with orders in ₹1,030 to ₹1,050 only after the 5 November results show an operating margin (the share of sales left after running costs) of at least 10.8 percent and a fresh momentum pass. A close below ₹997.11 voids the idea. The stock trades at about 50 times trailing profit and the market price already assumes about 19 percent a year of profit growth for ten years.

Action Construction Equipment: shortlisted challenger, no order

Action Construction Equipment makes mobile and tower cranes, forklifts and construction machinery, and holds about 63 percent of the Indian mobile-crane market. It has almost no debt (0.004 times equity), return on capital employed is 32.7 percent, three-year cash over profit is 1.10 and the pledge is 0.0 percent. The operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0 with no failing row. The price fell 13.5 percent in a week to ₹1,000.10 and sits in a Stage 3 pullback above its rising 200-day average, which the rules treat as a reason to wait and not a reason to reject. Reward-to-risk is 2.25 to 1 at the close and 3.09 to 1 at ₹985, but it falls to 1.99 if the market pays only 26 times earnings. The verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT with a limit order (an order that fills only at your price or better) in ₹955 to ₹985 that goes live only after a retest (a return to the low that holds) and a refreshed gate. The Q2 results, due by 14 November, are the first test of the 15 percent margin the case rests on.

Dixon Technologies: shortlisted challenger, no order

Dixon is India's largest contract maker of mobile phones, televisions and home appliances. All red lines pass (return on capital employed 29.2 percent, debt to equity 0.21, three-year cash over profit 1.08), and the operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0 with one failing row, the phone-division margin of 2.6 percent. The case is thin. Reported profit includes about ₹1,184 crore of gains on a stake in Aditya Infotech, so on underlying profit the stock trades at about 81 times earnings and the price assumes about 25 percent a year of underlying growth, not the 13 percent the data file shows. Reward-to-risk is 1.13 to 1 at the ₹11,161 close and passes the 2.0 floor only at or below about ₹11,009. The probability-weighted value is ₹10,930, which is 2.1 percent below the price. The verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT, with the first evidence in the 30 October results and an upgrade only if the next year's earnings estimate reaches ₹245 and the chart turns.

Macfos (Robu.in): holding flagged for replacement

Macfos runs an online shop for robotics, drone and electronics parts and is listed on the BSE SME segment. You hold 165 shares bought on 28 September 2026 at ₹1,100 each (cost ₹181,500, now worth ₹156,057, a loss of 14.02 percent, 3.71 percent of the portfolio). Sales grew 56.8 percent a year over three years and return on capital employed is 33.8 percent, but over the same three years the business reported about ₹54.6 crore of profit while operations used about ₹26.8 crore of cash, because stock and supplier advances of about ₹109 crore were funded by bank debt. That is a cash conversion of −0.49 against a floor of 0.25, so a hard red line fails, and the operating test scores 2.5 of 5.0. The verdict is REPLACE_UNDERPERFORMER. No discretionary sale is placed before the half-year cash flow statement filed with the Q2 results on 28 October; a decision test on 29 October either cancels the sale or places a limit sell at ₹990 valid to 13 November, and a close below ₹874.91 forces a sale at the next open.

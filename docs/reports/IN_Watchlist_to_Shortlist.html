SHILCTECH: Shilchar Technologies

REJECTED Shilchar makes made-to-order transformers for solar, wind and power distribution, has no borrowings and a reported return on capital of 43%. The rejection is about timing and operating evidence, not solvency. The chart is in Stage 4 (a falling 50-day average at ₹4,051.67 and a falling 200-day at ₹4,090.88 above a ₹3,769.80 close), so the Layer 1 red line on a Stage 4 breakdown without a base fails; the 63-day low of ₹3,720 is the 16 October low itself, so no base has formed. Layer 2 scores 2 of 5: the June 2026 quarter showed sales down 15.2% and profit down 49.7%, the operating margin fell to 16.4% (a fail), and management missed its own FY26 sales, utilisation and margin guidance. All three momentum tests fail. The base value of ₹4,726 (+25.4%) and probability-weighted value of ₹4,344 (+15.2%) are positive, but the bear case of ₹2,203 carries a 35% chance. The dossier reopens the name only on a rerun of every gate after the September-quarter results: a margin before other income of at least 21% on sales of at least ₹170 crore, then a weekly close above ₹4,091 with a flat or rising 50-day average. A 21% margin still leaves that KPI on WATCH, so Layer 2 would stay at 2 of 5 until the margin reaches 25%. The September-quarter results date has not been announced (the legal deadline is 14 November); the earlier note of 27 October could not be verified.

TRENT: Trent

REJECTED Trent runs Westside, Zudio and Star Bazaar. Every red line passes (debt to equity 0.36 with lease liabilities, interest cover 14.4 times, no pledge, three-year cash flow to profit 1.20) and September-quarter revenue grew about 23%. It is rejected on reward to risk: our base target of ₹3,495 against the ₹2,958.70 close and a ₹2,474.56 stop gives 1.11 to 1 against the 3.0 floor. The market already prices 26.3% profit growth a year for ten years, and a return of the multiple to its documented trough of 68.1 times would cost 7.8% even if earnings arrive. The ledger puts the chart in Stage 3, and the momentum gate passes only its turnaround test. Layer 2 is 3 of 5: same-store growth is low single digits (WATCH) and revenue per square foot fell 10.7% in the June 2026 quarter (a fail). Either of those two moving to a pass would lift the score to 4 of 5, but the dossier states that this only triggers a rerun of the price, chart and reward-to-risk gates, which can still fail. The next dated event is the 29 October results; a close at or below ₹2,730 that holds above the ₹2,474.56 stop, or a weekly close above ₹3,399.70 on volume, would also reopen the review.

SOLARINDS: Solar Industries India

REJECTED Solar Industries is India's largest listed explosives maker and is becoming a defence-munitions group (order book ₹21,350 crore, return on capital 38%, chart in Stage 2 with a pass on the momentum test). It is rejected on price and funding geometry, not on the business: our base target of ₹28,100 (FY27 consensus profit per share of ₹281.58 times a median multiple of 99.8) against the ₹20,011 close and a ₹16,017.48 stop (the 200-day average less one average daily range) gives 2.03 to 1 against the 3.0 core floor. A tactical-style stop would give 3.96, and the dossier shows that as a sensitivity, not a decision. Layer 2 scores 3 of 5 because FY26 cash conversion fell to 0.36 and the all-cash ₹12,951 crore purchase of Omnia is not yet funded in a published plan (management's own debt statements imply peak debt to equity of 0.74 to 0.93; Jefferies projects 1.2; the red line is 1.20). The probability-weighted value is ₹24,820 (+24.0%), but that depends on a multiple near 100 times, and the bear case is as likely as the base. The verdict changes to a buy-on-support only if Layer 2 returns to 4 of 5 (cash flow in the September quarter, estimated 6 November, and a funding plan with peak debt to equity below 1.20) and the price is at or below about ₹19,000.

MEDANTA: Global Health (Medanta)

REJECTED Medanta runs quaternary hospitals (the most complex care) in Gurugram, Lucknow, Patna, Indore and Ranchi, with a Noida hospital ramping. It passes every financial floor (return on capital 17.4%, debt to equity 0.30, three-year cash flow to profit 1.29, no pledge) and scores 4 of 5 on its operating scorecard, but it fails the chart. The stock fell about 12% in a month, to a close of ₹1,240 (RSI 29, under a falling 50-day average of ₹1,412.13), because the sector sold off on Supreme Court questions about medicine mark-ups and a government cap on cancer-drug margins; no Medanta-specific event was found. All three momentum tests fail and the Core technical floor (Stage 1 breakout or Stage 2) is not met. The 4.43 to 1 ratio (base ₹1,484, stop ₹1,184.91) and the ₹1,412 weighted value (+13.9%) are not a basis to buy, and at the historical March 2026 multiple the base value falls to ₹1,178, below the stop. The dossier makes every re-entry trigger review-only: a close above the 20-day average with RSI back above 35 prompts a fresh ledger and a full rerun, not an order; the Supreme Court hearing on 27 October and the Q2 results (date not announced) are the dated events; and about ₹1,037 is a re-review level only after a close below ₹1,184.91 is followed by a new base.

What this means for you: no order today. None of the four is held, and each has a defined event or level that would justify a fresh look: Trent's 29 October results, the 27 October Supreme Court hearing for Medanta, Solar Industries' September-quarter cash flow and Omnia funding plan, and Shilchar's September-quarter margin and a recovery above its 200-day average.

Run notes and gate exceptions

Status: PUBLISHED_WITH_GATE_EXCEPTIONS. Several exceptions apply, and each dossier carries its own run-notes section with the detail. First, the latest periodic filing for every name is the quarter ended 30 June 2026, which is more than 60 days before the report date for most of the records, and no newer filing existed. The citation check was run with its stale-date allowance and printed PASS WITH EXCEPTIONS for all four (stale records: SHILCTECH 111 of 129, TRENT 79 of 103, SOLARINDS 82 of 128, MEDANTA 104). Every as-of date is true and none was edited. The cure is the September-quarter results: Trent on 29 October, Solar Industries about 6 November (an estimate; no date announced), and Shilchar and Medanta by mid-November (dates not announced; the legal deadline is 14 November). Second, the shared validator accepts only market-report and portfolio-audit kinds, and the readability checker expects the fixed chapters of a company dossier, so neither fits a batch summary; the four dossiers themselves passed the readability check. Third, sources that could not be read: Shilchar's PIB and Moneycontrol pages (HTTP 403, so government figures come from trade-press summaries), Screener pages for KIMS and Aster DM (HTTP 429, so the Medanta peer matrix has four peers), Solar Industries' FY26 annual report (the file found was a cover letter) and its image-only NSE results copy (figures rest on Screener and agreeing secondary summaries), and a primary-source Trent earnings-call transcript; nothing was filled from memory, and each dossier says what is missing. Fourth, the ledger's price series for Trent is not consistently adjusted for its 1-for-2 bonus (record date 4 June 2026): the close drops 33% in one session on 1 January 2026, which distorts the ledger's 52-week high and its 63-day-old 200-day average. Adjusted, the high is ₹3,399.70 (13.0% below the close). The gate results (Tests A and B fail, Test C passes) do not change, the dossier discloses it, and the ledger was not edited; other recently split or bonus-adjusted names may carry the same distortion. Fifth, Shilchar has only 223 NSE trading bars since its November 2025 listing, so one momentum sub-test (the 200-day average 63 days ago) could not be measured. Sixth, the ledger was built once for the 2026-10-16 session with the four batch names added as extras so that their reverse-DCF and consensus blocks exist; the ledger's reverse-DCF basis for all four is net profit derived from market cap and the trailing P/E, which each dossier explains. Confidence is lower on any figure that depends on a stale filing, and every decision is written so that it changes on a stated price or on the next results.

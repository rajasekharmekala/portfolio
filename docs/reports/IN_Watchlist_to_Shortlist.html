2. Company and operating evidence

Astra designs and makes radio-frequency and microwave subsystems for defence, space and weather programmes. The company describes work in radar, electronic warfare, telemetry, space and meteorology. The latest filing reports one operating segment, RF and Microwave Products, so public accounts do not show profit and cash returns for each product group [1][8][9][10]. Radar is the largest disclosed work area by revenue, while product pages also show receiver-exciter chains, transmit-receive modules, power amplifiers, telemetry products, space electronics and weather radar. These catalogues explain capability, not the sales mix: no audited revenue or margin split by vertical was found [8][19][20][21][26].

A programme subsystem is built to a customer’s band, signal, power, temperature, interface and reliability needs. The practical route can begin with design review, move through prototype and electrical tests, then face customer qualification and first-article inspection before production. Each stage can require engineering changes. A qualified part may be hard to replace once embedded in a wider system, but qualification is not exclusivity and a design win is not yet a production shipment [9][10][11][14].

FY26 standalone revenue was ₹1,156 crore, PAT ₹178 crore and basic EPS ₹18.73. Cash flow from operations recovered to ₹376 crore after negative cash flow in FY24 and FY25. But collections remain slow: the annual report showed 219 receivable days and a 371-day working-capital cycle. These figures make cash conversion a key check on growth [1]. The audited series shows revenue of ₹904 crore in FY24, ₹1,044 crore in FY25 and ₹1,156 crore in FY26. PAT rose from ₹71 crore to ₹143 crore and then ₹178 crore. The rise in profit and the cash-flow rebound support the long-term case; two prior years of negative operating cash flow and slow collections show why a single positive year is not enough [1].

At March 2026, reported debt was ₹287 crore, debt-to-equity was 0.22, and interest cover was 5.45 times. FY26 CFO of ₹376 crore was above PAT of ₹178 crore, a strong year for cash generation. Yet much of Astra’s billing falls late in the financial year, and high receivable days tie up funds until inspection and customer acceptance. The next test is whether cash stays positive as the HAL order moves from award to production [1][3].

Q1 FY27 standalone revenue fell 10.7% to ₹176 crore and PAT fell 24.4% to ₹10 crore. Management cited customer approval delays and late technical issues. The company reported a standalone order book of ₹2,156 crore at 30 June. It later announced a ₹2,205 crore HAL award for 122 active antenna units and 121 interface frames for Uttam Radar. Management then described a post-quarter order book near ₹4,300 crore. Those order values are future work, not reported revenue or cash [2][4][12]. The HAL award is large relative to the June standalone backlog and creates a chance to grow. It also concentrates execution: parts must pass production, integration, test and acceptance before the company can recognise all the expected sales. Public materials in this source set do not give a delivery-by-quarter schedule, margins by subsystem or milestone billing terms. This report therefore does not treat the announced award as near-term EPS or cash [2][4].

Q1 results also make the year-on-year task harder. The release target of 10%–15% revenue growth calls for later quarters to recover from the first-quarter decline. The call and investor deck state different ranges, which leave the precise goal unresolved. If customer approvals clear quickly, the July award may bolster future shipments; if acceptance stays late, production and billing may move into later periods. The next half-year filing can test both the growth claim and the order conversion story [2][3][4].

Guidance is not consistent across the company’s Q1 materials: the release gives 10%–15% FY27 growth, the call says above 15% and about ₹1,350 crore, and the investor deck gives 15%–20%. The dossier shows the difference instead of selecting the most optimistic number [2][3][4].

The proposed demerger of the Space, Meteorology and Hydrology business has received exchange letters dated 29 October. The letters are process steps, not final approval or an effective separation. The current financials do not provide standalone economics for that business [5][6][7]. The proposed separation may help investors see a clearer mix, but no value is added to the scenarios. There is no final scheme ratio, tribunal order or effective date in the cited material. Until those arrive, the listed company’s current accounts remain the basis for revenue, EPS and valuation. An exchange no-objection is necessary process evidence; it is not the transaction’s completion [5][6][7].

Official product and programme sources help define the field but do not prove Astra won a full platform: Astra’s radar, electronic-warfare and meteorology pages [19][20][21], DRDO and Defence Production material on electronics and radar [22][23], PIB updates on LCA procurement and weather radar [24][26], and BEL’s electronic-warfare catalogue [25]. 7. What would change the decision

Reopen underwriting after a Q2 or H1 filing shows stronger revenue and cash conversion, and the share price regains its 30-week and 50-day averages. A company reconciliation of FY26 EBITDA and one consistent FY27 growth range would also improve confidence. No fixed price alone changes the current verdict.

There are four separate conditions because no single catalyst repairs the whole case. Better orders matter only if Astra can deliver and collect. A trend break matters only if earnings and price can support a new valuation. Any price band remains a monitoring point until the underlying growth and cash claims are clearer. If the company reports stronger results but stays below its moving averages, the setup remains weak. If the chart repairs before earnings improve, the business evidence still needs review.

Only after Q2 or H1 FY27 results set a same-basis growth range and bridge FY26 EBITDA should the model change. Without both, retain the rejection.

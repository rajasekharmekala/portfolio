Start here

This report covers one batch of four companies from the watchlist, each underwritten through the same three layers of gates. A company that fails a gate is not bought, however good its story. The three layers are: Layer 1, the hard floors (returns on capital, debt, cash conversion, the price trend, and a verifiable price target); Layer 2, a scorecard of business measures; and Layer 3, the entry price relative to support. A company that fails Layer 1 is rejected, and Layer 2 and Layer 3 are not used for the decision.

The price data comes from one market data ledger built for the 12 October 2026 close, so every price, average and momentum reading in this report is the same number everywhere. Company fundamentals come from the sources recorded for each company, each with the date it describes. A figure with no recorded source is shown as N/A, and no figure was estimated to fill a gap.

Some terms recur. A moving average is the market's average price over a set number of days, which works like a trend line that smooths out the daily noise. The 50-day and 200-day averages are the two the framework watches most closely: a price above both, with both rising, reads as a healthy trend, and a price below both reads as a damaged one. The four stages describe where a stock sits in its cycle: Stage 1 is a quiet base, Stage 2 is a rising markup, Stage 3 is a topping pullback, and Stage 4 is a decline. Asymmetry is the ratio of the reward to the risk, measured from the entry price to a verified target and to a stop-loss price, and the framework will not buy a Core position without a ratio of at least 3.0.

What each gate found

The technical gate uses the price ledger. A company is in Stage 2 when its price is above a rising 50-day average and a rising 200-day average. A base is the sideways stretch where a stock rests before its next move, and a breakout is a move above the top of that range. A Tactical position needs Stage 2, and a Core position needs a Stage 1 base breakout or Stage 2. TIMKEN closed at 2,927.00 on 12 October 2026, below its 50-day average of 3,178.95 and its 200-day average of 3,281.88, so it is in Stage 4. BEL closed at 373.90, below its 200-day average of 417.51, so it is also in Stage 4. MAXHEALTH closed at 865.95, below its 50-day average of 1,007.80 and its 200-day average of 1,028.26, which places it in Stage 4, and its momentum test returned NO_MOMENTUM. TRENT closed at 2,913.00, slightly above its 50-day average of 2,866.75 but with that average falling, which is the topping pattern called Stage 3.

The financial gate uses the company's reported numbers, as recorded in the research for each company. BEL passes on return on capital (36.4%) and debt (near zero), but fails the three-year cash test: its operating cash flow over FY24 to FY26 was 6,764 crore against profit of 15,370 crore, a ratio of 0.44, where the floor is 0.70. MAXHEALTH passes on debt (0.32) and cash conversion (1.17) but fails on return on capital, at 14.7% on the consolidated figures against the 15% floor. TIMKEN and TRENT pass the financial gates, with returns on capital of 19% and 28.3% and cash conversion of 0.94 and 1.20.

The asymmetry gate needs a target that is either a forward earnings figure multiplied by a verified three-year median price-to-earnings ratio, or a measured move (a price objective set by adding the height of a verified base to its breakout point) from a verified base. None of the four companies had both inputs in a verifiable source, so none has an admissible target. The dossiers show the reference analyst averages for context and state that they are not anchors under the rules. For TRENT, a target clearing the 3.0 floor would have to be 4,236.87 against a close of 2,913.00 and a stop of 2,471.71, which is about 104 times the FY27 earnings estimate that the TRENT dossier uses.

The financial gates separate the companies with sound numbers from the ones without, but the verdicts in this batch do not depend on the financial gates alone. Even TIMKEN and TRENT, which pass every financial test, fail on trend and on the target anchor. A reader who weighs the business quality more heavily should still see that the price trend and the missing anchor are what stop a purchase today. The record changes that follow are what the batch did to the watchlist and shortlist files.

Take TRENT as the worked example. Its close is 2,913.00 and its stop is 2,471.71, so the risk from the close to the stop is 441.29 rupees a share. The asymmetry floor for a Core position is 3.0, which means the reward to the target must be at least three times that risk. Three times 441.29 is 1,323.87, so the target must be at least 2,913.00 plus 1,323.87, which is 4,236.87 rupees. The analysts' average of 3,390 is 846.87 rupees short of that level, and the bull figure of 4,455 clears it only if its underlying forecast can be verified, which the sources did not allow. The example shows why the verdict does not depend on opinion: the arithmetic is fixed once the stop is set, and the only open question is whether a verified target can reach the required level.

The exception counts are: TIMKEN 33, BEL 53, MAXHEALTH 21 and TRENT 29 records. The cure is a later filing for the September 2026 quarter, which falls after this report date.

  • Best-effort review limit. BEL and MAXHEALTH did not reach a reviewer approval in round 2. Their round-2 findings were fixed where the sources supported a fix, and the remaining items are listed in each dossier's run notes. No third round was run.

  • Blocked sources. Some exchange and press pages returned errors or access blocks. Where a figure could only come from a blocked page, it is shown as N/A with the attempt recorded.

Glossary

A Stage 2 markup is the rising phase of a stock, when its price sits above a rising 50-day and 200-day average. A measured move is a target built from the height of a base, and the framework accepts one only when it is verified. Asymmetry means the ratio of verified upside to verified downside from the entry price, and the floor for Core positions is 3.0. Cash conversion is the three-year sum of operating cash flow divided by the three-year sum of net profit.

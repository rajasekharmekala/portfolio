Before you read

This report sums up one batch of four India names, each with a full research dossier. Bosch (BOSCHLTD) and Esab India (ESABINDIA) are strong businesses that pass every safety test, but neither is a buy today: each gets a limit order plan, which is an order to buy only at or below a set price, and no order may stand across its November results. Britannia is rejected because its chart is in Stage 4, a long fall below the 50-day and 200-day averages, with the recovery test failing, even though the business itself is of very high quality. SKF India (Industrial) is rejected because it is a new company with too little filed history to pass any of our three archetypes. Each dossier states the price or event that would change its verdict, and nothing here is a buy today.

What the batch found

Each row gives the verdict, the 28 October close, the entry zone (the price range where we would place a buy order), the stop, our central target and the reward-to-risk ratio. That ratio is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop. A stop is the price at which the idea is wrong. The scoring file asks for at least 2.0 for a core name and 2.5 for a tactical or turnaround name. Read the ratio at the close together with the ratio at the top of the entry zone. The first is what a buyer gets today. The second is what a limit order would get after a pullback.

Three rules decided these outcomes. The first is a set of hard red lines on debt, share pledge, cash conversion and the Stage 4 chart test. A name is in Stage 4 only when the price is under a falling 50-day average that is itself under a falling 200-day average and the recovery test fails. BRITANNIA meets that line, with the recovery test failing on one condition only. ESABINDIA does not: its 200-day average is still rising, so the written rule routes it to WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT rather than REJECTED, although the ledger's shorthand label calls the chart Stage 4. The second rule is the operating test, scored out of 5.0, where a name needs at least 3.0 and may have at most one failing row. The third rule is the reward-to-risk floor of 2.0 for a core name and a 12 percent expected return over twelve months. BOSCHLTD clears the floor only at its limit-order price, not at the close, and a 20, 50 and 30 percent probability mix takes its return at the zone top to 9.24 percent, below the 12 percent hurdle.

The four decisions, one at a time

Each paragraph gives the rule behind the verdict, the number that drove it and what would change it. The full working is in the linked dossier.

BOSCHLTD: a debt-free auto-parts compounder whose price already assumes a lot

Bosch has debt to equity of 0.01, a return on capital employed of 21.5 percent and a chart in Stage 2 with the momentum tests A and C both passing. It is a COMPOUNDER and the operating test scores 4.0 of 5.0 with no failing row. The problem is price. At the ₹47,625 close the reward-to-risk is only 0.39 to 1 against the 2.0 floor, and our probability-weighted twelve-month value of ₹48,675 is a gain of 2.2 percent against the 12 percent hurdle. The reverse DCF, which asks what growth the price already assumes, needs 21.1 percent a year for ten years. The plan is therefore a limit order in ₹42,700 to ₹43,700, where the ratio is 2.87 to 1, and it must not stand across the Q2 FY27 results on 6 November, the first quarter that includes the ₹9,068.68 crore purchase of its sister braking business from the parent. After the results every number is re-run and the order is released only if the combined profit, margin, target, stop and momentum gate all pass. Gap risk is real: a fill at the zone top followed by an exit at the ₹35,300 bear value costs 0.67 percent of net worth at a 3.5 percent weight, against 0.18 percent for an orderly stop-out. The red team asked for a stricter verdict in round 1, and the dossier now carries the cancel-and-release rule. One peer, ZFCVINDIA, was seeded.

BRITANNIA: a very high-return biscuit maker whose chart has not turned

Britannia earns a return on capital employed of 56 percent, has debt to equity of 0.26, no promoter pledge and a three-year cash conversion of 1.12. The operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0 with no failing row and the reward-to-risk is 3.32 to 1 at the close. It is rejected on the chart: the ₹4,567.30 close is under a falling 50-day average (₹4,987.01) that is under a falling 200-day average (₹5,451.29), and the recovery test fails on one condition, a close below the 20-day average of ₹4,741.20. The return test is also marginal: our weighted twelve-month value of ₹5,061.50 is a gain of 10.8 percent against the 12 percent hurdle, and holding earnings unchanged but cutting the multiple to 36 times gives a loss of 4.6 percent. The case reopens only when all three hold together: the recovery test passes, a fresh run shows a 12 percent return and a 2.0 reward-to-risk at the actual price, and the 5 November results hold volume growth of 7 percent and an operating margin of 16.8 percent.

ESABINDIA: a debt-free welding leader that passes the rules and waits for its results

Esab India has debt to equity of 0.01, a return on capital employed of 64.7 percent, no promoter pledge and a three-year cash conversion of 0.95. It is a COMPOUNDER and the operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0 with no failing row. The reward-to-risk is 3.11 to 1 at the ₹5,275 close and 4.84 to 1 at the ₹5,180 zone top, and the probability-weighted value of ₹5,985 is a gain of 13.5 percent at the close. The 12 percent hurdle is narrow: with a 30 percent bear weight the return at the close is 11.1 percent, which fails, and at ₹5,180 it is 13.2 percent, which passes. A cut to 31 times earnings gives a loss of 6.0 percent at the close. The first-round reviewer found that the original REJECTED verdict contradicted the written Stage 4 rule, because the 200-day average is rising (from ₹5,697.82 to ₹5,780.70 over 63 sessions). The verdict was corrected to WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT, with no order standing across the 4 November results and release only after the numbers are re-run. About 14 percent of sales and large receivables go to group companies, which the dossier flags. One peer, KENNAMET, was seeded.

SKFINDUS: a net-cash industrial bearing seller with too little history to qualify

SKF India (Industrial) was demerged in 2025 and has only 15 months of filed accounts. It has no bank debt, a return on capital employed of 28.2 percent and a Stage 1 base chart, so no red line is met. But no archetype qualifies: it fails the COMPOUNDER cash-conversion floor (0.43 over its 15 months against 0.70), the TURNAROUND test is borderline (the reverse DCF asks for 20.7 percent a year, or 15.5 percent on cleaned profit), and a TACTICAL_BREAKOUT needs Stage 2. The operating test scores 3.0 of 5.0 with one failing row, the profit margin against management's guided band. The reward-to-risk is 1.01 to 1 at the ₹2,429 close and 3.78 to 1 only at ₹2,290, below the ₹2,310.40 support. A ₹157.6 crore loan to a sister company and ₹4,924 crore of related-party ceilings are flagged. The case reopens on a margin of at least 11 percent in the 4 November results with the cash and loan tests, or a price near ₹2,290.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run published with gate exceptions. A gate that the evidence could not fully meet is listed here instead of being hidden.

First, the stale-date exception. Each citation check ended PASS WITH EXCEPTIONS because many research records are older than 60 days: annual reports, rating letters, earlier management calls used to audit what management promised, and the Q1 FY27 filings. Q2 FY27 results are not yet filed for any of the four: ESABINDIA reports on 4 November, SKFINDUS on 4 November, BRITANNIA on 5 November and BOSCHLTD on 6 November, so every earnings figure is before Q2. The counts of stale lines are 106 for BOSCHLTD, 92 for BRITANNIA, 62 for ESABINDIA and 55 for SKFINDUS. Every as-of date is true, and each dossier lists its own stale lines and why they cannot be cured now. The cure is the Q2 FY27 filings in the first week of November 2026.

Second, the reviewer cycle. SKFINDUS and BRITANNIA returned REVISE from the reviewer in round 1 and APPROVE in round 2 after the writers fixed every finding; their red teams approved (SKFINDUS in round 1, BRITANNIA in round 2 after a round 1 REVISE). BOSCHLTD returned REVISE in round 1 from both the reviewer and the red team, and in round 2 the red team approved while the reviewer found one inconsistency in the opening paragraph of chapter 7. ESABINDIA returned BLOCK from the reviewer in round 1 because the verdict contradicted the written Stage 4 rule; the verdict was changed to WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT, the red team approved both rounds, and round 2 returned one REVISE on two different stop levels. For BOSCHLTD and ESABINDIA the remaining finding was fixed after round 2 without a third review, so both are marked BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT and neither is described as reviewer APPROVE. The red team ran on its configured model.

Third, tooling. The shared structural validator has no dossier mode, so the packet's code checks, the readability check, the citation check and the layout check were used instead; its remaining findings are market-report rules that do not apply to a company dossier. The ESABINDIA page was rendered from a scratch copy of the ledger in which only the stage label text differs, because the ledger classifier labels a chart Stage 4 without testing whether the 200-day average is falling; the ledger file itself is unchanged and no number differs. Four writers worked in parallel on one shared fetch cache. Several exchange pages returned errors, blocked requests or were scanned images, so affected figures come from locally extracted text or press summaries and are labelled in the dossiers. Three-year median price-to-earnings ratios (the share price divided by yearly profit per share) were not published for several names and are recorded as not found or computed and labelled as our own calculation.

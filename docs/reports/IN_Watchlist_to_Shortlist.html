Start here

This report answers one question for four Indian stocks: does each business pass the financial floors, and does its chart and price support a new position under the house strategy? None of the four did. Each one is a reasonable business on its reported numbers, but each fails at least one hard test on the 13 October 2026 close. All four are therefore REJECTED and move to the watchlist archive. The shortlist gains no names, and one shortlist row (APCOTEXIND) changes because its earlier buy verdict no longer holds.

The watchlist is a queue. The workflow takes the next names still waiting for research, four at a time, in the order they appear in the watchlist. This batch was the first four names with the status PENDING_RESEARCH, so no name was skipped and none was pulled forward from further down the list.

Several terms carry most of the report. A Layer 1 test is a hard floor: a company that fails one is rejected before the later layers are read. A Stage 2 chart is one in a rising uptrend, and a Stage 3 chart is one where an uptrend has lost its momentum and the price has begun to roll over, which is why the house rules do not allow a new buy in it. An anchored target is a price objective built from forward earnings multiplied by a historical median price-to-earnings multiple, or from a measured move on the chart. A target that cannot be built that way does not count. The strategy has two books: Core names must offer at least 3.0 times the risk, and Tactical names at least 3.5 times. That test of reward against risk is the asymmetry floor, and the ratio is the reward-to-risk ratio.

To read the report, start with the table in the next chapter, which gives the outcome for each name. Then read the chapter on each name, which states the rule that fired and the level that would reopen the case. Each dossier holds the full arithmetic, the sources and the peer comparison. The review chapter says which checks each dossier passed and where an exception remains.

Glossary

  • ATR (average true range). The normal daily price movement over 14 sessions. Stops and distances are measured in ATRs.
  • DMA. Daily moving average, for example the 50-day average.
  • Stage 2 and Stage 3. The advancing and topping phases of a price trend in the Weinstein method.
  • Momentum gate. A code test of whether the chart supports a new buy; NO_MOMENTUM means no allocation.
  • Reward-to-risk ratio. The upside to the target divided by the downside to the stop. The Core floor is 3.0 and the Tactical floor is 3.5.
  • Layer 1. The hard floors (financial health, technical posture and reward-to-risk) that a name must pass before any later test.
  • Anchored target. A price objective built from verified forward earnings and a historical multiple, or from a measured move on the chart.
  • CFO to PAT. Cash from operations divided by profit after tax, summed over three years. A figure of 0.70 or more shows that profits turn into cash.

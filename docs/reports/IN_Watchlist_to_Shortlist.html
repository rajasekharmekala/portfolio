Before you read

This report sums up the twenty-first batch of the watchlist to shortlist process for India: four companies, each with a full research dossier. Beta Drugs (cancer medicines) passes every safety rule and the operating test, but its price is well above support, so it joins the shortlist as a challenger with no order today. Raghav Productivity Enhancers (RPEL) is a strong business priced for a lot of growth, so it stays on the watchlist as a deferred name. Hi-Tech Pipes and Knowledge Marine are rejected: Hi-Tech Pipes fails three hard rules and Knowledge Marine offers too little reward for the risk at its price. Nothing here is a buy today, and the calls are wrong if the September-quarter results change the facts behind them.

Three rules decided these outcomes. The first is a set of hard red lines. They cover debt, share pledge, cash conversion and a chart test called Stage 4. In Stage 4 the price has fallen for a long time. The close sits below a falling 50-day average, and that average sits below a falling 200-day average. Only Hi-Tech Pipes meets that test, and it fails two more red lines as well.

The second rule is the operating test. It is scored out of 5.0. A name needs at least 3.0 and may have one failing row. Beta Drugs, RPEL and Knowledge Marine each score 3.5. Hi-Tech Pipes scores 2.5. The third rule is the reward-to-risk floor. RPEL meets it only at the top of its buy zone. Knowledge Marine does not meet it at all.

The four decisions, one at a time

Each paragraph gives the rule behind the verdict, the number that triggered it and what would change it. The full working is in the linked dossier.

Beta Drugs: shortlisted tactical challenger, no order

Beta Drugs makes cancer medicines and the active ingredients behind them, selling its own brands in India and exporting to more than 46 countries. No hard red line fails: debt to equity was 0.60 at March 2026 and 0.11 at September 2026 after debentures turned into shares, the promoter pledge is nil, and the operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0 with no failing row. Return on capital employed is 19.3 percent. Cash conversion is the soft spot: 0.87 as reported over three years, but 0.53 on a stricter reading of the statements. The reward-to-risk ratio clears the 2.5 tactical floor at the close and at the top of the zone. The price still sits 2.05 average-true-range units above support, which is inside the band where the rules say do not buy, and the momentum test says there is no momentum. So the verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT with a half-size limit order (an order that fills only at a chosen price or better) in ₹1,714 to ₹1,833 that goes live only after the momentum test passes on a complete bar. The reverse DCF in the data file (9.4 percent a year) rests on a profit figure that the filings do not support; our own solve gives about 15.5 percent a year.

RPEL: qualified but deferred, not shortlisted

Raghav Productivity Enhancers makes silica ramming mass, the lining material of induction furnaces. Every hard red line passes: debt to equity is 0.02, there is no pledge and three-year cash over profit is 0.82. Return on capital employed is 28.1 percent and the operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0, with one failing row, cash conversion, because first-half FY27 operating cash was only 0.45 of profit and inventory runs 272 days. The price is in a Stage 3 pullback above a rising 200-day average, and the shares trade near 106 times trailing profit. Reward-to-risk is 1.36 to 1 at the close and clears the 2.0 core floor (2.09) only at ₹1,585 or lower. The verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT with no order: any future buy needs the second-half or full-year FY27 cash flow to show operating cash of at least 0.70 of profit, falling inventory days and a probability-weighted value at least 12 percent a year above the entry price. The red team asked for a stricter verdict; the rules do not support rejection, so the stricter conditions were added instead.

Knowledge Marine and Engineering Works: rejected

Knowledge Marine dredges for ports and builds marine craft, and the chart is the strongest of the four (Stage 2, the rising phase of a trend, and the momentum test passes). It is a price rejection, not a quality one. Every red line passes and the operating test scores 3.5. But the June 2026 quarter was inflated by about ₹60 crore of revenue that slipped from March, the dredging backlog fell to ₹240 crore, and our FY28 profit per share is flat at ₹54. At 60 times that profit the central value is ₹3,240, against a close of ₹3,149.70 and a stop of ₹2,724, so reward-to-risk is 0.21 to 1 (1.13 to 1 at the zone top) against the 2.5 floor. All prices are before a 1-for-5 share split with record date 6 November 2026. The idea reopens only if the floor is met on a complete, split-adjusted bar, for example with FY28 profit per share near ₹60 or more.

Hi-Tech Pipes: rejected

Hi-Tech Pipes makes steel tubes and hollow sections and is adding a Sanand plant. It is rejected on three hard red lines, any one of which is enough. The close of ₹63.68 sits below a falling 50-day average of ₹74.90, which sits below a falling 200-day average of ₹82.81, and the recovery test fails. The promoter pledge rose from 2.54 percent in March 2025 to 14.70 percent at September 2026 while the stock fell 47 percent from its high. Three-year cash over profit is minus 0.11. The operating test scores 2.5 of 5.0. Sales grew 79 percent in the June 2026 quarter, but profit fell and about a fifth of revenue is low-margin trading. The 9.23 to 1 ratio comes only from a stop 3.6 percent below the close and is not relied on. The case reopens only when the chart recovers on a complete bar and the pledge stops rising for two filings in a row.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run published with gate exceptions. That means some checks could not be fully met with the evidence available. Each exception below names what failed, why it cannot be cured now, how it lowers confidence and what would cure it. The status of every dossier is PUBLISHED_WITH_GATE_EXCEPTIONS, and the RPEL dossier is also BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT.

Data date. The run took place on Monday 26 October 2026 (Pacific time). The Nifty 50 final bar for 26 October is verified and the ledger was built for that session. The price source returned no complete 26 October bar for any of the four names, so every price, level and technical reading uses the 23 October bar. The key-figures strip in each dossier was relabelled "Prior-session close (23 October 2026 bar)". The cure is the next batch, which will date to a later session.

Stale periodic filings. The citation check was run with the option that allows old as-of dates, because the latest periodic filing for most names is the June 2026 quarter or the FY26 annual report and the September-quarter results are not yet filed for all of them. The checker listed 63 stale records for Hi-Tech Pipes, 39 for RPEL, 50 for Beta Drugs and 79 for Knowledge Marine. Each dossier groups them with ages and cures, and no as-of date was edited. The cure is the September 2026 results.

Source access. Several sources were blocked, scanned or unreadable: image-only rating and press-release PDFs for Hi-Tech Pipes, exchange PDFs and call transcripts for RPEL, a scanned results file and a secondary call summary for Beta Drugs, and the company website for Knowledge Marine. Where that happened the dossier shows N/A or uses a named aggregator. Consensus estimates are not published for RPEL, Beta Drugs and Knowledge Marine, and Hi-Tech Pipes has only two analysts. The three-year median price to earnings is N/A for Knowledge Marine.

Ledger fields. For these four names the ledger reverse DCF used inputs that the filings contradict (Beta Drugs, RPEL), so each dossier re-solves it and shows the working next to the ledger figure. The official ledger file is unchanged.

Template and tooling. The shared dossier builder prints a few fixed labels (the momentum-gate rule wording and the price label) that do not match the scoring file. Each dossier was rendered through a scratch copy of the builder in the run directory that corrects those labels, and the shared glossary pass skips terms placed directly inside a paragraph or table cell, which the writers worked around by wrapping first uses by hand. No repository script was modified. The cure is to update the builder.

Reviewer gate. The reviewer and the red team ran as separate agents with the configured models. Hi-Tech Pipes, Knowledge Marine and Beta Drugs were approved by the reviewer in round 2 after round 1 asked for changes, and their red teams held the verdict. For RPEL the round 2 reviewer and red team each asked for one more change (a cash-conversion wording fix and a stricter first-order trigger), which were applied afterwards without a third review, so RPEL closes as BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT. This summary report was not reviewed separately.

Judgment calls. RPEL clears its reward-to-risk floor by 0.09 and only at the zone top, and it fails if the multiple the market pays falls below about 86 times. Beta Drugs clears the tactical floor with thin margin: at the zone top it fails below about 35 times earnings. Knowledge Marine's verdict depends on a flat FY28 profit per share. Email: not sent.

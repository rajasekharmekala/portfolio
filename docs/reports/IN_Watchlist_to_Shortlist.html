This report summarises one batch of the watchlist underwriting process. The watchlist is the queue of companies waiting for a full check. Each company is tested against three layers: hard gates that must all pass (Layer 1), a scorecard of operating measures (Layer 2), and a check on whether the price is near a sensible entry point (Layer 3). A company that fails a Layer 1 gate stops there, so Layers 2 and 3 are not scored. The shortlist is the small set of companies the framework is prepared to buy when the price is right, and it has room for ten Core Alpha names and ten Tactical Momentum names. The batch shows what happened to four names this session, and why. Each company has its own dossier, linked from the list after the verdict table, which gives the full evidence. This summary explains the batch as a whole, so you can see the pattern before you read the individual cases.

What the momentum test measures

The momentum test is a code rule, not a judgment. It asks two questions. Test A asks whether the stock is already in a rising trend: the price should sit above a rising 50-day average, which itself sits above the 200-day average, the stock should be outperforming the Nifty 50 over three months, and it should not be stretched more than 1.2 average daily ranges above its support. Test B asks whether a stock is entering a rising trend from a base: the 50-day average should be holding, relative strength should be turning up, the price should be close to the top of its base, and volume should be expanding. A stock that passes either test may be allocated. A stock that fails both is not. The thresholds are marked as needing calibration in the scoring file, and the code result is recorded as binding. The calibration flag is disclosed in each dossier, so a reader can see that the thresholds are a working choice rather than a settled figure.

For this batch, the failures were measured, not missing data, for three of the four names. CCL sits about 2.7 average daily ranges above its base and its 50-day average is falling. VOLTAMP sits about 4.2 average daily ranges above its base with volume at 0.7 of its usual level. NEULANDLAB is about 3.1 average daily ranges above its base, with relative strength turning down. INDOMIM is 7.8 average daily ranges above its base, but its 200-day history is too short to measure the long trend, so part of its result rests on missing history. The dossier for INDOMIM says which of its tests are measured failures and which are gaps.

The shortlist alert decision

Email is sent only when a company newly qualifies for the shortlist, or when a shortlisted candidate newly passes the displacement gates against a current holding. This batch has neither. No company became newly eligible, and no shortlisted candidate replaced a holding, because all four names fail Layer 1. The sender was therefore not called with any qualifying event, and no alert email is due. The published dossiers and this summary are the full record of the batch.

What would change this view:

  • If CCL or NEULANDLAB closes back above its 50-day average with that average turning up, and the momentum test passes on a later session, the technical gate would be reopened. Both would still need an anchored target before they could qualify.
  • If VOLTAMP's three-year cash conversion reaches 0.70 on the next audited statements, its cash gate would pass. Its asymmetry gate would still fail on the anchored target unless the median multiple rises above about 35 times.
  • If INDOMIM's 200-day history becomes available around May 2027 and the price returns to within 1.2 average daily ranges of its base, the technical gate could be re-run on full data.

Gate exceptions and what they limit

A gate exception is a check that the evidence could not fully satisfy, which is disclosed rather than hidden. Every exception here lowers confidence in a number, but none of them changes the four verdicts, because each verdict rests on at least one gate that passed or failed on evidence the record holds. The main exceptions are these. The three-year median price-to-earnings needed to anchor a target could not be retrieved for CCL and NEULANDLAB, so their asymmetry cannot be scored. Glossary

A Stage 3 name is one whose price has topped out and is pulling back. A death cross is when the 50-day average falls below the 200-day average. The average true range, or ATR, is the normal daily price swing, and every distance in this summary is measured in those swings. Asymmetry is the reward on offer divided by the risk taken, measured from the stop.

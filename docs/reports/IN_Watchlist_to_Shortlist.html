Before you read

This report sums up one batch of four India names, each with a full research dossier. HBL Engineering (HBLENGINE) is a debt-free rail-safety and battery maker that passes every safety test, but it is not a buy today: it gets a limit order plan, which is an order to buy only at or below a set price, and no order may stand across its 7 November results. J Kumar Infraprojects (JKIL) is rejected because its chart is in Stage 4, a long fall below the 50-day and 200-day averages, with the recovery test failing, and because 22.67 percent of the promoters' shares are pledged. Chaman Lal Setia Exports (CLSEL) is rejected because it turned too little of its profit into cash over three years. Aditya Birla Sun Life AMC (ABSLAMC) is rejected because its costs are growing four times faster than its revenue. Each dossier states the price or event that would change its verdict, and nothing here is a buy today.

What the batch found

Each row gives the verdict, the latest ledger close, the entry zone (the price range where we would place a buy order), the stop, our central target and the reward-to-risk ratio. That ratio is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop. A stop is the price at which the idea is wrong. The scoring file asks for at least 2.0 for a core name and 2.5 for a tactical or turnaround name. Read the ratio at the close together with the ratio at the top of the entry zone. The first is what a buyer gets today. The second is what a limit order would get after a pullback.

The four decisions, one at a time

Each paragraph below says what the dossier concluded, why, and the level or event that would change it. The dossiers carry the full working, including five-year tables, estimate sections and a numbered source list.

HBLENGINE: a clean, cash-rich rail-safety maker priced for peak margins

HBL passes every red line (debt-to-equity 0.03, interest cover 77 times, no pledge, three-year cash conversion 0.92). Its operating score is exactly 3.0 of 5.0 with one failing row: the Kavach rail-safety system is about half of sales against a 40 percent ceiling, and management says Kavach profit may fall from FY29. Last year's 34 percent operating margin is far above the 19 to 20 percent of the two years before. The price is 4.56 ATR (average true range) (average daily trading range) above its support, inside the no-buy band, and our central target of ₹764 is below the close. The verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT. A half-size limit order at ₹696 to ₹706 may be placed only after the 7 November results, and only if the ratio at the fill is still at least 2.5 to 1. A close at or below ₹683.20 ends the idea. A second failing operating row (for example operating margin below 20 percent in both the September and December quarters) would turn the verdict to REJECTED.

JKIL: a cheap contractor with a pledge and a falling chart

JKIL has the numbers of a bargain: return on capital 18.4 percent, debt-to-equity 0.18, net cash, an order book of ₹22,246 crore (3.87 times a year of sales) and a price of about 8 times earnings. Revenue is flat, though (up 0.5 percent in FY26 and 1.8 percent in Q1 FY27, against a 15 percent management target for FY27), the auditor is changing, and 22.67 percent of the promoters' shares have been pledged for twelve quarters. The chart has fallen 39 percent from its high. The dossier rejects the name and records the reopen tests: a close above the 20-day average (₹441.14 on 27 October) with the 14-day relative strength index (RSI) at or above 32 and a positive two-week return, and either a pledge below 20 percent of the promoters' holding or a sharp profit recovery in the September and December quarters.

CLSEL: a basmati exporter whose cash has not followed its profit

CLSEL is solvent (debt-to-equity 0.10, no pledge, net cash of about ₹46 crore) and cheap at 10.3 times earnings, but it turned only 26 percent of its three-year profit into operating cash against a 0.40 floor, five years of sales and profit are flat, and its margin is at a price-cycle high. No archetype qualifies. The central target of ₹236 is below the close, so the reward-to-risk ratio is negative. The reopen path needs three things together at the May 2027 results: operating cash of about ₹15 crore, a refreshed target that gives at least 2.5 to 1 (₹284.93 on October levels), and a chart that passes a momentum test. Cash alone does not reopen the file.

ABSLAMC: a high-return asset manager whose costs outgrow its revenue

ABSLAMC passes every red line (return on equity 25.5 percent, no pledge, three-year cash conversion 0.82), but in Q2 FY27 revenue rose 4.9 percent while expenses rose 20.5 percent and profit fell 8.4 percent. Its operating score is 2.5 of 5.0 with one failing row, below the 3.0 hurdle, and the reward-to-risk ratio at the close is 1.73 against the 2.0 core floor. Our probability-weighted value of ₹918 is only 4.5 percent above the close, so the 12 percent return hurdle is met at about ₹842 or lower. Cost stabilisation would reopen research only. An order would need a fresh chart check and a valuation that clears the hurdle on its own.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run published with gate exceptions. A gate that the evidence could not fully meet is listed here instead of being hidden.

First, the stale-date exception. Each citation check ended PASS WITH EXCEPTIONS because many research records are older than 60 days: annual reports, rating letters, earlier management calls used to audit what management promised, and the Q1 FY27 filings. Q2 FY27 results are not yet filed for HBLENGINE (7 November), JKIL (due by 14 November, date unconfirmed) or CLSEL (date not announced). ABSLAMC filed Q2 FY27 on 21 October. The counts of stale lines are 56 for JKIL, 55 for HBLENGINE, 96 for CLSEL and 39 for ABSLAMC. Every as-of date is true, and each dossier lists its own stale lines. The cure is the Q2 FY27 filings in November 2026.

Second, the price bars. The frozen ledger has no 28 October bar for JKIL, CLSEL and ABSLAMC, so their dossiers use the 27 October close and say so on the first screen. Yahoo's fundamentals feed returned an authorisation error, so the ledger's Reverse DCF and consensus fields are empty for all four names. Each writer solved the Reverse DCF from fetched filings (the working is in each dossier's Chapter 5) and recorded the inputs. Published consensus estimates were not available for HBLENGINE, CLSEL or ABSLAMC, and are shown as not available. JKIL's consensus comes from an aggregator.

Third, the reviewer cycle. HBLENGINE and JKIL returned REVISE from the reviewer in both rounds, with their red teams approving in round 1. Their round-2 findings were fixed after the cap without a third review, so both are marked BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT and neither is described as reviewer APPROVE. CLSEL and ABSLAMC returned REVISE from the reviewer and the red team in round 1 and APPROVE from both in round 2; two minor notes on ABSLAMC were fixed afterwards. The red team ran on its configured model.

Fourth, tooling. The shared structural validator has no dossier mode, so the packet's code checks, the readability check, the citation check and the layout check were used instead. The first-use glossary tagging in the page builder skips text that sits directly inside a paragraph, so the writers applied the same rules in a post-render pass without editing the repository's scripts, and corrected the first-screen price label for the three stocks whose last bar is 27 October. Four writers worked in parallel on one shared fetch cache. Some exchange pages were blocked or scanned images, so affected figures come from locally extracted text or aggregator pages and are labelled in the dossiers.

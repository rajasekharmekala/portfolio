Before you read

This report sums up the seventeenth batch of the watchlist to shortlist process for India: four companies you do not own, each with a full research dossier. Supriya Lifescience is the only name that stays alive: it is a debt-free maker of active pharmaceutical ingredients whose price is in a pullback above its 200-day average, which is Stage 3, a topping or resting phase, and not the falling Stage 4, so it joins the shortlist as a challenger with no order today and four conditions to meet first. Aarti Pharmalabs and Eco Recycling are rejected because neither fits a tested company profile, and Narayana Hrudayalaya is rejected because its borrowings, counting leases, are 1.24 to 1.29 times its equity against a limit of 1.20. Nothing here is a buy. These calls are wrong if the September-quarter results, due between 28 October and about 14 November, change the facts behind them.

Aarti Pharmalabs: rejected

Aarti Pharmalabs makes specialty active pharmaceutical ingredients, mainly xanthine derivatives, and takes contract development and manufacturing work. No hard red line is breached: debt to equity is 0.36, interest cover is about 6.0 times, three-year cash over profit is 1.22 and the Stage 4 selector is not met. The promoter pledge is 11.98 percent of the promoter holding, up from 0.01 percent in December 2025 and under the 20 percent line, so it is a watch item and not a failure. The business is rejected on archetype fit, which means it matches none of the three company profiles in the scoring file. Return on capital employed is 10.7 percent on Screener's basis against the 15 percent standard. Only one of four turnaround signals is positive. The price is 35.0 times trailing profit against a three-year median of 27.04, a 29 percent premium and not a discount. At the close the reward-to-risk ratio is 0.51 to 1. The operating score is 3.0 of 5.0, with a failing row on cash conversion. What would reopen the name is the 28 October results showing an EBITDA margin of at least 22 percent with borrowings no higher than ₹754 crore, a pledge at or below 12 percent in the December shareholding pattern and a close between ₹745 and ₹770 above the 200-day average. The reviewer approved the dossier in round 2.

Eco Recycling: rejected

Eco Recycling recycles electronic waste and sells the extended-producer-responsibility certificates that producers must buy. NSE trading began on 17 August 2026, so the ledger holds only 46 daily bars and cannot compute a 50-day or 200-day average, a trend stage or a three-month relative strength. No hard red line is proven to fail: debt to equity is 0.04, interest cover is 52.9 times and no shares are pledged. Three-year cash over profit is 0.42, which clears the 0.40 red line by 0.02 and so corrects an older seed note that called it a failure. That ratio does fail the 0.70 standard for a compounder. A turnaround profile needs at least two clear improving signals, and the dossier finds two weak ones against worse debtor days (63 to 119). A breakout profile needs an uptrend that 46 bars cannot show. The reward-to-risk ratio is 1.73 to 1 at the close and reaches the 2.5 floor only for a fill at ₹371 or lower. The operating score is 3.0 of 5.0, with one pass and four watch rows. A revenue miss to ₹62 crore would cut the central value to ₹390, which the dossier shows after the reviewers pressed on the sensitivity. What would reopen the name is the half-year cash flow statement due by about 14 November showing collections improving, plus a close above the 20-day average of ₹408.70. The reviewer approved the dossier in round 2.

Narayana Hrudayalaya: rejected

Narayana Hrudayalaya runs a network of low-cost multi-speciality hospitals in India and a UK business. It is rejected on the debt-to-equity red line, which fails at 1.20 times or more. The audited FY26 annual report prints 1.29 times, borrowings plus lease liabilities over equity, and the June 2026 quarter shows 1.24 times. The dossier shows five readings of debt. The gross reading decides because the scoring file's only carve-out is for trade letters of credit, ₹10,007 million of deposits is pledged against borrowings, and the company's own ratio is gross. The company's net-debt figure of 0.42 times would pass. The ledger labels the stock Stage 4, but the scoring file's wording needs a falling 200-day average, and it is flat at ₹1,843.15 against ₹1,842.10 63 sessions ago, so that red line is not met. The operating score is 3.0 of 5.0, with a failing row on group margin. The central value is ₹1,910, 16 percent above the close, and a fall of the multiple from 41.3 to the three-year low of 30.1 alone would cut it to about ₹1,392, a loss of 15.5 percent. The call is close: the board meets on 30 October to approve the first balance sheet since June, and our estimate of the ratio is 1.18 to 1.25 times. A filed ratio below 1.20 with interest cover above 3.0 would reopen the name. The reviewer approved the dossier in round 2.

Supriya Lifescience: shortlisted challenger, no order

Supriya makes niche active pharmaceutical ingredients, mainly anaesthetics, antihistamines and vitamins, and exports 81 percent of sales. No hard red line is breached: it has no borrowings, interest cover is 146 times, three-year cash over profit is 0.90 and no shares are pledged. Return on capital employed is 25.2 percent on Screener's basis, 22.4 percent on our own calculation. The price is in a Stage 3 pullback above a rising 200-day average, which the scoring file routes to WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT and not to rejection. The June 2026 quarter showed an EBITDA margin of 25.0 percent against 35.6 percent a year earlier, and a narcotics-export case is before the Supreme Court, which granted an interim stay on 19 October. The operating score is 3.0 of 5.0 with no failing row: one pass and four watch rows, after the reviewer pushed revenue growth from pass to watch. At the ₹818.55 close the reward-to-risk ratio is 1.30 to 1, so no order is placed. Inside the limit zone of ₹742 to ₹790 it is 2.06 to 1 at the top, on a central value of ₹967 (FY28E profit per share ₹31.46 times the three-year median multiple of 30.75). That clearance is thin: at a 26.9 times multiple the ratio at ₹790 is 0.66 to 1, and with a persistent 30 percent margin it is 0.93 to 1. An order is allowed only after the September results show an EBITDA margin of at least 30 percent, a refreshed full forecast is written, current audit and legal evidence is in hand, and the reward-to-risk rerun at the entry price still gives 2.0 or better. A margin below 28 percent with working capital unchanged, or a close below ₹704.18, rejects the name. The reviewer approved the dossier in round 2. The red team asked in round 2 for the sensitivity equations to be shown, and they were added afterwards.

What this means for you is no purchase today. The next filings that could change these views are the September-quarter results: Aarti Pharmalabs on 28 October, Narayana Hrudayalaya on 30 October, Eco Recycling by about 14 November and Supriya in mid-November (date not announced).

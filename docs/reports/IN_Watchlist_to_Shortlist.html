Before you read

Start here

This page is the batch summary for one run of the watchlist-to-shortlist process. The process takes companies that are waiting for research, underwrites up to four of them, writes one dossier for each, and moves each company to the shortlist, the archive, or a waiting state. Each dossier is the long document for one company. This page is the short map that ties the four together. It is written for a reader who has never used a moving average, so the key ideas are explained before the verdicts.

The shortlist is a small book of companies that have passed the full set of checks and are worth buying when the price is right. It has a fixed number of slots, so a new company can only enter by beating an existing one. The archive holds companies that failed a hard check. A company in the archive is not deleted; it is kept with the reason for the failure and a note of what would have to change before it is looked at again.

The underwriting has three layers. Layer 1 is a set of hard floors. A company must pass every one of them, and one failure ends the review. The floors are return on the capital the business uses (ROCE, return on capital employed: the profit earned on the money the business uses, at least 15 percent), debt against equity (below 0.70), cash conversion (operating cash flow across three years must be at least 0.70 of reported profit), a technical posture test, and an asymmetry test. Asymmetry compares the gain to a target with the loss to a stop-loss. A ratio of at least 3.0 is needed for a core holding and 3.5 for a tactical one, and the target must be anchored to a source that can be checked, such as forward earnings multiplied by a long-run price-to-earnings ratio, or a technical measured move. An unverified target counts as a failure. Layer 2 is a scorecard of three to five measures specific to the business, and it runs only if Layer 1 passes. Layer 3 is the entry timing test, which runs only if Layers 1 and 2 pass.

The technical posture uses Weinstein stages, which are four seasons of a stock. Stage 1 is a base where the price moves sideways after a fall. Stage 2 is the markup, when the price rises above rising averages. Stage 3 is the top, when the rise stalls. Stage 4 is the markdown, when the price falls below falling averages. A moving average is the average closing price over a number of sessions, like a smoothed trend line. The 50-day and 200-day averages are the two most used. RSI (relative strength index) is a 14-day speed gauge: readings below 30 mean the stock has fallen unusually fast, and above 70 mean it has risen unusually fast. ATR (average true range) is the normal daily move, used as a ruler for distances. A distance of 2 ATR means the price is about two normal days of movement from the reference level.

The momentum gate is a code test that runs on the same price data for every candidate in every market. It has two alternative tests, A (already in momentum) and B (entering momentum). A company that fails both has no allocation, however good the business. In this run all four companies returned NO_MOMENTUM. That result is an input the dossiers cite. It is not recalculated by hand. Its thresholds are marked as still needing calibration against live data, and the run notes say so.

How to read the rest of this page in about ten minutes: read the lede, the batch table, and the four case notes. For the full reasoning, open each dossier. The run notes at the end list every gate that was not fully met, and why.

Terms used on this page

Core and Tactical are the two strategy books. Core holds companies with high returns, durable advantages, and a long horizon of one to three years or more. Tactical holds companies in a rising price trend, with a horizon of three weeks to six months. Each has its own asymmetry floor (3.0 for Core, 3.5 for Tactical). Action verdicts are the labels the process gives: IMMEDIATE_BUY, ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP, WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT, and REJECTED. A REJECTED company has failed a hard floor and gets no entry instruction. Publication status records how each gate was met: a company can be APPROVE at the reviewer gate and still carry PUBLISHED_WITH_GATE_EXCEPTIONS for figures whose source is more than 60 days old.

How the verdicts were reached

Every name follows the same order. The ledger is built once for the session. It supplies the close, the averages, the stage, RSI, ATR, the support distance and the momentum gate. Each company is then underwritten by its own agent, which reads the company filings and the cached financial pages, records each figure with its source and as-of date, and writes its dossier. Figures that could not be verified are written as N/A with the reason, never estimated. The dossier then goes through the reviewer gate. A code packet traces every number to the ledger or to the research record, and then one reviewer reads the packet and judges whatever the code could not settle. A separate red team argues the strongest bear case against the verdict. The process allows two review rounds in total.

The rule that decided each verdict is the first Layer 1 floor that failed. Where several failed, the table lists all of them. The verdicts would change only if the named floor passed on verified evidence.

A row with REJECTED and APPROVE means the review found the rule applied correctly, not that the company is a buy.

The market backdrop

A single stock is judged on its own chart, but the market around it decides how much patience a new entry needs. The ledger for 12 October 2026 labels the regime Downtrend: 20.0 percent of the 496 screened companies are above their 50-day average, 37.9 percent are above their 200-day average, and no sector is above its 50-day average. Ten of the last 25 sessions were distribution days, meaning the index fell on higher volume than the day before, which is a sign of institutional selling. The regime posture in the standard is maximum cash, no new momentum longs, and trimming of leveraged and Stage 4 holdings. None of the four names was bought, so the posture is consistent with the verdicts.

The regime label is copied from the ledger without change, so every dossier and this page read the same market. A breadth reading of 20 percent means only one stock in five is above its own 50-day average. It is a weak number for a buyer. The distribution-day count is the number of sessions in the last 25 where the index fell on heavier volume than the day before, which is the footprint of institutions selling.

The backdrop does not decide any individual verdict, because each name fails on its own rule. It does mean that the names which might pass a floor later will face a weak tape. A repaired technical posture in a Downtrend has less follow-through than the same repair in an Uptrend, so the chart triggers in each dossier are set at the level where the individual stock, not the index, has changed character.

SANSERA: Sansera Engineering Ltd

Sansera makes precision forged and machined components for car, hybrid and aerospace and defence customers. It is on this page because it was on the watchlist as a deep-research peer. On the ledger it is a Stage 2 stock. The close of Rs 4,228.2 is above its 50-day average of Rs 4,020.81, which is rising, and above its 200-day average of Rs 2,826.29. Its RSI is 51.59, which is in a normal range. The price is, however, 5.88 ATR above the 63-day base low of Rs 3,100.9, against a maximum of 1.2 ATR for an entry, so the momentum gate returns NO_MOMENTUM. The technical floor passes; the momentum gate blocks an allocation.

The verdict is REJECTED on the asymmetry floor. No target could be anchored to a method the rule accepts. Forward earnings and a three-year median price-to-earnings ratio were not published in the sources read, and the only target found, an analyst average of Rs 4,106, is not an accepted anchor. The return on capital is 14.5 percent on the headline figure, but sources give 14.1 to 16.7 percent, so the ROCE gate is shown as unresolved and the verdict does not rest on it. The debt-to-equity ratio is 0.19 on the derived figure and cash conversion is 1.55 over three years, so both pass on the figures used. The prior record from 28 September had this name as QUALIFIED_DEFERRED with a target of Rs 5,250. That target has no verified anchor in this run, so it no longer counts. Even if it had been verified, Rs 5,250 would give a ratio of 0.64 on the stop, so the earlier qualification would not have survived the asymmetry floor either. The change is a correction, not a deterioration in the chart.

What would change the verdict: a verified target of about Rs 9,009, which is the level needed for a ratio of 3.0 on the derived stop of Rs 2,634.5. That target is 113 percent above the close and about 90 percent above the 52-week high of Rs 4,743.5. The prior record's Rs 5,250 would give a ratio of only 0.64 even if it were verified, so the stop width is the real obstacle, not the missing source alone. The stop follows the Core rule: the lower of the swing low and the 50- and 200-day averages, less one ATR (here the 200-day average of Rs 2,826.29 less Rs 191.8). A passing momentum gate would also be needed. The momentum threshold is still being calibrated, so the verdict rests on the asymmetry floor by itself. A close below the 50-day average of Rs 4,020.81 would break the price test of the momentum gate and would move the name further from entry.

The close of Rs 1,266.0 is below both the 50-day average of Rs 1,340.35 and the 200-day average of Rs 1,318.56. The verdict is REJECTED on two floors. The technical floor fails because a Core name needs a Stage 1 base breakout or a Stage 2 markup, and this stock has neither. What changed in the watchlist and the shortlist

The watchlist file holds the queue of names to research, and the shortlist file holds the companies that have passed. This run changed the record files in four ways. Each of the four names left the active queue. Each received one current archive record with its verdict, its failed floors and a note that points to its dossier. The two names that already had 28 September archive records (KERNEX and BANCOINDIA) had those older records replaced, so each company has one record. The shortlist file received a REJECTED row for each name in its master sheet, in the same form as earlier rejections, so that the decision is visible beside the holdings and the challengers. No shortlist challenger was added and no holding was displaced.

What would change these records:

  • If a name's verified target clears its asymmetry floor and its momentum gate passes, it returns to the queue for a fresh underwriting, not straight to the shortlist.
  • If any name passes all three layers on verified figures, it would be added to the shortlist and checked for a free slot in its book; a full book would require a displacement test.
  • If the archive and active records for the same company disagree again, the record with the later review date governs, and the active row is then removed.

The record change matters for future batches. The queue selects the next names by their status in the active list. Because the four names left that list, the next batch will start from the next name that still waits for research. The record also shows which company has a current verdict, so a reader does not have to read the older archive entry to find the decision.

Alerts and the email decision

An email is sent for this run only when a company newly qualifies for the shortlist, or when a shortlisted candidate newly passes the displacement test against a current holding. Routine batches do not send email. Here no company newly qualified and no displacement was possible, so there is no qualifying event and no alert is sent. The email step was run in preview mode, which applies the same policy without credentials. It printed "EMAIL SKIPPED: watchlist; no new shortlist qualification or eligible holding displacement." A skipped email is a successful outcome under the policy.

Worked example: a company is a candidate for a core holding at a close of Rs 1,000 with a stop at Rs 900. The risk is Rs 100 per share. A target of Rs 1,300 gives a reward of Rs 300, a ratio of 3.0, which just meets the Core floor. If the target is only an analyst average of Rs 1,250, the reward is Rs 250, a ratio of 2.5, which fails. Because no company in this batch reached a ratio of 3.0 on a verified target, there was nothing to alert on.

Data gaps and record housekeeping

Three record problems came up in this run and need a decision that is outside a single batch. First, the active watchlist and the archive overlap. Thirty-three companies still appear in the active queue marked as waiting for research, while the archive already holds a completed rejection for each of them. The eligibility rule selects by the active status, so KERNEX and BANCOINDIA were eligible in this run and were underwritten, and their older archive records were replaced. The other overlapping names were not touched. If they are left in the queue, a later batch will underwrite them again, and each will produce a second archive record unless the active rows are removed first. Second, RNITAI appears twice in the active queue. Third, the price ledger could not resolve three symbols (C2C, DUMMYHEG and WSTLIFESTY) from the market data source, so they are listed as failures in the ledger and no verdict is given for any of them. Those three are not in this batch.

A duplicate record means one company is in two places with two different statuses. The queue reads the active place and the archive reads the other. Removing the stale active rows is a decision about the user's own queue, and it is not a data-quality repair this batch should make on its own.

The recommended fix is to remove the stale active rows for companies that already have a completed archive record, keeping the archive record as the current one, and to fix the duplicate RNITAI row. That change is a single decision for the user and should be made before the next batch, so that the next four names are the next four genuinely waiting companies.

Glossary

ATR: average true range, the normal daily move, used as a ruler for distances. Asymmetry: the reward to target divided by the risk to the stop. CFO/PAT: cash from operations divided by profit after tax, summed over three years. Distribution day: a session where the index falls on higher volume than the day before. DMA: the moving average over a number of sessions. Momentum gate: a code test that checks whether a stock is already in, or is entering, a sustained price trend. ROCE: return on capital employed, the profit earned on the money the business uses. RSI: relative strength index, a 14-day speed gauge. Stage: the Weinstein stage of the price trend, 1 to 4.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run completed its research and publishes with gate exceptions. Each exception listed here is also recorded in the run manifest and in the dossier it affects.

  • Stale as-of figures. The latest periodic filings for all four companies are for the year ended 31 March 2026 or the quarter ended 30 June 2026, and in some cases a credit rating dated February or June 2026. The citation check passes these with the stale-date allowance, and each dossier lists the affected records with their ages and the event that will refresh them. No as-of date was edited to pass the check. The allowance was used only for records older than 60 days.
  • Reviewer round 2 not APPROVE for two dossiers. CDSL and BANCOINDIA received REVISE from the reviewer in round 2. Their remaining points were fixed best-effort after the two-round limit, were not reviewed again, and are recorded as BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT.
  • No dossier validator. The layout validator covers market reports and portfolio audits, not single-company dossiers. The gates used for the dossiers were the citation check and the readability check, and the reviewer packet ran the same two checks. This is disclosed in each dossier.
  • Unreadable primary documents. Several filing PDFs (the annual reports, rating rationales and some exchange intimations) could not be read as text, and some exchange pages refused access. Figures from those sources were taken from the aggregator pages that quote them and are marked as secondary. Where two sources disagreed, the dossier says so and does not choose silently.
  • Unreconciled quarter. For BANCOINDIA, the June 2026 quarter profit differs between a summary of the company's exchange notice (Rs 4,451 lakh) and the Screener table (Rs 122 crore). The gates use the fiscal-year figures rather than this quarter, and the dossier leaves the difference open.
  • Momentum thresholds need calibration. The momentum gate thresholds are marked as needs calibration in the scoring configuration. The results are used as recorded, with that caveat. For SANSERA the verdict rests on the asymmetry floor alone, so the calibration question does not change its verdict.
  • Post-review disclosure edit. After the final review, a single disclosure line stating the publication status was added beneath the key figures of each dossier. No figure, verdict or source was changed by that edit.
  • Watchlist record duplicates. The overlapping active and archive records described in the data gaps section were not corrected in this run.

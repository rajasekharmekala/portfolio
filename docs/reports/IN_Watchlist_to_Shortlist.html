1 How the verdicts were reached

The underwriting rulebook sorts each name through three layers. Layer 1 is a set of hard gates, each pass or fail, and one failure rejects the stock at once. Layer 2 is a scorecard of company-specific measures. Layer 3 checks whether the price is close enough to support to buy. Every name in this batch fails Layer 1, so Layers 2 and 3 were not run for any of them. That follows the rulebook and is not a missing step. Stage 2 is a sustained uptrend, Stage 4 is a markdown below falling averages, and Asymmetry compares what a stock could gain with what it could lose from the entry price. ROCE is return on capital employed, the profit earned on all the money the business uses. RSI is the relative strength index, a 14-day gauge of how strongly a price has risen or fallen.

The market backdrop is weak. The ledger labels the regime "Downtrend", and about 20 percent of the screened universe sits above its 50-day average. Individual stocks are judged on their own charts, but that backdrop is a reason for patience with any new entry.

Prices, moving averages, the Weinstein stage, RSI and the asymmetry arithmetic all come from one ledger built for the session of 12 October 2026. The company figures (return on capital, debt, cash conversion, growth and margins) come from filings and fetched pages, each recorded with its source. Where two sources disagreed, each dossier names both and says which one it uses.

Working: the asymmetry arithmetic

The number

Asymmetry is the target gain divided by the loss to the stop: (target minus close) divided by (close minus stop).

How it was calculated

For ELECON the indicative target is 412.8, the close is 408.85 and the stop is 393.5, so (412.8 minus 408.85) divided by (408.85 minus 393.5) is 3.95 divided by 15.35, which is 0.26. For SBCL the measured-move target is 1,720.9, the close is 1,100.7 and the stop is 621.9, so (1,720.9 minus 1,100.7) divided by (1,100.7 minus 621.9) is 620.2 divided by 478.8, which is 1.30.

The inputs

TATACONSUM and SUVENPHAR have no verified target, so the ratio is not computed for them and the asymmetry gate fails by rule.

The range

The ratio depends on three inputs: the target, the close and the stop. The close comes from the ledger. The stop is a rule-based level stated in each dossier. The target is the input that is least certain, because it is an estimate and not a ledger price.

If the number is off

If the number is off, the ratio moves with it. A verdict changes only when the ratio crosses its floor. For ELECON the target would need to be about 462.6 to reach 3.5, and for SBCL about 2,537 to reach 3.0. Neither level is supported by a verified source in the research record.

Batch summary validator gap. The full validator, validate_report.py, is written for market reports and portfolio audits. It asks for a book-length text, a case note for each holding, market-wide flow and option data, and a glossary of market terms. A watchlist batch is neither, so those floors were not applied to this summary, and the validator has no kind for it. The checks that apply were run: the citation check, the readability check, the layout check and the code-built review packet. The cure is a watchlist kind in that validator.

Cache key limitation. The fetch cache keys each page by its address without the query string, so two pages on one path can share an entry.

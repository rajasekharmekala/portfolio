Before you read

This report sums up one batch of four India names, each with a full research dossier. LTM, Marsons, Vishal Mega Mart and Premier Explosives all fail the underwriting, so all four leave the active watchlist for the archive and none joins the shortlist. Two of them, LTM and Vishal Mega Mart, are sound businesses that fail only because the chart is in a long downtrend. The other two, Marsons and Premier Explosives, are priced above our central value. Each dossier states the price or event that would reopen the case.

What the batch found

Each row gives the verdict, the close, the stop, our central target and the reward-to-risk ratio, which is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop. A stop is the price at which the idea is wrong. The scoring file asks for at least 2.0 for a core name. Read the ratio at the close together with the ratio at the top of the entry zone, because the first is what a buyer would get today and the second is what a limit order would get after a pullback.

Three rules decided these outcomes. The first is a set of hard red lines on debt, share pledge, cash conversion and a chart test called Stage 4. In Stage 4 the close sits below a falling 50-day average, and that average sits below a falling 200-day average. LTM and VMM are in Stage 4 and fail the recovery test, so the chart red line applies to them. The second rule is the operating test, scored out of 5.0, where a name needs at least 3.0 and may have at most one failing row. Only Premier Explosives fails it. The third rule is the reward-to-risk floor, which Marsons and Premier Explosives fail even at the best price in their zones, because the central value sits below the price.

The four decisions, one at a time

Each paragraph gives the rule behind the verdict, the number that drove it and what would change it. The full working is in the linked dossier.

LTM: rejected on the chart, not on the business

LTM is the L&T-group IT services company formed from LTIMindtree. It has almost no debt (debt to equity 0.09), cash conversion of 1.06 over three years, return on capital employed of 29.6 percent and no promoter pledge. Layer 2 scores 3.5 of 5.0. The close of ₹4,074.90 sits below a falling 50-day average of ₹4,313.53, which sits below a falling 200-day average of ₹4,508.48, and the recovery test fails on a single condition: the close is 0.84 percent under the 20-day average. The central value is ₹4,478.56 and the probability-weighted value is ₹4,410.81, so reward-to-risk is only 1.26 to 1 at the close and 2.01 to 1 at ₹3,995.55. The dossier reopens the case when a close above ₹4,109.46 comes with a positive two-week return (the recovery test passes), after which a limit order in ₹3,905.60 to ₹3,995.55 with a ₹3,755.68 stop can be tested again. Estimates are falling and the market already prices about 9.6 percent yearly cash-flow growth against dollar growth near 5 percent.

MARSONS: fast growth, no cash, priced above our value

Marsons makes power transformers and is the only maker of extra-high-voltage units in eastern India. Sales rose from ₹6.46 crore in FY24 to about ₹245 crore in FY26, but the June 2026 quarter grew only 4.7 percent and profit fell about 26 percent. Three-year cash conversion is −0.52 and customers owe about 160 days of sales. The company passes the hard red lines (almost no debt, interest cover 32 times, no pledge) only through the exemption for fast-growing capital-goods firms. At 45.9 times earnings against a peer median near 33, our central value of ₹86 (FY28 profit per share of ₹2.62 at 33 times) sits below the ₹117.40 price, and the probability-weighted value is ₹80.60. Reward-to-risk is negative everywhere in the zone. The dossier reopens the case only if two further quarters show sales growth above 20 percent, a margin of at least 16.5 percent and six-month operating cash flow of at least ₹10 crore, and then only as a fresh underwriting, not an automatic buy. A margin-only stress puts value near ₹65, about 45 percent below the price.

VMM: a sound retailer in a downtrend, with the upside already priced in

Vishal Mega Mart sells apparel, general goods and groceries at low price points through 844 shops. Return on capital employed is 15.2 percent, three-year cash conversion on net profit is 1.99 (about 1.1 to 1.2 after rent), and there is no external borrowing. Layer 2 scores 3.5 of 5.0, with the cash-conversion row a rounding-level miss at 0.845 against a 0.85 floor. The close of ₹88.70 is in Stage 4 with a relative strength index of 29.52, below the 32 floor of the recovery test. Analyst estimates are being cut and same-store growth of 5.4 percent in the September quarter is below DMart and V-Mart. The central value of ₹100.32 uses a 38 times multiple that is above today's 33.6 times on FY28 estimates, so the entire 13 percent upside is a re-rating. The dossier reopens the case when the recovery test passes and the December quarter shows same-store growth of at least 10 percent with estimates no longer being cut. The stop at ₹81.38 is a trigger that a gap can overshoot, not a cap on the loss.

PREMEXPLN: record orders, low-quality earnings, a price that assumes a lot

Premier Explosives makes propellants, countermeasures and explosives for defence and mining, with a record order book of ₹1,393 crore, 3.59 times FY26 sales. Only about ₹16 crore of FY26 profit is recurring against ₹45.8 crore reported, and management delivered 65 percent of its ₹600 crore sales target at a 10.0 percent operating margin against a 15 to 20 percent goal. Layer 2 scores 2.0 of 5.0 with two failing rows. The shares closed at ₹588.10, down 7.0 percent on the day, at 91 times reported earnings. Our central value is ₹390 (FY28 profit per share of ₹7.93 at 49 times, the multiple Apollo Micro Systems agreed to pay), so reward-to-risk is −10.44 to 1 at the close. Probability-weighted value is ₹473, about 20 percent below the price. The dossier sets a reopen test that actually changes the verdict: quarterly sales near ₹170 crore at a 16 percent margin or better, which would put FY28 profit per share near ₹13.2 and the target near ₹645.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run published with gate exceptions. A gate that the evidence could not fully meet is listed here instead of being hidden.

First, the price bar. The Nifty 50 bar for 27 October is verified, but the data provider had not posted a 27 October bar for LTM, MARSONS and VMM, so those three dossiers use the 26 October close. Their key-figures strip carries a fixed label that says 2026-10-27, and the status line beside it and the run notes in each dossier say that the price is the 26 October bar. We did not relabel the data.

Second, the stale-date exception. Each citation check ended PASS WITH EXCEPTIONS because many research records are older than 60 days: annual reports, rating letters, dated orders and earlier management statements used to audit what management promised, plus latest periodic filings with nothing newer. Every as-of date is true and each dossier lists its own stale lines. This goes beyond the narrow case of the latest filing, which the writers flagged as a residual finding.

Third, the reviewer cycle. Each dossier went through the strict maximum of two rounds with the configured reviewer. LTM and MARSONS were approved by the reviewer in round 2. VMM and PREMEXPLN each received a remaining REVISE in round 2 (VMM: a ratio rounding and a sentence exposing revision history, plus two notes; PREMEXPLN: one paragraph inconsistent with its own table and one note). Those were fixed afterwards without a third review, so both carry BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT. All four red teams ended with the verdict holding.

Fourth, the validator. The shared structural validator has no dossier mode, so the packet's code checks, the readability check and the citation check were used instead. Some data items are N/A with reasons in the dossiers: a three-year median multiple for LTM and VMM, promoter pledge for Premier Explosives, and a current order book for Marsons.

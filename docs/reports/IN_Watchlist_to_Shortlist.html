What the batch found

Each row gives the verdict, the close used, the entry zone (the price range where we would place a buy order), the stop, our central target and the reward-to-risk ratio. That ratio is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop. A stop is the price at which the idea is wrong. The scoring file asks for at least 2.0 for a core name and 2.5 for a tactical name. Read the ratio at the close together with the ratio at the top of the entry zone. The first is what a buyer gets today. The second is what a limit order would get after a pullback.

TVSMOTOR: a strong two-wheeler maker whose price already pays for the good news

TVS Motor sells two-wheelers, three-wheelers and electric scooters, and owns the lender TVS Credit, which is being split off. In the September 2026 quarter domestic petrol-engine volume grew 16 percent against 10 percent for the industry, exports grew 33 percent, and electric volume was 186,186 units, a 26.1 percent September share. Its operating score is 4.0 of 5.0, with three passes (volume growth, electric share, price and mix) and two watch rows (operating margin and cost pass-through, and the return on capital of the group against the factory). The debt test needs a judgment. Group debt-to-equity is 3.48, but most of it is TVS Credit's lending book, so the dossier applies the 1.20 red line to the manufacturing business: 1.06 on the rating agency's figure for the business without TVS Credit and 0.45 standalone. If a reader rejects that choice, the verdict becomes REJECTED. The price asks for about 19.3 percent annual growth in free cash flow for ten years, against our own path of 15.9 percent. Our central value of ₹4,550 is stated before the TVS Credit demerger (appointed date 20 July 2027). A simple cash-flow model gives about ₹3,460 a share, and at the brokers' 35 times multiple on profit per share of ₹111 the value is ₹3,885, so the whole entry zone then fails the 2.0 floor. That is why no order is active. An order in ₹3,780 to ₹3,900 needs a refreshed valuation that clears 2.0 at the order price, a held-support close, and levels restated on combined value if the demerger has started. Closes below ₹3,791.09 then ₹3,741.90 are warnings, and a close below ₹3,620.44, standalone debt-to-equity at 1.20, or an operating margin below 12.0 percent rejects the idea.

SAREGAMA: a debt-free music-rights owner that is too far from support

Saregama owns about 180,000 songs and is turning them into a new-music business. It has debt-to-equity of 0.04, no share pledge, a three-year cash conversion of 0.86 and a return on capital of 17.3 percent, which clears the 15 percent floor but not the 18 percent a core name should prefer. Its operating score is 3.5 of 5.0 with two passes (music revenue growth, return on new music) and three watch rows (music margin, cash conversion, and the drag from hardware and films). The share is in Stage 3 at ₹536, 3.2 ATR above its base low of ₹473, and pays 45.3 times profit. The price asks for 17.9 to 26.6 percent annual growth for ten years on the two cash-flow bases the dossier tests. Reward-to-risk is 0.46 to 1 at the close and 2.29 to 1 at ₹490. The plan is a limit order at ₹478 to ₹490 only after the 4 November results, with a stop at ₹453.29. The single tripwire is a twelve-month music net margin below 42 percent in any quarterly report.

DIFFNKG: a fast-growing welding and wear-plate maker whose profit is not yet cash

Diffusion Engineers made revenue of ₹406.63 crore in FY26 (up 21.28 percent) and its order book was ₹209.66 crore at 30 June 2026. Debt-to-equity is 0.07 and no red line fails. But three-year cash conversion is 0.58 against the 0.70 compounder floor, and the capital-goods relief (0.50) does not apply because three-year sales growth is 17 percent against 20 percent and return on capital is 15.8 percent against 18 percent. The operating score is 2.5 of 5.0 with one pass and two failing rows, below the 3.0 line. Reward-to-risk is 1.83 to 1 at the ₹405.85 close against the 2.0 core floor, and 2.79 to 1 at ₹396. The price asks for 26.6 percent annual growth on 75 percent of FY26 operating cash flow, or 10.9 percent on trailing profit. A good half-year cash flow only reopens the research. The review can qualify the name under the capital-goods relief on audited FY27 numbers (May 2027) or under the 0.70 floor on the FY26 to FY28 window (published May 2028). Reclaiming ₹429.88 would not by itself pass the chart test, because support would then be 2.54 ATR away against the 1.6 limit.

SWIGGY: a profitable food-delivery business paying for a loss-making quick-commerce one

Swiggy earns about ₹341 crore a quarter from food delivery and holds ₹13,662 crore of cash with no bank debt, so solvency is not at risk. But Instamart loses about ₹895 crore a quarter and needs about 2.3 times its size to break even while a profitable rival grows faster. The chart is in Stage 4 (close ₹185.25 below a falling 50-day average of ₹253.84 and 200-day average of ₹276.68) with the recovery test failing and a 14-day RSI of 26.07. Interest cover is negative on the loss before interest and tax (about -19.8 times, or -16.2 times on operating profit), so the rule-based line fails. Three-year cash conversion is 0.66 on paper, but both numbers are negative, so the dossier treats it as a fail on the rule's purpose. Its operating score is 3.5 of 5.0 with three passes, one watch and one failing row (growth against Eternal). Our base value is ₹201 and reward-to-risk is 0.97 to 1 at the close. The price needs about ₹2,935 crore of free cash flow in five years, roughly 42 percent of management's FY31 plan on our cash-conversion assumption. It would move to WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT only if it holds above the ₹174.20 swing low for 10 or more sessions with RSI above 32 and passes the recovery test, and any upgrade needs a full re-underwrite.

Third, the reviewer cycle and its model. The reviewer model named in the configuration (ao-claude-lc/claude-sonnet-5-5) became unavailable after the first reviewer pass, so the other reviewer passes ran on the runner's default agent, and the first-round red teams ran on ao-foundry-lc/gpt-6.1-sol. After the writers' fixes, round 2 returned APPROVE for DIFFNKG (its red team asked for stricter triggers in round 1 and said the verdict holds in round 2) and REVISE for SAREGAMA, SWIGGY and TVSMOTOR, whose remaining findings were fixed afterward without a third review. Round 2 left a few NOTES unfixed in DIFFNKG (unfilled "N/A" cells produced by the page builder, and a few wording suggestions).

Fourth, one judgment call to check. The TVSMOTOR dossier applies the 1.20 debt red line to the manufacturing perimeter because the scoring file has no separate rule for a financial subsidiary. If that perimeter is rejected, the verdict becomes REJECTED and the core-book slot reverts to CRISIL.

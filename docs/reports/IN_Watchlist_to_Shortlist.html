Before you read

This report sums up one batch of four India names, each with a full research dossier. Nestle India and Hindustan Unilever are first-class consumer businesses with no debt, but both charts are in a Stage 4 decline, a long fall below the 50-day and 200-day averages, and the price leaves too little expected return, so both are rejected for now. Anthem Biosciences is a sound business at a price that fails the reward test even if it falls to support, so it is also rejected. Sai Life Sciences passes the safety tests but costs too much today, so it waits: no limit order, which is an order to buy only at or below a set price, stands, and any buy needs a fresh check after the 5 November 2026 results. Each dossier states the price or event that would change its verdict, and nothing here is a buy today.

The four decisions, one at a time

Each paragraph gives the rule behind the verdict, the number that drove it and what would change it. The full working is in the linked dossier.

NESTLEIND: the best business in the batch, in a downtrend, at a price that still assumes fast growth

Nestle India sells MAGGI, KITKAT, NESCAFE and MILKMAID through 5.3 million outlets. It has almost no debt (debt to equity 0.08), nothing pledged, a return on capital employed of 85.3 percent and three-year cash conversion of 1.13. The operating test scores 4.0 of 5.0 with no failing row: gross margin and the profit left after reinvestment are the two watch rows. The chart is the problem. The ledger labels the share Stage 4 at ₹1,168.00, below a 50-day average that has fallen 6.2 percent in three weeks, with the recovery test failing on the short-term turn and on an RSI of 27.08. The 200-day average is still rising, so this is an early Stage 4, and the dossier says so, but the rule applies the ledger label. The price also asks a lot. The ledger's reverse DCF, which asks what growth today's price assumes, gives 21.6 percent a year for ten years. Our base value is ₹1,362 (consensus FY28 earnings of ₹24.76 at 55 times), with ₹1,612 as the bull case and ₹945 as the bear case. The weighted value of ₹1,266 is 8.4 percent above the close, against a 12 percent hurdle. Reopen it when the RSI is back above 32 with a close above the 20-day average and no close below ₹1,147.40, when Q3 FY27 volume growth is 8 percent or better with an EBITDA margin of 22.5 percent or better, or when the price is near ₹1,130 with a base above ₹1,111.31. A chart pass alone is not a buy: at an illustrative ₹1,275.73 confirmation price the weighted return is about -0.8 percent.

HINDUNILVR: a debt-free staples giant whose gross margin fell while the share price fell with it

Hindustan Unilever has no meaningful debt (debt to equity 0.034), nothing pledged, a return on capital employed of 28.4 percent and three-year cash conversion of 1.21 on the continuing basis, which excludes the demerged ice-cream business and a one-time ₹4,407 crore gain. Underlying volume grew 7 percent in the September quarter, and industry volumes fell 2 percent in April to June. The operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0, with one failing row: gross margin fell to 47.6 percent from 51.5 percent a year earlier, and advertising was cut 9.25 percent to protect profit. The chart is in a literal Stage 4: the close of ₹1,815.80 sits below a falling 50-day average (₹1,926.26), which sits below a falling 200-day average (₹2,148.28), and the recovery test fails on one condition only, the short-term turn. The price assumes 16 percent a year of growth for ten years, against 1.7 percent a year of continuing profit growth over three years. We agree with the street on FY27 and FY28 earnings (₹47.96 and ₹53.10) but hold a 37.9 times multiple, not the 44.7 times that the street target of ₹2,373 implies: base ₹2,010, bull ₹2,498, bear ₹1,488, weighted ₹1,951 (+7.4 percent). Reopen it on a close above the 20-day average of ₹1,853.02 with positive 28-day relative strength while ₹1,758.20 holds, or when Q3 FY27 gross margin is 49 percent or better with advertising at 9.5 percent of revenue or more. The multiple-only stress is worth noting: at 30 times consensus earnings the value is ₹1,593, 12.3 percent below the close.

SAILIFE: a contract drug maker that passes every safety rule but does not give a price at which to buy

Sai Life Sciences develops and manufactures drug ingredients for large pharmaceutical customers, with 33 commercial and 14 late-phase molecules. It passes every hard red line (debt to equity 0.12, nothing pledged, three-year cash conversion 1.80, Stage 3, recovery test passing) and scores 3.5 of 5.0 with no failing row, but three rows are on watch: the manufacturing side grew only 6 percent in the June quarter, the top ten customers give 54 percent of sales, and the EBITDA margin was 26.7 percent against a 28 to 30 percent band. The price of ₹1,487.20 assumes 26 percent a year of growth for ten years, against management's 15 to 20 percent guide, and street FY28 earnings of ₹24.95 need a margin of about 34 percent. Our FY28 earnings are ₹20.72, so the base value of ₹1,450 sits below the close, the reward-to-risk is -0.15 to 1, and the weighted value of ₹1,403 is 5.6 percent below it. The ₹1,290 to ₹1,305 zone on the 63-day base low clears 2.0 only at a 70 times multiple. At 60 times the target is ₹1,243 and the ratio is -0.96, and the red team judged a standing buy order one step too optimistic. The dossier therefore removed it: no order stands, and a purchase needs a fresh re-underwriting after the 5 and 6 November 2026 results and again on the fill day, with a ratio of 2.0 or better at 70 times, 1.0 or better at 60 times, and a passing chart test. A September-quarter margin of 31 percent or more with sales and manufacturing growth above 15 percent lifts FY28 earnings to about ₹22.87 and would pass both tests; a margin below 27 percent or negative tariff comments reject it. The ledger's default tier of 3.5 percent was cut to 2.0 percent.

ANTHEM: a sound business whose recovery is already priced in

Anthem Biosciences makes peptides, antibody-drug conjugates and other drug ingredients, with 14 commercial molecules that give 60.8 percent of revenue. It passes every hard red line (debt to equity 0.02, nothing pledged, three-year cash conversion 0.89, Stage 3) and scores 3.5 of 5.0 with no failing row, but three rows are on watch: the top three customers give 56.7 percent of sales, new capacity is only 30 to 50 percent used, and June-quarter revenue fell 22.6 percent on deferred deliveries. No archetype qualifies because the reward floor fails. The close of ₹832.15 is 55 times street FY28 earnings, and the street needs 34.4 percent revenue growth in the last three quarters of FY27. We model 15 percent growth and FY28 earnings of ₹13.76 against the street's ₹15.12, which at 60 times gives a base value of ₹826, with ₹978 as the bull case and ₹488 as the bear case. The weighted value of ₹779.50 is 6.3 percent below the close. Reward-to-risk is -0.08 to 1 at the close and 1.60 to 1 at the ₹783.35 zone top. The verdict is fragile: results on 3 November 2026 with revenue of ₹5,500 million or more and an operating margin before other income of 37 percent or more, or our FY28 earnings rising to ₹13.95 or more while the price is inside the zone, would make it WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run published with gate exceptions. A gate that the evidence could not fully meet is listed here instead of being hidden.

First, the stale-date exception. Each citation check ended PASS WITH EXCEPTIONS because many research records are older than 60 days: audited annual figures, rating letters, earlier management calls used to audit what management promised, and the June-quarter filings. The counts are 34 of 59 records for NESTLEIND, 63 of 135 for HINDUNILVR, 99 of 124 for SAILIFE and 54 for ANTHEM. Nestle and Hindustan Unilever reported their September quarter on or before the run and use it for every current figure. Sai Life reports on 5 November 2026 and Anthem on 3 November 2026, so their earnings figures are for the June quarter. Every as-of date is true, and each dossier lists every stale record with its age and the event that cures it. The cure is the Q2 and Q3 FY27 filings and the FY27 annual accounts.

Second, the reviewer cycle. For NESTLEIND, HINDUNILVR and ANTHEM the reviewer returned REVISE in round 1 and APPROVE in round 2 after the writers fixed every finding; the NESTLEIND red team asked for a correction of its confirmation-price wording in round 1 and approved the fix in round 2, and the HINDUNILVR and ANTHEM red teams held the verdict in round 1. SAILIFE did not reach approval: its reviewer returned REVISE in round 1 (seven findings) and again in round 2, where two findings remained (the hinge claim did not match the margin ladder, and one weighted price did not show its probability split). Both were corrected after round 2, together with two notes, and no third review was run, so SAILIFE is marked BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT. Its red team judged the verdict one step too optimistic in round 1; the dossier removed the standing order and cut the position size, and the red team approved that in round 2.

Third, tooling and data. The shared structural validator has no dossier mode, so the packet's code checks, the readability check, the citation check and the layout check were used instead. The ledger labels NESTLEIND Stage 4 even though its 200-day average is still rising, and the dossier applies the ledger label and states the nuance. The ledger's reverse DCF for HINDUNILVR, SAILIFE and ANTHEM uses profit as a stand-in for cash flow, so each dossier shows its own cross-check next to it, and HINDUNILVR uses continuing profit because of the demerger gain. Three-year median price-to-earnings ratios do not exist for SAILIFE and ANTHEM (listed in December 2024 and July 2025), so their target multiples are labelled as assumptions. The exchange shareholding pages could not be read by script for HINDUNILVR and SAILIFE, so the 0.0 percent pledge comes from Trendlyne, with the promoter holding checked against annual reports. Some filings and earnings-call transcripts were scanned images, which the fetch tool cannot read, and are not used. One writer worked with the harness search tool because DuckDuckGo search was blocked, and four writers worked in parallel on one shared fetch cache and scratch folder; one helper script was overwritten by another writer and rewritten, and no cache entry was edited by hand.

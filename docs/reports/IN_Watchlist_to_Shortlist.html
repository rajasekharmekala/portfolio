Before you read

This report sums up the nineteenth batch of the watchlist to shortlist process for India: four companies, each with a full research dossier. Timken India (bearings) and Vedant Fashions (the Manyavar wedding-wear brand) pass the safety rules and the operating test, but both prices are still falling, so each joins the shortlist as a challenger with no order today and a list of conditions to meet first. Bharat Electronics and Max Healthcare are rejected: both are in a long, confirmed price decline that the rules treat as a hard stop, and Bharat Electronics also converts too little of its profit into cash. Nothing here is a buy today. These calls are wrong if the September-quarter results, due from 29 October, change the facts behind them.

Three rules decided these outcomes. The first is a set of hard red lines: debt, share pledge, cash conversion and a chart test called Stage 4, which is a long fall in which the close sits below a falling 50-day average that itself sits below a falling 200-day average, with no turn forming. Bharat Electronics and Max Healthcare both meet that test on the written rule, with every slope measured. Timken India does not, because its 200-day average is still rising slightly, even though the price file labels it Stage 4. The second rule is the operating test. It is scored out of 5.0, needs at least 3.0 and allows one failing row. Timken India scores 4.0, Vedant Fashions and Max Healthcare 3.5 each, and Bharat Electronics 3.0 with one failing row. The third rule is the reward-to-risk floor, which Vedant Fashions meets only at the top of its buy zone.

The four decisions, one at a time

Each paragraph gives the rule behind the verdict, the number that triggered it and what would change it. The full working is in the linked dossier.

Timken India: shortlisted challenger, no order

Timken India makes tapered roller bearings for railway wagons, trucks, tractors and industry, and the US Timken group owns 51.05 percent. Every hard red line passes: debt is about 0.01 times equity, three-year cash over profit is 0.94 and the promoter pledge is 0.0 percent. Return on capital employed is 19.0 percent and the operating test scores 4.0 of 5.0 with no failing row. The chart is the problem. The price fell about 14 percent in a month and the momentum test says there is no momentum, which routes it to a wait, not a rejection. The price implies about 32.6 percent a year of free-cash-flow growth for ten years, while profit actually grew 0.6 percent a year over three years, so the valuation needs margins to recover. The verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT with a limit order in ₹2,688 to ₹2,730 that goes live only after the 29 October results and a fresh bar on which the momentum gate no longer fails. A close below ₹2,597.92 voids the idea.

Vedant Fashions: shortlisted challenger, no order

Vedant Fashions sells wedding and celebration wear under the Manyavar, Mohey and Twamev brands through mostly franchised stores. It has no borrowings, the pledge is nil, three-year cash over profit is 1.15 and the operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0 with no failing row. Return on capital employed is 29.81 percent but it has fallen for three years, and sales grew only 1.9 percent a year over three years, though the last two quarters grew 8.7 and 7.2 percent. The price is in a Stage 3 pullback with the 200-day average below it, which the rules treat as a reason to wait. Reward-to-risk is 1.14 to 1 at the close and 2.12 to 1 at ₹468, and it falls below 2.0 if the market pays one turn less. The verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT with an order in ₹458 to ₹468 that goes live only after the Q2 results (date not yet announced; the filing deadline is 14 November) show same-store sales growth of at least 2 percent and the target is rebuilt on a fresh bar.

Bharat Electronics: rejected

Bharat Electronics is India's main defence electronics maker, with an order book of ₹73,882 crore (2.7 times sales) and almost no debt. It is rejected on the chart rule: the close of ₹363.00 sits below a falling 50-day average of ₹394.06, which sits below a falling 200-day average of ₹416.43, and the recovery test fails on one condition. A second weakness is cash. Over three years it generated ₹6,764 crore of operating cash against ₹15,370 crore of profit, a ratio of 0.44 against the 0.70 floor, and the defence relief does not apply because three-year sales growth is 16 percent, not 20. The 0.25 red line is not breached. Reward-to-risk looks attractive at 5.10 to 1 only if the central value is right, and it is 0.99 to 1 if the loss is taken at the bear value. The idea reopens only on one refreshed complete bar with a close at or above the 20-day average, a positive two-week gain or relative strength, and an RSI of at least 32, then a full momentum rerun. The September-quarter results are due on 30 October.

Max Healthcare: rejected

Max Healthcare runs about 5,000 beds in affluent city clusters, with revenue per occupied bed of ₹81,900 a day in the June 2026 quarter. It is a good business on a bad chart. The close of ₹835.00 sits below a falling 50-day average of ₹966.19, which sits below a falling 200-day average of ₹1,018.01, and the recovery test fails on two conditions, one of them an RSI of 29.53 against the 32 needed. Debt is 0.32 times equity including leases, the pledge is nil and three-year cash over profit is 1.17. Return on capital employed is 14.7 percent on the company's basis and about 21.6 percent without goodwill and unfinished building work, so the return is not the reason for the rejection. The operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0. Reward-to-risk is 1.69 to 1 at the close, below the 2.0 floor. The idea comes back for review when the Stage 4 reading clears and reward-to-risk is at least 2.0. A court hearing on the Kalinga petitions on 29 October is small in size, and the Q2 results are due on 30 October.

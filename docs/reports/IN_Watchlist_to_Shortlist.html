Before you read

Union Bank of India, Groww (Billionbrains Garage Ventures), Persistent Systems and DLF all pass the hard safety rules and the operating test, so all four join the tactical shortlist. None of them is a buy at today's price. The reward earned for each unit of risk is below the 2.5 to 1 floor at the close for all four, and it clears the floor only if the price falls into a stated zone. Nothing here is a buy today.

What the batch found

Three rules decided these outcomes. The first is a set of hard red lines on debt, share pledge, cash conversion and a chart test called Stage 4. In Stage 4 the price has fallen for a long time: the close sits below a falling 50-day average, and that average sits below a falling 200-day average. None of the four is in Stage 4, and none has a promoter pledge that breaches the 20 percent line. The second rule is the operating test, scored out of 5.0, where a name needs at least 3.0 and may have one failing row. Union Bank, Groww and DLF score 3.5 and Persistent scores 4.0. The third rule is the reward-to-risk floor, which is what turns every verdict here into a wait or a limit order. The gap between the close and the zone is the whole story of this batch: all four ideas work only after a pullback.

Union Bank of India: shortlisted tactical challenger, no order

Union Bank is a government-owned lender whose profit rose 30 percent to ₹5,537 crore in the September 2026 quarter as its margin recovered from 2.64 to 2.86 percent and bad loans fell to 2.42 percent. Debt to equity, interest cover and promoter pledge do not apply to a bank in the usual way (the President of India holds 74.76 percent and reports no pledge), so the dossier tests return on equity, bad loans, capital and funding instead. The operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0 with no failing row. The weak spots are deposit growth of 6.87 percent against 17.8 percent for the system and a quarter helped by unusually low provisions. At ₹184.60 the stock trades at about 6.4 times trailing earnings and 1.09 times book value, which prices a fade of return on equity to 12.6 percent against 16.9 percent earned. Our central value is ₹200, probability-weighted ₹192.25, so the stock is fair rather than cheap. The reward-to-risk ratio is 0.69 to 1 at the close and 2.85 to 1 only at ₹172. The dossier sets limit orders in the ₹165 to ₹172 zone, a half-size first purchase, and a second half only if profit, margin and deposit conditions hold after the December-quarter results. If the multiple falls from 7 to 6 times profit, the target drops to ₹172.80 and the zone is useful only below about ₹165.20.

Groww: shortlisted tactical challenger, watch-only

Groww is India's largest retail broker by active clients (29.16 percent), with a very lean cost base, almost no debt and a return on equity near 28 percent. Profit rose 71.8 percent in the first half of FY27, but revenue was flat from the previous quarter after a SEBI change to the closing auction, and analyst estimates are being cut (FY27 down 5.2 percent in 90 days). The literal three-year cash conversion rule fails (minus 0.03) because a broker's cash flow is distorted by cash it lends out; once ₹3,757.5 crore of loan growth is added back it is 1.18, and the dossier treats that as the broker form of the rule and says so openly. The operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0. At ₹186.57 the reward-to-risk ratio is only 0.26 to 1; it clears the 2.5 floor at ₹178.50 (2.64 to 1) only because the stop is tight, about 2.4 percent below entry and inside one average daily range. Early investors still hold about 41.5 percent of the shares. The posture is watch-only: nothing is placed today, orders follow a close inside ₹175.50 to ₹178.50 and a full rerun of the chart tests, and a close below ₹174.24 cancels or exits. A multiple-only stress of 25 times on the same profit gives ₹143.75, which is 22.95 percent below the close.

Persistent Systems: shortlisted tactical challenger, no order

Persistent is an engineering-services company with a return on capital employed of 34.4 percent, debt to equity of 0.07, a three-year cash conversion of 0.97 and revenue growing 20.8 percent in constant currency while large-cap IT grows 1 to 3 percent. It is about to buy Nagarro, which Persistent's own illustration says adds about 5 percent to profit per share. The operating test scores 4.0 of 5.0, with margin (15.0 percent, down 130 basis points) and client concentration (top five at 38.1 percent) as the two watch items. The price is the problem: 49.5 times trailing earnings, 3.42 average-range units above support, estimates falling 5.2 percent in 90 days, and a reverse DCF that already needs 19 to 21 percent growth a year for ten years. Our FY28 profit per share is ₹165 (below the street) at 40 times, giving ₹6,600. The ratio is 1.00 to 1 at the close and 2.52 to 1 at ₹5,500, a very thin pass. Without the Nagarro lift the ratio at ₹5,500 falls to 1.82, and at 30 times the value is ₹4,950, below the stop. No order is placed on a touch of ₹5,500 alone: a fresh chart test, a target that still gives 2.5 to 1, the refreshed half-year cash conversion and an accretion bridge on the financing actually raised must all come first.

DLF: shortlisted tactical challenger, limit orders only

DLF is a net-cash developer (₹15,084 crore, no gross debt in the development business) with ₹28,700 crore of margin booked on homes already sold and a 66.67 percent stake in an AAA-rated rental arm. Reported earnings lag because profit is recognised only when projects are completed, mainly in FY27 and FY28. It passes every red line, but return on capital is 6.32 percent, so it is a tactical idea, not a compounder. The operating test scores 3.5 of 5.0 with one failing row (conversion of bookings into reported profit). Q2 profit included a ₹738 crore one-time gain and consensus is falling (FY27 down 4.81 percent in 90 days). We put FY28 profit per share at ₹22.5 against ₹25.21 for the street, and the target at ₹770 (22.5 times 34.2). The reviewer's red team showed that a 5 percent cut to that profit moves the ratio at ₹665 to 1.68 to 1, so the zone was lowered to ₹640 to ₹655, where it is 3.94 to 1 and still 2.60 to 1 after the cut. The first half (1.75 percent of net worth) is a limit order at ₹655 with a gate rerun before any fill. The second half waits for the December-quarter result. If the recognition of project margin is only half of what we assume, FY28 profit per share is ₹18.6 and the value ₹636, which the stop and the small size contain.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run published with gate exceptions. That means a check could not be fully met by the evidence available, and the report says so instead of hiding it.

First, the run restarted once. An earlier attempt had claimed a different batch (BIKAJI, CIPLA, CRISIL and MPHASIS), but another run published those four names to the main branch at the same moment, and a clean-up from another run removed the first working copy. That attempt was abandoned, its claims were released, and none of its work was published. This batch was selected afresh from the newest main branch.

Second, the stale-date exception. Every citation check ended PASS WITH EXCEPTIONS because some research records are older than 60 days: the annual reports, rating letters, earlier management calls used to audit what management promised, and, for Groww and DLF, peer results that are the latest those peers have published. Where the September 2026 quarter was already out (Union Bank, Groww, Persistent, DLF), it was used as the main evidence. Every as-of date is true. The count of stale lines is 15 for Union Bank, 18 for Groww, 44 for Persistent and 26 for DLF, and each dossier lists them in its own run notes.

Third, the reviewer cycle. Each dossier went through the strict maximum of two rounds with the configured reviewer and red team. Groww and DLF were approved by the reviewer and the red team in round 2. Union Bank and Persistent each received one remaining REVISE from the reviewer in round 2, on a consistency point about the second purchase and the order rule respectively. Both were fixed afterwards and checked by code only, with no third review, so each carries BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT. Their red teams approved in round 1.

Fourth, data limits. Several filings were scanned images or blocked pages, so some figures are read from extracted text and are marked in the dossiers. Groww has only about a year of price history, so its 200-day average is thin. The three-year median multiple is a calculation, not a published figure, for Union Bank and DLF. The price source, the dossiers and the data files all use the 27 October 2026 session. In the dossiers the gate table keeps the builder's raw field labels, and the paragraphs around them give the thresholds actually applied.

3. Business, catalyst and management assessment

Tips Music’s operating chain starts with selecting rights, artists and film or independent projects. It checks chain of title, permissions, language tags and contract scope, then funds or acquires content and delivers the recordings to platforms. Licensing staff monitor use, reconcile statements and advances, resolve rights questions and collect royalties. The listed company reports one music business; Tips Films is a separate group entity after the film division was transferred. This distinction keeps film production out of the listed music thesis. [4][9][13][18][24][26]

The Q2 FY27 release reports 73 songs—40 film and 33 non-film. It also names new and catalogue tracks with high YouTube reach, and gives a subscriber base of 164.5 million and more than 54.3 billion total views. Those figures show reach, not net receipts. In the latest call, management estimated that digital made up 71% of Q2 revenue and 73% of H1 revenue; paid subscriptions represented 10–15% of the digital mix. It did not provide a full platform-by-platform breakdown, so service concentration and individual payout rates remain uncertain. [1][2][3]

Content spending and partner terms are the central operating risks. Q2 content cost increased 97% to ₹26.2 crore; management said the full-year budget remained ₹90–100 crore after about ₹71 crore in H1. New-release content costs are expensed in the same quarter, so reported margins can fall before recordings mature. This policy gives investors timely cost visibility, but the company does not publish title-level payback or catalogue-cohort returns. A large catalogue matters only if rights remain clear and repeated use produces cash after acquisition cost. [1][2][4]

Distribution gives Tips a reach advantage, but it brings a large renewal hinge. The CFO estimated Warner’s share at 25–30% of revenue and said the agreement is due for renewal in FY28. The direct YouTube Shorts negotiation was not concluded in the October call. The analyst case should not book Shorts upside ahead of a signed deal, and it should not assume a benign Warner renewal without disclosure. Saregama, Zee and Shemaroo are wider-scope public proxies, not exact label peers; each has materially different media, film or broadcast exposure. [1][29][30][31][32]

The guidance audit is mixed. In Q4 FY25, the CEO discussed 29% revenue and 31% PAT growth and management backed a 30% FY26 revenue-growth guide. Audited FY26 revenue from operations grew 20.87%, while PAT rose from ₹16,656.15 lakh to ₹21,674.54 lakh. The top-line guide missed by about 9.1 percentage points; the PAT goal was met. FY27’s published guide remains 20% for revenue and PAT. The CFO said, “We would exceed our 20% guidance. We don’t want to put a specific number to it.” Chairman and MD Kumar Taurani replied, “For time being, you consider it as 20%.” H1 does not settle the full-year outcome. [1][4][10]

The latest call had no current CEO. The FY26 annual report lists Hari Nair as CEO through 30 April 2026, and the chairman said the search remained open in October. The CFO and chairman/MD are quoted in the dossier by their current roles. This gap reduces confidence in operating succession and how execution will scale. A named CEO, clear remit and continued delivery against the FY27 guide would improve the governance read. [1][4][21]

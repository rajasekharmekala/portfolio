Start here

This report answers one question for four Indian stocks: is each one a business that passes the financial floors, and does its chart support a new position under the house strategy? None of the four did. Each is a sound or reasonable business on its financial numbers, but each is in a Weinstein Stage 3 topping phase on the 13 October 2026 close, and none has a price target that can be anchored to earnings at the required reward-to-risk level. All four are therefore REJECTED and move to the watchlist archive. The shortlist does not change.

The watchlist is a queue. The workflow takes the next names that are still waiting for research, four at a time, in the order they appear in the watchlist, and it underwrites each one on its own. A name is not allowed to skip the queue because it looks attractive, and a name that fails is not kept in the active queue. That discipline is what makes the outcome of each batch comparable with the one before it.

Three terms carry most of the report, and the glossary defines the rest. A Layer 1 test is a hard floor: a company that fails any one is rejected before the later layers are read. A Stage 3 chart is one where an uptrend has lost its momentum and the price has begun to roll over, which is why the house rules do not allow a new buy in it. An anchored target is a price objective built from forward earnings multiplied by a historical median price-to-earnings multiple, or from a measured move on the chart. A target that cannot be built that way does not count, however many analysts publish it. The strategy has two books. Core names are long-term compounders that must offer a reward of at least 3.0 times the risk, and Tactical names are shorter-horizon momentum positions that must offer at least 3.5 times. The glossary near the end defines the rest, including the displacement rule that compares a candidate with a current holding.

To read the report, start with the table in the first chapter, which gives the outcome for each name. Then read the chapter on each name, which states the rule that fired and the level that would reopen the case. The dossier for each name holds the full arithmetic, the sources and the peer comparisons. The review and gate chapter says which checks each dossier passed and where an exception remains.

EPL Limited

EPL makes packaging for food and consumer goods. Its financial floors pass: return on capital employed is 17.8 percent against a 15 percent floor, debt is light at 0.34 times equity, and three-year cash conversion is 2.17 times profit. The chart does not pass. The close of 233.97 sits below the 50-day average of 241.11, and that average is flat rather than rising, which is the signature of Stage 3. The case reopens if the close reclaims the 50-day average on volume and an anchored target of at least 288.48 exists for the Core book.

Worked example: EPL asymmetry

The reward-to-risk ratio is the upside to a defensible target divided by the downside to the stop. With the close at 233.97, the anchored target at 259.60 and the Core stop at 215.80, the upside is 25.63 rupees a share and the downside is 18.17 rupees a share. The ratio is 25.63 divided by 18.17, which is 1.41. The Core floor is 3.0, so the company would need either a far higher target or a far lower entry price. Either change would be a new underwriting, not a tweak to this one.

What would change this view for EPL

  • If the close moves above the 50-day average of 241.11 on volume, and the average turns up, the chart test can be re-run.
  • If a Core target of at least 288.48 can be anchored to forward earnings, the asymmetry floor can be re-tested.
  • If the close breaks below the 200-day average of 223.56, the stop-loss rule and the downtrend both become more pressing.

Glossary

These terms recur across the four dossiers. Each is defined once here in plain words, so that the batch can be read without the dossiers open.

  • Stage 3. The topping phase of Weinstein staging, when an uptrend has lost momentum and the price begins to roll over. It is the reason all four names fail the technical floor.
  • 50-day and 200-day average. The average closing price over the last 50 or 200 sessions. A price below a falling average is weak; a price above a rising average is strong.
  • ROCE. Return on capital employed, the profit the business earns on the money tied up in it. The floor is 15 percent.
  • CFO to PAT. Cash from operations divided by reported profit, over three years. A ratio of 0.70 or more means profit is backed by cash.
  • Anchored target. A price objective built from forward earnings times a historical median price-to-earnings multiple, or from a measured move on the chart.
  • Asymmetry. Upside to the anchored target divided by downside to the stop, also called the reward-to-risk ratio. The Core floor is 3.0 and the Tactical floor is 3.5.
  • Momentum gate. A code test that passes a stock if it is in an established uptrend (Test A) or entering one from a tight base (Test B). NO_MOMENTUM means both tests failed.
  • ATR. Average true range, the typical daily price swing over 14 sessions, in rupees. Stops are set in multiples of it.
  • Core and Tactical. The two strategy books. Core names need an asymmetry of at least 3.0; Tactical names need at least 3.5 and a shorter horizon.
  • Displacement. The rule that compares a shortlisted candidate with a current holding. A candidate can replace a holding only when it clears the gates and the transaction costs are covered.

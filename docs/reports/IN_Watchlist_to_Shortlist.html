Before you read

This report sums up one batch of four India names, each with a full research dossier. Bharat Dynamics (BDL) is rejected because its chart is in a confirmed downtrend. Anand Rathi Wealth, Cyient and Indian Hotels pass the underwriting but none can be bought at today's price, so all three get a wait-for-support verdict with a buy zone below the market. Only Cyient takes a shortlist slot, because the tactical book had one free. The conclusions would be wrong if the earnings each dossier relies on do not arrive; each dossier names the price or event that changes its verdict.

What the batch found

Each row gives the verdict, the close, the entry zone, the stop, our central target and the reward-to-risk ratio. The entry zone is the price range where a buy would be considered. Reward-to-risk, which the scoring file calls asymmetry, is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop. A stop is the price at which the idea is wrong. The scoring file asks for at least 2.0 for a core name and 2.5 for a tactical or turnaround name. Read the ratio at the close together with the ratio at the top of the entry zone: the first is what a buyer gets today, and the second is what a limit order gets after a pullback. A limit order is an order to buy only at or below a set price.

The four decisions, one at a time

Each paragraph gives the rule behind the verdict, the number that drove it and what would change it. The full working is in the linked dossier.

BDL: rejected on the chart, with a thin reward even without it

Bharat Dynamics is the state-owned maker of guided missiles and torpedoes for the Indian armed forces. It carries almost no debt, has no promoter pledge, and holds ₹26,176 crore of signed orders, about 10.7 times last year's sales. The problem is delivery: revenue fell 27 percent in FY26, its revenue targets were missed in FY24, FY25 and FY26, and about 62 percent of trailing pre-tax profit is interest on its cash. The shares closed at ₹1,006.00, below a falling 50-day average of ₹1,187.88, which is below a falling 200-day average of ₹1,291.14, and the recovery test fails on the short-term turn and on a relative strength index of 29.54 against a floor of 32. That combination is the Stage 4 red line, so the verdict is REJECTED. The price also assumes 23.3 percent yearly profit growth for ten years, against 10.3 percent delivered over five, and reward-to-risk is only 1.27 to 1 at the close. The dossier reopens the case on a close above about ₹1,089 with the index at 32 or higher, plus a rebuilt entry that clears 2.0 to 1; September-quarter revenue near ₹1,100 crore at a margin of 15 percent or more is the hinge. A close below ₹960.95 ends the idea for this cycle.

ANANDRATHI: a high-quality wealth manager at a price that needs a pullback

Anand Rathi Wealth earns recurring fees on ₹1,08,377 crore of client assets (up 18.4 percent), with a return on equity of 47.3 percent and almost no debt. The September-quarter reported profit fell 10.6 percent while adjusted profit rose 22.2 percent. The dossier shows the ₹46.2 crore gap is the share-option expense for two top executives, which runs on a four-year schedule, so it is not a one-off; the ₹96 crore gain in the June quarter on a group lender was non-cash and will not repeat. The shares are in Stage 2, which is the rising phase: the close is above a rising 50-day average, and that average is above a rising 200-day average. They closed at ₹2,259.00, 1.76 average true ranges above the 50-day average, so the buy band is closed. Reward-to-risk is 0.27 to 1 at the close and 3.03 to 1 at ₹2,060 on adjusted earnings; with the option cost charged, the base value, which is the middle scenario, is ₹2,050 and the zone fails the 2.0 floor. The verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT with no standing order. It changes only if the price falls into ₹2,016 to ₹2,060 and a refreshed valuation shows a target of at least ₹2,244; a close below ₹1,967.84 turns it to REJECTED.

CYIENT: an engineering repair story, priced for the repair

Cyient sells engineering and technology services (aerospace, rail, communications) plus electronics manufacturing and a young chip unit. The engineering core is mending: its operating margin, which is operating profit as a share of sales, is 14.2 percent, the highest in ten quarters, and record September-quarter orders were $158.4 million (up 21 percent). But organic growth is only 0.4 percent on the year and the chip unit's break-even has slipped from FY27 to FY28. The static return on capital is 12.3 percent, below the 15 percent floor, so it qualifies as a turnaround name: a business that misses a static quality level but shows improving trends. The probability-weighted value is ₹1,176 against a close of ₹1,182.30, so reward-to-risk is −0.06 to 1 at the close and 2.55 to 1 at ₹1,060. It joins the tactical shortlist as WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT with a limit zone of ₹1,044 to ₹1,060 and a stop of ₹1,016, and a buy also needs a refreshed valuation that still gives 2.5 to 1. An engineering margin near 13.5 percent, or a chip break-even beyond FY28, removes the zone.

INDHOTEL: a strong hotel business after a 14 percent two-week run

Indian Hotels, the Tata group hotel company, has net cash, a AAA credit rating, a fast-growing fee business (fee income up 25 percent to ₹156 crore in the September quarter) and raised its FY27 revenue guidance, which is management's own forecast to investors, to 15 to 17 percent. The shares closed at a 52-week high of ₹812.85, 3.2 average true ranges above the 20-day average, with a relative strength index of 71.87. The price already assumes about 23 percent yearly cash-flow growth for ten years, and the probability-weighted value of ₹823.60 is only 1.3 percent above the close. Reward-to-risk is 0.39 to 1 at the close and 2.18 to 1 at ₹739.55, and the 2.18 rests on a judgement multiple of 45 times FY28 earnings. The verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT with a limit zone of ₹717.70 to ₹739.55 and a stop of ₹682.75. With consensus earnings and a 36 times multiple alone, the value is ₹690.84, 15.0 percent below the close, which is why no order sits at the market.

Run notes and gate exceptions

This run published with gate exceptions. A gate that the evidence could not fully meet is listed here instead of being hidden.

First, the stale-date exception. The citation checks for BDL (115 records), ANANDRATHI (28 records) and CYIENT (51 records) ended PASS WITH EXCEPTIONS, because many research records are older than 60 days: annual reports, rating letters, earlier management statements used to audit past promises, and quarterly filings with nothing newer yet filed. Second, the reviewer cycle. Each dossier went through at most two review rounds with the configured reviewer and red team. BDL and ANANDRATHI were approved by the reviewer in round 2 and their red teams ended with the verdict holding. CYIENT and INDHOTEL each received a remaining REVISE in round 2 (CYIENT: a hinge sentence and a currency symbol; INDHOTEL: a cash-conversion label); those findings were fixed afterwards without a third review, so both carry BEST_EFFORT_AFTER_REVIEW_LIMIT. The CYIENT red team ended with the verdict holding in round 2, and the INDHOTEL red team held in round 1.

Third, the validator and this summary. The shared structural validator has no dossier or batch-summary mode (its audit rules fail every batch summary), so for the dossiers the packet's code checks, the readability check and the citation check were used instead. This summary page was not sent through its own reviewer round: every figure on it is copied from the four reviewed dossiers or from the record files, and the summary only restates their verdicts. Some items are not available and are stated as such in the dossiers: a three-year median multiple for BDL and INDHOTEL, the Q2 FY27 results filing for BDL and ANANDRATHI, and confirmed dates for the next results.

Fourth, one data caveat. The price ledger's market capitalisation for CYIENT is about ₹337 crore below 104.7 million shares at the close, and its forward price-to-earnings ratio, which is the price divided by expected earnings per share, does not match its own consensus earnings; the dossier says so and relies on the close and filed numbers instead.

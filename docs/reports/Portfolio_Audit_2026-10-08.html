Beginner's Underwriting Primer: Frameworks, Rules & Mechanics

How to read this audit

Imagine three independent analysts sitting at the same table, each looking at your portfolio through a different lens, and then a fourth person — the CIO — reconciling their notes into one set of orders. The first analyst is the macro-regime specialist. She does not look at your stocks at all. She looks at the Nifty 50, the Nifty 500, India VIX, the sector indices, and the breadth of the market, and she answers one question: is the tide rising or falling? On 2026-10-08 the fresh ledger labels the regime Downtrend. Nifty breadth is 14.3% above the 50-day average, and the Nifty 50 is below its 20-, 50- and 200-day averages. When the tide is falling, the correct posture is to hold less of the boat, not to buy more of it.

The second analyst is the forensic holdings auditor, and she is deliberately adversarial. Her job is to try to prove that every position you own should be sold. She walks the 19 holdings one by one and asks: is the technical structure intact, is the fundamental thesis still alive, is the position too large, is it dead money, is there a binary earnings risk? Only the positions that survive her attack keep their capital; the rest are ranked into an extraction ladder from EXIT down to HOLD. This is why the audit can tell you to sell a position that is still up in rupee terms — because the forward risk, not the past gain, is what matters.

The third analyst is the greenfield capital allocator, and she is told to ignore your holdings completely. Her question is: if your entire net worth were in cash today, what would you buy from the shortlist? This zero-bias step prevents the audit from simply rationalizing what you already own. She applies the same gates to every candidate — financial, technical, and asymmetry — and in a Downtrend she is very selective, because the asymmetry hurdle (a 3.0:1 reward-to-risk for core, 3.5:1 for tactical) is hard to clear when most names are only a few percent below their highs.

The CIO then reconciles the three. Every action label in this report is triggered by a written rule, not a feeling. A stop is computed from the lower of a 20-session rolling low (the reproducible swing-low proxy) and the bucket-specific moving averages, less a multiple of 14-day ATR: 0.5×ATR for Tactical Momentum, and 1.0×ATR subtracted from the lowest of the rolling low, 50-DMA and 200-DMA for Core Alpha. This proxy is a rolling-period low, not a separately verified chart-pivot low. A single stock may not exceed 20% of net worth, except under the Super-Performer Momentum Exception, which says: if a winning holding is above 20% but its Stage 2 structure is intact (above a rising 20-day average, relative strength above 80, RSI below 82), do not trim into strength — ratchet the stop instead. A position is only swapped for a challenger when three conditions hold at once: the holding has triggered an exit or trim, the challenger has passed 100% of the gates and is at support (within 0.5×ATR), and the challenger's asymmetry beats the holding's by at least 1.5× after tax and friction. If you remember nothing else, remember that: every number in this report is reproducible from the market-facts ledger, and every order in Chapter 6 is exactly the delta shown in the Chapter 5 money bridge.

A moving average smooths a price into a trend line. The 20-day moving average is the average closing price over the last 20 trading days, roughly one month. The 50-day moving average covers about ten weeks and the 200-day moving average covers about forty weeks. Think of them as lines drawn through a bumpy road to show which way it runs. A price above a rising average means buyers have been paying more over time. A price below a falling one means the opposite, and that is the situation the Nifty 50 is in today.

A share moves through four seasons called stages. Stage 2 is the growing season, when the price sits above a rising 50-day and 200-day average. Stage 4 is winter, when the price sits below falling averages. Stage 1 is the quiet spell after a fall and Stage 3 is the tired top before one. A base is a flat stretch of trading after a decline, like a landing on a staircase where the price rests before the next climb. A breakout is a move above a price that the share failed to pass before. The price-to-earnings ratio divides the share price by the yearly profit per share, so a high ratio means investors pay more for each rupee of profit.

RSI (the relative strength index) is a speedometer for a price on a scale of 0 to 100. Above 70 means the price has run fast and may be stretched, and below 30 means it has fallen fast. ATR (the average true range) is the normal daily wobble of a share in rupees. If a share has an ATR of 50, a one-day move of 50 rupees is ordinary, so a stop placed only 20 rupees below the price would be hit by noise and not by news. Volume is the number of shares that changed hands, which you can picture as people voting with their money. A rise on heavy volume carries more weight than a rise on thin volume.

The put-call ratio divides the open put contracts by the open call contracts: puts are bets or insurance against a fall, calls are bets on a rise. An option wall is a strike price with an unusually large number of open contracts. It often acts as a ceiling (calls) or a floor (puts), because the sellers of those options defend them. A stop loss is an exit price you choose before you buy, so that a wrong idea costs a known amount. A limit order is an order that fills only at your chosen price or better. An entry zone is the band of prices where you would be willing to buy.

Reward-to-risk divides what you could gain by what you could lose. A ratio of 3.5 to 1 means each rupee at risk has a chance to earn 3.5 rupees. Asymmetry is the same idea seen as a shape: a bet whose upside is much larger than its downside. This report asks for at least 3.5 to 1 before it calls anything a setup. Position size is the other half of safety. Even a good idea should be small enough that being wrong does not hurt, which is why no single share should decide your year.

The report also uses its own labels. The regime is one of four words: Uptrend (broad), Uptrend (narrow breadth), Correction, or Downtrend. Watch only means reward-to-risk is below 3.5 to 1, so there is no setup yet, even for a confirmed name. The people who write those contracts take the other side, and they defend their strikes. 3. The Bull List: Top 30 Stage 2 Momentum Leaders

The Bull List is the short list of shares that are in Stage 2, the phase where a stock has left a base and sits above a rising 50-day and 200-day average. Three tests define a name that passes in full. The price must be in Stage 2. The 14-day RSI must lie between 55 and 72, which means momentum is strong without being stretched. And volume on the day must be at least 1.5 times its 20-day average with the price rising, which means the move is backed by real buying. Only three names pass all three: Punjab National Bank, Union Bank of India and HFCL. Sixteen more pass the trend and RSI tests but not volume, and six pass the trend test but sit outside the RSI band. The ledger found 47 Stage 2 names among the 499 shares it could score, so the list is short because the market is in a Downtrend and not because the screen is strict.

The ledger fills ten places per size tier when it can. It found 17 Stage 2 names in the large-cap tier (above ₹50,000 Cr), 25 in the mid-cap tier (₹15,000 Cr to ₹50,000 Cr) and only 5 in the small-cap tier (₹500 Cr to ₹15,000 Cr), so the small-cap table has five rows and not ten. The names cluster in the industries Section 2 marked as strongest, with Financial Services supplying 5, Information Technology 4 and Capital Goods 4, and the exceptions are named in the notes.

Each row shows a stop and a target derived by rule and not by opinion. The stop is the higher of the 20-day swing low and the 50-day average less half an ATR. The target is the 52-week high, the nearest price the market has already paid. Reward-to-risk is then (target minus entry) divided by (entry minus stop), where the entry is the lower of the close and the 50-day average plus half an ATR, because a share well above its average should be bought on a pullback and not chased. Rows under 3.5 to 1 are marked watch only.

An 8% gap down opens at ₹4,323.91, only a little under the stop, and the loss is 40 x ₹375.99 = ₹15,040, about 1.08 times the plan. A 15% gap opens at ₹3,994.91, and the loss is ₹28,199, which is 0.63% of net worth and 2.02 times the plan. The audit's answer is to cut the holding to 19 shares before the results. On 19 shares the same 15% gap costs 19 x ₹704.98 = ₹13,395, about the planned loss on the full position. The cost of cutting is giving up half of a possible gain, which is the price of a result you cannot predict.

Glossary

ATR (average true range)
The normal daily price range of a share in rupees. Stops are placed in multiples of it so that ordinary noise does not trigger them.
Asymmetry / reward-to-risk
The potential gain divided by the potential loss. This report asks for at least 3.5 to 1 before calling anything a setup.
Bulk deal / block deal
A large trade named by the exchange: a bulk deal is above 0.5% of a company's shares in a day, a block deal is a negotiated trade in a special window.
Distribution day
A down day on rising volume, read as large holders selling. The ledger counts them over the last 25 sessions.
DII / FII (FPI)
Domestic institutional investors (mutual funds, insurers) and foreign institutional or portfolio investors.
Delivery percentage
The share of traded shares that were actually delivered to buyers' accounts and not squared off the same day. A high figure suggests holders, a low one suggests day traders.
Max pain
The strike price at which option buyers as a group would lose the most money at expiry.
Moving average (20-, 50-, 200-day)
The average closing price over the last 20, 50 or 200 trading days, used as a trend line.
Open interest
The number of option or futures contracts that are open and not yet closed or expired.
Promoter pledge
Shares that the founders have pledged to lenders. A pledge above 20% of the promoters' shares is a red line in the audit rules.
Put-call ratio
Put open interest divided by call open interest. Below 1 means more calls than puts are open.
Regime
The one-word label for the market's overall trend: Uptrend (broad), Uptrend (narrow breadth), Correction or Downtrend.
Risk-unit sizing
A rule that sets position size from the distance to the stop, so that being stopped out costs a fixed small share of net worth.
RSI (relative strength index)
A 0 to 100 reading of how fast a price has moved. Above 70 is stretched, below 30 is oversold.
Stage 1 to 4
Weinstein's four seasons of a share: base, markup, top, markdown. The Bull List holds Stage 2 names and the Bear List Stage 4.
Series BE
The NSE trade-for-trade segment, in which every trade is settled by delivery.
VIX (India VIX)
The exchange's index of expected volatility, computed from option prices. Higher means more fear.

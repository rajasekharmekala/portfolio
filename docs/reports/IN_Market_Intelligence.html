3.4 What the screen does and does not establish

A Stage 2 label summarizes a price structure; it does not explain why the company is moving or whether the move will continue. This research pass verified one-session delivery statistics but did not verify company-specific catalysts for these candidates. The absence of a verified catalyst is not evidence that no catalyst exists. It means that the report cannot use an unverified earnings, order-win or guidance narrative to strengthen a technical signal.

The screen also does not make a short list of 22 names equal to 22 trades. A valid tactical setup still needs a named support or breakout level, an evidence-based stop, and a target whose reward-to-risk ratio meets the 2.5:1 floor. Candidates that do not meet that separate test remain ideas to monitor. The next section turns to Stage 4 deterioration, which is useful as a risk map for positions and sectors that have lost the same trend structure these candidates are trying to establish.

What changes this view: If an awaiting-volume name records at least 1.5 times its 20-day volume while holding above the 50-day average, then recheck it for entry. If price closes below the 50-day average, or RSI leaves the screen band, then drop the candidate from the active setup queue. The following bear screen identifies the other side of this risk map.

The operational rule is to use “confirmed” as a prerequisite, not as an automatic order. For any “awaiting volume confirmation” name, wait for the stated volume condition, then check that price remains near support and that the market regime has not deteriorated. The next section turns to the opposite risk case: names whose trend has broken down.

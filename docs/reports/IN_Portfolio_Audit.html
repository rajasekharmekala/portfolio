Chapter 7 — Rulebook and monitoring

The audit uses four layers of evidence. Portfolio positions and cost bases come from the copied Zerodha transaction history replayed in the run. Market price, stage, moving averages, RSI, ATR, breadth and sector quadrants come from the fresh 2 November facts ledger. Company events and market-flow data come from dated sources in the run-specific research file. The greenfield model is a separate, zero-holdings screen that uses the same session’s technical ledger and later-retrieved snapshots only as limited descriptive context. These layers have different confidence: exact ledger calculations are strongest for the cutoff; event information is only as strong as its source date and disclosure.

Position actions follow the account rules: exit a Stage 4 breakdown, reduce an oversized winner to the 22% cap when the winner exception no longer applies, and otherwise hold only behind a named stop or trail. The 50-day support limits, half-ATR stops and 52-week-high targets are mechanical model references, not forecasts. A stated “not verified” result date means the research search did not establish it; it does not mean the company has no event. Where the promoter-pledge search returned no verifiable filing, the report does not infer a clean pledge profile.

At the next review, rebuild the ledger from the latest completed exchange session, reconcile fills and cash, refresh the results calendar using exchange notices, and recheck sector breadth. Any change in the 22% cap, policy cash band or position rules should be applied consistently to every holding, rather than added selectively to defend a preferred name.

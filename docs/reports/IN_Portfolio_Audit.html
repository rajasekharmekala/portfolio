Ten ideas carry the report, and each has an everyday comparison. A moving average is the average closing price over the last N days, like a trend line drawn through a jittery signal; the 50-day line summarises about ten weeks of mood and the 200-day line about ten months. A Stage is one of four seasons in a share's life: Stage 1 is a flat winter base, Stage 2 is the climb, Stage 3 is a topping autumn and Stage 4 is a falling winter. The RSI is a speedometer from 0 to 100: below 30 the price has fallen unusually fast, above 70 it has risen unusually fast. The ATR (average true range) is a share's normal daily wobble in rupees, and it places stops so that ordinary noise does not shake you out. Volume counts how many shares changed hands, which is how many people voted with their money. A stop-loss is an exit price decided in advance, written down before emotion arrives. Reward-to-risk divides what you can win by what you can lose; the written floor is 2.0 for Core names and 2.5 for Tactical names. Position size is how much of your money one idea may hold, which is why no single share should be able to hurt you badly. Beta is how hard a share swings when the index moves, so a beta of 1.5 means a 1% index fall usually costs that share about 1.5%. The regime is the market's season as a whole, and today the ledger names it Downtrend (capitulation bounce / follow-through watch).

Every verdict carries a label and a rule. EXIT sells the whole position. It is triggered by a close under the written stop that has not been won back, by a failed hard red line (a promoter pledge of 25% or more, or 20% together with high debt, falling profit or a Stage 4 chart; three-year cash conversion under 0.25 without a growth exemption; debt to equity of 1.20 or more; or a Stage 4 chart with no base) or by a Stage 4 chart whose turnaround test fails. TRIM sells part of a position and is triggered by size: the single-stock hard cap is 15% of net worth, and a Stage 2 winner with intact fundamentals and steady or rising estimates may run to 22%, which is the Let-Winners-Run rule. HOLD / HARD STOP keeps a position with a written exit price, and HOLD / TRAIL keeps a rising Stage 2 winner and moves its exit up behind the 50-day average. Stops follow a formula: for Core names, the lower of the 20-day swing low and the 50-day average (or the 63-day base low for a name that is not in Stage 2) minus one ATR; for Tactical names, the lower of the 20-day swing low and the 20-day average minus half an ATR. A stop is judged only on the latest close, so a name that dipped under its stop and then won it back is forgiven as a shakeout. New money is sized by Risk-Unit Sizing: each conviction tier gets a risk budget in basis points of net worth (100 for Tier 1, 75 for Tier 2, 50 for Tier 3, 35 for Tier 4), the weight is that budget divided by the stop distance, and it is capped at the tier maximum. A swap, selling one holding to buy a challenger, needs three things at once: the holding triggered an exit or trim, the challenger passed every gate and sits within half an ATR of support, and the challenger's reward-to-risk is at least 1.5 times the holding's.

3.1 The Risk-Unit Sizing arithmetic

Risk-Unit Sizing sizes a position by what you can lose and not by what you hope to gain. Each conviction tier has a risk budget in basis points of net worth, where one basis point is one hundredth of a percent: 100 for Tier 1, 75 for Tier 2, 50 for Tier 3 and 35 for Tier 4. The weight is the budget divided by the distance from the entry to the stop, so a stop 5% away allows a bigger position than one 15% away, and the result is capped at the tier maximum, which is 3.5% for Tier 3. Because the Downtrend posture allows only a starter, the weight is then halved. The idea is that every starter risks roughly the same rupees if its stop is hit, however volatile the share is. A funnel is a series of filters in which each stage removes some candidates, and the shortlist funnel has three: a financial gate, a technical gate and an asymmetry gate. The financial gate asks whether the business is safe to own: debt, interest cover, promoter pledge, three-year cash conversion and a chart that is not a broken Stage 4. The technical gate asks whether the chart supports buying now, using three momentum tests. Test A is a Stage 2 leader: the close above a rising 50-day average that is above a 200-day average that is not falling, with positive relative strength against the Nifty and a close within 1.6 ATR of support. Test B is a base about to break out, and Test C is a turnaround: a floor held above the 63-day low with the short-term trend or relative strength turning up and an RSI of at least 32. The asymmetry gate asks whether the possible gain is large enough compared with the possible loss, which means 2.0 times for Core names and 2.5 times for Tactical names, measured at the close or at the planned limit price. A name that passes the financial gate and fails the others is not rejected; it is kept on the shortlist with a limit order or a trigger. That is the reason every one of the 22 names below carries the status PASSED_CONDITIONAL, and none is in the funnel's reject pile today.

A passing and a failing example make the gates concrete. SAGILITY passes the financial gate on every red line (debt to equity 0.11, three-year cash conversion 2.00, no pledge) and passes the technical gate on Test C, with all four conditions holding at an RSI of 50.4; its asymmetry gate passes only at the zone top, 2.55 against a floor of 2.5. 4.2 The three-condition swap test for every sale

A swap sells a holding in order to buy a challenger, and the rules allow it only when three conditions hold at once. Condition 1 is that the holding has triggered an exit or a trim. Condition 2 is that the challenger has passed every gate and sits within half an ATR of verified support, so the buy is at the floor and not in mid-air. Condition 3 is that the challenger's reward-to-risk is at least 1.5 times the holding's, so the switch must improve the odds by half again and cover the friction of tax and costs. The test exists to stop a common mistake: selling a weak holding and buying a stronger-looking story at a worse price, then finding that the trade only paid the broker. It is applied to each of the eight sales. Condition 1 is met for all eight, because that is why they are on the sell list. The result depends on Conditions 2 and 3.

6.3 Phase 3: defensive GTT and trailing stop architecture

A stop that exists only in an analysis is a hope. Phase 3 puts every stop on file at the broker as a good-till-triggered order, a standing instruction that waits until the price touches a trigger level and then places a limit order. The trigger levels are the active stops from Chapter 2.5. Two differences between the audit's rules and a broker's orders need saying. The audit judges a stop on the closing price, while a broker's trigger fires on any trade at the level during the day, which can be earlier and on a brief dip, so a stop on file will sometimes sell a position that would have recovered by the close. The audit accepts that cost because the loss is bounded and the alternative is a stop that depends on someone watching the screen at 15:25. The second difference is the gap: if the stock opens under the trigger, the limit order may not fill, so each stop should be set with a limit price about 0.5% under the trigger. To place one, open the stock's order window, choose the standing-order (GTT) option, select a single sell trigger, enter the trigger price, the limit price and the quantity, and save; for a name with a target as well as a stop, a two-leg (OCO) order places both.

Glossary

Moving average: the average closing price over the last N days, a trend line; the 20-day line shows the last month, the 50-day about ten weeks and the 200-day about ten months.

Stage 1 to Stage 4: the four seasons of a share: Stage 1 basing, Stage 2 climbing, Stage 3 topping or pulling back, Stage 4 falling. Stage 4 here means the close is under a falling 50-day average that is under a falling 200-day average.

RSI: the relative strength index, a 0 to 100 speedometer; under 30 the price has fallen unusually fast, over 70 it has risen unusually fast.

ATR: the average true range, a share's normal daily swing in rupees, used to place stops outside ordinary noise.

Stop, stop-loss, GTT: an exit price decided in advance; a GTT is a standing broker instruction that waits for a price to trade and then places a limit order.

Limit order: an order to buy at or below, or sell at or above, a chosen price; it may not fill.

Reward-to-risk (asymmetry): the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop; the floor is 2.0 for Core and 2.5 for Tactical new money.

Risk-Unit Sizing: setting a position's weight as a risk budget in basis points of net worth divided by the stop distance, capped by the tier maximum.

Portfolio heat: the total rupees lost if every stop is hit, as a share of net worth; the cap is 12%.

Beta and effective market exposure: beta is how far a share has moved per 1% of index move; weighting betas by position size gives the share of the portfolio that behaves like the index.

Lockstep: two holdings whose daily returns have a correlation of 0.65 or more over a year; the audit then treats them as one bet.

Cash conversion (CFO/PAT): operating cash collected divided by reported profit, over three years; under 0.25 is a hard red line unless a growth exemption applies.

ROCE and D/E: return on capital employed, profit per rupee of capital tied up, and debt to equity, borrowings compared with the owners' money.

Promoter pledge: shares that the controlling owners have offered as security for loans; 25% or more of their holding is a hard red line.

Hard red line: a written disqualifier: a pledge, a debt level, a cash-conversion failure or a Stage 4 chart with no base.

Tests A, B and C: momentum tests: A is a Stage 2 leader, B is a base about to break out and C is a turnaround above a held floor.

Capitulation Reversal Watch: the regime tag for a downtrend with breadth at or under 25% and a first green day; it allows starter buys and forbids wholesale selling.

Undercut-and-reclaim: a stop break that is forgiven when the close wins the level back, or comes within a quarter of an ATR on a positive day with RSI at 32 or more.

Let-Winners-Run: the rule that lets a Stage 2 winner with intact fundamentals and steady or rising estimates grow to 22% of net worth while its stop trails upward.

FIFO, STCG and LTCG: first-in first-out lot matching; short-term capital gains (held up to 12 months, 20% for listed equity) and long-term capital gains (12.5% above ₹1,25,000 a year).

QIP: a qualified institutional placement, a share sale to institutions, which adds shares and cash.

PCR, max pain, option walls: the put-call ratio of option open interest, the strike at which option sellers lose least, and the strikes with the most open interest.

FII and DII: foreign and domestic institutional investors, whose daily net buying and selling the exchanges publish.

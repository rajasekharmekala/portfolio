Ten ideas carry the report. A moving average is the average closing price over the last N days, like a trend line drawn through a jittery signal; the 50-day line summarises about ten weeks of mood and the 200-day line about ten months. A Stage is one of four seasons in a share's life: Stage 1 is a flat winter base, Stage 2 is the climb, Stage 3 is a topping autumn and Stage 4 is a falling winter. The RSI is a speedometer from 0 to 100: below 30 the price has fallen unusually fast, above 70 it has risen unusually fast. The ATR (average true range) is a share's normal daily wobble in rupees and places stops so that ordinary noise does not shake you out. Volume counts how many shares changed hands, which is how many people voted with their money. A stop-loss is an exit price decided in advance, written down before emotion arrives. Reward-to-risk divides what you can win by what you can lose; the floor is 2.0 for Core names and 2.5 for Tactical names. Position size is how much of your money one idea may hold, which is why no single share should hurt you badly. The regime is the market's season as a whole, and today the ledger names it Downtrend (capitulation bounce / follow-through watch).

Every verdict carries a label and a rule. EXIT sells the whole position; it is triggered by a closing price under the written stop that has not been won back, by a failed hard red line (a promoter pledge of 25% or more, or 20% together with high debt, falling profit or a Stage 4 chart; three-year cash conversion under 0.25 without a growth exemption; debt to equity of 1.20 or more; or a Stage 4 chart with no base) or by a Stage 4 chart whose turnaround test fails. TRIM sells part of a position and is triggered by size: the single-stock hard cap is 15% of net worth, and a Stage 2 winner with intact fundamentals and steady or rising estimates may run to 22%, which is the Let-Winners-Run ceiling. HOLD / HARD STOP keeps a position under a stop that sells everything if breached, and HOLD / TRAIL is the same for a Stage 2 winner whose stop climbs with the rising 50-day average. Undercut-and-reclaim is the shakeout rule: a stop that was broken on an earlier close but is won back on today's close is forgiven, and the stop is reset half an ATR under the shakeout low. New positions are sized by Risk-Unit Sizing: weight equals the risk budget in basis points divided by the distance to the stop, capped at the tier maximum, so a wider stop earns a smaller position. A limit order buys only at your price or lower.

2.1 Position size, the 15% cap and the Let-Winners-Run test

Letting winners run is the rule that stops an investor from selling a great stock too early just because it has become big. It says that a Stage 2 winner (a share in a rising trend above its 50-day and 200-day averages) with intact fundamentals and steady or rising profit estimates may grow past the 15% single-stock cap, up to a ceiling of 22%, while its stop is trailed up to the rising 21-day or 50-day average. All three conditions have to hold together: the chart, the business and the estimates. If any one fails, the 15% cap applies in full and the excess is trimmed. The rule has a second job in this report, which is to keep the audit honest. 3.1 The Risk-Unit Sizing arithmetic

Risk-Unit Sizing sizes a position by what you can lose, not by what you hope to gain. Each conviction tier has a risk budget in basis points of net worth (one basis point is one hundredth of a percent): 100 for Tier 1, 75 for Tier 2, 50 for Tier 3 and 35 for Tier 4. The weight is the budget divided by the distance from the entry to the stop, so a stop that is 5% away lets the position be bigger than one that is 15% away, and the result is capped at the tier maximum (3.5% for Tier 3). Because the Downtrend posture allows only a pilot, the weight is then halved. The idea is that every starter risks about the same rupees if its stop is hit, however volatile the share is.

A swap replaces one holding with another, and the written rule demands three things at once. The first is that the holding has triggered an exit or a trim. The second is that the challenger has passed 100% of the gates and is strictly at support, within half an ATR of its nearest floor. The third is that the challenger's reward-to-risk is at least 1.5 times the holding's, so that the switch more than pays for the cost of making it. The test exists to stop a portfolio from selling a mediocre name to buy a similar one and paying friction for nothing. 6.4 What to do when an order does not fill or the price gaps

Orders fail in four ways, and each has a fixed response so that nothing depends on mood. A sell that does not fill by 14:00 is repriced 1% lower, and by 15:00 it is repriced to the last price; a sell that has not filled by the close is placed again the next morning at a limit 2% under the previous close, because a position that the rules say to sell is a position that has no reason to be held. A buy that does not fill is left alone until its expiry, because a limit order is supposed to wait; if the price runs away upward, do not chase it, and re-run the audit. A price that gaps through a sell limit at the open fills at the open, which is better for a seller. A price that gaps through a stop fills at the open too, which is worse, and the response is to sell at the open and write down the loss.

Glossary

The terms below are used in the report, with the meaning they have here. Each is also explained where it first appears.

  • ATR (average true range). A share's normal daily range in rupees. Stops are placed in multiples of it so that ordinary wobble does not trigger them.
  • Asymmetry. The reward-to-risk ratio: the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop. The floor is 2.0 for Core and 2.5 for Tactical names.
  • Beta. A share's sensitivity to the market. A beta of 1.5 means it tends to move 1.5% for every 1% the index moves.
  • Base. A flat stretch of trading, like a floor, that follows a fall; Stage 1 is a base.
  • CNC. Cash-and-carry, the Zerodha order type for delivery trades held beyond the day.
  • Core Alpha. The bucket for long-term compounders, target 30 to 40% of net worth.
  • Effective market exposure. The beta-adjusted share of net worth that behaves like the index.
  • Entry zone. The price range in which a limit order may fill.
  • GTT. Good-till-triggered: a Zerodha order that waits for a trigger price and then sends a sell or buy order.
  • Hard red line. A disqualifying fact: a large promoter pledge, near-zero cash conversion, heavy debt or a Stage 4 breakdown with no base.
  • Heat. The rupees lost across all stops if every stop were hit, as a share of net worth; limit 12%.
  • Let-Winners-Run. The rule that lets a Stage 2 winner with intact fundamentals and steady estimates grow to 22% of net worth.
  • Limit order. An order to buy at your price or lower, or to sell at your price or higher.
  • Lockstep. Two holdings whose daily returns have a correlation of 0.65 or more, so they act as one bet.
  • Pilot (starter). A first position at half the standard size, used in a Downtrend.
  • Promoter pledge. Shares that the controlling shareholders have pledged to lenders as security.
  • Risk-Unit Sizing. Weight equals the risk budget in basis points divided by the stop distance, capped at the tier maximum.
  • RSI (relative strength index). A speedometer from 0 to 100; under 30 means a fast fall, over 70 a fast rise.
  • Stage. One of four phases of a share's life: 1 base, 2 climb, 3 top, 4 decline.
  • Tactical Momentum. The bucket for shorter-term trend positions, target 50 to 65% of net worth.
  • Undercut-and-reclaim. A stop broken on an earlier close and won back on today's close is forgiven as a shakeout.
  • WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT. A pipeline label for a name that passes its safety tests but is not at a buyable price; used for holdings as a hold-and-add-none label.

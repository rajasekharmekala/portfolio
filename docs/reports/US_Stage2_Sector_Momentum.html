What would change the market call

The current stance stays selective until the benchmark and breadth improve together. A single S&P close above its 50-day average would be a useful first signal, but it would not automatically reverse the Correction regime because participation is only slightly above half the pool and the 200-day breadth is similarly mixed. A stronger repair would include more industries above both the 50-day and 200-day averages and fewer leaders whose returns are concentrated in a small number of outliers. Conversely, a break below the 200-day average would turn a normal correction into a more serious market-health warning and should reduce the willingness to add high-beta breakouts.

The candidate plan also depends on price behavior. A pullback is constructive only if sellers fail to push through the stated invalidation level and the name stabilizes around support. A gap lower through the stop does not qualify as a cheaper entry. A breakout that keeps rising without offering a disciplined entry is simply missed; no catalyst forces a purchase. Watchlist additions are therefore conditional: updated filings and earnings guidance must continue to support the thesis, and the trend setup must still exist when the quote is refreshed.

Market repair trigger: If the S&P 500 closes above its 7,697.08 50-day average and broad participation improves, then rerun the correction call; until then, keep new exposure selective.

Market failure trigger: If the S&P 500 closes below its 7,309.76 200-day average, then treat the market regime as materially weaker and tighten the bar for momentum additions.

Data-quality exceptions and limitations

The Stage 2 scanner output is an incomplete extraction because it does not contain sector_chart_complete or stage2_universe_complete flags. The 23 outperformance bars and 145-industry matrix are shown at their returned scope, but completeness cannot be certified; the 102 breakout rows should not be presented as the whole universe. The dashboard’s market-cap selector visibly read $1.00B on the accepted pass; the initial pass with a zero-dollar threshold was discarded. The return matrix includes unusually large observations, such as 569.24% for Food Distribution over one week and 805.06% for Textile Manufacturing over one month. They are retained without an interpretation pending verification of the underlying component series and aggregation.

The TradingView/Finviz capture returned a live Monday price and technical fields, while its Finviz card supplied only a URL and no parsed valuation values. The report therefore uses the available TradingView trailing P/E, ROE and growth fields and labels them accordingly; unavailable Finviz fields are not silently filled. A small number of candidates are absent from the Friday S&P 1500 market-facts ledger, so their close and stage remain unreported. One market-facts input, ACA, is flagged stale relative to the selected session; it is not used as a candidate or as evidence for this sector decision. The watchlist scan pruned zero names.

Issuer research comes from recent Q3 2026 release pages. OSW and BTSG are syndications of company press releases hosted by Morningstar; DFIN’s issuer release is hosted by PR Newswire; the Nasdaq pages carry issuer or GlobeNewswire releases. These sources support the exact quoted figures, but a company’s adjusted measures are not interchangeable with GAAP. The report keeps them labeled as adjusted or non-GAAP and does not convert a single quarter into a forward estimate. The verified market-session cutoff is Friday 30 October; current technical levels must be refreshed for the next trading session. The dashboard can help make a list. It cannot make a trade. Verify each claim at its source and keep the price date in view.

The configured reviewer model, ao-claude-lc/claude-sonnet-5-5, was not available in this agent runner. The review therefore uses GPT-6.1-Sol, an available independent reviewer, under the required fallback. This model substitution is recorded in the run manifest.

Method and source notes

The latest completed session was resolved by verifying the S&P 500 close, then building the US facts ledger for 30 October 2026. It is separate from the local run date and from the live technical capture. TheWrap reported 30 October as its dashboard date and was independently checked through the US dashboard. The scanner’s visible $1B minimum was verified after a browser-native input update; the discarded $0B pass was not used. The completed extraction was copied into the private run cache with its retrieval timestamp and hash, and the tracked live dashboard file was restored so the dated snapshot remains the auditable input.

The Industry breadth table reproduces the extracted return, correlation, 52-week high/low and moving-average participation fields for all 145 returned rows. The separate 23-row outperformance list is left separate because the dashboard did not provide a full joined taxonomy. Company snapshots were captured after the Monday open and carry a precise UTC timestamp. Where the Friday ledger has a matching instrument, the report shows its date and stage separately; there is no attempt to reconcile differences by replacing one source’s close with another source’s quote.

Company fundamentals were researched for nine candidate setups. Found records in the run-specific research file connect each published number to a fetched page, verbatim quote, retrieval time and as-of date; the private fetch cache stores the source text. Every case note is a found symbol in that research file and has a matching TradingView/TheWrap snapshot. Grid Dynamics also maintains a quarterly financial-report archive for its Q3 materials. The automatic bibliography includes the source pages. The layout and modern report checks are applied after assembling the body, so presentation does not alter the underlying data, sources or matrix rows.

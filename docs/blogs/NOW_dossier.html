ServiceNow sells software that records, routes, governs, and measures work inside large organizations. A bank, hospital, or government department can use one platform for service requests, incidents, changes, security response, employee cases, and customer cases. The everyday comparison is a shared operating desk: once many teams put their rules and records there, replacing it is costly because the same data and controls connect the organization.

The verified close is $139.75. The price is above the 50-day average of $131.61 and the 200-day average of $115.41; RSI is 56.36 and the ledger labels the chart Stage 2. The US hard floors require ROIC at least 12%, free-cash-flow margin at least 12%, net debt to EBITDA below 3.0x, and stock-based compensation below 5% of revenue. NOW clears the first three on the working record but fails the fourth.

Decision: a strong business that fails the admission rule

The decision is REJECTED, because the Layer 1 hard gate treats stock-based compensation above 5% of revenue as disqualifying. ServiceNow has a valuable recurring software franchise and strong Q2 2026 operating figures, but dilution is a capital-allocation issue, not a product-quality opinion. Reconsideration requires a period-matched filing below 5% without weakening cash generation.[5][6][7]

The chart is constructive: $139.75 is 6.19% above the 50-DMA and 21.09% above the 200-DMA, RSI 56.36 is neutral, and Stage 2 means the trend is rising. A weekly close below $115.41 would invalidate that evidence. A rally alone improves timing, not eligibility.[28][29][30]

Business model, platform architecture, and moat

ServiceNow began in IT service management and expanded into a system of action. IT teams manage incidents, problems, changes, assets, and operations; security teams route vulnerabilities and response; HR and customer-service teams use the same governed workflow. The advantage is the shared data model, permissions, audit trail, and administrator knowledge connecting departments.[11][12][13][14][15]

The platform resembles a railway network. Applications are routes and the common data layer is track. New routes become easier to sell when the track already exists, and a customer with many routes has a reason to buy more rather than rebuild elsewhere. That makes renewal quality, cross-product adoption, and large-account expansion more important than a single quarter of seat growth.[5][8][9]

AI products extend the same architecture. Now Assist, Otto, AI Control Tower, Context Engine, and Action Fabric are positioned as governed ways for agents to understand requests and act across systems. The economic proof is paid, renewed ACV and cash generation, not the number of launches. If agents bypass the governed workflow, the moat weakens; if they increase work through it, the platform expands.[17][18][19][20][21]

Customers, contracts, and renewal mechanics

Q2 2026 disclosed 123 transactions above $1 million of net new ACV and 658 customers above $5 million ACV. Current RPO was $13.20B and total RPO $29.0B. These figures provide visibility because work is contracted, but they do not remove implementation, renewal, pricing, or concentration risk. RPO is evidence of demand, not a substitute for cash or per-share value.[5]

The cleanest renewal engine shows subscription growth, RPO growth, large-customer expansion, and cash conversion together. It also shows SBC falling as a share of revenue so the existing shareholder receives more of the economics. The next filing is the information event that can change this gate.[6][7]

Financial record and owner economics

Q2 2026 reported subscription revenue of $3.877B, up 24.5% year over year, and total revenue of $3.987B, up 24%. cRPO grew 21% and total RPO reached $29.0B. Operating margin was 29.5%, above guidance, and full-year subscription guidance was raised to $15.755–$15.770B. These are strong growth and execution signals.[5]

A useful financial reading separates subscription growth, margin conversion, and per-share economics. Revenue shows demand, operating margin shows scale, free cash flow shows cash conversion, and diluted shares show how much of that cash belongs to each share. Profit can rise while per-share value lags if awards grow faster than repurchases.[2][3][4][7]

Three-layer underwriting

Layer 1 is binary and prevents a compelling story from skipping minimum financial and technical standards. NOW passes ROIC, FCF margin, net leverage, and trend posture on the working record, but fails SBC and asymmetry. The constitution therefore rejects the candidate, even though the operating engine deserves monitoring.[1][2][28][29]

Three fatal flaw scenarios

AI disintermediation without paid conversion

Customers may use general-purpose agents outside ServiceNow, reducing the value of the governed workflow layer. The warning is subscription growth below 20%, cRPO below 15%, and AI ACV that grows in announcements but not renewals. The thesis fails when agents bypass the system of record rather than expand it.[5][17][18]

Dilution remains structurally high

SBC above 5% while buybacks offset only part of issuance means revenue and operating income can rise without comparable per-share progress. Three consecutive quarters above the ceiling, with diluted shares flat or rising, would confirm a structural owner-economics problem. Lower SBC reopens review; higher SBC keeps NOW out.[2][3][4]

Enterprise budget and execution shock

Large customers can delay projects while ServiceNow still carries sales and research costs. Two quarters of margin contraction with cRPO below 15% growth would show that scale economics are reversing, even if the price remains above its 200-DMA.[5][6][7]

Red-team conclusion

The strongest honest bear case says NOW is a high-quality platform priced and compensated as though AI monetization and scale are already durable. The hinge claim is owner economics: can subscription growth stay above 20% while SBC falls below 5% of revenue? Until a filing answers yes, rejection is safer.

Catalysts, invalidation, and execution playbook

The primary catalyst is paid AI adoption lifting subscription growth and large-customer expansion while SBC falls. Q2 supplied demand evidence: more than $1B of ServiceNow AI ACV, 123 large net-new ACV transactions, and 658 customers above $5M. The next earnings release and annual filing must show whether those indicators become durable revenue, cash, and per-share value.[5][7][10]

What this means for an owner

ServiceNow is worth monitoring because contract visibility, workflow expansion, and AI distribution are strong. It is not admitted because the present evidence gives too little protection from dilution and too little reward relative to a support-anchored stop. Re-check the next filing and the $131.61/$115.41 levels.

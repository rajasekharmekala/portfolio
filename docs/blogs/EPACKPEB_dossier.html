1. What the company does

EPACK makes pre-engineered steel buildings, usually called PEBs. A PEB is a steel frame that is cut and drilled in a factory, shipped flat, and bolted together on site. That method is quicker than building with concrete, which is why warehouses and factories often use it. The company describes itself as a turnkey provider, which means it designs, makes, delivers and erects the building. It also makes sandwich panels, the insulated wall and roof sheets that go on the frame. The company was incorporated in February 1999 and is described as one of India's leading players in the PEB and prefabricated construction industry. [1]

The shares were listed on 1 October 2025. [10] That means the stock has about one year of trading history, which matters for the valuation tests in chapter 3. A three-year record does not yet exist.

How the three layers work

Every candidate passes three layers in order. Layer 1 is a set of hard gates, each a pass or fail question with no room for judgement. Layer 2 is a scorecard of three to five operating numbers that matter for this type of business. Layer 3 is timing, which asks how far the price sits above a verified support level. The distance is measured in ATR, or average true range, the typical daily price swing over the last 14 sessions. Dividing a distance by ATR tells you how many normal days of movement separate the price from its support.

The verdict words

For a holding, the verdict is one of four words. KEEP_COMPOUNDING means the position stays at its current size. ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP means adding on a pullback to support. TRIM_PROFIT_BOOKING means selling 30 to 50 percent to lock in gains because the stock is stretched. REPLACE_UNDERPERFORMER means the thesis or the trend has broken, and the capital should move elsewhere. Each word has a rule that triggers it, and this dossier names that rule. Provisional means the verdict rests on today's evidence and will be read again when a gate that cannot yet be tested becomes testable.

Three rules that shape the numbers

The stop is set by a formula. For a Core Alpha name it is the lowest of the recent swing low and the 50-day and 200-day averages, minus one ATR. The asymmetry ratio compares the distance to a target with the distance to the stop. A Core name needs at least 3.0 to 1. The target itself must be anchored. The approved anchors are forward earnings per share multiplied by a three-year median price-to-earnings ratio, or a measured move on a documented base. A price-to-earnings ratio (P/E) is the share price divided by earnings per share, so a P/E of 20 means buyers pay 20 rupees for each rupee of yearly profit. A measured move adds the height of a base to the level at which the price breaks out of it. A broker's target that is not built this way does not count. The last rule is the 60-day rule: every number should be no older than 60 days. Annual and quarterly figures often are older, so this dossier lists each one in the run notes.

What the stage words mean

Stage 2 is a sustained uptrend, with the price above rising long-term averages. Stage 3 is the top or pullback phase, where the uptrend stalls and the price drifts sideways or down. Stage 4 is a breakdown, usually below a falling long-term average. The ledger reads Stage 3 for this stock. That is the reason the technical gate fails. It is not a Stage 4 breakdown, so the structural exit rule has not fired.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

A business is strong when it earns good returns on the money invested in it, turns those earnings into cash, and does not need heavy borrowing to grow. It is weak when one of those three is missing. EPACK passes the first two tests on the evidence in hand. The third test is comfortable, but the company has also been stretching its working capital, and margins have come under pressure this year.

The keep arithmetic

The keep decision compares three outcomes for the 1,050 shares. All figures below are the ones already shown in this dossier, and each product or difference is shown with its working. Exiting now at the close locks in the market value of ₹2,66,427 (1,050 × ₹253.74). Against the cost of ₹2,39,202.07, that is the unrealised gain of ₹27,224.93 (2,66,427 − 2,39,202.07). Holding until the formula stop is hit, and then exiting there, gives 1,050 × ₹201.04 = ₹2,11,092, which is ₹28,110.07 below cost. The difference between exiting now and being stopped out is 2,66,427 − 2,11,092 = ₹55,335, the same figure as 1,050 × ₹52.70 (the per-share risk to the stop). That is the cost of holding through the stop rule. Keeping the position is justified only if the expected gain from holding is larger than that cost. On the analyst-average target of ₹297.50 [23], the gain from today's close is 1,050 × (297.50 − 253.74) = ₹45,948, which is less than the ₹55,335 at risk, so the reward-to-risk ratio is 45,948 ÷ 55,335 = 0.83. That is the figure in the asymmetry gate. It is an assumption about the analyst view, not an approved anchor. The keep case therefore does not rest on reward-to-risk. It rests on the rule that no exit trigger has fired, and on the stop bounding the loss at ₹55,335. The price at which the reward would reach the 3.0 floor is ₹411.84, which is 1,050 × (411.84 − 253.74) = ₹1,66,005 of gain from today's close.

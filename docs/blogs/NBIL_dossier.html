1 Product Architecture & Single-Stock Leverage Mechanics

The GraniteShares 2x Long NBIS Daily ETF (NASDAQ: NBIL) is a leveraged single-stock exchange-traded fund issued by the GraniteShares ETF Trust[1]. The fund seeks daily investment results, before fees and expenses, of 200% (2x) the daily percentage price return of the Class A ordinary shares of Nebius Group N.V. (NASDAQ: NBIS)[2]. The fund obtains its leveraged exposure through physical share holdings, equity total return swaps, and forward contracts structured with major institutional counterparties[3].

Because NBIL resets its 200% leverage exposure at the close of every trading day, its long-term return diverges from twice the cumulative return of Nebius Group shares over multi-week or multi-month holding periods[4]. Given that Nebius Group exhibits extreme annualized realized volatility exceeding 75–85%, the mathematical compounding decay on NBIL is severe, imposing a drag of 1.5% to 3.0% per month during sideways or volatile consolidation periods[5].

NBIL was launched in October 2025 to satisfy speculative retail and tactical hedge fund demand for amplified exposure to European AI cloud infrastructure expansion[6]. The fund carries a management expense ratio of 1.15% plus swap financing costs, resulting in an all-in holding fee of approximately 1.40% per annum[7].

2 Underlying Corporate Analysis: Nebius Group N.V. (NBIS)

Nebius Group N.V. is an Amsterdam-headquartered artificial intelligence infrastructure company formed following the landmark corporate restructuring and full divestment of Yandex N.V.'s Russian operating assets in July 2024 for $5.4 billion in cash and shares[8]. Led by founder and serial technology entrepreneur Arkady Volozh, Nebius retained full ownership of core Western technology assets, intellectual property, international patents, and substantial cash reserves to build a vertically integrated AI hyperscale cloud provider[9].

Nebius operates specialized AI cloud infrastructure engineered from silicon to application programming interfaces (APIs), offering large language model (LLM) developers and enterprise AI startups high-performance GPU compute clusters, proprietary network fabrics, and specialized cloud software (Nebius AI Studio)[10]. The company's primary flagship data center in Mäntsälä, Finland, utilizes highly energy-efficient direct liquid cooling with excess heat channeled into local district heating grids, operating with an industry-leading Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) below 1.10[11].

In addition to its core GPU cloud business, Nebius operates three auxiliary technology business units: Toloka AI (data generation and RLHF annotation for AI foundation models), TripleTen (an international edtech coding academy), and Avride (an autonomous driving technology company developing level 4 robotaxis and sidewalk delivery robots)[12].

3 AI Infrastructure Buildout, GPU Clusters & Power Contracts

Nebius has secured Preferred Partner status within NVIDIA's Cloud Partner (NCP) program, enabling priority allocation of next-generation NVIDIA H100, H200, and Blackwell GB200 NVL72 compute clusters[13]. The company has aggressively contracted electrical power capacity across Europe and North America, expanding its active grid capacity from 35 MW to over 140 MW contracted, with a medium-term pipeline exceeding 500 MW across Finland, France (Paris DC), and the United States[14].

Revenue growth has been explosive: quarterly revenues expanded from $50.9 million in Q1 2025 to $399.0 million in Q1 2026 (+684% YoY) and reached $582.3 million in Q2 2026, bringing annualized run-rate revenue (ARR) toward the $2.0 billion threshold[15]. However, this hyper-growth requires astronomical capital expenditures on GPU servers and data center shell buildouts, consuming over $1.2 billion in cash outlays in 2025 alone[16].

6 Technical Posture & Volatility Decay Profile

Underlying NBIS common stock trades at $29.80, exhibiting Stage 2 momentum above its 50-day moving average ($27.40) and 200-day moving average ($22.10)[19]. However, NBIS has a high 30-day realized volatility of 78.5% and an ATR of $2.40 (8.05% daily range)[20].

For the 2x leveraged ETF NBIL (trading at $25.20 with an ATR of $3.60, or 14.3% daily swing), this extreme underlying volatility induces severe mathematical path degradation[21]. Any two-week consolidation or 10% pullback in NBIS causes a ~22–25% drawdown in NBIL that requires a disproportionately massive recovery move to break even[22].

7 Fatal Flaw & Hyperscaler Disintermediation Risks

Fatal Flaw 1: Hyperscaler GPU Cloud Price War & Margin Compression
As Microsoft Azure, AWS, and Google Cloud deploy millions of internal custom AI ASICs (Maia, Trainium, TPU v5/v6) alongside Blackwell clusters, commodity GPU compute rental rates are projected to compress by 30–45% by 2027, undermining Nebius's gross margin trajectory[23].
Fatal Flaw 2: GPU Depreciation & Rapid Technological Obsolescence
AI accelerators experience brutal 3-year economic depreciation cycles. If next-generation Blackwell and Rubin architectures render H100 clusters economically uncompetitive, Nebius faces massive asset write-downs on its capitalized server fleet[24].
Fatal Flaw 3: Single-Stock 2X Leverage Bleed on High-Beta Speculation
The combination of negative GAAP earnings, massive cash burn, and high retail momentum creates intense two-way volatility that accelerates NBIL's daily compounding decay, making long-term exposure untenable[25].

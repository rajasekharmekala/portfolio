1. What the company does

TSMC is a foundry: a factory that makes chips for other companies instead of selling chips under its own name. A customer such as a phone maker or an AI-chip designer creates a chip design on a computer, and TSMC turns that design into thousands of finished chips on a polished silicon disc called a wafer. In 2025 the company ran 305 distinct process technologies and made 12,682 products for 534 customers.[1] Because the customers compete with each other, a neutral manufacturer that does not sell competing products is trusted by all of them. That trust is the core of the business.

The company earns money by selling wafers, and the price of a wafer depends on how small and advanced its circuits are. The size is described by a node name such as 3-nanometer or 2-nanometer, where a smaller number means more transistors in the same space and a more expensive wafer. In the third quarter of 2026, 2-nanometer wafers were 8% of wafer revenue, 3-nanometer 33%, 5-nanometer 28% and 7-nanometer 10%, so chips at 7-nanometer and smaller made up 79%.[1] Shipments were 4,476 thousand 12-inch-equivalent wafers, up 3.2% from the second quarter and 9.6% from a year earlier.[2] Since price per wafer rises with each new node, revenue grows faster than shipments.

Three ratios show how much of each dollar of sales the company keeps. Gross margin is revenue minus the direct cost of making the product, as a share of revenue. Operating margin is revenue minus all running costs, including research, as a share of revenue. Net margin is what is left after interest and tax. For the third quarter these were 66.4%, 58.7% and 51.1%.[1] The wafer-making rivals in the Chapter 3 peer matrix earned gross margins of 25% to 40% in the second quarter, which is why the gap matters.

The customers sit mostly in the United States, and a handful of them dominate. In 2025 the ten largest customers were about 78% of net revenue, the largest was 19% and the second largest 17%. North America took 75% of 2025 revenue, China 9% and Japan 4%.[17] High-performance computing, the platform that holds AI accelerators, was 68% of third-quarter revenue and rose 22% from the prior quarter, while smartphones were 21%.[25] Substantially all sales are priced in US dollars, but most costs are paid in New Taiwan dollars, so the exchange rate affects profit.[17]

Making advanced chips takes enormous spending. Capital expenditure is money spent on plants and equipment rather than on running costs. TSMC spent $40.9 billion in 2025 and now plans $60 billion to $64 billion for 2026.[28][25] Cash flow from operations is the cash the business itself generates before financing. Free cash flow, written FCF for short, is that cash flow from operations less capital expenditure, which makes it the cash an owner could take out without starving the business. Management says it has nearly 20 factories in progress worldwide, 13 of them leading-edge or advanced-packaging factories in Taiwan and five or six overseas.[25] The advanced packaging step, called CoWoS (chip-on-wafer-on-substrate), is the process that joins a processor and its memory into one module, and management says back-end capacity is short.[17][25]

Valuation and cash flow use US dollars, and each NT$ figure is converted with a stated rate.

Three exchange rates matter. The Federal Reserve's weekly table put one dollar at NT$32.54 on 2026-10-23, the last print before our price date.[24] TSMC's own planning rate for the fourth quarter is NT$31.8, and the third-quarter average was NT$31.98.[1][2] A dollar that buys more NT dollars lowers the dollar cost of Taiwan-based wages and plant costs, which helps margin if it lasts. 2. Why the business is strong or weak

The competitive position rests on three things: technology that is ahead, scale that others cannot match, and customers who plan years of capacity with the company. On technology, the 2-nanometer node entered high-volume production in the fourth quarter of 2025 with good yield and is already 8% of wafer revenue. The next node, called A14, enters risk production in 2027 and volume production in 2028, and management expects it to be a bigger node than 2-nanometer.[25] On packaging, the CEO said he does not believe anyone else can run a five-times-reticle-size package in real production today, and that CoWoS is still customers' first choice.[25] These are management statements, so we treat them as claims and check them against the margin evidence below.

The evidence for pricing power is in the margins. TSMC's gross margin was 59.9% for 2025 and 66.4% in the third quarter of 2026, even though the 2-nanometer ramp cost margin that quarter.[17][1] Peers that sell mostly older nodes earned far less in the second quarter: SMIC 25.3%, GlobalFoundries 28.3% and UMC 32.5%.[30][31][32] Management also says demand is above supply and is working to narrow the gap, and its pricing is described as strategic by segment, location and technology.[25] When a supplier can run full and still raise margin, customers have few other places to go.

Customers reinforce the moat from the demand side. NVIDIA, an AI-chip designer whose results act as a read-across for AI demand, reported revenue of $96.2 billion for the quarter ended 2026-07-26, up 106% from a year earlier, and guided the next quarter to $108.0 billion.[33] The CEO says all the AI players work with TSMC, and the 20-F shows that the ten largest TSMC customers took 78% of revenue.[25][17] Customers also gain from TSMC's design tools and from yield learning on each node, which a new foundry would have to rebuild. The credit agencies agree with the strength: Moody's raised TSMC to Aa2 on 2026-10-21, and S&P rates it AA- with a positive outlook.[22][21]

The weaknesses are real. First, production is concentrated in Taiwan, and the annual report lists geopolitical tension and utility shortages among the risks to operations.[17] Second, United States trade policy can change the economics: a 25% tariff on certain advanced computing chips took effect on 2026-01-15 with broad exemptions for US data-centre use, and a January 2026 agreement with Taiwan promised preferential treatment for firms that invest in US capacity.[23][17] Third, rivals are not standing still: Intel Foundry reported second-quarter revenue of $5.8 billion, up 31%, entered high-volume manufacturing for a subset of its Core Ultra Series 3 processors, and moved its 18A-P process into risk production.[29] Fourth, TSMC plans to adopt the newest lithography tool, called High NA EUV, only around 2030, later than some rivals, and the CEO argued this is a cost choice and not a disadvantage.[25]

Capacity and the technology roadmap

A foundry's moat is only as wide as its capacity to serve customers, so the build plan matters as much as the technology. In 2025 the company shipped about 15.0 million 12-inch-equivalent wafers, and its annual capacity exceeded 17 million wafers, about the same as the year before.[17] The addition is coming from several directions at once. A new 3-nanometer factory in Tainan starts volume production in the first half of 2027, and a further one follows in the first half of 2028. The second Arizona factory starts volume production in the third quarter of 2027, slightly ahead of plan, and the second Japanese factory moves to 3-nanometer in 2028.[25]

The board approved capital appropriations of about US$29,442.5 million at one meeting in August, mainly for advanced technology capacity, packaging and factory construction.[15] On the technology side, the company announced an initiative with ASML on large-format photomasks for High NA EUV, a lithography tool that it plans to adopt in 2030.[2][25] For an owner, the point is that capacity is being added in steps tied to customer commitments. That lowers the risk of building empty factories, although it does not remove it, and Chapter 6 asks what happens if the commitments are cut.

What a skeptical reader should check

The claim most worth testing is that TSMC can hold margin while it builds capacity at a record pace. This matters because margin is where the moat shows up in the accounts, and a moat that cannot hold its margin is not much of a moat. Management guides gross margin of 65% to 67% for the fourth quarter and says overseas factories will cost about 2 points of margin now and 3 to 4 points in later years.[1][25] If those costs arrive faster, margin falls toward the long-term target of 56% and above, which is still high but would be a lot lower than the 66.4% investors see today.[8] Chapter 2B scores this as one of five operating KPIs, and Chapter 6 uses it as one of three bear cases. A second claim to test is that demand stays above supply. The best check is whether customers keep paying for capacity ahead of need, and the monthly sales and company forecasts tell us that every few weeks.

Credit quality and who owns the shares

Lenders are a useful second opinion on strength. TSMC's own investor-relations page lists S&P at AA- with a positive outlook and Moody's at Aa2 with a stable outlook.[21] Moody's raised the rating from Aa3 on 2026-10-21 and said it expects revenue growth of 30% to 45% over the next 12 to 18 months and adjusted debt of about 0.3 times EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, a rough measure of operating cash profit).[22] Near-term further upgrades are unlikely, the agency added, because of capital intensity and regional concentration. These ratings matter to a shareholder because they show the company can borrow cheaply if it must fund the build-out.

On ownership, the National Development Fund of the R.O.C. held 6.38% of the shares on 2026-02-28 and has sold in the past, so its sales are a technical overhang for the ADR.[17] Management holds modest stakes: the CEO held 8,356,985 shares at the end of September.[14] Short interest is small, about 0.63% of shares, so a squeeze is not a driver of the price.[35]

The bear case assumes a pause in demand, not a blockade, and a gap down would pass through any stop. The weighted figure is the number that decides whether the stock is worth owning. It is respectable but not wide. That is the reason the verdict asks for a pullback entry and not a purchase at the market. Each scenario's probability is a judgment, and a shift of five points from base to bear would lower the weighted value to about $481.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate in Chapter 5 rests on a weakest assumption. For the earnings estimate it is that 2027 sales growth stays near 30%. For the reverse DCF it is that capital spending falls as a share of revenue after 2028. For the price target it is that the market keeps paying 24 times earnings for a business whose growth is slowing. A red-team reading looks for the way each one can fail, and the three cases below are the ones that move the price most.

Bear case 1: AI spending pauses and consumer demand stays weak

The business now depends on one end market, since high-performance computing was 68% of third-quarter revenue.[25] The CEO said consumer and price-sensitive segments are challenged by rising component prices and economic uncertainty, and that TSMC is being prudent in its 2027 planning.[25] If the large buyers of AI chips slow their own spending, the capacity TSMC is building would arrive just as demand flattens. In our bear case sales grow 10% in 2027 and operating margin falls to 50%, giving $16.12 per ADR and a price of $290 at 18 times, a loss of 33.6%. The first signal would be October sales under NT$540 billion on 2026-11-10 (Chapter 7). A second signal would be a customer cutting its own capital plans, which the customers' earnings reports in November would show. The weakest link in this case is its own probability. We give it 25%, because the stock has already absorbed a drop and because management reports no sign of slowdown.

Bear case 2: a Taiwan, tariff or export-control event

The 20-F names geopolitical tension, military conflict, export controls and tariffs among the risks, and 75% of revenue comes from North America while most output is in Taiwan.[17] A 25% tariff on certain advanced chips has applied since 2026-01-15 with wide exemptions, and the preferential treatment promised to Taiwan has not been fully clarified after a US court decision on earlier tariffs.[23][17] The probability is small but cannot be estimated, and the loss is a gap, not a slide, so a stop-loss order may not execute near its level. That is why the position size in Chapter 7 is set by the stop but also capped at 3.5% of the portfolio. The cap, not the stop, is the real protection here. A holder who wants more protection could hold fewer shares or hedge with options, which this report does not recommend.

Bear case 3: margins fall faster than guided and the multiple compresses

Management says overseas fabs cost about 2 points of gross margin now and 3 to 4 points later, and the 2-nanometer ramp costs another 3 to 4 points in the second half of 2026.[25][27] Capital spending also raises depreciation, which together were NT$688.1 billion in 2025 and rise with each new fab.[17] If margin disappoints while sales are on plan, earnings fall to about $19.2 per ADR, and the market may pay 20 times, giving about $384, a drop of 12.1%. Rival Intel could also keep more of its own products in-house, which would reduce the pool of foundry demand; the CEO said TSMC treats every customer fairly and does not comment on any one.[25][29] The warning sign is a gross-margin guide below 63% at the January report. The weakest link is the multiple: at 20 times the price is below the close even with sales on plan, so the stock needs 20.4 times just to stand still.

What we could not verify, and why it matters

Five gaps limit how far the numbers can be trusted. First, the 90-day change in consensus earnings is not published on any page we could reach, and the session ledger has no consensus block for TSMC, so we cannot say if analysts are raising or cutting numbers.[34] Second, the full September balance sheet had not been filed by our price date, so net cash is our estimate. Third, S&P's own rating page returned an access error, so its AA- positive reading comes from TSMC's investor-relations page. Fourth, Moody's upgrade is reported through a news site and not the agency's own page.[22][21]

Fifth, Intel's third-quarter date comes from a search result that we could not cache. None of these gaps changes the verdict by itself. The first one matters most, because rising estimates would support the 30% growth assumption and falling estimates would argue against it. We record it as not published and do not fill the cell with a guess. The next report should look for the figure again.

7. What we would do, and when

The verdict is ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP. The rule behind it is the asymmetry floor. TSMC passes every Layer 1 floor and scores 4.0 of 5.0 on Layer 2. It sits 0.21 of a daily swing above its 50-day average, which is the full-size band of the support ladder. But the reward-to-risk at the $436.87 close is 1.81. A Core Alpha name needs 2.0. Buying at the market would break our own rule. A buy order at $434.00 or lower meets the rule at 2.02. The gap between the two prices is only $2.87, or 0.7%, so the verdict is narrow. It is decided by the price target, not by the chart.

Several things changed since the 2026-10-23 cycle. That cycle said WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT with a $398.00 to $410.00 limit zone and a $359.92 stop. We treat those as prior-cycle records and do not reuse them. The close was then $418.51 and is now $436.87. The chart moved from a pullback, where all three tests failed, to Stage 2 with Test A passing. A stock in an uptrend is stopped under its recent swing low, not under a distant base. So the stop is now $394.34. The risk per share is smaller, and the zone moves up to $421.50 to $434.00. The target moved from the prior $545 to $514 because we rebuilt the earnings estimate and used a lower multiple. That is a more cautious view, not a new fact.

The stop sets the position size. At the $434.00 top of the zone the stop is 9.14% away. We rate this name TIER_3, a conviction rating that sets a size cap and a loss budget (the tiers are explained in the last paragraph of this chapter). The TIER_3 loss budget is 50 basis points of portfolio value, and dividing 0.50% by 9.14% gives 5.47%. The TIER_3 cap of 3.5% is lower, so the cap decides. We chose TIER_3 and not TIER_2 for three reasons. The reward-to-risk sits right at the floor. The multiple is full. A Taiwan shock cannot be stopped.

The order is one ladder with two buy limits. Half the position goes in at $434.00 or lower. The other half goes in at $425.00 or lower. Both are good until the 2026-11-10 sales release. The orders are cancelled in only three cases: October sales come in below NT$540 billion, the stock closes above $460 before the first fill, or a gap opens below $394.34 before a fill. There is no other downside cancel, because a fall through the limits is what fills them. After a fill, the levels are marks for the position and not new orders. A close below $421.50, the 50-day average minus one daily swing, means no more adds. A close below $407.13 puts the chart back in last cycle's pullback state and calls for a review. The only stop is $394.34. For the portfolio, compare TSMC with the weakest Core Alpha name. TSMC offers a Layer 2 score of 4.0 of 5.0, a distance from support of 0.21 swings (0.89 last cycle) and a reward-to-risk of 2.02 at the zone top (2.70 last cycle on a wider stop). The orchestrator decides any displacement.

Layer 3 is the set of rules for how and where to buy and how much. Price decides this verdict, so these rules matter most here. A conviction tier is our rating of how sure we are. It sets the largest share of the portfolio the name may take and the loss we will risk if the stop is hit. A basis point is 0.01%, so 50 basis points is 0.50% of portfolio value. In the scoring rules, TIER_3 allows 2% to 3.5% of the portfolio with a 50 basis point loss budget, and TIER_2 allows 4% to 6.5% with 75 basis points. The single-name limit is 15%. The table below shows the numbers: the zone, the stop, the targets and the size. Its formula line shows the TIER_3 range of 2% to 3.5%, and the cap that binds is the top of that range. Let winners run is the rule for a stock we own that keeps rising. We do not sell just because the relative strength index passes 70 or the weight passes 15%. Citation gate (scripts/citation_check.py, run with --allow-stale-asof). Every value in the research record was found in its cached page and the 25-source floor was cleared with 37 numbered sources. Why it cannot be cured now. These records describe periods that have closed, and no newer version exists. The next annual report is not due until about April 2027. How it lowers confidence. The effect is moderate. The five-year history and the margin gap to peers change slowly and are unlikely to move the verdict. The inputs that matter most for the entry price, which are the third-quarter results, the fourth-quarter guidance and the session ledger, are fresh.

What cures it and when. TSMC's full third-quarter financial statements should replace the estimated debt figure when they are filed, which we expect in mid-November. Peer third-quarter releases arrive from 2026-10-29 through November, and the 2026 annual report follows in about April 2027. The next run should refresh these records and drop the exception.

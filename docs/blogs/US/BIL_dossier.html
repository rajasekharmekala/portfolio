Before you read

BIL is an exchange-traded fund that holds short U.S. Treasury bills and seeks to track a one-to-three-month Treasury-bill index.[1] The decision is REJECTED for a new shortlist allocation because both momentum tests fail and no anchored reward-to-risk target is verifiable. The fund may still be useful for cash management, but that purpose is different from a shortlist purchase. The conclusion would change after a confirmed base breakout, improving relative strength, and a support-aware entry plan.

What the company does

BIL is a pooled portfolio. Investors buy shares on NYSE Arca; the fund, in turn, holds Treasury bills and passes through the bills’ income after expenses. A Treasury bill is a short-term U.S. government obligation issued at a discount or with a low coupon and redeemed at par. The benchmark includes fixed-rate, dollar-denominated, investment-grade Treasury bills with one to three months remaining to maturity.[10] [11] [12]

The fund does not promise a fixed share price, a fixed distribution or a fixed yield. The NAV is the value of assets less liabilities divided by shares outstanding, while the exchange price is set by buyers and sellers. Creation and redemption by authorized participants help align the two, but the shares can trade above or below NAV in stressed markets.[13] [14]

The economic engine is maturity turnover. Bills mature quickly, so proceeds are reinvested at the then-current bill rate. When policy rates fall, income on new purchases usually falls after a short lag; when rates rise, income can reset higher. That makes BIL a rate-sensitive cash-management tool, not a growth asset. Its short maturity limits price sensitivity, but it does not remove reinvestment risk, inflation risk or liquidity risk.[15] [16] [17]

Glossary. NAV is the market value of an ETF’s assets minus liabilities divided by shares. A 50-day moving average is the average closing price over the latest 50 sessions. A 200-day moving average is the corresponding long trend line. RSI, or relative strength index, is a bounded measure of recent upward versus downward price movement. ATR, or average true range, estimates typical daily movement. Asymmetry means potential reward compared with the loss allowed by a stop. A limit order specifies the highest purchase price an investor will accept. A stop loss is a pre-set exit level used to cap a trading loss. FCF, or free cash flow, is cash left after capital spending; it is not a meaningful fund KPI here because BIL is not an operating issuer.

Why the business is strong or weak

The fund’s strength is transparency and short duration. The portfolio is designed around a narrow maturity window, and the index methodology is explicit about security type, currency, rating and remaining maturity.[18] [19] This reduces the duration shock that affects longer bond funds. It does not mean the fund is risk-free: a share can move away from NAV, and an investor can lose purchasing power if inflation exceeds the distribution.

What could happen next

The scenarios are about total return and reinvestment income, not business earnings. A precise twelve-month price target is not supportable: BIL is designed to stay near its NAV while its distributions change with short-term bill rates. The probabilities below are judgmental, not a forecast guarantee.

Bull case

The number

1.5% to 4.5% total return over 12 months; probability 25%.

How it was calculated

We use a range, not one target. Bill income, fees and the exchange price all affect return.

The inputs

Inputs are the 0.1353% fee, the 2.75% index result and the 1–3 month mandate. The market close was $91.51 and NAV was $91.463563.[31] [32]

The range

The endpoints allow for rate change and a brief premium or discount. Future distributions are not published in the supplied evidence.

If the number is off

If rates fall fast, income falls. If price leaves NAV, execution risk rises.

Base case

The number

1.0% to 3.5% total return over 12 months; probability 50%.

How it was calculated

The base range uses the recent index result as a guide. It then allows for fees and a small price gap to NAV.

The inputs

For the base case, we use the same fee and maturity inputs. We reduce the income range because rates may fall.[31] [32]

The range

The base range is wide because the next bill rate is unknown. The price may also differ from NAV.

If the number is off

If the rate path is wrong, the base return is wrong. Check the next issuer update.

Bear case

The number

−1.0% to 1.0% total return over 12 months; probability 25%.

How it was calculated

The bear range allows for lower bill income and a short-lived price gap. It is not a prediction of Treasury default.

The inputs

For the bear case, the fee remains known. Future bill income and any price gap are not known.[31] [32]

The range

The bear range covers a fall in bill rates and a brief market discount. It does not assume a Treasury default.

If the number is off

If the discount lasts, the result is worse. If it closes, the loss is smaller.

The expected outcome is therefore a low-volatility income profile, not a capital-growth thesis. The main catalyst is a confirmed technical base plus stable or rising short-bill rates. The main fatal flaw is confusing a stable-looking chart with a guaranteed return.

Why these estimates might be wrong

The weakest assumption in the bull case is that short rates remain high enough to replenish distributions. A Treasury policy change or recession could reduce bill yields before the investor earns the expected income. We would know at the next issuer performance update and the next Treasury bill-rate release.[33]

The weakest assumption in the base case is that the exchange price stays close to NAV. A disruption in authorized-participant activity or a broad liquidity shock could widen the spread. We would know through the next NAV calculation, the exchange quote and the issuer’s market-price disclosure.

The weakest assumption in the bear case is that the temporary discount persists long enough to matter. If it closes quickly, the loss range overstates realized damage; if it coincides with falling income, it understates the total opportunity cost. The evidence date for each assumption is the next completed exchange session, not an intraday quote.

Fatal-flaw scenarios: (1) the fund’s mandate changes materially; (2) tracking error becomes persistent and unexplained; (3) liquidity becomes impaired so shares cannot be bought or sold near NAV; (4) a tax or account-specific rule makes the distribution unattractive; or (5) an investor uses BIL as a substitute for a guaranteed bank deposit. The supplied evidence does not verify any of these today; each is a monitoring item, not an asserted event.

Decision: REJECTED for the watchlist-to-shortlist workflow. The rule is deterministic: a pipeline challenger must pass a momentum test, meet the hard underwriting gates, and have a support-aware posture. BIL fails both momentum tests and has no verifiable anchored reward-to-risk target. It receives no new allocation, no shortlist qualification and no displacement recommendation.

Entry zone: N/A today. A future review would require a confirmed base breakout or a retest that holds, with price back within the support-distance ladder and positive relative strength. Stop: N/A for a rejected pipeline candidate; the ledger’s 63-day base low is $91.39 and is an invalidation reference, not an approved trade stop. Targets: N/A because a near-cash ETF does not provide a reliable price-target framework from the supplied evidence.

Trigger to reconsider: price must close above the base pivot with volume expansion, Test A or Test B must pass, relative strength must turn positive, and the issuer must continue to show transparent index tracking and liquidity. A close below $91.39, a persistent discount to NAV, or unexplained tracking deterioration keeps the verdict rejected.

Trigger that changes the income view: a verified change in the 1–3 month Treasury-bill rate curve or an issuer distribution notice. Do not infer a future yield from the prior distribution or from the 9 October market price alone.

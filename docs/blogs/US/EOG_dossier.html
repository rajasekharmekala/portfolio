1. What the company does

EOG finds, drills and sells crude oil, natural gas liquids (NGLs) and natural gas. It earns the market price of each product, adjusted for where and what it sells. In the second quarter of 2026 its US oil sold for $98.18 a barrel against a WTI benchmark average of $92.85, which is a premium of $5.33, and its US gas sold for $0.12 under the Henry Hub benchmark.[1][2] WTI is the US crude oil price that traders quote, and Henry Hub is the matching natural gas price. EOG does not choose those prices, so the company's profit moves with them every quarter.

The company is large. In 2025 it produced 1,232.2 thousand barrels of oil equivalent per day, made up of 521.9 thousand barrels of oil, 288.2 thousand barrels of NGLs and 2,533 million cubic feet of gas.[1] A barrel of oil equivalent (Boe) counts six thousand cubic feet of gas as one barrel, so it lets a reader add gas to oil. At December 31, 2025, proved reserves were 5,514 million Boe, and 99 percent of them are in the United States.[4] Proved reserves are the oil and gas that engineers are reasonably certain can be produced at today's prices and costs.

The wells sit in a handful of basins. The 10-K lists about 395,000 net acres in the Delaware Basin of Texas and New Mexico, 565,000 in the Eagle Ford oil play, 160,000 in the Dorado dry-gas play and 1,100,000 in the Utica, with 585 net wells planned across the United States in 2026.[4] The Utica position doubled in size on August 1, 2025, when EOG closed its purchase of Encino Acquisition Partners, which added 675,000 core net acres.[4][13] Outside the United States, EOG produces gas and oil offshore Trinidad, started oil production in the United Arab Emirates in June 2026, and says its Bahrain operations have been intermittent because of the conflict.[3][18]

How the 2025 acquisition changed the product mix

On May 30, 2025, EOG agreed to buy Encino for $5.6 billion including Encino's net debt, to be paid with $3.5 billion of new debt and $2.1 billion of cash, and it said the deal would add more than $150 million of savings in the first year.[12] The deal closed on August 1, 2025, and the cash outflow shown in the cash flow statement was $4,451 million after cash acquired.[13][4] Encino brought gas and NGL volumes with it. Between the second quarter of 2025 and the second quarter of 2026, EOG's gas volume rose from 2,229 to 3,089 million cubic feet per day, NGLs from 258.4 to 346.8 thousand barrels per day and oil from 504.2 to 548.8 thousand barrels per day.[1] By our calculation gas was 32.8 percent of volume in the second quarter of 2025 and 36.5 percent in the second quarter of 2026, while oil fell from 44.5 percent to 38.9 percent of volume. The company is still an oil business by revenue, but it is now more exposed to gas and NGL prices than it was a year ago.

Management said on the August 2026 call that it has already beaten the $150 million synergy target, ahead of schedule, with Utica well costs below $600 per foot and in-basin sand supply expected by the end of 2026.[18] Those statements are management's own and are not audited, so Chapter 2B tests them against reported costs.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

EOG's competitive position rests on three things: cost, balance sheet and the quality of its drilling inventory. Each has evidence behind it. On cost, EOG's cash operating costs per Boe cover lease and well, gathering and transport, and overhead. They were $10.14, $10.47, $10.33, $10.17 and $10.09 from 2021 to 2025. That is a band of less than 4 percent, while volumes rose 49 percent and the oil price ranged from $64.78 to $94.23.[1] In the second quarter of 2026 the figure was $10.57 per Boe. Revenue from product sales was $50.52 per Boe. That left a cash margin of $36.07 per Boe before depreciation.[2][1] That margin is why profit swings so sharply with the oil price. The flat cost line is why the company stays profitable when prices fall.

On the balance sheet, net debt was $3,019 million at June 30, 2026. EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) over the last four quarters was $14.8 billion by our calculation. The $3.0 billion revolving credit facility was undrawn.[1][3] EOG went from net cash of $980 million at June 30, 2025 to net debt of $4,164 million at September 30, 2025 because it paid for Encino. It has since cut net debt to $3,019 million.[15][1] When it announced the deal, EOG said it expected no material effect on its target of less than one times total debt to EBITDA at $45 WTI oil.[12] EOG's own sensitivity is $221 million of pretax cash flow per $1 of oil. On that basis we estimate total debt to EBITDA at $45 oil would be about 0.9 times. The target holds on our arithmetic, and Chapter 3 shows the working.

On inventory and returns, EOG's 2026 proxy statement reports a 57 percent direct after-tax rate of return and a 27.2 percent all-in rate of return on 2025 capital spending. Both are measured at $45 WTI and $2.50 Henry Hub. The company's own test classed 83 percent of wells drilled as premium.[8] On the August 2026 call, management added a new Austin Chalk area of 60,000 net acres, leased at an average of $1,200 per acre. It has paid back in less than one year at $65 WTI and holds about 125 two-mile locations.[18] These return figures are company-defined and not audited. We weigh them less than reported costs and cash flow.

Where the business is weak

EOG is a price taker. Its profit follows WTI. WTI in 2026 has been lifted by the conflict in the Middle East, which EOG's own filing names as the reason for higher oil prices than it expected at the start of the year.[3] According to management, the 2026 plan funds production growth and the dividend at a WTI price below $50. At today's strip, management says the plan produces $8 billion of free cash flow.[18] That is a large cushion. It also shows how much of this year's cash is a gift from the oil market and not from the company.

The record also has blemishes. EOG took $843 million of impairments in 2025. That included $646 million of write-downs in the fourth quarter on Barnett Shale and Woodford Oil Window assets. The company says they reflected play economics and its choice of where to spend.[4][1] General and administrative cost was $1.66 per Boe in the second quarter against a $1.50 guidance midpoint.[2] The chief financial officer, Ann Janssen, will leave on December 31, 2026. Jeff Hibbard, who joined in August 2025 from Morgan Stanley, will take over on January 1, 2027. The person who has to hold the 70 percent cash return promise changes in a quarter.[11] The street is also cautious on the stock. MarketBeat lists 17 hold ratings out of 28 and a consensus target of $161.24, below the $163.03 close.[23]

EOG's second-quarter free cash flow of $2.8 billion was 64 percent of its $4.4 billion of operating cash flow before working capital, which compares with about 58 to 70 percent at ConocoPhillips, Diamondback, Occidental and Devon by our arithmetic from their releases, and EOG carries less debt than Diamondback, Occidental or Devon (Chapter 3 shows the peer table).[2][25][26][28][27] EOG is smaller than ConocoPhillips by volume, close to Occidental and Devon, and larger than Diamondback. Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Management made specific promises in the last two years, and the record can be checked against reported figures. The largest promise is the cash return framework: since fiscal 2024, EOG has committed to return at least 70 percent of its annual operating cash flow before balance-sheet changes, less capital spending, through regular dividends, special dividends and buybacks.[3] In 2024 EOG paid $2,087 million of dividends and $3,246 million of buybacks, $5,333 million in all, against company-defined free cash flow of $5,367 million, or 99 percent. In 2025 it paid $2,161 million and $2,564 million, $4,725 million against $4,663 million, or 101 percent.[4][1] In the first half of 2026 it paid $1,084 million and $1,717 million, $2,801 million against $4,292 million, or 65 percent, so the company is behind the pace it needs for a full-year 70 percent unless the second half brings larger buybacks. The CFO restated the promise on the August call, and the board raised the buyback authorization from $10 billion to $20 billion effective May 20, 2026, with $11.7 billion left at June 30.[18][3]

The second test is the 2025 goals in the proxy statement. EOG aimed for $5.0 billion of free cash flow and delivered $4.7 billion, aimed for a 20 percent ROCE and delivered 18.7 percent, and aimed for controllable cash costs of $10.25 per Boe or less and delivered $10.09. The compensation committee itself labeled the first two 'slightly missed' and the cost goal 'exceeded'.[8] In the same year EOG guided 2025 capital spending of $6.0 to $6.4 billion in February 2025 and spent $6,294 million on the company's measure.[7][1] A company that grades its own misses in a public document is easier to trust than one that does not, though the grading is still the company's own.

The third test is Encino. In May 2025 EOG promised more than $150 million of first-year savings, a funding mix of $3.5 billion of debt and $2.1 billion of cash, and no material effect on its target of less than one times total debt to EBITDA at $45 oil.[12] By August 2026, management said it had beaten the $150 million target ahead of schedule, total debt was $7,926 million against $7,936 million at the end of 2025, and net debt had fallen to $3,019 million from $4,540 million.[18][1] The savings claim is unaudited, but the debt claim is not, and the debt is where the promise was made.

5. What could happen next

A reverse DCF (discounted cash flow) turns the usual valuation question around. Instead of forecasting cash flow and finding a price, it takes the price and asks what yearly growth in cash flow, for ten years, a 10 percent discount rate and an 18 times exit multiple would need to justify it. A low implied growth rate means the price asks for little, and a high one means the price already assumes a lot. The frozen ledger ran this test on EOG and printed +5.49 percent a year, but its cash-flow base of $4.479 billion does not match the filings, which show $13,358 million of operating cash flow and $6,744 million of capital spending over the last four quarters, or $6,614 million of free cash flow.[1] We replaced the ledger's base with the filing figures, used an enterprise value of $88.5 billion (524.5 million shares at $163.03 plus $3.0 billion of net debt) and got +0.30 percent a year.[3][1] The $4.45 billion Encino purchase is left out of the base because it was a one-time cash outflow, and its debt is already counted in the net debt that sits in the enterprise value.

Read plainly, the market is paying for flat cash flow at the recent oil price, and that oil price is high. The last four quarters averaged $72.29 WTI by our calculation, and the NYMEX WTI average was $72.17 in the first quarter of 2026, $92.85 in the second and $85.68 in the third.[2][9] At a mid-cycle $65 WTI our model gives free cash flow of $5.6 billion, and the same test then asks for 2.6 percent growth a year, which is a normal, undemanding figure for a company that guides to low single-digit oil growth.[18] So the price is neither a bargain nor a stretch on cash flow alone. It becomes a bargain only at a lower price, which is the case for waiting.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

This chapter makes the bear case against our own work. It takes each estimate in Chapter 5, names its weakest assumption, says what evidence would show it wrong and when that evidence arrives. The bear case, stated in one sentence, is that EOG's earnings are a function of an oil price that is being held up by a war, so the stock is priced for a state of the world that can end with a headline.

Fatal flaw 1: the conflict ends and oil gaps down

EOG's 10-Q says it has realized higher oil prices than it expected at the start of 2026 because of the conflict in the Middle East, and the NYMEX WTI average fell from $92.85 in the second quarter to $85.68 in the third while the Dec 26 contract closed at $103.27 on October 23.[3][9][10][21] A settlement could take $20 off the oil price in days, and on EOG's sensitivity $20 is about $6.50 of yearly EPS, or $69 a share at 10.68 times. A stop order does not cap that loss, because the stock can open below the stop and the order then fills at the next available price. Our bear value of $121.01 is below the $135.36 stop by $14.35, so a gap from the zone top to that value would cost $22.71 a share, or 15.8 percent.

Fatal flaw 2: gas, NGL and cost drag as the mix shifts

Gas is now 36.5 percent of volume and NGLs another 24.6 percent, and neither shares oil's rally. US gas realized $2.77 in the second quarter, $0.12 below Henry Hub, and the EIA expects Henry Hub to average $3.16 in 2027, 9 percent below 2026.[1][24] EOG's cost guidance is also not guaranteed. General and administrative cost was above its guidance midpoint in the second quarter, management says service costs show slight inflation, and gathering, processing and transport cost rose $221 million year on year to $676 million in the second quarter as Utica volumes grew.[2][18][3] If gas and cost drag are worth $1 a share of EPS more than we assume, the base value falls by about $11 at 10.68 times.

Fatal flaw 3: capital allocation errs at the top of the cycle

EOG repurchased shares at $125 in the first quarter and $135 in the second, and the 70 percent promise means it will keep buying at higher prices if cash flow stays high. A $20 billion authorization with $11.7 billion left is large relative to a market value of $85.5 billion.[14][2][3] EOG also bought Encino in 2025 for $5.6 billion, which turned its net cash of $980 million into net debt of $4,164 million within a quarter, and it has said it will make acquisitions that fit its strategy.[15][3] A second large purchase at a high oil price, or a CFO handover that loosens the cash return rules on January 1, 2027, would hurt the owner case even with oil at $80.

Several terms in the sizing table are easier to follow once defined. Layer 3 is the third test, which sets the entry posture, the position size and the exit rules. CMP stands for current market price, here the $163.03 close of 2026-10-23. A basis point (bps) is one hundredth of a percent, so 50 bps is 0.5 percent of net worth. Portfolio heat is the share of net worth that would be lost if every open position hit its stop at once, and the framework caps it at 12 percent. Risk-unit sizing sets a position by what a loss would cost and not by how much the buyer likes the idea: the size is the lower of the tier's maximum weight and the tier's risk budget divided by the distance to the stop. A tier is a conviction grade that fixes the maximum weight and the risk budget. TIER_1 allows 7 to 10 percent of net worth with a 100 bps budget, TIER_3 allows 2 to 3.5 percent with a 50 bps budget, and TIER_0 means zero size. We use TIER_3, the yardstick for this report, because the quality is high but the price asks for patience, the weighted reward-to-risk of 2.63 to 1 is only just above the floor, and an oil shock can hit the whole position at once.

The reason is that the TACTICAL_MOMENTUM rule places the stop below the lower of the swing low and the 20-day or 50-day average, minus half an ATR, which is $137.35 minus $1.99, or $135.36. We applied that tactical rule and not the generic Stage 2 wording that the table below prints, which uses a full ATR for core positions. The rest of the plan is simple. The let-winners-run rule governs what happens after a fill. The framework never trims a Stage 2 winner just because RSI is above 70 or the weight passes 15 percent, and it lets a winner grow to 22 percent of net worth while the stop trails. The trailing level is the rising 21-day EMA or 50-day SMA. An SMA is the plain average of the last N closing prices, and an EMA is the same average with more weight on recent days, so it turns sooner. For EOG we would raise the stop to the 50-day average once the price closes above the base target of $173.12, and we would hold for up to 12 months, to about October 2027, which matches the horizon of the base target. A TIER_3 position is unlikely to reach 15 percent, so the rule matters mostly as a promise not to sell a good trade early.

Structural invalidation is the price or event that shows the thesis wrong and not merely noisy. For price it is a trade at or below $135.36. For events it is any of these: a third-quarter report or call that cuts the 2026 volume or capital plan, a 2027 plan that spends well above the $6.5 billion of 2026, cash returns that stay below 70 percent of free cash flow without a stated reason, or a new large acquisition. The verdict would move to ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP if the price falls into the zone and a rerun of the momentum gate shows the close within 1.6 ATR of a rising support, and it would move to REJECTED only on a close below the stop or the exact Stage 4 condition.

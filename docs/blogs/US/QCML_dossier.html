1. What the company does

QCML is an exchange-traded fund run by GraniteShares Advisors LLC and listed on Nasdaq. Its goal is to deliver twice (200%) the daily percentage change of Qualcomm Incorporated common stock, before fees.[1][2] It does not own Qualcomm shares. It signs swap contracts, which are agreements with a bank to swap the return on a set dollar amount of Qualcomm stock for a financing payment, and it keeps cash and bills as collateral.[2] At June 30, 2026 it had two swap counterparties, Nomura Holdings and TD Cowen, with a combined notional amount of $279,805,991 against net assets of $136,171,312, which is 2.055 times net assets (our calculation).[4]

The fund resets every day. Each night it rebuilds exposure to 200% of its net asset value, which means a gain today raises tomorrow's exposure in dollars and a loss lowers it.[2] The result is that returns over weeks do not equal twice the stock's return. The prospectus says the fund can lose money over a year while the stock is flat, and that it could lose more than its net assets if the stock falls more than 50%.[2] The sponsor's own fact sheet calls the fund a short-term trading vehicle.[5] This report therefore does not treat QCML as a company. It covers the stock underneath, the trend, the cost and the decay.

The fund is young. It began operations on February 12, 2025 at a share value of $25.00, and the exchange data show its first trade on April 3, 2025, so there are only 19 months of trading history.[4][13] The sponsor's page lists February 13, 2025 as inception, and one data site lists October 24, 2025, which matches the prospectus date, not a launch.[1][18] We use the filed date. The fund had $3,180 thousand in net assets at June 30, 2025 and $136,171 thousand a year later, after $134.22 million of inflows in May 2026 alone.[4][18]

The business underneath is Qualcomm, which designs phone, car and device chips and licenses patents. In fiscal 2025 Apple, Samsung and Xiaomi each supplied 10% or more of its revenue, and the June 2026 quarterly report says Apple is expected to use its own modem more, which would hurt chip revenue.[24][27] Chapter 3 shows the stock's five-year numbers, because they set the value of the fund.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Daily reset means the fund rebuilds its 2x exposure at each close, so each day's gain or loss compounds into the next.
  • Swap means a contract with a bank that pays the fund the stock's return in exchange for a financing charge.
  • Net asset value (NAV) means the fund's assets minus its liabilities, shown per share; the market price can differ slightly.
  • Notional amount means the dollar size of stock exposure that a swap refers to; it is not cash paid.
  • Volatility drag means the loss a daily-reset fund suffers when the stock swings up and down, even if the stock ends flat.
  • Counterparty means the bank on the other side of a swap, which must pay when the stock rises.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

A leveraged fund has no brand, patents or customers, so its strength is how well it does one job: deliver twice the daily move at a fair price. On the job itself, QCML does well. Across 391 daily returns from April 2025 to October 2026, a straight-line fit of QCML's return on Qualcomm's return gives a slope of 2.0085 and a correlation of 0.9969 (our calculation).[13][14] Only 9 days missed twice the move by more than one percentage point, and 4 missed it by more than two.

The weakness is price, in two parts. First, the fund trails twice the stock by 5.27 percentage points a year on average, measured on price returns, or about 9.4 points once Qualcomm's dividends are counted (our calculation).[13][14] The sponsor charges 1.50% net, and the swaps carry spreads of 300 and 375 basis points over SOFR.[2][4] Second, volatility drag is far larger than the fee. Over the year to October 23, 2026 Qualcomm rose 3.46% and QCML fell 24.50%.[13][14]

The structure adds risks that a share of Qualcomm does not have. The swap partners can close a contract after a dramatic adverse move, and the prospectus warns this may stop the fund meeting its goal even if the stock later recovers.[2] On July 14, 2026, the same sponsor's 2x Long LCID fund ended with a negative net asset value after its stock fell more than 50% in a day.[9] QCML has not had that event, and Qualcomm's largest one-day fall in our window was 11.46%, but the mechanism is the same.

Strengths: the match, the swap partners and the sponsor's compliance

Three facts support the vehicle. The daily match is tight. Two named banks share the swaps, with TD Cowen valued at 108.90% and Nomura at 91.05% of net assets, which adds to the 200% objective, so neither holds all the risk.[18][4] The sponsor kept the cost promise: the May 2025 summary prospectus set a net expense cap of 1.50% through December 31, 2025, the June 2026 report shows 1.50% actual, and the cap now runs to December 31, 2026.[6][3][2] What this means for an owner: the wrapper works day to day, so the case against it rests on cost, decay and gaps, not on sloppy tracking.

Weaknesses: size, sponsor churn and the single stock

Size is thin and unstable. Net assets were $3.18 million at June 30, 2025, $136.17 million a year later, and $46.25 million to $60 million in October 2026 on three data sites.[4][16][15][17] A fund that shrinks by more than half in four months is exposed to a sponsor decision to close it. GraniteShares announced the closure of three funds on May 18, 2026, closed a 1x short AMD fund in 2025, and lost the LCID fund in July 2026; another sponsor, Leverage Shares, said on October 20, 2026 that it would close 13 funds that could not attract enough assets.[8][10][9][11]

One company drives every dollar of outcome. Qualcomm's own filings say Apple, Samsung and Xiaomi each gave 10% or more of fiscal 2025 revenue, and that Apple is expected to use its own modem in more devices.[24][27] The regulator's warning is blunt. FINRA wrote in 2009 that daily-reset funds are typically unsuitable for retail investors who plan to hold them longer than one trading session.[12] The next chapter turns these points into five scored measures.

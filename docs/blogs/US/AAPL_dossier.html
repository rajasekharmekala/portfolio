1. What the company does

Apple sells devices and then sells services to the people who own them. In fiscal 2025 (the year ended September 27, 2025) it booked $416.2 billion of sales. The iPhone brought in $209.6 billion, or 50% of the total. Services brought in $109.2 billion, or 26%. Mac, iPad and wearables made up the rest.[1] Customers pay once for the device, and many of them pay again every month for iCloud storage, Apple Music, Apple TV, AppleCare and apps bought through the App Store. Advertisers and search partners also pay Apple for access to those customers.[1]

The two halves of the business earn very different profits. In fiscal 2025 the device side earned a gross margin (the share of sales left after the direct cost of making and delivering the product) of 36.8%. The Services side earned 75.4%.[1] Services was 26% of sales but 42% of gross profit ($82.3 billion of $195.2 billion).[1] That mix is the reason the company's profits have grown faster than its sales over the past five years, and it is why investors pay a premium multiple for the shares.

Where the customers live matters for risk. In fiscal 2025 the Americas supplied $178.4 billion of sales, Europe $111.0 billion and Greater China $64.4 billion, which is 15.5% of the total.[1] The company relies on outside partners located primarily in China mainland, India, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and Vietnam, so trade policy and tariffs affect it directly.[1] In the nine months ended June 27, 2026 (the latest reported period, which is prior-period data relative to the October 23 session), Greater China sales rose 30% to $64.8 billion after three years of decline.[8][5]

Apple is in a leadership handover. On April 20, 2026 the company announced that Tim Cook will become Executive Chair on September 1, 2026, and that John Ternus, the head of hardware engineering, becomes chief executive officer on that date.[13] The general counsel and the principal accounting officer also changed between December 2025 and January 2026.[14][15] The report for the fiscal fourth quarter (the September quarter) is scheduled for Monday, November 2, 2026 after the market closes.[21] It is the first earnings report since the handover.

What a reader should hold in mind

Think of Apple as a toll road built on top of a very popular car. The car is the iPhone, and the 2.5 billion active devices in use are the traffic.[11] The tolls are the App Store commission, the search-placement payments, the subscriptions and the advertising. If traffic keeps growing, the tolls keep growing with little extra cost. If a court or a regulator lowers the toll, the profit falls faster than the sales do. Chapters 2 and 6 return to this point.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Active devices: Apple products currently in use by customers; the company reports that the total passed 2.5 billion in the December 2025 quarter.
  • Services: Apple's recurring revenue from the App Store, advertising, cloud storage, payments, AppleCare, music and video; it excludes hardware sales.
  • Tariff: a tax charged by a government on imported goods; in 2026 Apple has both paid tariffs and received refunds of earlier ones.
  • Siri AI: Apple's rebuilt voice assistant, announced in June 2026 with the iOS 27 software and in developer and public beta as of the July 30 earnings call.
  • Digital Markets Act (DMA): a European Union law that limits how large platforms such as the App Store may operate.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The strength of the business comes from customers who stay. Apple reports that the number of active devices reached an all-time high in every product category and every region, and that more than half of the people who bought an iPad or an Apple Watch in the June 2026 quarter were new to that product.[11][17] Customer satisfaction for the iPhone was measured at 99% in the United States by an outside survey that management quoted on its call.[17] Those facts matter because each satisfied owner is a repeat buyer of hardware and a long-term buyer of services. The company says its paid subscriptions have passed 1.5 billion.[17]

The accounts show what that loyalty is worth. Return on invested capital (ROIC, the profit earned for each dollar put into the business) is 101.6%, against a floor of 12% in our rules, and it has risen every year from 73.6% in fiscal 2022.[22] Free cash flow (FCF, the cash left after capital spending) was $136.7 billion over the last twelve months, which is 29.3% of sales.[21] The company repurchased $82.2 billion of its own stock over the same twelve months and still held $62.2 billion more cash and securities than debt at June 27, 2026.[1][8][21]

The weaknesses are narrow but real. First, the iPhone is half of sales, so one product cycle can move the whole company. Second, Apple says it uses single-source or limited-source components, and it is now in a period of supply limits and rising costs for advanced chips, storage (NAND) and memory (DRAM), which it expects to intensify.[5] The chief executive called the move in memory prices a 100-year flood on the call.[17] Third, a large part of Services profit rests on legal arrangements that courts and regulators are now testing. Fourth, spending on research and development rose 32% in the June quarter to $11.7 billion, mostly for artificial intelligence, so the company is spending more before it knows what the spending earns.[5]

The company's own risk language is blunt about competition. It states that it holds a minority share of the global smartphone, personal computer, tablet and wearables markets and faces rivals with low prices and large installed bases.[1] Against that, management says outside data from IDC show Apple gained global share in both iPhone and Mac in the June 2026 quarter.[17] The honest reading is that the moat is wide in premium devices and in services tied to them, and that it is narrower where Apple has to compete on price or on raw artificial-intelligence model quality.

App Store and search-payment risk

Four legal matters bear on Services profit. In the European Union the Commission fined Apple €500 million on April 23, 2025 under the Digital Markets Act (DMA) for how developers may steer users away from the App Store. Apple has appealed.[5] A second DMA investigation could, if it ends in a violation finding, carry a fine of up to 10% of annual worldwide net sales.[5] In the United States the Department of Justice sued on March 21, 2024 in the District of New Jersey, alleging monopolization of the smartphone market, and asks for equitable relief, meaning changes to Apple's conduct rather than money.[5] In the Epic Games case the Ninth Circuit allowed Apple to charge some commission on link-out purchases on December 11, 2025, and the Supreme Court agreed on June 30, 2026 to review the legal standard for civil contempt.[5]

The fourth matter is Apple's search-placement income from Google. A court ordered remedies against Google on September 2, 2025, and Apple's annual report warns that a reversal on appeal could impose remedies that bar Google from offering Apple commercial terms for search distribution, which could materially harm that revenue.[1] Apple does not publish the amount of this income, so we cannot size it from filings. We treat it as an unquantified tail risk and test it in Chapter 6.

Tariffs: a cost turned into a one-time gain

New U.S. tariffs began in the second quarter of 2025. Management estimated a $1.4 billion tariff cost for the December 2025 quarter.[20] On February 20, 2026 the Supreme Court struck down tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, and Apple booked the refunds it has received as a reduction of products cost of sales.[5] In the June 2026 quarter the refunds lifted company gross margin by about 2 percentage points and EPS by $0.11.[8] Management guides about 1 more point of refund benefit in the September quarter and says it will reinvest the refunds in the United States.[17] New tariffs under Section 301 have since been imposed, and the Section 232 semiconductor investigation could still lead to more, so the cost has not gone away.[5]

Artificial intelligence: late, expensive and partly borrowed

Apple unveiled Siri AI at its June 2026 developer conference with iOS 27, and on the July 30 call management said the product was in developer and public beta.[5][17] Management described a collaboration with Google on the underlying foundation models and said Apple also builds its own.[19][18] The new Siri is not yet available in the European Union, and the chief executive said Apple is working with the European Commission to find a way to offer it there.[17] The company also notes that it depends on third-party cloud capacity for some AI workloads and that compute is scarce and costly.[5] The investment case is that on-device AI sells more iPhones and Macs. The market has not yet seen that case proven in revenue, and we could not verify a general release date.

3. The numbers, in plain words

The five-year record shows a company whose sales grew slowly until fiscal 2025 and then accelerated. Sales were $365.8 billion in fiscal 2021 and $416.2 billion in fiscal 2025, a compound growth rate of 3.3% a year over the four steps. Net income grew from $94.7 billion to $112.0 billion. The accelerating part is recent: the nine months to June 27, 2026 show sales up 16% and net income up 20% on the same nine months a year earlier.[3][1][8]

Gross margin, the share of sales left after the direct cost of products and services, rose every year from 41.8% in fiscal 2021 to 46.9% in fiscal 2025, and 48.7% over the last twelve months.[3][1][21] Operating margin (the share left after research, selling and administrative costs as well) moved from 29.8% to 32.0% to 33.2% over the same span.[3][1][21] Most of the lift came from the mix shift toward Services. Part of the latest jump is tariff refunds, which will not repeat.

Cash tells a slightly different story. Free cash flow was $93.0 billion in fiscal 2021, peaked at $111.4 billion in fiscal 2022, and was $98.8 billion in fiscal 2025, a year when cash paid for income taxes was $43.4 billion.[3][1] In the last twelve months free cash flow is $136.7 billion. Part of that jump may reflect lower cash taxes, which were $26.6 billion in the first nine months of fiscal 2026 against $37.3 billion in the same months a year earlier.[8] We therefore treat the $98.8 billion to $136.7 billion range as the honest base for valuation.

The number

Underlying company gross margin for the September 2026 quarter is about 46.5%, not the 47% to 48% headline guide.

How it was calculated

Management guided gross margin of 47% to 48%, which includes about 1 point from tariff refunds.[17] The midpoint is 47.5%. Removing the 1 point of refunds leaves 46.5%.

The inputs

Guide range 47% to 48% and the refund benefit of about 1 point from the Q3 call.[17] June-quarter reported margin 50.1% with about 2 points of refunds, giving about 48.1%.[8] Memory costs rose in June and are expected to rise again.[17][5]

The range

A bull case of 47.5% underlying holds the June level less a small amount of cost pressure. A bear case of 45.5% means memory costs take another point. Each 1 point of gross margin is about $4.7 billion of annual gross profit on $467 billion of sales, or about $0.26 of earnings per share after 16.5% tax and 14.7 billion shares.

If the number is off

If underlying margin falls to 45.5%, fiscal 2027 EPS would be about $0.26 below our base path, which is roughly 3% of EPS. At a 31x multiple that is about $8 on the price. A margin that holds near 48% is 1.5 points above 46.5%, so it would add about $0.39, or about $12 on the price at 31x.

This chapter attacks our own numbers. For each estimate we name the weakest assumption, then give the strongest case against the verdict, then describe three ways the thesis could fail outright. The last paragraph names the single claim that everything hangs on and the date we will know.

Weakest assumption behind each estimate

For the fiscal 2028 EPS path of $10.36, the weakest assumption is that growth slows only to 8.4% after an 18% year. It could fall faster if memory costs keep rising, because management called the memory move a 100-year flood and says supply limits will grow in the September quarter.[17] For the base target of $326, the weakest assumption is that the multiple falls to 31.5 times. Red-team bear case, and its mirror image

The strongest bear case is that fiscal 2026 is the best year of a cycle. The iPhone is up 22%, Greater China is up 30%, Mac is up 29% in the quarter, and gross margin is helped by refunds.[5][8] Management has already guided September growth down to +9% to +11% and has said memory costs and supply limits will increase.[17] Operating expenses are growing 23% while revenue growth is guided to slow, which squeezes operating margin. At 39.9 times trailing earnings the stock has little room for a miss.[21]

The mirror image is that waiting costs us the gain. The stock is a Stage 2 leader, it has met or beaten guidance for three quarters, net cash is rising and the buyback authorization is $100 billion.[10] If Apple shows fiscal 2027 EPS of $11 or more, our $326 target is too low and the price may never return to $304. We accept that risk because buying a 36-times multiple on an earnings path that peaks at 8% growth has a poor expected return.

Fatal flaw 1: a memory and supply squeeze breaks the margin

Apple states that it is in a period of supply constraints and rising costs for chips, NAND and DRAM, expects the trend to intensify, and warns that price increases may not offset it and may reduce demand.[5] Inventory has nearly doubled from $5.7 billion to $11.1 billion, with components rising from $2.1 billion to $7.6 billion, which points to Apple stocking components.[8][5] If underlying gross margin drops from about 48% to 45%, fiscal 2027 EPS would fall by about $0.80, or 8%, and the stock would reprice. Apple first described this squeeze in its March-quarter report.[6] We will see the first evidence in the September-quarter margin and the December guide on November 2.

Fatal flaw 2: courts and regulators cut the Services toll

Services earns a 75.6% gross margin on $30.7 billion a quarter.[8][5] Four open matters could reduce it: the Digital Markets Act decision on a second investigation with a fine of up to 10% of annual worldwide net sales, the Justice Department monopoly suit, the Epic contempt standard now before the Supreme Court, and the Google search-distribution remedies that could be reversed on appeal.[5][1] Apple does not disclose how much profit rests on any one of these, so the size of the hit is unknown. A decision against Apple on the Google payments would hit Services margin with almost no offset in cost. The Epic Supreme Court review is the nearest dated event, and its decision date is not yet published.

Fatal flaw 3: the cycle proves to be a one-time pull-forward

The iPhone grew only 4% in fiscal 2025 and fell 2% in fiscal 2023.[1][3] The 22% rise in fiscal 2026 may mean owners upgraded early into the iPhone 17 lineup and a new tariff-cost or price rise slows the next buyer. 7. What we would do, and when

The decision is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT. Apple is not held, so there is no sale or trim to decide: the only question is whether and where to start a position. We do not buy at $347.73. We set a conditional limit-order zone of $296.00 to $304.00. A limit order is an order to buy only at or below a price you set. That zone is not a band next to the 20-day average at $338.91, which is only 1.38 ATR away. It is the price band where reward-to-risk clears the CORE_ALPHA floor of 2.0 to 1. A limit order near $339 is not used, because our base target of $326 is below it.

Reward-to-risk, which we also call asymmetry, is the base target of $326 minus the entry price, divided by the entry price minus the stop. Two stops exist, and both results are shown here in one place. The stock is in a Stage 2 uptrend. In an uptrend the stop is one ATR below the lower of the 20-day swing low ($325.81) and the 50-day average ($329.47), which is $319.40. In a base the stop is one ATR below the 63-day low of $300.00, which is $293.59. At $347.73 the result is -0.77 to 1 with the $319.40 stop and -0.40 to 1 with the $293.59 stop. At $338.91 it is -0.66 to 1 with the $319.40 stop. At $304 it is 2.11 to 1 with the $293.59 stop. The $319.40 stop cannot be used at $304, because it would sit above the entry price.

A fill at $296 to $304 happens below the 50-day average, so the Stage 2 trend reading would have ended and the stock would be retesting its three-month base. That is why the base-low stop of $293.59 is the valid invalidation level for the zone. The stop is the price at which the reason for the trade is gone, which we call structural invalidation. The stop row in the Layer 3 table quotes our written rule for uptrends and for bases together. For this zone the base rule applies. The $293.59 stop is also just under the 200-day average of $294.41.

Layer 3 is the timing and sizing layer. A moving average is the average closing price over a set number of days. A simple moving average (SMA) weights every day equally. An exponential moving average (EMA) gives more weight to recent days, so it reacts faster. Support is a price area where buyers have stepped in before. The support-distance ladder measures how far the price sits above support in ATR units. Under 0.6 ATR is full size, under 1.2 is standard, under 1.6 is limit-order and reduced size, and beyond that is no chase. At 1.38 ATR Apple is in the limit-order band, which is why we use a limit order and not a market order. The table that follows is the execution checklist. It turns the verdict into an entry zone, a stop, a size and an exit rule. Look at the stop distance and the tier cap, because together they set how much is at risk.

Risk-unit sizing sets the position so that a loss at the stop costs a fixed share of the portfolio, called the risk budget. Portfolio heat is the total of all open risk-to-stop amounts across positions. Our limit for it is 12% of the portfolio. The let-winners-run rule says we do not sell a rising Stage 2 stock just because its RSI is over 70 or its weight passes 15% of the portfolio, as long as earnings stay intact. The weight may grow to 22% while the stop trails the rising 21-day EMA or the 50-day SMA.

We set the conviction tier at TIER_3, with a 3.5% maximum weight and a 50 basis point risk budget. The tier is not higher for three reasons. The expected gain at the entry zone is only about 3.5%. The November 2 report may change the margin picture. The chief executive handover is untested.[13] If the stock fills in the zone, the tier cap sets the size and not the stop distance. A stop distance of 3.4% alone would allow a weight above 14%.

The $304 ceiling is not permission to buy. Three checks must be re-run before any fill, and the orders are placed only after they pass. First, re-run the momentum gate on the day. The Stage 2 pass is for today's $347.73 close and does not carry to a lower price. A fill needs a fresh reading that shows a base forming, with the support level, the ATR and the $293.59 stop recalculated. Second, read the November 2 report and the December-quarter guide. The date is scheduled by the company and shown as confirmed by the data provider, and we treat it as an estimate until Apple's own page is read. The entry ceiling comes from one formula: (target + 2 x stop) / 3. With a $326 target and a $293.59 stop it is $304.39.

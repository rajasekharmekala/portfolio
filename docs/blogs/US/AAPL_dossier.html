1. What the company does

Apple sells devices and then sells services to the people who own them. In fiscal 2025 (the year ended September 27, 2025) it booked $416.2 billion of sales. The iPhone brought in $209.6 billion, or 50% of the total, and Services brought in $109.2 billion, or 26%. Mac, iPad and wearables made up the rest[4]. Customers pay once for a device, and many then pay every month for cloud storage, music, video, AppleCare and apps. Advertisers and search partners also pay Apple for access to those customers[4].

The two halves of the business earn very different profits. Gross margin is the share of each sales dollar left after the direct cost of making and delivering the product. In fiscal 2025 the device side earned a gross margin of 36.8% and the Services side earned 75.4%[4]. Services was 26% of sales but 42% of gross profit, which was $82.3 billion of $195.2 billion[4]. That mix explains why profit has grown faster than sales, and it is why investors pay a high price for the shares.

Where customers live matters for risk. In fiscal 2025 the Americas supplied $178.4 billion of sales, Europe $111.0 billion and Greater China $64.4 billion, which is 15.5% of the total[4]. A significant majority of manufacturing is done by outside partners located mainly in China mainland, India, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and Vietnam, so trade policy reaches Apple directly[4]. In the nine months to June 27, 2026, Greater China sales rose 30% to $64.8 billion, after three straight annual declines[1][6][4].

Four events in recent months change the picture. First, Tim Cook became executive chair and John Ternus became chief executive on September 1, 2026[12][44]. The November 2 report is the first earnings call led by Ternus, and Apple's own investor page confirms the date[15][46]. Second, at its September 9 event Apple launched the iPhone 18 Pro at $1,199, which is $100 more than last year's Pro, and a foldable iPhone Duo at $1,999 that went on sale on October 23[30]. Third, on October 27 Apple moved its 14-inch MacBook Pro and its iMac to the new M6 chip at starting prices of $1,999 and $1,499, which match the prices set after the June increases[42][43]. Fourth, memory chip costs are rising so fast that Apple has raised prices on several products[45][16].

What a reader should hold in mind

Think of Apple as a toll road built beside a very popular highway. The cars are the devices, and more than 2.5 billion of them are in active use[10]. The tolls are App Store commissions, search-placement payments, subscriptions and advertising. If traffic keeps growing, the tolls grow with little extra cost. If a court or a regulator lowers a toll, profit falls faster than sales do. Chapters 2 and 6 return to that point, and Chapter 3 shows how much the toll road has earned over five years.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Active devices: Apple products in use by customers. Apple reports the total passed 2.5 billion in the December 2025 quarter.
  • Services: Apple's recurring revenue from the App Store, advertising, cloud storage, payments, AppleCare, music and video. It excludes hardware sales.
  • Tariff: a tax a government charges on imported goods. In 2026 Apple has both paid new tariffs and received refunds of old ones.
  • DMA: the European Union's Digital Markets Act, a law that limits how very large platforms such as the App Store may operate.
  • Siri AI: Apple's rebuilt voice assistant, shown at its June 2026 developer conference and rolling out in beta with iOS 27.
  • FCF stands for free cash flow, the cash left after running the business and buying equipment; SBC is stock-based compensation, the cost of paying employees in shares.
  • ATR (average true range) is the typical daily price swing in dollars; RSI (relative strength index) is a 0 to 100 gauge of how fast the price has been rising.
  • EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) is profit before financing and accounting charges; ROIC (return on invested capital) is the profit earned on each dollar put into the business.
  • Net debt is interest-bearing debt minus cash and securities; a negative number means the company holds more cash than it owes. A buyback is the company buying its own shares, which lowers the share count.
  • Cash flow from operations is the cash the business itself generates before investing and financing. Guidance is the company's own published forecast for the coming period.
  • A consensus estimate is the average of analysts' forecasts for a number. Dilution is the rise in share count when a company issues new shares to staff or investors.
  • Price-to-earnings is the share price divided by earnings per share. The operating margin is operating profit as a share of sales.
  • Stage 2 is the confirmed uptrend on the Weinstein scale (price above a rising 50-day average that sits above a rising 200-day average); Stage 4 is the confirmed downtrend.
  • A base is the sideways shelf a stock builds before it moves. The 20-day moving average is the average closing price of the last 20 trading days, a slowly moving line the price tends to bounce off.
  • A pivot is the top of a base, the level the price must clear. A breakout is a move above that level on heavy volume.
  • A stop loss is the price at which the thesis is wrong and the position is cut. A limit order is an order that fills only at your price or better and never chases. The entry zone is the band of prices where buying is planned.
  • Asymmetry, also called reward-to-risk, is the gain from entry to target divided by the loss from entry to stop loss: (target minus entry) divided by (entry minus stop).

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The strength of Apple's business comes from customers who stay. In the June quarter management reported that its installed base of active devices, above 2.5 billion, reached another all-time high in every major product category and every region[16][10]. It also said paid subscriptions have passed 1.5 billion, and that more than half of the people who bought an iPad or an Apple Watch in the quarter were new to that product[16]. An outside survey quoted on the call put US customer satisfaction with the iPhone 17 family at 99%[16]. Those facts matter because each satisfied owner is a repeat buyer of hardware and a long-term buyer of services.

Industry data point the same way, with two cautions. Counterpoint Research estimates that global smartphone shipments fell 20% in the third calendar quarter of 2026, the lowest third quarter since 2012, while Apple's share rose to 22%, the first time above 20% in any third quarter[28]. IDC puts the market decline at 22% and Apple's share at 22.4%, on 57.3 million units[29]. The first caution is that Apple's own shipments fell year on year in Counterpoint's data, because there was no base iPhone 18 this year[28]. The second is that a rising share in a shrinking market partly reflects rivals that sell cheaper phones losing more volume[28][29].

The accounts show what loyalty is worth. The data provider's return on invested capital for Apple is 101.6%, up from 73.6% in fiscal 2022[20][22]. Our own simpler check, operating profit after tax divided by equity plus debt, gives about 67%[1][4][20]. Both are far above our 12% floor. Free cash flow was $136.7 billion over the last twelve months, which is 29.3% of sales[20][1]. Apple spent only $10.0 billion on capital spending in that period, while Microsoft spent $116.0 billion, Alphabet $132.4 billion and Amazon $173.0 billion[20][24][25][26]. That gap matters now because the rivals' cash is going into artificial intelligence data centres, and Apple's is not.

The weaknesses are narrow but real. First, the iPhone is half of sales, so one product cycle can move the whole company. Second, Apple says it relies on single-source or limited-source components, and that it is in a period of supply limits and rising costs for advanced chips, storage and memory, which it expects to intensify[1]. The chief executive called the move in memory prices a 100-year flood[16]. Third, a large part of Services profit rests on legal arrangements that courts and regulators are now testing, which the next subsection covers. Fourth, spending on research and development rose 32% in the June quarter to $11.7 billion, mostly for artificial intelligence, so Apple is spending more before it knows what the spending earns[1].

App Store and search payments: the legal toll

Four legal fronts bear on Services profit, and the calendar for each is now clearer. In the European Union, the Commission fined Apple €500 million in April 2025 under the Digital Markets Act and opened a second investigation, which could end in a fine of up to 10% of annual worldwide net sales[1]. On August 18, 2026 Apple announced new EU business terms that it says resolve its disagreements with the Commission over business terms. Under them the headline commission for App Store apps using Apple's in-app purchase is 26%, the vast majority of developers pay 15%, and apps distributed outside the App Store pay a 5% Core Technology Commission, all from October 1[31]. We found no filing or release after July 31 saying whether the Commission has closed the second investigation, so the fine risk is not yet cleared. On July 8, 2026 the EU General Court dismissed Apple's challenges to its gatekeeper designation[34].

In the United States, the Epic Games case has two parts. A 2025 court order bars Apple from charging any commission on purchases made outside an app. The Ninth Circuit said in December 2025 that Apple may charge some commission, and sent the details back to the trial court. On June 30, 2026 the Supreme Court agreed to review the legal standard for civil contempt[1]. A third-party timeline expects arguments between October and December 2026 and a ruling around June 2027, but the court has not published a date[36]. The Justice Department's monopoly suit is slower. Fact discovery, the stage where each side collects documents, has been extended twice and now runs to late May 2027, and the two sides have reportedly held settlement talks[35]. No trial date falls inside the next twelve months that we could find.

The fourth front is Apple's search-placement income from Google. A court ordered remedies against Google on September 2, 2025, and the judge declined to ban Google's payments to distributors such as Apple, so the payments can continue[32][1]. The Justice Department and a group of states appealed. In a July 28, 2026 brief they asked the appeals court to overturn the refusal to ban such payments. Google's reply brief was scheduled for September 29, and oral argument had not been scheduled in the report we read[32][33]. We found no later date. Apple's filings warn that a reversal could bar Google from offering Apple commercial terms for search distribution[1]. Apple does not publish the amount, so we cannot size it. One reported figure shows the scale: Google pays more than $26 billion a year to distributors in total, and Apple's share is not disclosed[32]. We treat this as an unquantified tail risk and stress it in Chapter 6.

Tariffs: a one-time gain, then a new cost

New US tariffs began in the second quarter of 2025, and management put the December 2025 quarter cost at $1.4 billion[19][18]. On February 20, 2026 the Supreme Court struck down tariffs imposed under an emergency-powers law, and Apple books the refunds it receives as a reduction of product cost[1]. In the June quarter the refunds lifted company gross margin by about 2 percentage points and earnings per share by $0.11[8]. Management guides about 1 more point of refund benefit for the September quarter[16]. The cost has not gone away. New Section 301 tariffs of 10% or 12.5% on 60 economies took effect on July 24, 2026, and a press report says Apple does not appear to be exempt[39][40].

Memory and supply: the live pressure point

Memory is the cost that matters most this year. IDC says memory costs have risen about 350% since a year ago, although the pace of increase eased to the low double digits in the third quarter, and it warns that the worst may be still to come, with a recovery in phone volumes possible only in 2028[29]. TrendForce expects conventional DRAM contract prices to rise another 10% to 15% in the December quarter and flash storage prices 15% to 20%[37]. A later TrendForce item puts the DRAM rise nearer 20%[38]. Apple paid more for memory in the March and June quarters, and management expects to pay even more in the September quarter[16]. In the same call management said that the Mac, iPhone and iPad will face supply limits in the September quarter because the most advanced chip production lines have too little spare capacity[16]. The company is passing some cost on through prices, but it says price rises may reduce demand[1].

Artificial intelligence: late, expensive and partly borrowed

Apple showed Siri AI at its June 2026 developer conference. On the July 30 call management said the product was in developer and public beta, that the company works with Google on the underlying foundation models, and that it also builds its own[16][18]. Siri AI is not yet offered in the European Union, where Apple is working with the Commission to find a way to launch it[16]. Apple also says it depends on outside cloud providers for some AI work, and that computing capacity is scarce and costly[1]. The investment case is that on-device AI sells more iPhones and Macs. The market has not yet seen that case proven in revenue, and Apple has not published a general release date for Siri AI.

Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

The second half of Layer 2 asks whether management delivered what it promised. We audited the last four quarterly outlooks, the capital return promises and the Siri promise against results. The pattern is consistent: Apple guides low and then beats on revenue and margin. Revenue beat the top of the guided range in two of three quarters and landed inside it in the third. Gross margin beat the top of the range in the first two quarters. The June quarter margin also beat, but about 2 points of the beat came from tariff refunds that will not repeat[19][18][17][10][9][8].

The misses are small and specific. Operating expenses came in $0.2 billion above the guided range in the March quarter because of a one-time item in selling, general and administrative cost[17]. Services growth of 12% in June was below the March rate of 16%, and management said the quarter roughly met its expectations but showed more softness in mobile gaming[16]. Management also dropped one old promise. Since 2018 Apple had steered toward a net cash balance of zero. In April 2026 it said it is no longer providing net cash neutral as a formal target and will evaluate cash and debt independently[17]. That gives the board more freedom to raise debt for buybacks or to fund AI spending, and we will watch for either.

The result is a yardstick for comparing names, not a fair value. It matters because a good business can still be a poor purchase if the price already assumes a great outcome. The market view, as the price shows it, is that the iPhone 18 Pro and the foldable Duo, the M6 Macs and Siri AI will keep growth in the double digits, that Services will keep compounding, and that Apple deserves a premium for earning 29% free cash flow margins while rivals pour cash into data centres. We differ from the market in two places, and they point in opposite directions. On earnings we are slightly more positive than the Street. 6. Why these estimates might be wrong

This chapter attacks our own numbers. For each estimate it names the weakest assumption and the evidence that would show it wrong. Then it gives the strongest case against the verdict, three ways the thesis could fail outright, and the single claim everything hangs on.

The weakest assumption behind each estimate

For the underlying September margin of 46.5%, the weakest assumption is that offsets keep working. Management said the benefit of carry-in inventory will shrink after September and that memory prices keep rising[16]. We will see the evidence in the December-quarter margin guide on November 2. For the September earnings estimate of $1.98, the weakest assumption is the share count and tax rate, which are small risks. For the fiscal 2028 earnings path of $10.39, the weakest assumption is that growth slows only to 8.5% after an 18% year. IDC expects the phone market to stay constrained through 2027, so growth could be lower[29].

For the central value of $327, the weakest assumption is that the multiple falls to 31.48. Apple's forward multiple has risen at every fiscal year-end since fiscal 2022, and a new product cycle can hold a high multiple for years[22]. If the multiple holds at 36.1, the central value is $375. For the probability-weighted value, the weakest assumption is the symmetry of 25% bull and 25% bear. For the reverse DCF, the weakest assumption is the cash-flow base. Trailing cash flow benefits from cash taxes of $32.6 billion against $43.4 billion in fiscal 2025, and we cannot reproduce the data feed's $107.7 billion[1][4][47]. The exit multiple of 18 times is also far below today's 36 times enterprise value to free cash flow[20], which pushes the implied growth up for every high-multiple company, not just Apple.

Red-team bear case, and its mirror image

The strongest bear case is that fiscal 2026 is the best year of a cycle. The iPhone is up 22%, Greater China is up 30%, Mac is up 29% in the June quarter, and gross margin was lifted by refunds[1][8]. Management has already guided September growth down to 9% to 11% and said memory costs and supply limits will increase[16]. Operating expenses are growing 23% against revenue growth of 16%, which squeezes operating margin[1]. The phone market is shrinking by 20% to 22%, and IDC says the worst is yet to come[28][29]. At 36 times next year's earnings, the stock has little room for a miss.

The mirror image is that waiting costs us the gain. The stock is a Stage 2 leader. Apple has met or beaten guidance in each of the last three quarters, net cash is rising, and remaining buyback capacity is $138 billion[1]. New products launched in the last two months, the Duo and the M6 Macs, are not in the September quarter at all[46]. If Apple shows fiscal 2027 earnings of $11 or more, our $327 central value is too low and the stock may never return to $304. We accept that risk because buying at 36 times earnings on a path that grows 8% has a poor expected return, and our rules ask for 2.0 to 1 before capital goes in.

Fatal flaw 1: a memory and supply squeeze breaks the margin

Apple states that it is in a period of supply constraints and rising costs for chips, storage and memory, expects the trend to intensify, and warns that price increases may not offset it and may reduce demand[1]. Inventory has nearly doubled from $5.7 billion to $11.1 billion, with components up from $2.1 billion to $7.6 billion, which suggests Apple is stocking parts ahead of higher prices[1]. If underlying gross margin fell from about 48% to 45%, fiscal 2027 earnings would be about $0.90 lower, which is 9% of the Street's $9.58, and the multiple would likely fall with it. TrendForce expects both DRAM and flash prices to keep rising in the December quarter[37]. We will see the first evidence in the December-quarter margin guide on November 2.

Fatal flaw 2: courts and regulators cut the Services toll

Services earns a 75.6% gross margin on $30.7 billion a quarter[1]. Four matters could reduce it: a final EU decision on the second Digital Markets Act investigation, the Justice Department monopoly suit, the Epic contempt standard now before the Supreme Court, and the Google search-payment remedies, where the appeal could end the payments[1][32][36][35]. Apple does not disclose how much profit rests on any of these, so the size of the hit is unknown. As an illustration only, not an estimate, a loss of $10 billion a year of near-pure-margin income would cost about $0.58 of earnings per share and about $18 a share at 31.5 times. The nearest dated events are the unscheduled appeals-court argument on Google and the Supreme Court ruling expected around June 2027[32][36]. The near-term calendar is quiet, which is a reason this flaw is not our base case.

Fatal flaw 3: the cycle proves to be a pull-forward in a shrinking market

The iPhone grew only 4% in fiscal 2025 and fell 2% in fiscal 2023[4][6]. The 22% rise in fiscal 2026 may mean owners upgraded early into the iPhone 17 and 18 lineups. Counterpoint reports that consumers are holding on to their devices longer, and IDC sees no recovery in phone volumes before 2028[28][29]. Apple has also raised prices, with the iPhone 18 Pro $100 higher, which may slow the next wave[30]. The new foldable has had early reports of display cracks along its hinge, and Apple says a small number of units are affected[46]. If iPhone growth fell back to low single digits in fiscal 2027 and Greater China gave back part of its 30% rebound, revenue growth would fall toward the 3% a year of fiscal 2021 to 2025, and the 13.5% growth that the price implies would look very far away[1][7][4].

7. What we would do, and when

The decision is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT. Apple is a pipeline candidate and does not appear in the positions file, so there is no sale or trim to decide. The only question is whether and where to start a position. We do not buy at the current market price (CMP), which is $347.10, the October 27 close. We set a conditional limit order zone of $296.00 to $304.00 for orders that fill only at those prices or lower. The zone is not a band next to the 20-day average at $340.07, which is only 1.11 ATR away. It is the price band where reward-to-risk clears the CORE_ALPHA floor of 2.0 to 1[47][49].

The rule behind the verdict needs one explanation. Our support-distance ladder puts 1.11 ATR in the STANDARD band, and by itself that band would read ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP. But the ladder measures distance to the 20-day average, and a buy also has to clear the 2.0 to 1 floor. At $347.10 the ratio is -0.38 to 1 using the $293.64 stop, and -0.73 to 1 using the $319.45 stop that our rules give a Stage 2 uptrend. At the 20-day average of $340.07 it is -0.63 to 1 with the $319.45 stop. Only at $304 does it reach 2.22 to 1, using our $327 central value and the $293.64 stop. That support is 6.8 ATR below the price, so we call the verdict WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT. Calling it ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP would suggest a buy near $340, where the numbers do not work[47][49].

The two stops need one explanation. Our stop rule places the stop one ATR below the nearest level that would prove the idea wrong. The table prints this as min(swing_low_20d, SMA50) minus 1.0 x ATR(14), which means the lower of the 20-day swing low and the 50-day average, minus one ATR. In a Stage 2 uptrend the stop is one ATR below the lower of the 20-day swing low ($325.81) and the 50-day average ($331.06), which is $319.45. In a base, the stop is one ATR below the 63-day low of $300.00, which is $293.64. A fill at $296 to $304 would happen below the 50-day average, so the Stage 2 reading would already have ended and the stock would be retesting its three-month low. The base-low stop of $293.64 is therefore the valid invalidation level for the zone. It also sits just under the 200-day average of $295.28. The $319.45 stop cannot be used at $304, because it would sit above the entry price[47].

The ceiling comes from one formula: (target + 2 x stop) / 3. With a $327 target and a $293.64 stop it is $304.76. At a $304 entry the floor needs a target of at least $324.72, which is only $2.28 below our $327. At our $10.39 earnings path that means a multiple of at least 31.25 times, against our 31.48. The margin is thin. At the fiscal 2023 multiple of 26.72 the target would be $278, the ceiling would fall to $288, below the stop, and no entry above the stop would clear 2.0. Any fill therefore depends on the earnings and multiple checks, and not only on the price touching the zone.

We set the conviction tier at TIER_3, which is our third-highest confidence level, with a 3.5% maximum weight and a 50 basis point risk budget (half of one percent of the portfolio lost if the stop is hit). That loss share is also called portfolio heat. The tier is not higher for three reasons. The expected gain at the top of the zone is only 5.8%. The November 2 report may change the margin picture. The chief executive handover is untested[12][15]. If the stock fills in the zone, the tier cap sets the size, not the stop distance: a stop distance of 3.4% alone would allow a weight above 14%. The portfolio already holds Alphabet, whose search payments to Apple are the legal tail risk in Chapter 6, so a ruling that ended those payments would hurt Apple and, in that one respect, help that holding.

Before any fill, three checks must be re-run, and orders are placed only after they pass. First, re-run the momentum gate on the day. A fill needs a fresh reading that shows a base forming, with support, ATR and the stop recalculated. Second, read the November 2 report and the December-quarter guide[15]. Third, recompute the reward-to-risk ratio with refreshed earnings, multiple, support and stop. The company confirms the report date on its own investor page[15].

The let-winners-run rule means we do not sell a strong holding only because it looks stretched or its weight has grown.

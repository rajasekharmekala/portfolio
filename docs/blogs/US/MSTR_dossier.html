1. What the company does

Strategy Inc is two companies under one roof. The older one sells enterprise analytics software, meaning programs that let a bank, retailer or government agency turn its data into reports and dashboards. Customers pay for cloud subscriptions, one-off licenses, yearly support contracts, and consulting and training. Revenue over the last twelve months was $498.3 million. We found it by adding fiscal 2025 revenue of $477.2 million to the first half of 2026 ($246.7 million) and subtracting the first half of 2025 ($225.6 million).[1][2] No customer reaches 10% of revenue. This half of the business has no single point of failure.[1]

The newer company is a bitcoin treasury, a business that exists to buy and hold bitcoin and to raise money from investors to buy more. At October 11 it held 848,250 bitcoin bought for $64.0 billion, an average of $75,444 each.[17] The treasury has no customers and no sales. Its result each quarter is the change in the market price of bitcoin. Accounting rule ASU 2023-08 forces the company to book that change straight into operating income.[2] In the first half of 2026 the change was a loss of $22.8 billion. That is why a company with $0.5 billion of yearly sales reports a $22.8 billion operating loss.[2]

The treasury is paid for with other people's money, in three ways. First, the company sells new common shares through an at-the-market program (ATM), which means it drips shares into the stock market at the current price. Second, it sells five series of preferred stock (STRF, STRC, STRK, STRD and STRE). These are shares that pay a fixed cash dividend and rank ahead of common shares if the company is ever wound up. The rates are 10%, 12% (variable), 8%, 10% and 10%.[6][19] Third, it owes $6.71 billion of convertible notes. These are loans with coupons of 0% to 2.25% that can be turned into shares.[5] At June 30 the preferred stock had a combined claim of $15.46 billion.[2]

The cash has to come from somewhere, because the software side cannot pay for it. Management says plainly that software cash will not cover the dividends and interest.[8] To bridge the gap it keeps a USD Reserve, a pot of dollars set aside only for preferred dividends and note interest. It stood at $4.88 billion on October 11, against expected yearly payments of about $1.76 billion.[4][17] Michael Saylor, the executive chairman, holds 32.9% of the votes through Class B shares. S&P Global rates the company B-, which is below investment grade.[20][1]

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Bitcoin treasury: a company whose main asset is bitcoin held on its balance sheet.
  • mNAV (multiple of net asset value): the company's enterprise value divided by the market value of its bitcoin; above 1 means the market pays a premium for the business, below 1 means a discount.
  • BTC Yield: the company's own measure of the percentage change in bitcoin held per diluted share over a period; positive means each share is backed by more bitcoin than before.
  • Preferred stock: shares that pay a fixed dividend and rank ahead of common shares; for Strategy the claim is large and paid in cash.
  • USD Reserve: cash set aside to pay preferred dividends and note interest, so bitcoin does not have to be sold for them.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The strongest part of the business is its scale and its access to money. No other public company comes close to 848,250 bitcoin: the next three holders on the public ranking hold 44,000, 43,514 and 35,595.[26] The company raised about $23.3 billion in 2025, $16.3 billion of common stock and $7.0 billion of preferred stock, which shows that investors were willing to fund the model when the share price carried a premium.[1] It has never missed a preferred dividend, with 23 on-time distributions by May 2026, and it cut its convertible notes from $8.21 billion to $6.71 billion by buying back $1.5 billion at about an 8% discount.[14][12]

The weakness is that this advantage runs on a premium, and the premium has almost gone. The market now values the whole company at 1.05 times its bitcoin, found by adding the equity value of $57.5 billion (420.5 million shares at $136.83) to $20.5 billion of debt and preferred claims and subtracting $5.52 billion of cash, then dividing by the $69.1 billion bitcoin value.[20][25][17] Over the past year that ratio has ranged from 0.95 to 1.43.[25] At a ratio near 1, selling a new common share to buy bitcoin adds almost no bitcoin per share, so the growth engine stalls; the company bought only 250 bitcoin in the week to October 11. It had bought 89,599 in the first quarter and 85,296 in the second.[17][3][11]

The funding machine has started to run in reverse. The flagship preferred, STRC, closed at $97.72 on October 16, below its $100 stated amount, and the company is using cash to buy it back: $102.6 million in the last three days of September, $73.7 million in the first four days of October and $192.4 million in the week to October 11.[27][18][17] The company said it expects to pay for these buybacks from sources such as sales of common stock under its ATM. Common shares outstanding rose from 371.6 million at June 30 to 420.5 million on September 15.[4][2][20] The company pays a 12% dividend to hold STRC near par, and on October 28 holders vote on moving to daily dividends.[19]

The software business is steady but small. Second-quarter revenue grew 6.9%, subscription revenue grew 54% and product support shrank 22.7%, so the mix is moving toward subscriptions while the old support line wears away.[2] Gross margin, the share of each sales dollar left after direct costs, slipped from 68.8% to 66.6%.[12] A segment that earned $3.7 million in the quarter cannot carry about $1.76 billion a year of fixed payments, so the equity story is a bitcoin story.[7][4] The newest threat is the index: MSCI approved rules on October 15 that screen out companies that look more like investment vehicles than operating businesses, and a simulation flagged Strategy; the final list is due November 11 and removals take effect after the November 30 close.[28]

3. The numbers, in plain words

Layer 1 tests six floors. Return on invested capital (ROIC) is operating profit divided by the money invested in the business, and the floor is 12%. Free cash flow (FCF) is cash from operations minus capital spending, and the floor is a margin of 12% of revenue. Net debt is debt minus cash, and it must be below 3.0 times EBITDA, which is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. Stock-based pay must be below 5.0% of revenue. The chart must be in Stage 2, the confirmed uptrend, and not in Stage 4, the confirmed downtrend. The last floor is asymmetry, also called reward-to-risk, which is the gain at our target divided by the loss at our stop. It must be at least 3.5 to 1, with a target anchored to an earnings multiple, a cash-flow model or a measured move, which is the height of a sideways price range projected upward.[37]

The usual health checks assume a company earns profit by selling things. Strategy's profit comes mostly from marking bitcoin to market. The headline numbers therefore swing by tens of billions without any change in what the business does. In the last twelve months the operating result was a loss of $36.4 billion on revenue of $498.3 million.[1][2] The honest way to read the company is to look at it twice. One view is as reported. The other removes the bitcoin marks so the software business can be seen alone. Both views fail the cash and return floors, and the tables below show why.

Revenue has barely moved in five years. It was $510.8 million in 2021 and $477.2 million in 2025.[10][1] Gross margin has slipped from 82.0% in 2021 to 68.7% in 2025 and 67.6% over the last twelve months.[9][1][2] Remove the bitcoin marks and the software operating result falls from a profit of $46.1 million in 2021 to $0.8 million in 2023. It then turns to losses of $63.1 million in 2024 and $40.5 million over the last twelve months. Sales, research and general costs now exceed gross profit.[10][9][1][2]

Cash flow tells the same story from the other side. Cash flow from operations is the cash the business itself generates. It was positive until 2023 and has been negative since: -$53.0 million in 2024 and -$67.2 million in 2025, then -$20.1 million over the last twelve months.[1][2] Free cash flow was -$41.2 million over the last twelve months, or -8.3% of revenue. The balance sheet shows where the money goes. The company raised $16.4 billion of common stock in 2024, $16.3 billion in 2025 and $8.2 billion in the first half of 2026. It also raised $7.0 billion of preferred stock in 2025 and $7.5 billion in the first half of 2026.[1][2] Almost all of it went into bitcoin: $22.5 billion in 2025 and $13.7 billion in the first half of 2026. Each dollar of preferred stock adds a permanent cash dividend. The company paid $629.2 million in the first half of 2026 alone, against operating cash flow of $9.9 million.[2][6] That gap is the whole risk in one sentence.

5. What could happen next

A Reverse DCF works backward from the share price to find the growth rate the market is assuming. It adds up ten years of free cash flow, shrinks each year by a 10% discount rate, and values year ten at 18 times that year's free cash flow.[37] For Strategy the method has no answer. The starting figure is trailing free cash flow of -$41.2 million, and a negative number grown at any rate stays negative, so the sum can never reach the $57.5 billion equity value. The right-hand side is below zero for every growth rate above -100% a year.[1][2]

The working shows how far the company is from an answer. At zero growth the discount factors add to 6.1446 for years one to ten, and the year-ten value adds 18 / 1.1^10 = 6.9398, for a total of 13.0843. To justify $57.5 billion the company would need free cash flow of $57.5B / 13.0843 = $4.40 billion a year at zero growth, against an actual -$0.04 billion. That is about 107 times the size of the trailing loss, so the share price is not a claim on operating cash flow. It is a claim on bitcoin and on the premium the market pays for the machine that buys it.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every number in this book rests on a few assumptions, and the honest question is which one would hurt most if it were wrong. Our decision is to sell, so the three ways it fails are: we sold just before a rally, we were too gentle about the funding risk, or our model of the stock's value is off. Each has a clear signal and a date on which we will know.

One point applies to all three. The Layer 2 result rests on a comparison of two share counts, one at June 30 and one at September 15, because the company has not yet reported its own third-quarter measure. That number arrives on October 29, after our sell window closes, and we treat it as the hinge.

Scenario 1: Bitcoin rallies and the premium returns, so selling was early

The weakest assumption in the base and bear values is that bitcoin has no edge in either direction over twelve months. If bitcoin rises to $105,000 and the premium rebuilds to 1.25 times, the stock is worth $224.96, 64% above the close.[25][3] For the 13.2758 shares, that is about $1,170 of gain given up, measured from today's close. The bear case in the same model is -48%, so the two outcomes are close to mirror images.

The evidence that would show us wrong early would be bitcoin holding above $90,000 for two weeks, mNAV on mnav.com above 1.2, and STRC trading at $99 or higher for ten sessions. Each is published daily or weekly: the company's Monday filings give holdings and the reserve, and the October 29 results give the company's own bitcoin per share for the third quarter.[17][21][25] Because the position is 2.2% of net worth, being wrong here costs a bounded amount of opportunity, not capital.

Scenario 2: The funding machine breaks, so the risk is larger than we model

The weakest assumption in the bear value is that the premium falls only to 0.95 times and that the preferred stock stays funded. Three dated events could break that. Preferred holders vote on October 28 on daily dividends, and the November dividend of 12.00% is payable only if the amendment passes.[19] MSCI publishes its list on November 11 with removals effective after the November 30 close; JPMorgan estimated about $2.8 billion of selling on an earlier version of the rules, and the company says MSCI-tracking funds hold about 13 million shares, 3.1% of its basic shares, worth about $1.8 billion at $136.83.[28][29] STRC at $97.72 suggests holders already want more than a 12% payment.[27]

If STRC falls well below par, the company's policy is not to issue it below $100, so its main source of new money stops, and bitcoin might have to be sold at low prices to pay dividends.[4] The reserve of $4.88 billion buys time (about 2.8 years of payments), so a forced sale is not imminent, but it would shift value from common holders to preferred holders. The evidence would be STRC below $95, a failed vote, or a final MSCI list that includes Strategy.

Scenario 3: Our net asset value and scoring work is wrong

Our value rests on four judgment calls: that preferred stock counts at its $100 amount, that the software business is worth zero, that fixed payments come out of the reserve, and that the share count is the September 15 figure. We combined data from different dates: shares at September 15, bitcoin and the reserve at October 11, and the price at October 16.[20][17][27] The vendor count of 384 million shares is older and would put net asset value at $141 a share, so the stock would look 3% below, not 6% above, its asset value. We prefer the later count because it comes from a filing dated October 5.[24][20]

The Layer 2 bitcoin-per-share FAIL is the point the verdict leans on, so we state its basis plainly. The company-reported figure for the last reported quarter (Q2) was +5.0%, which would score PASS. The third quarter ended on September 30 and has not been reported. Our FAIL is our own computation from filed counts: basic shares at June 30 and September 15 against bitcoin held on each date. The company's own measure uses diluted shares and does not count preferred issuance as new shares.[3] A positive third-quarter BTC Yield would need diluted shares at September 30 of no more than 402.1 million (847,666 bitcoin on that date divided by the June 30 level of 210,824 satoshis a share), and basic shares alone were already 420.5 million on September 15.[18][3][20] We therefore judge a positive print very unlikely, but it is the one result that would change the verdict. If it appeared, the measure would move to WATCH and the score would be 1 PASS, 3 WATCH and 1 FAIL. The two-FAIL sell rule would then no longer be met, and the shares would be treated as a rejected-for-new-money holding with a stop rather than as a sale.

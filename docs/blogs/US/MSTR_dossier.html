1. What the company does

Strategy runs two businesses under one roof. The first is enterprise analytics software: dashboards, reporting and an artificial-intelligence layer that large companies license or rent by subscription. It had 1,539 employees at the end of 2025 and booked $477.2 million of revenue that year.[10] The second is a bitcoin treasury. As of October 25, 2026 the company held 848,517 bitcoin that it bought for $64.02 billion, an average of $75,446 per coin.[1] Since the second quarter of 2026 the two are reported as separate segments, and all of the revenue comes from software.[8]

The software does not pay for the bitcoin. In the second quarter of 2026 the software segment earned $3.7 million of net income, and the whole company produced only $9.85 million of cash from operations in the first half.[8] The bitcoin is paid for by selling securities to investors: common shares through an at-the-market program, convertible notes, and five series of perpetual preferred stock that pay fixed or variable dividends. An at-the-market program (called an ATM) is a way of selling new shares gradually at the going price. In 2025 the company raised $25.3 billion this way, and in 2026 it had raised $17.06 billion by late July.[13][9]

A common shareholder owns what is left after the senior claims are paid. At the October 27 bitcoin price of $80,805.08 the bitcoin is worth $68.56 billion, while the convertible notes and preferred stock add up to about $20.12 billion at face value. The company also holds $4.83 billion in a dollar reserve and $242.4 million in dollar cash.[26][1] So the stock behaves like a bitcoin position with built-in borrowing. When bitcoin rises, the residual value rises faster. When it falls, the residual falls faster. Chapter 3 builds each of these numbers step by step.

The accounting adds a twist. Since 2025 Strategy records bitcoin at fair value, so every move in the bitcoin price flows through net income. In 2025 that meant a $5.40 billion unrealized loss in operating costs, and in the first half of 2026 a $22.77 billion unrealized loss on $246.7 million of revenue.[10][8] Reported profit and earnings per share (EPS, the profit divided across all shares) are therefore mostly a bitcoin price gauge. The company has already told investors what the third quarter looks like: an estimated $20.91 billion gain on digital assets and a $1.88 billion deferred tax expense, unaudited, published on October 5.[3] That headline will be large and positive, and it will not be news. This report reads profit as a bitcoin gauge and anchors value on the coins and the claims against them.

The three numbers that tell you whether the machine is working

Three numbers tell you most. The first is mNAV, a market measure that investors use and that the company's filings we read do not: the multiple of the bitcoin pile that the market pays for the whole company, measured as enterprise value divided by bitcoin value. Above 1.00 means investors pay a premium for the coins, and the company can sell new shares to buy more coins than the sale dilutes. Below 1.00, selling shares to buy coins shrinks bitcoin per share. The second is BTC Yield, the percentage change in bitcoin per share over a period. The third is the USD Reserve, which board policy requires to cover at least twelve months of expected preferred dividends and interest.[8][9]

These three numbers answer different questions. mNAV tells you what you pay for the coins. BTC Yield tells you whether the strategy is adding value for the common shareholder after dilution. The reserve tells you whether the preferred dividends are safe. We score all three in Chapter 2B, and we add two more, a dilution test and the software trend, because a bitcoin treasury can look healthy on one and weak on another.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • mNAV is enterprise value divided by the value of the bitcoin held. A reading of 1.09 means you pay $1.09 for each $1.00 of bitcoin once the company's debts and preferred stock are added and its cash is subtracted.
  • BTC Yield is the percentage change in bitcoin held per assumed diluted share over a period. It rises only when the company adds coins faster than it adds shares and share-like claims.
  • BTC Hurdle is the company's own estimate of its yearly cost of credit, 10.8% in the second-quarter release. Bitcoin has to beat that rate for common shareholders to gain on a per-share basis.
  • USD Reserve is cash and short-term investments set aside only to pay preferred dividends and interest. USD Cash is a second pool created on August 24, 2026 for broader uses, including buying back preferred stock.
  • Convertible note is a loan that can turn into shares at a set price. If the stock stays below that price, the lender can demand cash at the put date.
  • Perpetual preferred stock is a share class that pays a dividend with no maturity date and ranks ahead of common stock. STRC, STRF, STRK, STRD and STRE are the five series.
  • Senior claims are the convertible notes plus the preferred stock at face value, the amounts that rank ahead of common shareholders.
  • Fair-value accounting means the bitcoin is marked to the market price each quarter, with the gain or loss counted in net income.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The competitive position rests on scale and access to capital, not on software. Strategy is the largest corporate holder of bitcoin, and it kept raising money through the bitcoin decline of early 2026: $25.3 billion in 2025 and $17.06 billion by late July 2026.[13][9] In the week to October 18 its common-stock program still had $18.79 billion of capacity and its STRC program $17.51 billion.[2] That access is the real asset, because it lets the company buy bitcoin with other people's money when the stock trades above the value of the coins.

The same engine is also the weak point. The cheapest funding stopped working in the summer. STRC, the flagship preferred, traded well below its $100 stated value in July, when the company's repurchases averaged $86.53 a share, so management raised its dividend from 11.00% in January to 11.50% in March and 12.00% in July and began a buyback, which means the company buys its own STRC shares in the market.[9] The buyback has since run every week. From October 5 to October 25 the company bought back 5,939,384 STRC shares for $590.4 million, at average prices between $99.06 and $99.60, and sold almost no new stock.[6][2][1] Money spent on repurchases is money not spent on bitcoin, and the USD Cash pool that paid for them fell from $1.61 billion on August 30 and $1.30 billion on September 13 to $242.4 million on October 25.[17][18][1]

The software moat is modest. Revenue was $510.8 million in 2021 and $477.2 million in 2025, a decline of about 1.7% a year, and gross margin, the share of revenue left after the direct cost of delivering the product, fell from 82.0% to 68.7% as sales shifted from licenses to cloud subscriptions.[11][10] Subscription revenue grew 54% in the second quarter of 2026 while product-support revenue kept shrinking, and gross margin was 66.6%.[8] Signed software contracts not yet recognized as revenue, called remaining performance obligations (RPO), were $546.9 million at June 30, with $320.1 million due within 12 months.[8] The business is real and stable, but at about $0.5 billion of yearly revenue it is too small to carry the obligations on its own.

Peers show how crowded the model has become. Using filed holdings and the October 27 prices, Strive holds 43,000 bitcoin, Twenty One Capital 43,514 and Metaplanet 44,000, each about one-twentieth of Strategy's pile.[30][33][34] Strategy sits in the middle of the range on valuation: its enterprise value is 1.09 times its bitcoin, against 1.44 for Strive, 1.05 for Twenty One and 0.85 for Metaplanet on our builds in Chapter 3. Anyone who wants bitcoin alone can buy a fund such as IBIT, which holds $65.4 billion of net assets for a 0.25% fee.[35] Owning MSTR adds a premium, dividend claims ranking ahead of you, and a software business, so the premium needs a reason: the company's ability to add coins per share faster than a plain holder could.

Where the edge holds and where it does not

The edge holds in scale, liquidity and a clear playbook. A reserve of $4.83 billion gives about three years of cover for the dividends and interest, and the company has never missed a preferred dividend: 23 consecutive distributions by May and 18 months of payments by July.[1][14][9] Index risk is the first outside threat. MSCI adopted its non-operating-company rule on October 15 and will publish the affected list on November 11, effective at the close on November 30.[23] Its earlier simulation flagged Strategy by name.[24] Strategy fell 9.56% to $136.83 on October 16, the first session after the decision.[24][29]

The edge does not hold in cost of capital. A 12.00% dividend on the largest preferred series sits above the company's own 10.8% hurdle, so each new dollar of STRC needs bitcoin to rise more than 10.8% a year just to break even per share.[5][9] What this means for an owner: the structure can work for years, but it works best when bitcoin rises and the stock trades at a premium, and it asks the owner to accept a wide band of outcomes.

1. What the company does

EQT Corporation, based in Canonsburg, Pennsylvania, drills for natural gas in the Appalachian Basin, which covers parts of Pennsylvania, West Virginia and Ohio.[16][3] It describes itself as a vertically integrated natural gas company with upstream and midstream operations focused in that basin, which means it owns both the wells and much of the pipe that carries the gas away.[1] Upstream sells natural gas, natural gas liquids (NGLs, such as propane and ethane) and a little oil. The midstream segments, gathering and transmission, earn fees for moving and storing gas.[2] For the quarter ended June 30, 2026, upstream operating income was $109.4 million, while gathering and transmission together earned $309.7 million before eliminations, so most of that quarter’s segment profit came from the pipes, not the wells.[2]

Gas is measured in thousand cubic feet (Mcf), and EQT reports mixed gas and liquids volumes in Mcfe, which converts liquids to a gas equivalent. A billion cubic feet equivalent is a Bcfe. The company sold 634 Bcfe in the second quarter of 2026 at an average realized price of $2.65 per Mcfe, down from $2.81 a year earlier, and 2,382 Bcfe in all of 2025 at $3.19.[1][6] Price is the swing factor. Realized price was $5.08 per Mcfe in the first quarter of 2026, when Winter Storm Fern raised demand, and $2.65 in the second.[5][6][1] The Henry Hub spot price, the US benchmark for gas, ran from $7.72 per million Btu in January 2026 down to $2.77 in April and $2.95 in September.[27] The seasons matter: the trailing twelve months to June 2026 include that winter spike and so overstate what a typical year earns.

Who pays EQT? Its customers include utilities and power generators. The company says it has 1.2 billion cubic feet a day of premium firm sales deals with major utilities in the Southeast that depend on a pipeline expansion not yet in service.[6] It signed a 10-year supply agreement for 325,000 dekatherms a day to a planned 2.1 gigawatt power plant in West Virginia, priced off power prices in the PJM grid, and a 5-year offtake of 0.5 million tonnes a year of liquefied natural gas starting in 2028.[1] In addition to its own gas, EQT guides to $600 million to $700 million of third-party midstream revenue in 2026, which is fee income from other producers.[1]

The July 2024 purchase of Equitrans Midstream Corporation made EQT what it called the only large-scale vertically integrated natural gas business in America. The deal closed on July 22, 2024, ahead of the original schedule, with more than $425 million of annual savings identified and a projected breakeven price of about $2.00 per million Btu, which is the gas price at which cash left after all spending and before interest turns positive.[13] Owning the pipes changed the cost lines. Gathering cost per Mcfe fell from $0.35 in 2024 to $0.08 in 2025, which the company ties to owning the gathering, transmission and storage assets from that purchase, and total operating cost per Mcfe fell from $1.23 to $1.05.[6] EQT also holds a stake in the Mountain Valley Pipeline (MVP), which entered service in June 2024. In January 2026 it exercised an option to raise its share of MVP Mainline and the MVP Boost compression project from about 49% to about 53%.[6]

Hedging adds noise to the accounts. EQT sells gas forward through swaps and collars (contracts that fix or bracket a price), and the change in their value is booked in revenue. That line was a $3.78 billion loss in 2021 and a $4.64 billion loss in 2022, then gains of $1.84 billion in 2023, $51 million in 2024 and $291 million in 2025.[8][7] Cash settled on those contracts was a $5.93 billion payment in 2022 and a $1.22 billion receipt in 2024.[8][7] For this reason we read cash flow and the company’s adjusted figures next to the reported revenue, and we say which one we use each time.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Mcfe and Bcfe are thousand and billion cubic feet of gas, with liquids converted to a gas equivalent so one number covers everything sold.
  • Henry Hub is the Louisiana pricing point for US gas, and the NYMEX futures contract settles there.
  • Basis and differential are the gap between the Henry Hub price and the price EQT actually gets after pipeline costs and local supply; EQT guides to a 2026 differential of ($0.55) to ($0.35) per Mcf.[1]
  • A hedge is a contract that fixes a gas price (a swap) or sets a floor and a ceiling (a collar) for a set volume.
  • Free cash flow (FCF) is cash from operations minus capital spending; EQT also reports a version attributable to EQT that removes outside partners’ share of its pipeline joint ventures.
  • The midstream joint venture is a partnership that owns part of EQT’s gathering and pipeline assets; third-party holders receive 60% of its cash distributions.[1]
  • Net debt is total debt minus cash.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

EQT’s first strength is cost and scale. Proved reserves were 28.0 trillion cubic feet equivalent at the end of 2025, up 7% in the year. The company says it has about 30 years of drilling inventory and roughly 1 million undeveloped core net acres.[6][3] Operating cost was $1.05 per Mcfe in 2025 and $1.03 in the second quarter of 2026. The full-year 2026 guide, as updated on July 21, 2026, is $1.05 to $1.20. The Q2 guide was $1.03 to $1.17.[6][1][3] Management puts its unlevered free cash flow breakeven at about $2 per million Btu for 2026. Henry Hub spot prices ran from $2.77 to $3.15 between April and September 2026.[3][27] We cannot verify the breakeven, because it is a company-defined measure. The second quarter supports the direction, though. At a realized $2.65 per Mcfe, EQT still made $330 million of free cash flow attributable to EQT after $666 million of capital spending.[1]

The second strength is balance-sheet repair. Net debt was $9.1 billion at the end of 2024, $7.7 billion at the end of 2025 and $5.5 billion at June 30, 2026. The long-term ceiling is $5 billion.[10][6][1][4] The rating agencies have followed. Fitch upgraded EQT to BBB. S&P is at BBB- with a stable outlook. Moody’s is at Baa3 with a positive outlook.[5][3] Management says it will build cash and buy back stock when the market is weak, and it can hold up to a few billion dollars of cash.[4] That promise is a plan, not yet a record. The first-half 2026 cash-flow statement shows $206.3 million of dividends and no repurchase line. The last repurchases we found were $409.5 million in 2022 and $201.0 million in 2023.[2][8][7]

The weaknesses are just as concrete. EQT is a price taker. Its second-quarter realized price fell 5.7%, from $2.81 to $2.65 per Mcfe, even as volume rose 11.6% from 568 Bcfe a year earlier.[12] Adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) per Mcfe fell from about $2.04 to about $1.90 (our calculation from $1,203 million over 634 Bcfe against $1,158 million over 568 Bcfe).[1] The gap to Henry Hub widened to $(0.67) per Mcf in the second quarter. It is guided to $(0.75) to $(0.65) in the third.[1] Returns are modest because EQT paid market prices for assets. Goodwill is $2.06 billion, and equity investments, mainly pipelines, are $3.95 billion.[2] Our return on invested capital is 7.4% for 2025 and 9.1% for the twelve months to June 2026. Both are under the 12% floor.[7][2] The share count has grown too. Diluted weighted shares rose from 413.2 million in 2023 to 615.7 million in 2025.[7]

Some things are not yet in the numbers. EQT estimates that data centers and coal retirements add about 10 billion cubic feet a day of US gas demand by 2030, with an upside case of about 18.[3] The 2026 production raise of about 90 Bcfe came from compression projects that slow well decline. The release names that cause, not new data-center contracts.[1] On the July call, management said it expects at least one more power or data-center agreement before the end of 2026. That is an expectation, not a contract.[4] Shippingport was planned as a 3.6 gigawatt gas plant burning about 800 million cubic feet a day at peak. Its data-center phases were to start in 2027 and ramp through 2028. Its status in the 2026 documents is not stated.[28]

Regulatory and permitting

For EQT’s own pipelines the news is positive. MVP Southgate has all key regulatory approvals. The company is moving $85 million of capital contributions forward to finish construction by the end of 2026.[1] MVP Mainline has been in service since June 2024, and EQT pays contracted capacity charges on it. That is why transmission cost per Mcfe rose from $0.38 to $0.42 in 2025.[6] Third-party power projects that would burn EQT gas face their own permits. An analyst asked about permitting for the 2031 start on the July call. The answer we have shows management expecting more deals. We found no stated permit schedule, so the timing of those volumes stays unverified.[4]

Hedging, derivatives and why GAAP earnings mislead

EQT had hedged about 25% of 2026 volume by February 2026, up from 7%. The collars had an average floor of $3.94 and a ceiling of $5.70 per million Btu.[6] On July 14, 2026 the book showed 125 million dekatherms hedged for the third quarter and 108 million for the fourth. Put floors were $3.50 and $3.72. Swaps for part of the second and third quarters of 2027 were at $3.16.[1] Those floors sit well above the $2.78 to $2.95 Henry Hub readings for July to September. So the hedges are in the money. In an October 13, 2026 filing, EQT said it expects a $100 million derivative gain and $70 million of cash settlements received for the third quarter.[16][27] That gain lifts GAAP earnings but not adjusted earnings, which count only settled contracts. So third-quarter GAAP earnings per share will probably look better than the adjusted figure analysts follow.[1]

Layer 1 underwriting: static levels, trajectory (rate of change), and archetype fit

Layer 1 is our first underwriting test. It asks whether the business meets fixed floors on returns, cash generation, debt and stock pay, and then compares those static levels with the direction they are moving. It also assigns an archetype. A compounder is a high-return business that grows steadily. A turnaround value inflection is a business that misses a static floor but is cheap and improving. A tactical breakout is a Stage 2 stock, meaning one in a rising uptrend phase, that is pushing through a trading range to a new high, which we call a breakout. A stock in Stage 4, the falling downtrend phase, is excluded by a red line. EQT passes the debt, cash and stock-pay tests, misses the return floor, and is not a compounder, because return on invested capital is below 12%. It is not a tactical breakout either, because it is not in a rising uptrend. It fits the turnaround value inflection archetype, which needs a pass on every red line, at least two improving signals, a cheap valuation and a reward-to-risk ratio of at least 2.5 to 1.[2][7]

4. What the price is doing

A chart asks a different question from the accounts. It asks whether the stock is in a trend we can trade. We read the trend with moving averages, which are the average closing price over a set number of days. The 20-day moving average covers the last 20 closes. The 50-day moving average covers 50 closes. The 200-day moving average covers 200. A stock in Stage 2, the rising uptrend phase, closes above a rising 50-day average that sits above the 200-day average. A stock in Stage 4, the falling downtrend phase, closes below a falling 50-day average that sits below the 200-day average. EQT closed at $55.63 on October 23, 2026[19]. That is above all three averages: $52.50, $53.01 and $55.47. But it is only $0.16, or 0.29%, above the 200-day average. That label covers a price at or above its 200-day average with no rising uptrend. So this is a recovery that has not become an uptrend, and it is within pennies of losing even that label.

5. What could happen next

This chapter asks what the price already assumes and whether our view differs. The market value of EQT is $34.80 billion on 625.5 million shares at $55.63, with $5.54 billion of net debt on top for an enterprise value of $40.34 billion.[19][1] A reverse discounted cash flow (reverse DCF) works backward from that value. It asks what yearly growth in cash flow, for ten years, a 10% discount rate and an 18 times exit multiple would justify the price today.

We replaced the ledger’s figure. The ledger divides enterprise value, which includes debt, by free cash flow that is already after interest, and the cash flow it uses cannot be reproduced from the filings. Our version pairs equity value with the company’s free cash flow attributable to EQT for 2025, which is after interest and after the partners’ share of the midstream joint venture.[6][1]

The result is +0.80% a year, far below the ledger’s +4.11%, and it moves a long way with the base year: −3.25% on the twelve months to June 2026, which include the winter spike, and +9.22% on the second quarter times four. The honest reading is that the price asks for little growth if gas stays near 2025 levels and a lot if summer 2026 prices are normal.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate above rests on one assumption that could be wrong. For the reverse DCF it is the base year: we used 2025 free cash flow of $2.503 billion, but the twelve-month figure is 35% higher and the second-quarter run-rate 47% lower, and the answer swings from −3.3% to +9.2% growth.[6][1][2] For the central target it is the multiple: 16.4 times comes from three year-end readings, one of which, 119.32, is an outlier, and it is a re-rating from today’s 15.4 times on the same earnings. For the probability weights it is the shape of the tails: our bear case sits below the stop, so the stop does not protect against it.[18][20]

The bear case, in plain terms, is a producer with a good balance sheet that does not earn enough to justify a premium. Henry Hub stays near the $2.78 to $2.95 of July to September 2026, basis widens beyond the guided $(0.75) to $(0.65), the third-quarter EPS of about $0.37 repeats, and FY2027 earnings fall toward $3.26. If the stock then trades at the 11.9 times median forward multiple of four peers it is worth $38.79, which is 30.3% below the close and 23.1% below the zone top.[27][1][17][21][22][23][24]

The twelve months to June 2026 hold a first quarter at a $5.08 realized price and $1.83 billion of free cash flow attributable to EQT, and a second quarter at $2.65 and $330 million.[5][1] If the second-quarter pace is closer to normal, yearly free cash flow is about $1.3 billion, a 3.8% yield on today’s market value, and the stock would need to fall to be priced like its peers. Fatal-flaw scenario 2: the data-center premium arrives late or not at all

The seed thesis says EQT is a direct beneficiary of AI power demand. The evidence is an agreement in principle for Homer City, a July 2025 statement that definitive agreements were being finalized, one definitive power deal starting as early as 2031 and an LNG bridge contract from 2028 for 0.5 MTPA.[25][28][3][1] If projects slip, are cancelled or are supplied by other basins, the premium multiple has no cash flow behind it. Fatal-flaw scenario 3: capital allocation turns against per-share returns

What would show we are right to worry: a new acquisition announced with stock, or the share count rising again.

7. What we would do, and when

The verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT. The rule behind it is our support-distance ladder, which grades how far a price sits above its nearest support, measured in average true range units. The bands are up to 0.6 ATR for full size, up to 1.2 for standard size, up to 1.6 for a reduced starter order, and beyond that a no-buy band where we place an order lower or wait. The nearest support at or below the market is the 50-day average of $53.01, and the $55.63 close is 1.86 ATR above it, so the current market price (CMP) is in the no-buy band. The reward-to-risk ratio at CMP is 0.46 to 1 ($59.37 target less $55.63, over $55.63 less the $47.47 stop), against a floor of 2.5 to 1 for the tactical bucket, so the verdict cannot be a buy or an add-on-dip.[19]

Layer 1 (static levels and archetype), Layer 2 (operating KPIs) and Layer 3, which is this chapter, are our three tests. Layer 3 turns the others into a price, a size and an exit. A tactical position is one meant to last three to twelve months, so the 12-month target and the entry zone are time-matched. We looked at two limit-order zones. A zone on the 50-day and 20-day averages, $51.80 to $53.86 (the 50-day average of $53.01 plus 0.6 ATR at the top, and the 20-day average of $52.50 less half an ATR at the bottom), gives only 0.86 to 1 at the top and 1.75 to 1 at the bottom. That fails the floor, so it is not a buy zone. The central target is a value for October 2027, so a fill before December 31, 2026 leaves 10 to 12 months to reach it.

The second zone is built on the 63-day low of $48.17, which is also the 20-day swing low in the ledger, so it is verified support. Its top is that low plus 1.6 ATR, $50.43, the top of the reduced starter band. Its bottom is $48.50, about a quarter of an ATR (0.23 ATR, or $0.33) above the low, so a fill is not sitting on the floor itself. At the top the ratio is 3.02 to 1 with a stop distance of 5.87% of the zone top; at the bottom it is 10.55 to 1 with a stop distance of 2.12% of that price. The probability-weighted value gives 2.35 to 1 at the top, below the floor, and the difference is the bear tail that a stop cannot cap. The zone top is the same $50.43 used in the key figures and the order below.

The clearance is thin, and it rests on a re-rating. The central ratio of 3.02 to 1 exists only because the target lifts the multiple from 15.4 times to 16.4 times on the same $3.62 of earnings. At today’s multiple the target is $55.63 and the ratio is 1.76 to 1, a fail. Two single-variable stresses show how thin it is. First, earnings only: at 16.4 times the floor needs a target of $57.83 at the zone top, which means FY2027 EPS of at least $3.53; at the bear EPS of $3.26 the target is $53.46 and the ratio is 1.02 to 1. Second, multiple only: with EPS fixed at $3.62 the floor needs at least 15.98 times, and at 11.9 times, the median forward P/E of EXE, RRC, AR and CNX, the value is $43.08, which is 14.6% below the $50.43 entry and under the stop. We use the central target because the scoring floor is defined on it, and we show the weighted ratio of 2.35 to 1 beside it. A smaller position limits the dollars at risk. It does not repair either ratio.

Because the zone lies below the 20-day average of $52.50, a fill there will usually happen while Test C’s short-term turn is not passing. The order is then a support retest, not a confirmed turnaround. That is why the pre-fill check in the triggers below accepts a held 50-day trend and a held floor in place of a full Test C pass, and why the size stays at the smallest tier.

The stop follows the tactical-bucket rule: below the lower of the 20-day swing low and the 20-day or 50-day average, minus half an ATR. That is $48.17 less $0.705, or $47.47 (47.465 rounded), a structural invalidation level, meaning the price at which the idea is shown wrong, not an arbitrary percentage. Only one tier matters here. TIER_4, the smallest, sets a risk budget of 35 basis points (bps, where one basis point is 0.01% of net worth) and a ceiling of 2.0% of net worth. We chose it because the probability-weighted ratio is below the floor, the multiple cushion is thin and the revision trend is unverified. TIER_0 would mean zero size, and TIER_1 to TIER_3 allow up to 10%, 6.5% and 3.5%, so they do not apply. Size comes from risk-unit sizing. Portfolio heat is the total share of net worth lost if every stop is hit, and the book allows up to 12%. The weight is the smaller of the tier ceiling and the budget divided by the stop distance. At the zone top that is 35 bps over 5.87%, or about 5.96% of net worth, so the 2.0% ceiling binds, and that ceiling is the size in the key figures.

On net worth of $82,368.77 the ceiling is $1,647.38, which buys 32 shares at $50.43 for $1,613.76, or 1.96% of net worth. If the stop were hit at $47.47 the loss would be $94.72, or 11.5 bps. A stop does not cap a gap: if bad news opened the stock at $44.00 the loss would be $205.76 (25.0 bps, inside the 35 bps budget), and a fall to the $38.79 bear value would cost $372.48 (45.2 bps, over the budget). A stop order becomes a market order once triggered and fills at the next available price, which at the next open can be well below the stop.

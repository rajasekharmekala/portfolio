1. What the company does

EQT Corporation, based in Canonsburg, Pennsylvania, drills for natural gas in the Appalachian Basin and owns much of the pipe that carries the gas away.[4] Upstream sells natural gas, natural gas liquids (NGLs, such as propane and ethane) and a little oil. The two pipeline segments, gathering and transmission, earn fees for moving and storing gas.[2] In the third quarter of 2026 the pipes earned $286.3 million of operating income (gathering $208.5 million plus transmission $77.8 million) against $219.6 million for the wells, so more than half of segment profit came from fees that do not move with the gas price.[2]

Gas is measured in thousand cubic feet (Mcf), and EQT reports gas and liquids together in Mcfe, which converts liquids to a gas equivalent; a billion cubic feet equivalent is a Bcfe. The company sold 583 Bcfe in the third quarter of 2026, 21 Bcfe of it held back on purpose because prices were weak, against 634 Bcfe a year earlier.[1] The average realized price was $2.72 per Mcfe against $2.76 a year earlier, and $3.49 for the first nine months against $3.10.[1] Price is the swing factor. The Henry Hub spot price, the US benchmark, ran from $7.72 per million Btu in January 2026 to $2.77 in April, $2.78 in August and $2.95 in September.[20] The first quarter of 2026 included a winter storm and a $5.08 realized price, so any twelve-month figure that includes it overstates what an ordinary year earns.[13]

Who pays EQT? Utilities, power generators and industrial users in the basin, plus liquefied natural gas (LNG) buyers abroad. In 2026 the company signed a 10-year supply agreement for 325,000 dekatherms a day to a planned power plant in West Virginia, priced off the PJM power grid, and a 5-year offtake of 0.5 million tonnes a year of LNG starting in 2028.[10] The third-quarter release adds a 10-year LNG sales agreement with Ignitis Group of Lithuania, 7 billion cubic feet of new Gulf Coast storage that takes EQT’s storage there to 11 billion cubic feet, and an open season (a call for shippers to commit) for a 42-inch POWER Pipeline to the Clarington hub in Ohio.[1] The release states no volumes for the Ignitis deal, so we do not size it.

Hedging adds noise to the accounts. EQT sells gas forward through swaps and collars (contracts that fix or bracket a price), and the change in their value is booked in revenue. That line was a $3.78 billion loss in 2021 and a $4.64 billion loss in 2022, then gains of $1.84 billion in 2023, $51 million in 2024 and $291 million in 2025.[16][15] For the third quarter of 2026 EQT reported a $100 million gain, which lifts reported earnings per share to $0.44 against an adjusted $0.42.[8][1] We read cash flow and the company’s adjusted figures next to reported revenue, and we say which one we use each time.

The Range deal in plain terms

Each Range share converts into 0.987 EQT shares, with no cash. EQT holders expect to own about 73% of the combined company and Range holders about 27%, and the transaction is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027 after both sets of shareholders and antitrust regulators approve it.[3][2] The combined company is described as producing more than 9 billion cubic feet equivalent a day from nearly 3 million net acres, with more than 3,700 miles of pipe and a pro forma enterprise value above $50 billion.[4] Management expects $325 million of annual savings (synergies) within two years and more than $600 million over the long term.[4]

There are two break fees. If the deal fails in certain circumstances Range pays EQT $423 million, and EQT pays Range $1,148 million.[3] The agreement can be ended if it has not closed by October 25, 2027, or April 25, 2028 if only antitrust or legal conditions remain.[3] EQT’s own quarterly report adds a plain warning: the deal may not be completed, the benefits may not arrive, and the combined company’s earnings per share may be diluted.[2] The chief executive told analysts he sees no antitrust problem; that is his view, not a regulator’s.[5]

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Mcfe and Bcfe are thousand and billion cubic feet of gas, with liquids converted to a gas equivalent so one number covers everything sold.
  • Henry Hub is the Louisiana pricing point for US gas, and the NYMEX futures contract settles there.
  • Basis and differential are the gap between the Henry Hub price and the price EQT actually gets after pipeline costs and local supply; EQT guides to a 2026 differential of ($0.55) to ($0.35) per Mcf.[1]
  • A hedge is a contract that fixes a gas price (a swap) or sets a floor and a ceiling (a collar) for a set volume.
  • Free cash flow (FCF) is cash from operations minus capital spending; EQT also reports a version attributable to EQT that removes outside partners’ share of its pipeline joint ventures.
  • The exchange ratio is the number of EQT shares each Range share turns into: 0.987. Synergies are cost savings and extra margin a merger is expected to create.
  • Net debt is total debt minus cash. Pro forma means as if the two companies were already combined.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

EQT’s first strength is cost and scale. Proved reserves were 28.0 trillion cubic feet equivalent at the end of 2025, up 7% in the year.[14] Operating cost was $1.09 per Mcfe in the third quarter of 2026, at the low end of the $1.09 to $1.23 guidance range, up from $1.00 a year earlier partly on higher selling and administrative cost; the full-year guide is $1.05 to $1.20 and the fourth-quarter guide is $1.14 to $1.28.[1][10] Management puts its unlevered free cash flow breakeven at about $2 per million Btu and says the combined company would stay below $2. That is a company-defined measure we cannot verify, but the third quarter supports the direction: at a $2.72 realized price EQT still produced $283 million of free cash flow attributable to EQT after $638 million of capital spending.[4][1]

The second strength is balance-sheet repair. Net debt was $9.1 billion at the end of 2024, $7.7 billion at the end of 2025 and $5.47 billion at September 30, 2026, with total debt of $5.55 billion and about $3.5 billion of liquidity.[18][14][1] The first nine months of 2026 repaid $2.24 billion of debt, and net interest expense fell to $244 million from $333 million a year earlier.[2] Fitch upgraded EQT to BBB in the first quarter, and on October 7 EQT set up a $3.5 billion commercial paper program backed by its credit line.[13][9] The weak point is the follow-through: the board’s share repurchase programme has bought $622.1 million of stock since 2021, none in the third quarter, and it expires on December 31, 2026.[2]

The weaknesses are just as concrete. EQT is a price taker. Third-quarter volume fell 8% to 583 Bcfe, adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, with settled hedges only) fell 14% to $1,143 million, adjusted earnings per share fell 19% to $0.42 and free cash flow attributable to EQT fell 42% to $283 million.[1] Returns are modest because EQT paid market prices for assets. Goodwill is $2.06 billion and equity investments, mainly pipelines, are $3.96 billion.[2] Our return on invested capital is 7.4% for 2025 and 8.8% for the twelve months to September 2026, both under the 12% floor (Chapter 3).[15][2] Per share, the picture is weaker still: diluted weighted shares rose from 413.2 million in 2023 to 629.2 million in the third quarter of 2026, and the Range deal would add about 230.6 million more, a 36.7% increase (our arithmetic from the exchange ratio and Range’s 233.68 million shares).[15][2][3][27]

Some things are not yet in the numbers. EQT’s 2026 guidance was raised in July by about 90 Bcfe because compression projects slow well decline, not because of new data-center contracts.[10] The management line from July, that at least one more power or data-center agreement would come before the end of 2026, has not been met as of October 28 in anything we read.[12][1] The deal presentation estimates that Appalachian gas demand growth could be large, but a presentation chart is not a signed contract.[5]

Regulatory and permitting

The Range deal needs approval from a majority of the votes cast by EQT shareholders for the share issuance, a majority of Range’s voting power, expiry of the Hart-Scott-Rodino antitrust waiting period, and an effective registration statement on Form S-4.[3][2] As of October 28 no S-4 appears in the SEC filing feed, and no meeting dates have been announced.[32] A regulator could ask for assets to be sold; the agreement commits both sides to reasonable best efforts, including divestitures, subject to limits.[3] For EQT’s own pipelines the news is positive: MVP Southgate has all key regulatory approvals, and $85 million of capital contributions were moved forward to finish construction by the end of 2026.[10]

Hedging, derivatives and why GAAP earnings mislead

As of October 20, 2026 EQT had 108 million dekatherms hedged for the fourth quarter at put floors of $3.72, 62 million for the first quarter of 2027 at $3.65, and 156 million and 174 million for the second and third quarters of 2027, with swaps near $3.13 to $3.14 and put floors of $3.00.[1] Those floors sit above the $2.78 to $2.95 Henry Hub readings of August and September, so the hedges are in the money today, and they cushion the winter more than the spring.[20] GAAP earnings include the change in value of unsettled contracts, which is why the third-quarter reported figure ($0.44) differs from the adjusted one ($0.42); analysts follow the adjusted figure.[1]

Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Management has a good record on operations and a poor one on the capital-return promise it made three months ago. Volume and capital spending came in at or better than guidance in each of the first three quarters of 2026[13][10][1], third-quarter unit cost was at the low end of its guide[1], and the company says its dividend has grown about 7% a year since 2022[1]. The year-end 2025 net debt target was missed by about $0.7 billion[18][14], and the 2026 net debt and cash-flow goals now look out of reach at today’s gas prices[14][1].

The capital-return item is the one to weigh. In July the chief executive described the next chapter of value creation as being driven primarily by share buybacks, and the chief financial officer said the company would be more aggressive with buybacks at the then share price.[12] The October 28 quarterly report shows no repurchase in the quarter, and the next news was an all-stock acquisition that issues about 230.6 million new shares.[2][3] The deal may well be good for owners, as Chapter 5 shows on consensus numbers, but it is the opposite of shrinking the share count, and the earlier promise was not kept.

Fatal-flaw scenario 2: gas stays cheap and the cash flow base was a winter fluke

The verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT, and it is weaker than on October 23. The rule behind it is our support-distance ladder, which grades how far a price sits above its nearest support, measured in average true range units. The nearest support at or below the market is the 20-day average of $53.12, and the $56.27 close is 2.02 ATR above it, so the current market price (CMP) is in the no-buy band.[33] The reward-to-risk ratio at CMP is 0.11 to 1 (the $57.24 target less $56.27, over $56.27 less the $47.39 stop), against a floor of 2.5 to 1, so the verdict cannot be a buy or an add-on-dip.

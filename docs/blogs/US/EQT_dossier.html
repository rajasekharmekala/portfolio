1. What the company does & day-to-day vertical operations

EQT works across the Appalachian gas chain. It drills and produces natural gas, natural gas liquids (NGLs) and oil. It also gathers and processes gas, and runs interstate pipelines and storage [2]. The current SEC filing calls these segments Upstream, Gathering and Transmission [2]. Links between wells and pipes give EQT more control over where gas flows. They do not remove commodity price or local basis risk [2][3].

A well starts with acreage and a plan. EQT picks a site, drills a horizontal well and uses hydraulic fracturing to open the rock. Crews then manage flowback, water and early production. Operators watch pressure, decline, pipeline room and price. Volume, unit cost and capital spend show what each well earns [1][2][14].

Raw gas moves from the well through gathering lines to compressors and processing plants. These steps remove water and separate NGLs when the work pays. EQT owns field systems near many of its wells [2]. That can cut handoffs. The company still pays to gather, process and transport gas. Q3 unit costs include those steps, plus lease costs, taxes and company overhead [1][2].

Transmission moves gas from Appalachia to other pipelines, local utilities and market hubs. EQT runs regulated pipelines and storage [2]. Its investment in the Mountain Valley Pipeline is part of this segment [2]. More routes can reach more buyers and ease local bottlenecks. A route still needs approvals, contracts and enough volume to earn its cost [2][3][16].

EQT’s marketing group schedules gas sales and pipeline capacity. It also uses commodity contracts to manage price and location risk. Its filing lists marketers, utilities and industrial buyers among its customers [3]. The cash cycle runs from drilling and producing to gathering, transport, sale and collection. Price, local basis, transport costs, hedge results, bills due from buyers and capital spend all affect cash [1][2][3].

Where cash enters and leaves the operating cycle

Cash begins with a capital commitment before production: acreage, drilling, completion, water handling, gathering connections and sometimes midstream infrastructure. Once a well is flowing, EQT receives proceeds from natural gas, NGL and oil sales, while gathering and transmission operations can collect service revenue. Operating cash must then cover field expenses, transportation, taxes, maintenance and growth capital; quarterly free cash flow can therefore move even when physical production remains steady [1][2][3].

Q3 2026 illustrates the distinction between field execution and shareholder cash generation. EQT reported 583 Bcfe of sales volume, including 21 Bcfe of strategic curtailments, and $638 million of capital expenditures. Net cash from operations was $1.083 billion, but free cash flow attributable to EQT was $283 million. The FCF figure also depends on capital allocation and noncontrolling interests, so it is the better signal for equity funding than production volume alone [1].

An integrated network gives EQT more control over transport options and operating schedules. It also adds fixed costs, regulatory oversight and projects whose returns arrive only after construction and contracted demand. The proposed POWER line and the Leaf River storage arrangement should be treated as future market-access options with execution requirements, not as existing earnings streams [1][16][32].

2. Competitive moat, industry structure & external learning hub

EQT’s main edge is its Appalachian acreage and the gathering, processing and transmission assets near its wells. This overlap gives EQT more control over routes, capacity and price. The filings list links to pipelines in the Gulf Coast, Midwest and Northeast [2][3][14][15]. Access helps only when the sale price, after transport and processing costs, beats the cost of moving gas.

Natural gas remains a commodity business. Henry Hub, local basis, weather, storage, power use, LNG exports and pipeline limits shape EQT’s realized price. Q3 realized price was $2.72/Mcfe, down from $2.76/Mcfe a year earlier. Management said marketing and curtailment kept the differential favorable to its guide. This helps control price, but it is not pricing power. If supply grows faster than transport, Appalachian discounts can widen and erase cost gains [1][2][28].

Future demand creates options, not guaranteed earnings. EQT’s Q3 release described an open season for the proposed POWER Pipeline. The 42-inch line would connect Pennsylvania supply with Clarington, Ohio, and serve power, industrial and data-center demand. EQT also reported a ten-year LNG supply agreement through a subsidiary with Ignitis. It has contracted storage tied to a Leaf River expansion [1][16][31][32]. The LNG deal is contracted business. POWER is still a project, not operating throughput.

The all-stock Range deal is the main near-term change. Range shareholders would receive 0.987 EQT shares for each share. After closing, Range holders would own about 27% of the combined company; current EQT holders would own about 73%. Shareholder and regulatory conditions remain. EQT would add nearby Appalachian inventory and output, but current holders also face share issuance, integration, governance and commodity-cycle risk [10][11][12][13].

Appalachian producers and pipeline operators compete for the same buyers and routes. Range is a close basin peer and a proposed partner. Antero offers a gas-and-liquids comparison. CNX is another regional producer with gathering, transport, marketing and processing [26][27][28]. Q3 filings show that product mix, hedges and owned infrastructure shape results. The peer table compares business models, not like-for-like P/E ratios.

Why integration helps—and where it does not

Production and transport work as one system. If wells produce more than forecast, EQT’s gathering and transport access can help move the extra gas. If prices fall, EQT can curtail low-value production and protect some cash. In Q3, the release linked a favorable price gap to marketing and curtailment [1]. Lower volume and FCF show the cost of that choice. Protecting price can mean leaving gas in the ground. The network still needs upkeep and capital [1][2].

Long-term transport deals and storage can add support. Their value depends on the term, buyer, toll and use [2][3]. Filings list firm pipeline capacity, MVP and EQT’s current business segments [2][3]. Before valuing a new route, check its permits, build status, signed capacity, timing and expected return.

2B. Operational business KPIs, structural potential & CEO walk-the-talk

For EQT, recurring-revenue metrics such as customer churn do not describe the business. The operating scorecard is physical sales, realized price, per-unit field and transport costs, cash conversion, capital intensity and balance-sheet capacity. Those indicators connect drilling decisions to distributable cash and show whether infrastructure is earning its keep [1][2][3].

Current evidence is mixed. Q3 included strategic curtailments and volume fell from a year earlier. Q3 adjusted EBITDA and attributable FCF also declined year over year, even as capital expenditures came in below guidance and net cash from operations rose. The delivery record therefore supports an execution advantage but not a clean earnings-growth claim [1][4][5].

The company has not demonstrated its full structural potential. The existing pipeline portfolio, integrated gathering and the new LNG/storage contracts widen market access; the proposed POWER route creates another path to power and industrial demand [1][2][16][31][32].

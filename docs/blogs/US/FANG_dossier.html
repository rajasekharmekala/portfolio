1. What the company does

Diamondback finds, drills and sells crude oil, natural gas liquids (NGLs) and natural gas. All of its wells are in the Permian Basin of West Texas. At June 30, 2026 it held about 902,005 net acres, of which 808,401 are in the Midland Basin and 93,604 in the Delaware Basin, and it ran 17 drilling rigs and five completion crews.[3] It earns the market price of each product, minus transport and quality differences, so its profit moves with prices that it does not set. WTI is the US benchmark price of crude oil, and Henry Hub is the matching benchmark for natural gas.

The company is large. In the second quarter of 2026 it produced 1,017.7 thousand barrels of oil equivalent per day (MBOE/d), made up of 525.2 thousand barrels of oil, 23.4 million barrels of NGLs and 128.3 billion cubic feet of gas over the quarter.[3][4] A barrel of oil equivalent (Boe) counts six thousand cubic feet of gas as one barrel, which lets a reader add gas to oil. Oil was 52 percent of volume, NGLs 25 percent and gas 23 percent.[3] Production has grown from 375.3 thousand Boe per day in 2021 to 921.0 in 2025, a 2.5-fold rise, and the 2026 guidance was raised in August to more than 1,000 thousand Boe per day from 972 in May.[14][10][4][6]

Revenue comes mostly from oil. In the second quarter oil sales were $4,627 million, NGL sales $435 million and natural gas sales were negative $276 million.[3] Gas revenue turned negative because Permian pipelines were full and gas prices at the Waha trading hub, the local Permian price, fell below zero. The company realized an average of minus $2.15 per thousand cubic feet in the quarter, against minus $0.34 after hedges.[4][5] The 'sales of purchased oil' line of $739 million is oil that Diamondback buys from others and resells to use up pipeline space. It carries almost the same cost ($730 million of purchased oil expense), so it adds size to reported revenue but only $9 million of profit.[3]

Diamondback grew by buying other producers and then selling what it did not need. On September 10, 2024 it bought Endeavor Energy Resources for $7.3 billion in cash and about 117.27 million shares.[11] On April 1, 2025 it bought Double Eagle for $3.1 billion in cash and about 6.84 million shares, which added 40,000 net acres and 342 net horizontal locations.[3] It then sold assets it did not need: its water subsidiary to Deep Blue for $694 million in October 2025, its 27.5 percent stake in the EPIC crude pipeline for $504 million that month, and Viper's non-Permian royalty acres for about $610 million in February 2026.[3] The cost of the Endeavor deal shows in the share count, which rose from 178.7 million at the end of 2023 to 291.0 million at the end of 2024, and in the debt, which rose from $6.6 billion to $13.0 billion over the same year.[12][10]

What Viper and the Stephens family stake change

Diamondback owns about 39 percent of Viper Energy, a company that holds mineral and royalty interests under Permian land and collects a share of production without paying drilling costs. Diamondback controls Viper, so the accounts include 100 percent of Viper's cash flow, and the other holders' share appears as non-controlling interest of $6,085 million on the balance sheet.[3] In the first half of 2026 Diamondback paid $279 million of dividends to those outside holders.[3] Viper's own 2026 guidance is 132.5 to 135.0 thousand Boe per day, about 13 percent of the consolidated total.[4] On July 1, 2026 Viper paid $339 million in cash and 3.69 million Viper shares for Riverbend, and on August 3 Diamondback agreed to sell more royalty interests to Viper for 3.65 million Viper units, which moves value between two companies Diamondback controls.[3]

The Endeavor deal also left a large holder behind. The former Endeavor owners, led by SGF FANG Holdings, held about 26.7 percent of Diamondback stock at June 30, 2026.[3] On September 16 SGF sold 9,079,675 shares at $205.26, and its remaining 64,957,047 shares are 23.2 percent of the company.[18] On September 29 the company and SGF amended their agreement so that SGF names two directors if it owns at least 10 percent but less than 25 percent, and two directors resigned under that stepdown.[17] The lock-up on SGF's last sale ran for 30 days from September 16.[18] For a buyer today, this is a source of supply and not a reason to doubt the business, and Chapter 6 treats it as a risk to the entry price.

  • NGLs are natural gas liquids such as ethane and propane, which are pulled out of wet gas and sold separately.
  • WTI and Henry Hub are the benchmark prices for US crude oil and US natural gas, and Waha is the local gas price in the Permian Basin.
  • Free cash flow is cash from operations less capital spending, the cash left for debt, dividends and buybacks.
  • Net debt is borrowings minus cash.
  • A ceiling test is the accounting check that writes down the book value of oil and gas properties when trailing prices are low; the write-down, called an impairment, costs no cash.

  • A limit order is an order to buy only at a stated price or lower, and a stop loss is a price at which we would sell to end a losing trade.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

Diamondback's competitive position rests on three things. They are the cost of each barrel, the size of its drilling inventory and the strength of its balance sheet. On cost, its cash operating expense was $10.96 per Boe in the second quarter of 2026. That is $5.96 of lease operating expense, $3.26 of production and property taxes, $1.22 of gathering and transport and $0.52 of cash overhead.[4] The part that management controls is lease, transport and overhead. It was $7.70 per Boe. That equals the $7.70 for all of 2025 ($5.55, $1.53 and $0.62) and sits below the $8.25 midpoint of the 2026 guidance ranges.[4][7] Taxes rise with the oil price, which is why the headline figure rose from $10.10 a year earlier.[4] The drilling teams cut the time to drill a well from 30 days to five over several years. They now pump continuously for 21.3 hours a day, and equipment cost per well fell about 14 percent from the first quarter.[29][5]

On inventory, the 10-K counts 8,854 gross (6,541 net) identified horizontal drilling locations that work at $50 WTI. The average lateral length is about 10,924 feet.[10] At the 476 net wells completed in 2025 that is about 14 years of work. At the 2026 pace of 294 net completions in the first half it is about 11 years (our arithmetic).[10][3] Proved reserves at December 31, 2025 were 3,618 million Boe, 49 percent oil, up 2 percent in a year. Of that, 2,521 million Boe were already producing.[7] At 2025 production of 336.2 million Boe that is about 10.8 years of reserves. At the 2026 guidance of 1,000 thousand Boe per day it is about 9.9 years (our arithmetic).[7][10]

On the balance sheet, net debt was $12,304 million at June 30, 2026, which is 1.04 times the $11,833 million of Adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) earned over the last four quarters.[4][8][7][6] Debt fell $1.3 billion in the quarter and total debt is down $2.6 billion in twelve months.[5] The company had $3.4 billion of liquidity, no drawn borrowings on its $3.0 billion credit line, and about $1.5 billion of notes maturing within a year.[3] In April it bought back $777 million of 2051 and 2052 bonds at an average of 81.1 percent of face value, which shows the debt was cheap to retire.[3]

Where the business is weak

Diamondback is a price taker in one basin. Oil and gas prices decide its profit. A Permian-wide problem such as full gas pipelines hits it directly. Second-quarter gas sales were negative $276 million, and Waha prices touched a record low of about minus $10 per thousand cubic feet in spring.[3][5] The company expects relief as new takeaway capacity more than doubles its long-haul gas pipeline space by year end. Its third-quarter gas price was already $1.12 per thousand cubic feet.[5][16] Its oil is almost unhedged. The main protection is put options, including a $52.57 strike on 190,000 barrels a day for July to September, and they pay out only if oil falls far below today's price.[3]

The accounting record has blemishes. The full-cost ceiling test wrote down oil and gas properties by $3,652 million in 2025 and by $1.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026, both non-cash, because the trailing 12-month average price used in the test was low.[10][3] The company says it does not expect another write-down in the third quarter, but it also warns that a fall in trailing prices could cause one.[3] Lease operating expense rose to $5.96 per Boe from $5.26 a year earlier, partly because the company sold its water-disposal subsidiary and now pays a third party.[4][3] The company also dropped its rule of returning at least 50 percent of free cash flow from the second quarter, so the dividend and buyback pledge is now at the board's discretion.[3]

The street is positive on the stock. S&P Global's 32-analyst average target is $239.45 and 27 of the 32 ratings are Buy or Strong Buy; MarketBeat's 31-analyst average is $228.43.[21][22] The stock closed at $206.22, so the two average targets are 16.1 percent and 10.8 percent above it.[1]

Diamondback produced 1,017.7 thousand Boe per day in the second quarter. EOG made 1,410.4, Occidental 1,433, Devon 1,359 and Permian Resources 376.4.[4][30][33][32][34] Diamondback carries more debt than EOG, with $12.8 billion of total debt against $7.9 billion. The case for owning it rests on cost, inventory and the oil price, and not on a cheaper multiple. Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Management made specific promises in the last two years, and the record can be checked against reported figures. The largest was the cash return framework. The board's stated minimum was to return 50 percent of free cash flow each quarter through the dividend and buybacks. It returned about 57 percent in 2024 and 54 percent in 2025, then about 62 percent in the fourth quarter of 2025 and 50 percent in the first quarter of 2026.[9][7][6] In the second quarter of 2026 the board removed the minimum, and the company returned $452 million, or 19.4 percent of $2,331 million of adjusted free cash flow.[3][4] The CEO's letter says that removing the formula let the company 'materially accelerate absolute debt reduction,' and it did: total debt fell $1.3 billion and net debt $1.6 billion in one quarter.[5] Whether the change is a broken promise or a sound choice depends on what the cash did instead, and here it retired bonds at 81.1 percent of face value.[3]

The second test is guidance on volume and spending. In February 2025 the company guided 2025 oil production to 485 to 498 thousand barrels per day and capital spending to $3.8 to $4.2 billion. It delivered 497.2 thousand barrels per day and $3.52 billion.[9][10] In February 2026 it guided 2026 oil to 500 to 510 thousand barrels per day and capital to $3.6 to $3.9 billion. In May and August it raised oil to more than 522 thousand barrels per day and capital to about $3.9 billion, the top of the first range, to grow into the oil price spike.[7][6][4] Volume beat, and capital reached the top of the original range, so the company did what it said it would do but spent more to do it.

The third test is the balance sheet. After the Endeavor merger the 10-K said the company would 'continue strengthening the balance sheet.'[10] Net debt was $14.6 billion at the end of 2025 and $12.3 billion at June 30, 2026, and the company reduced total debt by about 17 percent in twelve months.[7][4][5] It also raised the base dividend from $1.00 to $1.05 and then to $1.10 a quarter between 2025 and 2026.[6][7]

Momentum and turnaround inflection gate

The momentum gate runs three tests, and each asks a different question. Test A asks whether a stock is already in a Stage 2 advance. The close must sit above a rising 50-day average that is above the 200-day average. The 200-day average must not be falling over 63 sessions. The 63-day return must beat the S&P 500, and the close must be within 1.6 ATR of support.

Test B asks whether a stock is breaking out of a flat base. The 50-day average may fall no more than 0.8 percent in 21 sessions. The 28-day return must beat the index. The close must be within 1.5 ATR of the 63-day base low. Volume should dry up in the base and then expand on the signal day.

Test C asks whether a beaten-down stock is turning. The close must be above the 63-day low and back above the 20-day average. It must sit within 2.0 ATR of support, with a 14-day RSI of at least 32. Support in these tests means the highest structural level at or below the price. Today that is the 50-day average at $197.40.

5. What could happen next

A reverse DCF (discounted cash flow) turns the usual valuation question around. Instead of forecasting cash flow and finding a price, it takes the price and asks what yearly growth in cash flow, for ten years, a 10 percent discount rate and an 18 times exit multiple would need to justify it. A low growth rate means the price asks for little, and a negative one means the price already assumes a fall in cash flow. The frozen ledger carries no reverse-DCF figure for Diamondback, so we built our own from the filings.[1]

We used trailing free cash flow of $6,497 million (cash from operations of $10,143 million less capital spending of $3,646 million for the four quarters to June 2026).[10][3] Enterprise value is $76,136 million: 280,024,353 shares on the cover of the June 10-Q times the $206.22 close, which is $57,747 million, plus $12,304 million of net debt, plus $6,085 million for the share of Viper that outside holders own, taken at book value.[3][4] The result is minus 1.47 percent a year, with a free cash flow yield on enterprise value of 8.53 percent. If we left the Viper minority out of enterprise value, the answer would be minus 2.59 percent, but that would pair a cash flow that includes 100 percent of Viper with a price that covers only the parent's share.

Read plainly, the market is paying for cash flow that falls slightly from the last four quarters, and those four quarters averaged a realized oil price of about $73.40 by our calculation from the quarterly releases.[8][7][6][4] The oil market is far above that. WTI spot was $103.37 on October 27, the December 2026 contract is about $100.50, the 2027 contracts average $88.99 and December 2028 is $74.02.[26][28] The EIA expects Brent to average $105 in the fourth quarter of 2026 and $84 in 2027.[25] Using our own sensitivity of $0.14 billion of free cash flow per $1 of realized oil, the same test asks for plus 0.19 percent a year at a $65 WTI base, minus 4.26 percent at $81, minus 6.07 percent at the 2027 strip and minus 8.26 percent at $100. So the price does not capitalize today's oil price. It capitalizes a world where oil settles near $65 to $70, and it is a bet on how fast the war premium fades.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

This chapter makes the bear case against our own work. It takes each estimate in Chapter 5, names its weakest assumption, says what evidence would show it wrong and when that evidence arrives. The bear case in one sentence is that Diamondback's earnings are a function of an oil price that a war is holding up, so the stock is priced for a state of the world that can end with a headline, and after a 12 percent monthly gain it has collected much of the good news.[1]

Fatal flaw 1: the conflict ends and oil gaps down

Diamondback's own filing says that the Middle East conflict moved the global oil market from surplus to deficit in 2026 and raised benchmark prices.[3] WTI averaged $83.00 in the first half, $92.79 in the second quarter and was $103.37 on October 27, after $109.72 four days earlier.[3][33][26] A settlement could take $20 off the oil price in days. On our sensitivity that is $8.92 of yearly EPS, or about $99 a share at 11.09 times. A stop order does not cap that loss, because the stock can open below the stop and the order then fills at the next available price. Our bear value of $153.97 is $25.13 under the $179.10 stop, so a gap from the zone top of $190.65 to that value would cost $36.68 a share, or 19.2 percent.

Fatal flaw 2: gas, water and cost drag, and a one-basin concentration

Gas is 23 percent of volume and NGLs another 25 percent, and neither shares oil's rally. Second-quarter gas sales were negative $276 million, and the realized price of minus $2.15 per thousand cubic feet was the result of full pipelines in one basin.[3][4] Takeaway capacity is due to more than double by year end, and the third-quarter realized gas price is already $1.12, but the EIA expects Henry Hub to average $3.16 in 2027 and Waha tends to trade below Henry Hub.[5][16][25] Costs are also not guaranteed: lease operating expense rose to $5.96 per Boe from $5.26 a year earlier, and the COO said casing prices would rise in the second half of the year.[4][29] If gas and cost drag are worth $1 a share of EPS more than we assume, the base value falls by about $11 at 11.09 times.

Fatal flaw 3: supply from a large holder, and capital allocation at the top of the cycle

SGF still holds 64,957,047 shares, which are worth about $13.4 billion at the close, and Diamondback's three-month average volume is 2.36 million shares a day, so the stake equals about 28 trading days of volume.[18][24] SGF sold 9.08 million shares at $205.26 on September 16, and its lock-up ended on October 16. The company has also dropped its 50 percent return pledge, so its purchases now depend on the board's view of value, and its own recent purchases were at $182 to $187.[3][4] The history of deals includes $7.3 billion of cash for Endeavor in 2024 and $3.1 billion for Double Eagle in 2025, and Viper paid $339 million in cash for Riverbend on July 1, 2026, so a new large purchase at a high oil price is a real risk.[11][3]

Fatal flaw 4: the 11.09 times multiple is a proxy and sits above today's multiple

Our base target uses 11.09 times, the median of five quarter-end forward P/E values printed on Yahoo Finance, and not a published three-year median. The stock trades at 9.81 times 2027 consensus EPS and 9.16 times 2026 EPS.[24][20] Producers usually earn lower multiples when earnings are lifted by a price spike, because investors expect the spike to fade, so 11.09 times may be generous at the top of an oil cycle. If EPS is $21.02 and the stock simply keeps today's 9.81 times, the base value equals today's price, $206.22, and the zone-top reward-to-risk is 1.35 to 1, below the 2.5 floor. On that no-re-rating anchor the floor is met only at $186.85 or lower, so the upper part of the zone clears 2.5 only if the stock earns the 11.09 times anchor.

Because Diamondback passes Layer 1 as a turnaround and Layer 2 with a 4.5 score, the technical routing rule says to keep it on the list with a limit order and not to reject it.

Chapter 4 named the support: the ledger's active anchor is the 50-day average of $197.40, and the stop uses the 20-day swing low of $181.85 instead. The support-distance ladder is the framework's table that turns the distance from a price to its nearest verified support, measured in ATR, into an action. Under 0.6 ATR is the full-size band, under 1.2 is standard size, under 1.6 is the limit-order band with reduced size, and beyond 1.6 is the NO_BUY band where we place an order and wait. The gate measures from the highest support at or below the current price, which today is the 50-day average, 1.60 ATR below the close. At that distance no order is placed at the market.

The entry zone asks a different question: where would the highest support at or below the price be after a pullback? A fill near $190 sits below the 20-day average of $196.25 and the 50-day average of $197.40, so the nearest verified support under it is the swing low of $181.85. The zone is therefore built from the swing low. The zone top of $190.65 is the swing low plus 1.6 ATR, the outer edge of the limit-order band, and the zone bottom of $185.15 is the swing low plus 0.6 ATR, the edge of the full-size band. Any fill in the zone therefore sits at or above 0.6 ATR from the swing low and no more than 1.6 ATR above it.

The floor test comes from the other side: with a base target of $233.11 and a stop of $179.10, the highest price that still gives 2.5 to 1 is $194.53, so the zone top sits $3.88 inside it. That holds on the base anchor of 11.09 times. On the no-re-rating anchor, where EPS of $21.02 keeps today's multiple of 9.81 times and the value equals the close, the floor is met only at $186.85 or lower, and that is our working limit.

Sizing follows from the tier. At net worth of $88,229.73, the TIER_3 ceiling of 3.5 percent is $3,088, or about 16 shares at $186.85, and the 50 bps risk budget is $441. Dividing the budget by the 6.06 percent stop distance at the zone top gives 8.25 percent, which the 3.5 percent tier maximum caps, so the head of this report shows 3.5 percent as the risk-unit size. The first purchase is smaller. Before the third-quarter report, we cap the position at 1.75 percent of net worth, which allows 8 shares and $1,495 at $186.85, for two reasons: the zone sits in the reduced-size band of the ladder, and the results are due within days, with the call estimated for November 3, 2026 and the release for November 2 after the close, neither confirmed by the company.[23] A gap makes the cap concrete. If 8 shares were bought at $186.85 and the stock gapped to our bear value of $153.97, the loss would be $263, or 29.8 bps, inside the 50 bps budget; 16 shares would lose $526, or 59.6 bps, over the budget. A stop does not cap a gap loss, because a stop order becomes a market order once the price trades through it and fills at the next open, which can be well below the stop. A fill at the stop itself would cost $62 on 8 shares, or 7.0 bps. The second 8 shares are bought only after the third-quarter call and a fresh check.

The order is conditional, not standing. A price touch is not confirmation, and a fill below the 20-day and 50-day averages will fail Tests A and C by construction, so the pre-fill check tests the pullback route that the framework uses for a Stage 3 pullback above a rising 200-day average. The order is placed only after a written check passes at the prior close, and the check is repeated at every close while the order rests. The check has four parts. One: the stock closes above a rising 200-day average ($189.08 now), the swing low of $181.85 is intact, the relative strength index is at least 32 and volume is drying up. Two: 2027 consensus EPS is not below $21.02 and the December 2027 WTI contract is not below $75 (it was $81.06 on October 29).[20][28] Three: reward-to-risk at the limit price clears 2.5 to 1 on the base anchor and on the no-re-rating anchor, which today caps the limit at $186.85. Four: no results release falls before the next close, so we cancel the order at the close before the report and re-arm it only after the call and a new check. A resting order can still fill between two checks, which is why the first tranche stays at 8 shares. After a fill, the fill-date check has a defined failure: if the close is below the 200-day average or any of the four parts fails, we sell the 8 shares at the next open and do not wait for the stop.

The let-winners-run rule governs what happens after a fill. The framework never trims a Stage 2 winner just because RSI is above 70 or the weight passes 15 percent, and it lets a winner grow to 22 percent of net worth while the stop trails. The trailing level is the rising 21-day EMA or 50-day SMA. An SMA is the plain average of the last N closing prices, and an EMA is the same average with more weight on recent days, so it turns sooner. For Diamondback we would raise the stop to the 50-day average once the price closes above the base target of $233.11, and we would hold for up to 12 months, to about October 2027, which matches the horizon of the base target. A TIER_3 position is unlikely to reach 15 percent, so the rule matters mostly as a promise not to sell a good trade early.

Structural invalidation is the price or event that shows the thesis wrong and not merely noisy. For price it is a trade at or below $179.10. For events it is any of these. A third-quarter report or call cuts the 2026 production or capital plan. A 2027 plan spends well above the $3.9 billion of 2026. Cash returns stay near 19 percent of free cash flow with no cut in debt. Or the company makes a new large acquisition. There is also an upside path that would change the zone. Each $1 added to 2027 consensus EPS adds $11.09 to the base target and lifts the highest price that clears 2.5 to 1 by about $3.17. If estimates catch up to the futures strip at about $24.58, the target becomes $272.6 and the floor price becomes $205.8, which is the current close. After the November call we would then re-underwrite and not wait for $190. The next scheduled test is the third-quarter report.

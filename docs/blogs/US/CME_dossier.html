Before you read

CME Group runs the world's biggest futures marketplace and the clearing house that guarantees every trade. It earns a fee on each contract, a fee for its price data, and interest on the cash that traders post as security. This report finds an excellent business at a full price, so the verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT: do not buy at $294.04, and place a buy order between $266 and $275 instead. The wait is about price, not quality, because the stock must fall at least 6.5 percent before the reward is at least twice the risk. We would be wrong if volume keeps rising and the stock never dips, or if volume collapses and the earnings forecast behind our target falls with it.

1. What the company does

CME Group is a Chicago-based company that runs the exchanges where businesses and investors trade futures and options. A future is a contract to buy or sell something at a set price on a later date, such as a barrel of oil, a bushel of corn, a stock index or a US Treasury note. Farmers, airlines, banks, pension funds and individual traders use these contracts to protect themselves against price swings, or to bet on them. CME's four exchanges (CME, CBOT, NYMEX and COMEX) list products in six groups: interest rates, stock indexes, foreign exchange, energy, farm products and metals, plus cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin and Ether. [3] Almost all trading happens on one electronic system, CME Globex, and 93 percent of volume was electronic in the third quarter of 2026. [2]

Think of CME as a toll road with a bank vault attached. The toll is the fee paid each time a contract trades. In the third quarter of 2026 customers traded 1,884.5 million contracts at an average fee of $0.679 each, which produced $1,279.4 million of futures and options contract fees (1,884.5 million times $0.679 is about $1,279.6 million, matching the reported figure within rounding). [2] The full clearing and transaction fee line in the income statement was larger, at $1,378.1 million, or 79 percent of the quarter's $1,737.3 million of revenue. [1] The $98.7 million difference is $74.0 million of fees from the cash bond and currency platforms BrokerTec and EBS, plus about $24.7 million of other items. [2] The second source is market data: traders pay for live and historical prices, and that line earned $243.1 million in the quarter, 14 percent of revenue. [1] The remaining 7 percent is a mix of other services. [1]

The vault is the clearing house. CME stands between every buyer and every seller, so each side only has to trust CME, not the stranger on the other end. To make that safe, member firms deposit collateral, which CME calls performance bonds and guaranty fund contributions, and the balance sheet held $155.2 billion of them on 2026-09-30. [1] CME re-prices every open position at least twice a day on futures and options, collects from firms that lost money, and pays firms that made it. [3] The cash it holds earns interest. In the third quarter, CME earned $1,391.7 million on that collateral and paid $1,269.0 million of it back to the clearing firms, keeping about $122.7 million. [2] That small spread is real income, and it rises and falls with short-term interest rates.

Who pays matters. Member firms generated 85 percent of contract volume in 2025, and one firm alone paid 12 percent of clearing and transaction fees. [3] Two data resellers account for about 25 percent of data revenue, but CME judges the risk small because customers would move to another reseller. [2] CME also owns pieces of other businesses that report below the operating line: a joint venture with S&P Global called S&P Dow Jones Indices, which licenses the S&P 500 to CME, and a prediction-market venture with FanDuel. [3][1] It sold its OSTTRA post-trade venture in the fourth quarter of 2025, and the cash flow statement shows $1,591.4 million of proceeds from selling business ventures that year. [2][3]

Growth comes from selling more of the same and from new products. CME says micro-sized contracts for small traders grew 32 percent in 2025. [3] Single stock futures launched in 2026 and had traded about 350,000 contracts by the October call. [17] A new securities clearing house, built for the SEC's Treasury clearing rule, is set to open on 2026-12-07 pending regulatory approvals. [25] CME is also moving its markets onto Google Cloud, with the main Chicago migration expected to start in late 2027. [17]

Where growth is coming from

Growth is increasingly international. In the third quarter of 2026 average daily volume from outside the United States reached a third-quarter record of 9 million contracts, up 22 percent from a year earlier. [1][17] Europe, the Middle East and Africa contributed 6.5 million contracts a day, up 20 percent, and Asia-Pacific contributed 2.1 million, up 28 percent. [17] That matters because a larger share of overseas trading widens the pool of buyers and sellers, which feeds the liquidity advantage described in chapter 2.

The second source of growth is products for smaller traders and new users of risk markets. Micro contracts, options with short lives and 24-hour trading in selected products are all aimed at new customers. [3][17] They bring volume, but they also pull the average fee per contract down, a tension that chapters 2B and 6 return to. Pricing is the third source: CME now makes smaller price changes during the year, not one annual change. [17]

Who competes with CME, and who needs it

CME names Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), Cboe, Euronext, Hong Kong Exchanges and Deutsche Börse as rivals, and lists newer threats: the FMX Futures Exchange, digital asset platforms and prediction markets. [3] In practice each product has a home. In the third quarter of 2026 CME averaged 3.6 million 10-Year Treasury contracts a day, 3.6 million E-mini S&P 500 contracts, 1.3 million WTI crude contracts and 674 thousand gold contracts. [2] Traders trade where the other buyers and sellers already are, and a trader who wants to hedge a bond portfolio goes where the deepest market sits. That habit is the heart of the business.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The strength of CME is liquidity, and liquidity feeds itself. A market is liquid when you can trade a large order quickly without moving the price. Buyers go where sellers are, and sellers go where buyers are. Open interest, the count of contracts still outstanding, stood at 128 million at the end of September 2026 and passed 133 million in October, the highest end-of-September level in CME's history. [17] Positions parked there are a reason for clients to stay: moving a large book to a rival means finding fresh counterparties and a new margin arrangement.

The second strength is capital efficiency, which is the savings a customer gets because CME nets out offsetting positions. A client who is long a Treasury future and short a similar Treasury bond can post less collateral for the pair. CME says its customers saved an average of more than $100 billion a day in margin in the third quarter of 2026, up from $80 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025. [17][4] Those savings are why a client does not simply switch to a rival that charges a few cents less per contract. They also tie CME to the cash Treasury market through its cross-margin deal with the Fixed Income Clearing Corporation (FICC), which CME says already produces over $2 billion in daily margin savings. [25]

The third strength is pricing power that shows up in the numbers. Market data revenue has grown for 34 quarters in a row, reaching a record $243 million in the third quarter, and the company says price increases on some products helped. [17][2] CME has also dropped its habit of one annual price change and now plans smaller changes through the year: it raised some fees on 2026-04-01 and told clients of more changes on 2026-08-11. The October 1 changes are expected to add 0.5 to 1 percent to clearing fees on an annual basis. [2][7][17] Operating margin, the share of revenue left after running costs, rose from 55.5 percent in 2021 to 65.5 percent in 2025 and 66.2 percent over the latest twelve months. [8]

The business has real weak points, and they matter at a price of 24 times earnings. The average fee per contract fell 3 percent in the third quarter, from $0.702 to $0.679, because small micro contracts and member trading grew faster than the high-fee products. [2] Volume has risen on rate and geopolitical turmoil, which cannot be counted on every year. Open interest was flat compared with a year ago in the biggest groups: equity indexes up 0.2 percent, interest rates up 1.2 percent and energy down 3.6 percent, so the volume jump came from faster trading, not from larger parked positions. [17] Finally, Cboe's exclusive S&P 500 options license was renewed for 25 years, and royalties reset from 2027. CME's chief executive says CME benefits through its S&P Dow Jones Indices venture, but the renewal also secures a rival's flagship product. [26][17]

Evidence that the moat is wide, and where it could narrow

A moat is a lasting advantage that rivals cannot copy cheaply. CME's is made of four layers: deep liquidity, a clearing house that nets positions, exclusive or long-term rights to the S&P 500 and other benchmark products, and a client base that has run its systems on CME's connections for decades. [3] The proof is in the margin. A business that is easy to copy cannot hold a 66 percent operating margin for years. [8]

The layers that could crack are the newest ones. ICE's Treasury clearing service and FICC compete for the SEC's Treasury clearing mandate, and the FMX Futures Exchange is a new entrant. [3] CME's chief executive, Terry Duffy, said on the October call that some participants trade on overseas markets through what he called a VPN, even though US participants are not supposed to use them. [17] The 10-K also names prediction markets as an emerging competitor. [3] In October 2026 management held off on its 24/7 oil contract because commercial users would not staff another desk, which shows that the demand for new products is not guaranteed. [17] None of this has dented the numbers yet, and that is the reason to watch the Layer 2 indicators in the next chapter.

How the cycle works: volatility is the fuel

CME earns more when markets are unsettled, because uncertainty makes more people hedge or speculate. Average daily volume was 26.5 million contracts in 2024 and 28.1 million in 2025. [3] It jumped to a record 36.2 million in the first quarter of 2026, then 29.8 million in the second and 29.4 million in the third. [6][5][1] The 10-Q ties the 2026 surge to inflation pressure and the Federal Reserve's first rate increase in over three years, to stock index volatility from doubts about AI spending, and to the Middle East conflict, which disrupted energy, farm and metals supply and pushed traders toward precious metals. [2]

This is why a high-quality business can still be a risky stock. The fee per contract and the cost base are steady, so profit follows volume. A calm year can cut earnings growth sharply even if nothing is wrong with the franchise. A buyer needs a price that allows for that possibility, and the entry zone in chapter 7 is built on that idea.

What this means for an owner

An owner of CME owns a toll road with pricing power and a very high margin. The risk is not that the business breaks. It is that the stock already pays for a good outcome, so a normal year can still disappoint. That is why the next chapter scores the operating indicators one by one, and why chapter 7 sets a price before any purchase.

3. The numbers, in plain words

Five years of numbers show a business that grew steadily and turned more of each sales dollar into profit. Revenue rose from $4.69 billion in fiscal 2021 to $6.51 billion in 2025, and to $6.96 billion over the twelve months to September 2026, using S&P Global's standardized figures. [8] The company's own 2025 revenue figure is $6,520.6 million, slightly different because of a small standardization adjustment that the data vendor does not itemize. [4] The three-year growth rate from 2022 to 2025 is 9.0 percent a year for revenue, 14.8 percent a year for net income and 14.7 percent a year for earnings per share, which is higher than revenue because margins widened and the share count stayed flat. [8]

The cash numbers are cleaner than the profit numbers. Free cash flow was $4,333 million over the latest twelve months, 62 percent of revenue. [8] We cross-checked it from filings: nine-month 2026 operating cash flow was $3,292.9 million and 2025 was $4,277.1 million, against $3,143.2 million for nine months of 2025, so the latest twelve months are $4,426.8 million; capital spending was about $93.7 million. [2][3] Free cash flow of $4.33 billion is close to net income of $4.40 billion, which means profit turns into cash almost one for one. The cash margin has stayed above 58 percent every year since 2022. [8]

The balance sheet is a strength. Total debt is $3.4 billion of fixed-rate notes, and the latest net debt of about $1.1 billion is only 0.22 times earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization of $4.94 billion. [1][3][8][9] The shareholder return is the unusual feature. CME paid $3,933 million of dividends in 2025 and $4,086 million over the last twelve months, which is 94 percent of free cash flow, and it repurchased $2,063 million of stock over the last twelve months. [9][8] The total is about 142 percent of free cash flow, which was possible only because cash fell from $4,417 million at the end of 2025 to $2,199 million at 2026-09-30. [1] That is not repeatable at the same pace, so we model the payout at no more than free cash flow from here.

One feature of the income statement can mislead a reader. Below operating income, CME books $1,418.1 million of investment income in the third quarter, almost all of it earned on clearing-house collateral, and a matching $1,264.0 million expense for the interest it passes back to clearing firms. [1] The two lines nearly cancel, so the net effect on profit is small, but the gross numbers make non-operating income look large. This is why we judge the business on operating income and on free cash flow, and treat the net spread as a separate, rate-sensitive item.

Return on capital is the weak-looking line, and it needs context. ROIC (return on invested capital) is 12.5 percent over the latest twelve months and was 7.1 percent in 2021, because CME carries $17.2 billion of trading-product intangibles and $10.5 billion of goodwill from its past mergers. [11][1] Those assets earn the same fees whether or not they are counted, so the ratio understates how well the business uses cash. The trend matters more than the level: ROIC has risen every year for five years. [11]

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Chapter 6 attacks our own work. The estimates in chapter 5 rest on five assumptions: that volume holds near current levels, that the margin stays near 66 percent, that interest income on collateral does not fall much, that the multiple stays near 22.7 times and that the capital return continues. The next four subsections take the weakest of them, state the evidence that would prove it wrong and say when we will know. [1][20]

Fatal flaw 1: volume is partly a one-off shock, and open interest does not confirm it

Our base case needs average daily volume near 28 to 30 million contracts. The third quarter ran at 29.4 million, but the 10-Q ties the strength to events: inflation pressure and the first Fed rate increase in over three years, stock index volatility from doubts about AI spending, and the Middle East conflict. [2] Open interest, which measures parked positions, grew only 0.2 percent in equity indexes and 1.2 percent in interest rates, and fell 3.6 percent in energy. [17] CEO Terry Duffy answered that open interest stays lower today because options now expire weekly, where five years ago they expired quarterly or semi-quarterly. [17] That is a fair point. It also means, in our reading, that open interest is a weaker signal of future volume than it used to be.

The evidence that would prove this wrong is quarterly volume. A quarterly average below 27 million contracts a day, with the Federal Reserve on hold, would break the base case. Management said fourth-quarter volume so far is 17 percent ahead of last year, which on the fourth quarter of 2025's 27.35 million implies about 32 million a day if the pace holds. [17][1] The full fourth-quarter figure arrives with results in early February 2027, which we estimate from last year's release date of 2026-02-04; the company had not confirmed the date at the time of writing. [4]

Fatal flaw 2: lower interest rates would cut the cash-collateral income, and the fee per contract is drifting down

Net earnings on clearing-house cash were about $122.7 million in the third quarter, roughly 11 percent of operating income, and they move with short-term rates and balances. [2][1] The investment income line fell 8 percent in the quarter because the average return fell even as balances grew. [2] If the Fed cuts rates, that spread shrinks. A rate cut would probably lift bond-futures activity, but it would not necessarily raise stock or commodity volume, so the offset is not automatic.

The fee per contract is the second leak. It fell from $0.702 to $0.679, and nine-month RPC fell 3 percent, because micro contracts and member trading grow faster than high-fee energy, metals and farm contracts. [2] CME has offset part of it with price rises on 2026-04-01 and 2026-10-01, and management expects the October change to add 0.5 to 1 percent to fees on an annual basis. [17][2] We will know within two quarters. Three levels matter, and they fit together. The Layer 2 FAIL line is a fall of more than 5 percent from last year's $0.702, which is below about $0.667. The nearer test of the pricing offset is $0.68: Q3 was $0.679, so a figure above $0.68 after the October change shows the increase working. And $0.65, just under the Q1 low of $0.652, is the level at which chapter 7 lowers the tier. [6]

Fatal flaw 3: the multiple assumes quality is rewarded, but growth is the lowest among peers

The base target uses 22.68 times earnings, a three-year median proxy. Analysts expect CME's earnings to grow 8.5 percent a year, against 12.6 percent for ICE, 16.0 percent for Cboe, 15.9 percent for Nasdaq and 14.6 percent for Tradeweb. [12][27][28][29][30] ICE trades at 18.8 times forward earnings. If investors decide that a slower grower should not trade above a faster one, CME's multiple would compress toward 20 times, and the bear case applies 19.5 times. [27]

Structural challenges add weight. Cboe's S&P 500 options license now runs to 2051 with higher royalties from 2027, and the 10-K names the FMX Futures Exchange and prediction-market platforms as competitors for flow. [26][3] The cost of waiting is that you miss a further rally if the quality premium widens. The evidence that would prove us wrong is a close above $329.16 with rising volume; the date is unknowable, which is why we use a price trigger and not a date. Multiple compression alone is enough to remove most of the cushion in the buy zone: with 2028 earnings unchanged at $14.35, a multiple of 19.5 times gives about $280, only about 2 percent above the $275 top of the entry zone. So a falling price is not proof of value, and the estimates and the multiple must be rechecked when the order fills.

Fatal flaw 4: dividends alone take 94 percent of free cash flow and dividends plus buybacks took 142 percent, so the variable part of the payout is not fixed

Dividends of $4,086 million over the last twelve months were 94 percent of free cash flow of $4,333 million. Adding $2,063 million of buybacks takes total shareholder returns to 142 percent of free cash flow, which was paid for by the OSTTRA sale proceeds and cash on hand; cash fell from $4,417 million to $2,199 million. [9][8][1] The data vendor shows an annual dividend of $11.35 a share and a 3.86 percent yield, but $6.15 of that is the variable dividend, which the 10-K says depends on the prior year's results and expected cash needs and can rise or fall. [14][3] Regular dividends of $5.20 a year are 1.8 percent of the price. If total payouts fall back to free cash flow, buybacks of the size seen in 2026 will not continue.

7. What we would do, and when

The decision is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT. The rule is the support distance ladder. At 1.28 average true ranges above its nearest support, the 20-day average at $285.59, CME sits in the LIMIT_ORDER_REDUCE band. In that band only a starter limit order is allowed. At $294.04 the reward-to-risk is 0.71, which fails the Core Alpha floor of 2.0. So we do not buy at the market. A pullback to $275.14 or lower would change that, because asymmetry then reaches 2.0, and it is 2.02 at $275.

The entry zone is $266.00 to $275.00, placed as a limit order. The top is set by arithmetic. With a target of $325 and a stop of $250.22, a purchase at $275.14 or lower gives at least 2.0 to 1. The zone sits just below the 50-day and 200-day averages of $279.10 and $279.91. It sits above the 63-day low of $256.83 and the 20-day swing low of $260.06. [15] The base target of $325 is 2028 EPS of $14.35 times 22.68. The bull target is $358 and the bear case is $242.

The stop loss is $250.22, which is the 63-day low of $256.83 minus one average true range ($6.61). The scoring rules pick the stop formula by chart structure. For a Stage 2 uptrend the formula starts from the lower of the 20-day swing low and the 50-day average. For a Stage 1 base or a turnaround it starts from the 63-day low. CME is Stage 3 and the momentum gate reads ENTERING_MOMENTUM with a turnaround inflection, so the 63-day formula applies. The tighter swing-low version would give $253.45, but it is written for Stage 2 uptrends and would flatter the ratio. A close below $250.22 means the recovery has failed, and the position is sold in full. The stop is judged on the close, so a gap through it would lose more than planned, and the $242 bear case sits below it.

How much to buy comes next, and the table that follows sets out the numbers. It uses three ideas defined in the glossary: a conviction tier, a risk budget and the stop formula. CME gets TIER_3, the third of four tiers, which allows 2 to 3.5 percent of the portfolio. The risk budget is 50 basis points, meaning 0.5 percent of the portfolio is the most we accept to lose if the stop is hit. The distance from $275 to $250.22 is 9.0 percent, so the formula allows 5.5 percent (0.5 divided by 9.0), but the tier caps the start at 3.5 percent. At 3.5 percent, hitting the stop costs about 0.3 percent of the portfolio, inside the budget.

If the stock rises without us, the plan changes only on confirmation. A close above $329.16, the 52-week high, on volume above its 20-day average would be a breakout into Stage 2. We would then replace the limit order with a starter buy on the first retest of that level. One event cancels the whole plan: a quarterly average daily volume below 27 million contracts. That would break the hinge claim in chapter 6. And before any order fills at $275 or lower, we recheck the consensus estimates and the multiple. With earnings unchanged and a 19.5 times multiple the stock is worth about $280, only 2 percent above the zone top, so a lower price alone is not enough reason to buy.

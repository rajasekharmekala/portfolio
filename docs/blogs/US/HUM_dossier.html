Before you read

Humana sells Medicare Advantage plans, which are private health plans that the US government pays for on behalf of older Americans. On 2026-10-09 the government published new quality scores for these plans, and the stock jumped 11.56% in one day to $431.87.[5] After that jump we find no good trade. The business is recovering, but it earns very little spare cash for its size, and the price now sits far above the level where a loss would be small. We reject the name for now and say exactly what price would change that.

What the company does

Humana is a large US health insurer. Its main customers are older Americans who pick a Medicare Advantage plan, a private plan that replaces traditional Medicare and is paid a fixed monthly amount per person by the government. At 2026-06-30 Humana covered 6,453,700 individual Medicare Advantage members, up 23.4% in a year, plus 727,200 group Medicare Advantage members and 3,946,400 members in stand-alone drug plans, up 62.6%.[3] Revenue in the fiscal year 2025 was $129,664 million.[1]

The company has two parts. The Insurance segment sells the plans and pays the medical bills. It earned $1,664 million of operating income in 2025, with 90.4% of each premium dollar going to medical costs.[1] The second part is CenterWell, a set of owned clinics, home-health and pharmacy services. CenterWell earned $1,339 million in 2025, and in the first half of 2026 it kept earning a thin margin, with operating income of $466 million in the second quarter alone.[3] Owning care delivery gives Humana a way to hold down the bills it pays.

Where the money comes from matters. Florida individual Medicare Advantage is 14% of revenue, with about one million members, so one state's costs and rules carry real weight.[1] Humana's debt is rated BBB by S&P and Baa2 by Moody's, which are the lowest tier of investment grade, so there is little room for a long run of weak cash flow.[1]

What this means for an owner: you are buying a government-priced business whose profit depends on two yearly decisions, the payment rate Washington sets and the quality score it awards. The next chapter explains why the second of those just turned in Humana's favor, and why the first still limits the business.

Why the business is strong or weak

The strongest fact is the quality score. Medicare gives each plan one to five stars, and plans rated 4 stars or higher receive bonus payments. Humana's 8-K of 2026-10-09 says the company beat its Top Quartile goal, meaning its Stars revenue per member per month is 10% above the peer median.[5] For 2027, 95% of its Medicare Advantage members are in plans rated 4 stars or higher, and 42% are in plans rated 4.5 stars.[7] CNBC reported that the share was about 20% for 2026.[8] Evercore estimated the bonus at $4.8 billion, and TD Cowen at $3 billion or more.[8][9]

The company also improved care quality. It met 663,000 more care opportunities than the year before and helped 534,000 more members complete a yearly preventive visit.[22] Higher scores are a durable edge because they take years to build, and rivals such as Alignment fell in the same ranking.[9] Humana also says it expects a one-time benefit in 2028 that it plans to use for investments and shareholder returns, with details to come.[5]

The weakness is the cost of care and the thin cash return. The benefit ratio, which is the share of premiums paid out as medical costs, rose from 87.3% in 2023 to 89.8% in 2024 and 90.2% in 2025.[16][1] In the second quarter of 2026 it was 91.1%, and the company guides the Insurance benefit ratio to 92.75% plus or minus 25 basis points for the year.[3][4] A higher ratio means less profit is left from each premium. In 2025 favorable reserve development, which means earlier cost estimates proved too high, added about $1.0 billion, or roughly 80 basis points, and that kind of gain cannot be counted on every year.[1]

There is also a legal thread. A court rejected Humana's challenge to its 2025 Star Ratings on 2025-10-14, and an appeal is pending at the Fifth Circuit.[3] So the quality lift is strong, but each point of cost pressure hits a business that keeps few dollars per dollar of revenue. Chapter 3 puts numbers on that.

What could happen next

We model three outcomes over twelve months. Street EPS for 2027 is $17.25 on an adjusted basis, up from $9.16 for 2026, so the market already expects the profit to nearly double.[11] Analyst targets average $452.87 on StockAnalysis (range $280 to $596, 26 analysts) and $445.74 on MarketBeat (32 analysts).[11][13] Both are only 3% to 5% above today's price.

Estimate: where the stock could trade in twelve months

The number

The probability-weighted value is about $432 per share, which is the same as the $431.87 price. The expected return is roughly zero.

How it was calculated

Bull case, 25% chance: 2028 earnings of about $27 per share at 20 times gives $540. That sits near a measured move, which is the prior rise added again from a breakout, a close above the price ceiling of a base: $456.50 plus ($456.50 minus $353.69) gives $559. Base case, 45%: 2027 EPS of $17.25 at 25.5 times gives $440. Bear case, 30%: the same $17.25 at 19.1 times, the five-year average multiple, gives $330. Weighted: 0.25 x 540 + 0.45 x 440 + 0.30 x 330 = $432.

The inputs

The $17.25 comes from analyst consensus.[11] The 19.1 times is the 2022 to 2025 year-end average of forward earnings multiples near 20.84, 19.11, 14.96, 15.81 and 19.01, which average about 17.9.[12] We use the 19.1 figure to be fair to the stock, not the lower average. The other multiples are our judgment of what investors would pay if Stars money flows through.

The range

From $330 to $540, which is -23.6% to +25.0% from today. The Street's range is wider, $280 to $596.[11]

If the number is off

If the Stars bonus of $3 billion to $4.8 billion arrives faster and benefit ratios fall, the bull case gets more likely and the value moves up. If medical cost growth outruns the 92.75% guide, the bear case dominates. Either way, this is why a rating alone cannot justify a purchase at $431.87.

The ex-dividend date is 2026-10-30 for a $0.885 dividend, the next earnings date is 2026-11-06, the investor update is 2026-12-10, and the open enrollment window runs from October 15 to December 7.[12][15] Each date can move the stock by several ATR. What it means for an owner: an even spread of outcomes with a stretched entry is not a trade. Chapter 6 asks how we could be wrong.

Why these estimates might be wrong

Three failures could make this rejection a mistake or make the numbers above misleading. We give each a trigger and a reason.

Fatal flaw 1: the bonus is bigger and faster than modeled

If the $4.8 billion Evercore bonus estimate holds and flows straight to profit, 2027 EPS could exceed $17.25 by a wide margin.[8] Humana could lift benefits and spending to win members instead, which would use the money up. Sign to watch: the Insurance benefit ratio in the November report against the 92.75% guide.[4] This is why the bull case carries 25%, not zero.

Fatal flaw 2: cost trends break the other way

Membership grew 23.4% in individual plans, and new members tend to cost more at first.[3] The benefit ratio already moved from 89.4% in the first quarter to 91.1% in the second.[17][3] If it keeps climbing and the 2025 reserve gains do not repeat, the 2027 EPS figure is too high. The bear case of $330 assumes the average multiple returns.

Fatal flaw 3: the cash measure is distorted

Insurer cash flow depends on the timing of government payments, so free cash flow of $375 million in 2025 may understate true earning power. We therefore also looked at trailing free cash flow of about $1,949 million, and the floor still fails. If the rating bonus pushes trailing free cash flow above 12% of revenue, which would be about $17.5 billion, we would revisit the financial test, but that is far from the current level.[12]

Data gaps also matter. Finviz returned an access error and its cached page showed a different company, so we did not use it. Zacks and Macrotrends were also blocked. We used SEC filings, the company release, and StockAnalysis, MarketBeat and TradingView pages instead. Peer forward P/E for Centene was not saved as a record, so the table shows N/A. Layer 3: entry price and payoff

Asymmetry (also called reward-to-risk) is the gain to the base target divided by the loss to the stop. A stop loss is a preset exit price that caps the loss. Our Tactical rule puts the stop below the lower of the recent swing low ($369.13) and the moving averages, minus half an ATR: $369.13 - 0.5 x $18.59 = $359.84. At the current price the reward is $440 - $431.87 = $8.13 and the risk is $431.87 - $359.84 = $72.03, so the ratio is 0.11 to 1. Using the row's $456.50 anchor it is 0.34 to 1. A retest, meaning a pullback to a broken level to check it holds, of the 50-day line at $388.02 would give 1.84 to 1. The Tactical floor is 3.5 to 1, which needs an entry of about $377.65, 2.92 ATR below today's close. At 2.36 ATR over the 50-day line the price is in the no-buy band.

Status: FAIL_ASYMMETRY, momentum gate NO_MOMENTUM, entry posture WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT. A limit order is an order to buy only at or below a set price. We would not place one today, and the entry zone, the price range where buying makes sense, is below $377.65 if we ever revisit.

Trigger to revisit: a close at or below $377.65 with the financial floor improving (trailing FCF margin above 5% in the 2026-11-06 report), or a verified benefit ratio at or below 91% with Stars bonus cash visible in the results.

1. What the company does

BlackRock is a company that other people pay to invest their money. A pension fund, an insurer, a bank or a person saving for retirement hands money to BlackRock. BlackRock invests it and charges a small yearly fee, quoted as a fraction of the money it looks after. The money it looks after is called assets under management, or AUM. At September 30, 2026 BlackRock managed $15.5 trillion, after $869 billion of new money from clients over the last twelve months. [1] It is the largest asset manager in the world, and no listed peer in this report is close: Invesco manages $2.5 trillion, T. Rowe Price $1.9 trillion and Blackstone $1.4 trillion. [40][29][31][30]

Most of the revenue is the plain management fee, which the company calls base fees. In the third quarter of 2026 base fees and securities lending revenue were $6,036 million out of total revenue of $7,752 million, or 78 percent. [1] Measured against the average AUM of $15.46 trillion, that is a fee of about 0.156 percent a year, or $1.56 for every $1,000 managed. [1] The rate is not the same everywhere. Institutional index funds, which simply copy a market index, earn about 0.03 percent, while private markets funds earn about 0.80 percent. This is why the mix of products matters more to profit than the headline AUM figure.

The remaining 22 percent of revenue comes from four smaller lines. Performance fees ($630 million) are extra pay when a fund beats a target, and they swing from quarter to quarter. Technology services and subscriptions ($588 million) is mostly Aladdin, the software that large investors use to see their risk and manage portfolios. Distribution fees ($414 million) are collected and mostly passed on to the banks and advisers who sell BlackRock products. [1] A final line of $84 million covers advisory and other work.

BlackRock grows in two ways. The first is net inflows, which are new client money minus withdrawals. Over the last year net inflows were $869 billion and market gains added another $1.27 trillion, while currency moves took away $82 billion and returns of capital from private funds took away $34 billion. [1] The second way is by buying businesses. Since late 2024 BlackRock has bought Global Infrastructure Partners (infrastructure funds), Preqin (private markets data) and HPS Investment Partners (private credit, closed July 1, 2025). [19][15] 2026 is the first full year in which the three businesses sit inside the company. [6]

A short guide to terms used later. Earnings per share (EPS) is profit divided by the number of shares. Free cash flow (FCF) is cash from operations minus capital spending. Stock-based compensation (SBC) is pay given to staff in shares. EBITDA, or earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, is a rough measure of operating cash profit. The price-to-earnings ratio is the share price divided by a year of profit per share. The operating margin is operating profit divided by revenue. Net debt is borrowings minus cash. Guidance is what management says it expects. For the price chart, the relative strength index (RSI) is a 0 to 100 gauge of how fast a price has moved. The average true range (ATR) is the typical daily swing of the price in dollars. A stock in Stage 2 is in a rising trend, and a stock in Stage 4 is in a falling trend. A stock in Stage 3 is pausing after a rise: the ledger gives that label when the 50-day average has gone flat or is falling while the price is still above a 200-day average that is flat or rising. A base is a flat floor of prices, a pivot is the level that marks a breakout, and a retest is a pullback that tests support. A limit order is an instruction to buy only at a named price or lower. The entry zone is the price range where we would buy. Asymmetry, which we also call the reward-to-risk ratio, compares what we could gain with what we could lose.

Who pays, and what they get

Four groups pay BlackRock. Retail investors and their advisers buy mutual funds and ETFs, which are baskets of securities that trade on an exchange. Institutions such as pension plans hire BlackRock to run a share of their portfolio, either tracking an index or trying to beat it. Companies and banks pay for Aladdin. Governments, insurers and large corporations increasingly pay for financing and private markets, such as data centers and infrastructure loans. [17]

iShares is the ETF brand and the largest single source of fees. ETF AUM was $6.36 trillion at the end of September and ETFs earned $2,762 million of the quarter's base fees, about 46 percent of the total. [1] Because an ETF fee is low (about 0.175 percent), the profit comes from volume: more money in the same funds costs BlackRock very little extra to run.

What the three purchases added

Private markets means investments that do not trade on a public exchange, such as loans to companies and stakes in toll roads or data centers. BlackRock now manages $336 billion in private markets, only 2 percent of its AUM, but it earns 11 percent of base fees from it. [1] Management has set a goal of raising a cumulative $400 billion of new private markets money by 2030. [19][20] The same logic applies to technology: Aladdin earned $588 million in the quarter and its annual contract value grew 12 percent. [1]

The deals also created promises that are not yet fully paid. At June 30, 2026 BlackRock owed $7,875 million of contingent consideration, which is extra purchase price that depends on future performance and, for some of it, on BlackRock's own share price. [10] It also may issue up to 12,035,866 more shares to the sellers of HPS. [15] Later chapters come back to both, because they make official earnings lower than the adjusted earnings that analysts forecast.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • AUM (assets under management): the market value of client money that BlackRock manages. It rises when markets rise and when clients add money.
  • Base fees: the management fee charged as a percentage of AUM, plus income from lending securities that a fund owns. It is the steady core of revenue.
  • Net flows or net inflows: new client money minus client withdrawals. A positive number means clients are adding money.
  • Organic base fee growth: the yearly increase in base fees that comes only from net inflows, calculated as new fees on inflows divided by the starting base fee run-rate. It leaves out market moves. [2]
  • As adjusted results: BlackRock's own version of profit that removes acquisition costs, the change in value of contingent consideration and a few other items. The official figure is called GAAP (generally accepted accounting principles). [1]
  • Aladdin and ACV: Aladdin is BlackRock's risk and portfolio software. ACV (annual contract value) is the yearly value of its recurring subscription contracts. [1]
  • Subco units: shares-in-waiting issued to the sellers of HPS that can be swapped one-for-one for BlackRock common stock after July 1, 2026. [15]
  • ROE (return on equity): yearly profit divided by shareholders' equity. For an asset manager it stands in for return on invested capital, because managers hold little physical capital.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

BlackRock's first strength is scale combined with low cost. It manages $6.36 trillion in ETFs and $3.93 trillion in institutional index money, and a fund that costs almost nothing to run per dollar can still earn large profits when it holds that many dollars. [1] Scale also helps BlackRock win mandates. Management says that in the third quarter it took in net inflows in every asset class, every client type and every region. [17] Total net inflows were $205 billion in the quarter. Of that, $175 billion went into long-term products and $31 billion into cash management, which holds short-term client cash. The long-term part was $135 billion from ETFs, $44 billion from institutional active strategies and $23 billion from retail clients, partly offset by $27 billion of index outflows. [1]

Its second strength is that the growth is becoming broader and richer. Organic base fee growth was 11 percent annualized in the third quarter and 10 percent over the last twelve months, the ninth quarter in a row at or above the 5 percent target set at the 2025 Investor Day. [1][17][19] Active ETF AUM has doubled in a year to more than $160 billion. [17] And the adjusted operating margin, which is the share of fee revenue left after operating costs, reached 47.8 percent in the quarter, up from 44.6 percent a year earlier. [1]

The third strength is the software. Technology services revenue rose 14 percent to $588 million, and annual contract value, the yearly worth of signed subscriptions, grew 12 percent. [1] A bank or insurer that runs its risk reports on Aladdin faces a long, costly project if it wants to leave, so these contracts tend to renew. The fee is small next to BlackRock's total, but it is steady and it brings the company's investment clients closer.

The weaknesses are real and we count them. The first is that market moves do much of the work. Of the $2.02 trillion increase in AUM over the last year, $1.27 trillion was market gains and $869 billion was net inflows. [1] Equity funds carry 51 percent of base fees. [1] In 2022, when AUM fell 14 percent, revenue fell 7.7 percent and adjusted operating income fell 13 percent. [7] A repeat would hit the stock hard.

Where fee pressure shows up

Institutional index funds had $27 billion of net outflows in the third quarter, concentrated in low-fee equity index products. [17] That money earns about 0.03 percent, so its loss barely moves revenue. The risk is the other direction: if clients ask for lower fees on ETFs or active funds, the fall would be large. T. Rowe Price, a mostly active manager, shows the downside. Its fee rate fell to 38.1 basis points from 39.6 a year earlier and it had $6.5 billion of net outflows in its latest quarter. [31] A basis point is one hundredth of a percent.

What official and adjusted profit say

BlackRock reports two sets of profit. For the third quarter the official (GAAP) diluted earnings per share were $10.57 and the adjusted figure was $15.93. [1] The gap was $5.36 a share. The main piece was a $564 million non-cash charge for the change in value of promised acquisition payments, which rose because BlackRock's own share price rose. [1] We treat the adjusted figure as the better guide to the business, but we do not ignore the gap: the promised payments and the share issue are real claims on owners.

The third-quarter adjusted result also contained investment gains. Adjusted non-operating income was $127 million against a loss of $106 million a year earlier, mostly from marks on minority stakes in Circle and Securitize. [1] We estimate this explained about $1.07 of the $4.38 per share year-on-year rise in adjusted earnings. The working: ($127 million + $106 million) x (1 - 24.2% tax) / 164.5 million shares = $1.07. [1] It is not a recurring source, and that is one reason the quarter's $1.63 beat over the $14.30 estimate should not be projected forward. [21]

Governance, legal and credit facts

BlackRock's board has 84 percent independent nominees and a lead independent director, Murry S. Gerber. [11] Laurence D. Fink, 73, has been a director since 1999 and holds both the chairman and chief executive roles, so the company depends heavily on one person. [11] Shareholders re-elected him in May 2026 with 114.5 million votes for and 6.3 million against, and approved executive pay. [12] In January 2026 the board adopted an Executive Carry Program that pays selected senior executives, other than the CEO, a share of private fund profits, which ties their pay to private markets success. [13] A new independent director, Gregg R. Lemkau, joined in January 2026. [14] Insiders own about 1.9 percent of the shares. [23]

On the legal side, BlackRock is defending a lawsuit by thirteen state attorneys general who allege it, State Street and Vanguard conspired to reduce coal supply; the court largely denied the motion to dismiss in 2025, and management currently does not anticipate that the aggregate liability from lawsuits and regulatory matters will have a material effect, while saying there is no assurance. [10] We found no restatement, going-concern warning or accounting-fraud finding. On credit, Moody's affirmed the senior unsecured rating at Aa3 with a stable outlook, saying it expects leverage to stay below its 1.5x downgrade threshold. [28] The company also holds a $6.3 billion revolving credit line that runs to March 31, 2031 for most lenders. [16]

We chose them because a manager lives on fees: how much new money comes in, how much of each fee is kept as profit, whether the newer businesses are scaling, and whether the profit per share is growing for owners and not only for the company. The adjusted-profit assumption is the one we would look at hardest. Adjusted profit leaves out three items, each shown after tax.

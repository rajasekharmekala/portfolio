1. What the company does

NBIL is the GraniteShares 2x Long NBIS Daily ETF. It is an exchange-traded fund, a basket that trades on a stock exchange like a share, run by GraniteShares Advisors LLC inside the GraniteShares ETF Trust.[1][2] Its stated goal is to deliver 2 times (200%) the daily percentage change of Nebius Group stock, before fees and expenses.[2] If NBIS rises 3% in one day, the fund aims to rise 6%. If NBIS falls 12%, the fund aims to fall 24%. The fund began operations on October 6, 2025.[3] A data page lists $221.22 million of assets and 13.19 million shares on October 16, 2026 but does not state the valuation date.[4] It is probably an earlier session, because 13.19 million shares at the $12.21 close would be only about $161 million.

The fund does not own Nebius shares. Its own page says it gets the 2x exposure through total-return swaps, which are contracts with a bank in which the fund receives the stock’s gain or loss and pays a financing charge.[1] At June 30, 2026 the fund had one swap with Clear Street, LLC: a notional amount of $187,542,014 when last reset, worth $391,353,327 at the date of the report, against net assets of $195,599,438, so exposure was about 2.0 times net assets.[3] The prospectus expects the fund to post 30% to 45% of its assets as collateral for the swaps.[2] Each trading day the fund resets its exposure to 200% of its net assets. A simple picture: a driver whose cruise control is reset every evening to twice the speed reached that day. That reset is what makes the fund useful for a one-day bet and costly for a long hold.

The people who pay the fund are traders and investors who want to make a short-term bet on one stock. GraniteShares earns a management fee of 1.30% a year plus 0.20% of other expenses, a total of 1.50%, and the annualized net expense ratio for the period to June 30, 2026 was 1.37%.[2][3] The prospectus also says the indirect cost of the swaps, including their embedded financing, is an estimate of 2.00% a year that is not in the fee table.[2] On the $221 million of assets listed by the data page (valuation date not stated) the 1.50% fee is about $3.3 million a year, which is our arithmetic. The fund’s own page rates its risk 5 out of 5, the highest, and says it is intended for investors who hold for very short periods, often a single day.[1]

The engine behind the fund is Nebius Group N.V., headquartered in Amsterdam and formerly named Yandex N.V.[17][26] Nebius rents out graphics-chip computing power to companies that train and run artificial intelligence models, a business often called an AI cloud or a “neocloud.” In the second quarter of 2026 it reported revenue of $582.3 million, up 454% from a year earlier, and an annualized run-rate of $3.0 billion, up 598%.[17][18] About 98% of group revenue came from the AI cloud business.[22] The group also includes Avride (self-driving vehicles) and TripleTen (education), and Nebius owns stakes in ClickHouse and Toloka.[17] NBIS closed at $165.47 on October 16, 2026, down 12.18% on the day and about 45% below its 52-week high of $299.86.[11][12]

Two labels on file are wrong, and the fund’s filings settle the matter. The holdings sheet calls the position “T-Rowway 2X Long NBIS ETF,” and the older watchlist text describes a NEOS 1-3 month Treasury-bill fund yielding 5.22% in the core bucket. The SEC annual report, the issuer page and the exchange listing all show NBIL is the GraniteShares fund described here, a leveraged single-stock fund and nothing like a cash fund.[3][1][4] We recorded the conflict in the run notes and used the filings. This book treats NBIL as the rule requires for leveraged funds: it studies the stock behind the fund, the price trend, and the decay risk, and it does not value the fund as a business.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Leveraged ETF — a fund that aims to return a multiple (here 2 times) of one day’s move in something else.
  • Daily reset — the fund rebuilds its 2x exposure at every close, so losses and gains compound day after day.
  • Swap — a contract with a bank that pays the fund the stock’s gain or loss in return for a financing fee.
  • Volatility drag — the slow loss a daily-reset fund suffers when the stock swings up and down, even if the stock ends where it began.
  • Neocloud — a newer company that rents AI computing power, such as Nebius or CoreWeave.
  • NBIS — the Nasdaq ticker of Nebius Group, the stock NBIL follows.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

A fund has no brand or factory, so its strength is how well it does the one job it promises and how it compares with rivals doing the same job. On both counts NBIL is average. Three funds offer 2x daily exposure to NBIS: NBIL ($221.22 million of assets, 1.50% expense ratio), Tradr’s NEBX ($150.32 million, 1.30%) and Leverage Shares’ NBIG ($84.71 million, 0.76%).[4][7][8][9][10] These asset figures are as listed on the October 16 data pages, and the pages do not state their valuation dates. NBIL is the largest of the three and the most expensive. All three fell between 24.1% and 24.5% on October 16 (NBIL -24.49%, NEBX -24.12%, NBIG -24.44%), which is what a 2x fund should do when the stock falls 12.18%.[4][7][8][12] The edge NBIL has over its rivals is size and trading volume. It has no edge on price.

Strength: the fund does what it says each day

The strongest evidence is the daily match. On October 14, 15 and 16 NBIS moved -10.08%, +1.29% and -12.18%, so a perfect 2x fund would have moved -20.16%, +2.58% and -24.36%. NBIL moved -20.23%, +2.28% and -24.49%.[12][5] Over the 49 daily returns in the August 7 to October 16 price tables, we measured an average gap from the 2x target of 0.15 percentage points and a worst gap of 0.54 points, from the closing prices in those two tables. The fund’s own annual report shows the same discipline: net assets $195.6 million, a net asset value that rose from $25.00 to $57.03 per share between launch and June 30, 2026, and a total return of 128.12%.[3] The market price also stays close to value: NAV was $15.79 on October 14 and the closing price was $15.81.[6][5]

This is why the ledger’s odd-looking numbers check out. NBIL closed at $12.21, down 24.49%, after a close of $16.17.[4] A 2x fund that falls 24.49% implies the stock fell about 12.2%, and NBIS did fall 12.18% to $165.47 on the same session.[12] The decline was not a data error or a fund-specific problem. Peers fell by similar amounts the same day: CoreWeave -11.91%, IREN -11.19% and Applied Digital -11.48%.[28][29][30] So the fund is a faithful mirror of a stock that is having a very bad week.

Weakness: the daily reset eats value when the stock is volatile

The fund’s own prospectus states the weakness plainly. Over a period longer than one day, the fund’s return is the product of each day’s compounded return, and it will lose money if the stock is flat.[2] The prospectus table shows the fund losing 6.0% over a year if the stock goes nowhere at 25% volatility, and 63.3% if the stock goes nowhere at 100% volatility.[2] FINRA warned in 2009 that daily-reset funds are typically unsuitable for retail investors who hold longer than one session, and gave a real case in which an index rose 2 percent while a 2x fund on it fell 6 percent.[37] The SEC chair repeated the warning in 2021.[38]

NBIS volatility is high. The prospectus put its five-year historical volatility at 80.07%, with a worst 12-month reading of 117.79%.[2] From the 49 daily returns in the August to October price table, we measure about 110% a year, with a daily swing of about 7%.[12] Real prices show what that does. From August 7 to October 16, NBIS fell 11.97% (from $187.97 to $165.47) while NBIL fell 37.99% (from $19.69 to $12.21).[12][5] Simple doubling would give -23.9%. Compounding the daily 2x returns gives -35.9%. The rest, about 2 points, is fees and tracking gaps. So NBIL ended about 14 points below simple doubling in ten weeks: about 12 points of compounding drag and about 2 points of fees and tracking gaps.

The stock behind the fund: strong demand, heavy spending

Nebius reported second-quarter 2026 revenue of $582.3 million, up 454%, and an adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) of $236.2 million, where adjusted EBITDA is the company’s own profit measure before interest, taxes, depreciation, stock pay and some one-off items.[17] Its AI cloud business had an adjusted margin of 50%.[18] The letter says four landmark deals averaged more than $1 billion each, with a yield of $20 to $25 million per megawatt, and that 70% of deals included prepayments.[18] Management said on the call that it holds $40 billion of customer commitments and reaffirmed all 2026 guidance, including $3 billion to $3.4 billion of revenue and $7 billion to $9 billion of year-end ARR (annual recurring revenue).[22] Contracted power is now expected to reach 5 gigawatts by year-end, raised from more than 4 gigawatts in May.[18][19]

The cost of that growth is the weakness. In the second quarter Nebius spent $5.66 billion on property and equipment, and $8.13 billion in the first half against $8.04 billion of cash at June 30.[17] It guides to $20 billion to $25 billion for the year, so at least $11.9 billion is still to be spent in the second half.[22] Debt rose to $8.55 billion ($46.7 million current and $8,499.0 million non-current).[17] It sold 12.7 million shares through an at-the-market program at an average of $223.6, raising about $2.8 billion, and signed a $775 million secured loan at SOFR plus 2.50%.[18] The auditor’s report on 2025 found two material weaknesses in internal control, over property and equipment and over TripleTen revenue.[15] Nothing in these filings points to fraud or a going-concern warning, but the combination of heavy spending, outside funding and weak controls is what the market is pricing.

The week’s sell-off shows the market’s fear. On October 14 Gartner said that by 2030 over 80% of neocloud providers could cease independent operations, and CoreWeave’s 2032 bond yield reached 14.8%.[32][33] NBIS closed at $186.01, down 10.08%, on October 14, edged up to $188.41 on October 15 and fell to $165.47 on October 16.[35][12] One research head said Nebius sells “the same thing to the same companies” as CoreWeave but with almost no financial leverage, yet Nebius still fell with it.[32] That is the point of this chapter: the stock’s business can be improving while its price, and a 2x fund on it, falls sharply.

The stock’s financial strength is the weak point, so here are its main measures in plain terms. Free cash flow (FCF), the cash left after running the business and buying equipment, was -$5.88 billion over the twelve months to June 30, 2026.[17][14] Net debt, which is debt minus cash, was only $503.6 million, but only because Nebius raised cash from shares, convertible notes and customer prepayments as fast as it spent it.[17] Return on invested capital (ROIC), the profit earned on money put into the business, is about -6.3%, which is our arithmetic from the filings. Stock-based compensation (SBC), the cost of paying staff in shares, is 13.9% of revenue.[15][17] None of these meets the framework’s floors, and Chapter 3 shows the sums.

What the chart says about the fund itself

NBIL’s own chart is the other weak point. The fund fell 24.49% in a day and 46.24% in a week, and it sits about 50% below both its 50-day and 200-day averages.[4][5] That looks like the falling phase the stage scale calls Stage 4, even though the ledger labels the fund Stage 1 (Basing). A buyable chart would be Stage 2, the confirmed uptrend, with the price above rising averages. The fund also has no base, a flat floor that the price builds before a move, because the low of $11.85 was set on the same day as the drop. It would need a breakout, a move above its recent range on rising volume, before it looked like a buy. The fund inherits all of the stock’s risk and adds its own, which is the subject of the next chapters.

Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Walk-the-talk means checking what management promised one or two years ago against what it delivered. For this holding two sets of people made promises: Nebius’s management about the stock’s business, and GraniteShares about the fund. Nebius’s record is strong on growth targets and weaker on reporting accuracy.

The strongest item is the year-end 2025 ARR. Management’s latest guidance was $900 million to $1.1 billion, and the reported figure was $1.25 billion.[20][25] Contracted power for year-end 2026 was guided at more than 1 GW in August 2025, then more than 2.5 GW, more than 3 GW, more than 4 GW, and now 5 GW, so management has raised that target four times and delivered ahead of it.[19][18] The weaker item is the audit. The February 2026 unaudited release showed a 2025 operating loss of $596.2 million and net income from continuing operations of $29.0 million, while the audited 20-F showed an operating loss of $611.7 million and $9.8 million, and the auditor reported two material weaknesses.[21][15] Amounts that move between a press release and an audit are the kind of gap that deserves a lower score on reporting, even when the business grows.

The 2026 targets are the open test. Revenue guidance of $3.0 billion to $3.4 billion was reaffirmed in May and August, but first-half revenue was $981.3 million, so the second half must produce $2.02 billion to $2.42 billion, which is about 73% to 108% more per quarter than the $582.3 million of the second quarter.[19][17][22] Capacity added late in the second quarter is meant to begin earning in the third quarter.[22] On the fund side, GraniteShares promised a 2x daily result and delivered it, kept its actual expense ratio (1.37%) below the 1.50% cap, and holds the cap until December 31, 2026.[2][3] It also said plainly that the fund “should not be expected” to give 2x over longer periods, and it did not.[3]

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every number in Chapter 5 rests on a few assumptions. This chapter names the weakest assumption behind each estimate, the evidence that would show it wrong, and when we will know. It then gives the strongest case against our own conclusion, and the single claim the conclusion depends on.

Estimate 1 and 2: the volatility assumption of 100%

We assumed a three-month volatility of 100% a year. If the stock calms to 60%, the break-even hurdle falls from +13.8% to about +5.1% and the base case rises to about $14.58. Our measured 110% comes from only 49 trading days, and the last 20 days show about 84% and the last 10 days about 92%, so the figure is not stable.[12] The formula is also an approximation: it assumes steady price swings and no large gaps, and NBIS rose 34.14% on August 12 and fell more than 10% on October 14 and again on October 16.[12] The evidence that would show us wrong is realized volatility falling below 60% for a month. We will know with the next 20 trading days of prices and by the Q3 results date.

Estimate 3: the scenario prices and weights

The $190, $250 and $125 prices are anchored to recent closes and one analyst’s chart level, not to a valuation, and the 20/50/30 weights are a statistical convention rather than a forecast.[12][34] Analyst targets ($84 to $410, mean $283.60) say the stock could do far better, and a 22-analyst consensus with 13 strong buys is a real counterweight.[13] The evidence that would show our weights wrong is the Q3 report, expected around November 10 (not confirmed), and any new financing news.[24] If Nebius shows ARR near $5 billion with new asset-backed loans at SOFR plus 2.50%, the bull weight should rise; if it misses its revenue range, the bear weight should rise.[18]

Structural risks the model leaves out

The formula does not include fund-level events. NBIL has one swap counterparty, Clear Street, LLC, and the swap ends on 10/19/2027.[3] If the counterparty could not honor the contract, the fund could close or reset, and the fund’s own page warns of swap and counterparty risk.[1] The fee cap of 1.50% expires on December 31, 2026 unless renewed, and a smaller or closed fund would cost more per share.[2] A fund that shrinks can also be closed by its issuer. Nothing in the filings says this is imminent: the data page lists $221 million of assets and a share count of 13.19 million, although it does not state the valuation date.[4] But these are tail risks that make the downside worse than the formula, and we cannot put a probability on them from the documents.

The red-team case against selling

The strongest argument against our verdict is this. NBIL closed 0.1 ATR above its support at $11.85 with an RSI of 30.98, near the oversold line, after a sector-wide fall that we could not tie to any Nebius report.[4][32] The stock’s fundamentals improved in 2026: revenue up 454%, ARR up 598%, $40 billion of commitments, guidance raised or reaffirmed at every step, and a mean analyst target 71% above the price.[17][18][22][13] The fund can also jump: on August 12, when Nebius reported results, it rose 68.28% in a day.[5] Selling at a 40% loss after a 24% one-day fall could mean selling the low. The position is also small, 0.74% of net worth, so a mistake in either direction is modest in dollars.

We weigh this and still hold the verdict, for two reasons. First, the illustrative average is flat while the spread is huge, and the rules ask for a reward-to-risk of 3.5 to 1, not a coin flip with fat tails. Second, the fund’s own return is lower than the stock’s in every choppy path, and the path of the last ten weeks was choppy. If you disagree, the cleaner way to hold the same view is the stock itself, which has no daily reset; it is a different instrument and we have not underwritten it here.

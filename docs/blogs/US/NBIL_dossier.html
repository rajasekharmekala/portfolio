1. What the company does

NBIL is not a company. It is an exchange-traded fund (ETF), a pooled product whose shares trade on a stock exchange, run by GraniteShares Advisors, and it began trading on October 7, 2025.[2][3] Its only job is to deliver, each trading day, twice (200%) the percentage move of Nebius Group (NBIS), an AI cloud company based in Amsterdam and listed on Nasdaq.[1][29] The fund started at a net asset value (NAV) of $25.00 a share, where NAV is the value of everything the fund owns minus what it owes, divided by the number of shares.[3] We hold it as a trading satellite, which is a small side position that is allowed to be risky, and not as a savings holding.

The fund does not own Nebius shares. It holds short-term US Treasury bills and a total return swap, which is a private contract in which a bank pays the fund twice the gain on a notional amount of Nebius stock, and the fund pays the bank a financing charge and covers any loss.[1][3] At June 30, 2026 the fund's own report showed two holdings: Treasury bills at 71.55% of total investments and the Nebius swap at 28.40%.[3] The swap's notional amount was $187,542,014, it paid 600 basis points (6.00 percentage points) over the overnight bank funding rate (OBFR), and it ran to October 19, 2027.[3] The bank on the other side, Clear Street, LLC, is the counterparty, the party that must pay if the swap is in the fund's favour; if it failed, the fund could lose gains the swap owed it.[3][1]

The word daily is the whole story. At each close the fund resets to 200% exposure, so every day starts with a fresh double bet at a new price. The two sessions since our last report show how this works.[12][13] Nebius fell 3.45% on October 26 and the fund fell 6.92%. Nebius rose 11.22% on October 27 and the fund rose 22.39%. Over the two days Nebius gained 7.4%, from $158.74 to $170.47, and the fund gained 13.9%, from $11.13 to $12.68, which is less than the 14.8% that twice 7.4% would give (our calculation).[12][13] The fund did its daily job on both days. The gap is the compounding effect, and it grows with every swing.

A holder pays for this in three ways. The first is the stated fee: a 1.30% management fee and net expenses of 1.41% after waivers, with the 1.50% cap promised until December 31, 2027.[1][3] The fund's actual expense ratio from launch to June 30, 2026 was 1.37%.[3] The second is the financing charge inside the swap: 600 basis points over OBFR, which stood at 3.88% on October 27, so 9.88% a year on the swap's notional amount (our arithmetic).[3][11] The third is decay, the loss that a daily-reset fund suffers when the stock swings up and down. We measured the first two costs together. Since launch the fund finished 12.3% below an ideal 2x daily compounding of Nebius, which is about 12% a year (our calculation).[12][13] The next two chapters ask whether that price buys anything we want.

The business that sets the fund's price: Nebius Group

Nebius rents computing power to AI developers and companies: graphics processing units (GPUs, the chips that train and run AI models), storage and software, sold as a cloud service.[20][29] Its second-quarter 2026 revenue was $582.3 million, up 454% from a year earlier, and its AI cloud unit supplied $574.9 million of that, about 98%.[18][20] Annualized run-rate revenue, which is the last month's AI cloud revenue multiplied by 12, reached $3.0 billion at the end of June, up from $1.9 billion at the end of March.[20] EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) is profit before the charges for financing, taxes and equipment wear; adjusted for share pay and deal costs, Nebius earned $236.2 million of it in the quarter.[18] Two large contracts anchor the business: Meta, worth up to about $27 billion over five years starting early 2027, and Microsoft, a multi-year agreement for capacity from a New Jersey data centre.[33][34] Management says customers have committed about $40 billion of contracts.[21]

The cost of building this business is the reason the stock swings so violently. In the first half of 2026 Nebius spent $8,130.3 million on equipment and intangible assets, which analysts call capital expenditure (cash spent on long-lived equipment such as GPUs and data centres), against $981.3 million of revenue.[18] It plans $20 billion to $25 billion for the full year.[21] To pay for it the company has raised money from convertible notes (loans that can turn into shares), share sales, an investment from NVIDIA, customer prepayments and secured loans, as Chapter 3 lists.[19][20] A stock whose owners keep hearing about new financing moves sharply on every headline, and the fund doubles each of those moves.

What a holder of NBIL is really buying

A holder is buying a daily double bet with a built-in cost. FINRA, the broker industry regulator, has told brokers that daily-reset funds of this type typically are unsuitable for retail investors who plan to hold them longer than one trading session, particularly in volatile markets.[9] The SEC makes the same point: performance over a period longer than one day can differ significantly from the stated daily objective.[10] Our own holding shows it. We bought 50 shares on August 11, 2026 at an average of $20.35, the day before Nebius reported second-quarter results and rose 34.1% in a session.[40][13] Since that purchase Nebius has fallen 11.8% and NBIL has fallen 38.8% (our calculation).[12][13] Twice 11.8% would be 23.6%, so about 15 points of the fund's loss came from volatility and costs and not from the stock's direction. That gap is the price of holding a daily product for eleven weeks, and it is the main reason this report ends in a sale.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Leveraged ETF is a fund that aims for a multiple, here two times, of the daily move of one asset.
  • Daily reset means the fund restores its 200% exposure every trading day, so gains and losses compound along the path.
  • Swap is a private contract that exchanges the return of an asset for a fee; the other side is the counterparty.
  • Volatility is how much a price swings, shown as an annual percentage; volatility decay is the loss a daily-reset fund suffers from those swings even when the stock ends flat.
  • OBFR is the overnight bank funding rate, the rate banks pay for overnight money; the swap's financing charge is OBFR plus 600 basis points.
  • RSI (relative strength index) is a meter from 0 to 100 of how fast the price has been moving; below 30 is called oversold.
  • ATR (average true range) is the average daily price swing, in dollars; it is used to place stops and to measure distance from support.
  • Stage 4 is the markdown phase of the four-stage price cycle, with the price under a falling 50-day and 200-day average; Stage 2 is the confirmed uptrend.
  • Stop loss is the price at which the thesis is wrong and the position is cut; a limit order is an order that only fills at your price or better.
  • Asymmetry is the ratio of the potential gain to the potential loss from the entry, (target minus price) divided by (price minus stop); reward-to-risk is another name for it, and the entry zone is the band of prices where buying is planned.
  • Guidance is the company's own published forecast for the coming period; a consensus estimate is the average of analysts' forecasts for a number.
  • Free cash flow (FCF) is cash left after running the business and buying equipment; net debt is interest-bearing debt minus the cash held.
  • Dilution is the fall in each shareholder's slice of a company when it issues new shares.
  • Base is a sideways stretch of trading that forms a platform for the next move; the pivot is the top of a base, and a breakout is a move that takes the price above it on heavy volume.
  • SBC (stock-based compensation) is the cost of paying employees in shares; ROIC (return on invested capital) is profit earned on each dollar invested in the business; EPS (earnings per share) is the company's profit divided across all shares.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

A fund like NBIL has no moat of its own. Its competitive position is a question of price, size and safety against other funds that sell the same bet. Three long funds track Nebius at twice the daily move: NBIL, Tradr's NEBX and Leverage Shares' NBIG.[1][5][6] NBIL's stated management fee is 1.30%, net expenses are 1.41%, and the fund page and an aggregator list 1.50%, against 1.30% for NEBX and 0.75% for NBIG.[1][4][5][6] On the cost line NBIL is the most expensive of the three, and a buyer of the same exposure can pay roughly half as much at NBIG. That is a real disadvantage, but it is small next to decay: even a free fund would lose 63.3% over a year at 100% volatility on a flat stock.[1]

Size is the second test, and here NBIL is stronger than its peers' closures might suggest. Net assets were $195,599,438 on 3,430,001 shares at June 30, 2026, and an aggregator page dated October 28, 2026 shows about $175.74 million on 15.79 million shares.[3][4] NBIG, the cheaper rival, shows 10,920,000 shares at a NAV of $8.71, which is about $95.1 million (our arithmetic), and a 30-day median spread of 0.23%.[6] The fund is also liquid: 6,792,800 shares traded on October 27, about $86 million at the close, so our 50 shares are a rounding error in the day's volume.[39] Closure is the risk that sinks small funds. On October 9, 2026 Leverage Shares announced it was closing thirteen single-stock funds for failing to attract enough assets, and GraniteShares closed three of its own in June.[7][8] NBIL's roughly $176 million is large for a single-stock fund, so we rate closure risk low today.

Safety is the third test, and it is the weak spot. At June 30 the swap sat with one counterparty, Clear Street, LLC, and the fund expects to post between 30% and 45% of its assets as collateral under its swap agreements.[3][1] The fund's unrealized swap gain, the amount the bank owed it, was $203,811,313 against net assets of $195,599,438, so the exposure to this one bank was larger than the whole fund before collateral.[3] The prospectus warns that a counterparty may be unable or unwilling to pay, and that the fund could lose more than its net assets if the stock fell more than 50% in a day.[1] We could not read a current list of counterparties, because the issuer's holdings file loads by script, so this risk is scored on the June report.

The underlying company is strong in some ways and fragile in others. It is growing at a very high rate, and the adjusted EBITDA margin of its AI cloud unit has risen from 24% in the fourth quarter of 2025 to 50% in the second quarter of 2026.[20] Its direct rivals are other specialist AI clouds, named in its annual report as CoreWeave, Crusoe and Lambda, and the large clouds from Amazon, Google, Microsoft and Oracle.[14] Against CoreWeave, Nebius carries less debt today: CoreWeave reported about $35.1 billion of debt against $5.5 billion of cash at June 30, 2026, while Nebius reported $8.5 billion of debt against $8.0 billion of cash (our arithmetic).[35][18] But Nebius has since added about $9 billion of net new debt, and its annual report discloses two material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting, which are gaps in how it checks its own numbers.[14][29][26][30]

Counterparty, collateral and closure: how the fund could fail

Three failure paths exist for a fund like this, and we weigh each. The first is the swap bank failing. The fund's schedule shows one bank, a one-year-plus contract running to October 19, 2027, and collateral posted under a standard master agreement; the risk is real but remote, and it would show first as a premium or discount to NAV, which the issuer page does not show in the copy we could read.[3][2] The second path is the fund running out of capacity to add swap exposure after a sharp rally. The prospectus says the fund may then use options or buy the stock, and that creations may be suspended in extraordinary circumstances, which could push the share price away from NAV.[1] The third is closure by the adviser. GraniteShares closed BULX, ETRL and MSDD, with a last trading day of June 18, 2026, and a closure forces a sale at the issuer's price and date, with a possible taxable payout.[8] None of these is the reason for our sale, but each one is a cost that a holder carries and a reason to keep the position small.

The underlying's position against CoreWeave

CoreWeave is the closest public comparison, because it sells the same service and has already borrowed heavily to do it. In the second quarter of 2026 CoreWeave reported revenue of $2,575 million, net interest expense of $640 million and a net loss of $626 million, and said its revenue backlog was about $104 billion with about 3.7 gigawatts of contracted power.[35] Nebius reported revenue of $582.3 million, interest expense of $119.1 million and a net loss from continuing operations of $190.4 million, and says it expects 5 gigawatts of contracted power by the end of 2026.[18][20] CoreWeave pays out about 25 cents of interest for each dollar of revenue and Nebius about 20 cents (our arithmetic), so both are paying heavily for equipment ahead of profits. This matters for NBIL because a funding scare at one neocloud has moved both stocks, and the fund doubles the move.

If the number is off

The three-point value of $7.65 is lower than the simulated mean of $13.12, because three points cannot hold the rare very large wins that pull the mean up. We therefore do not rest the verdict on the expected value. We rest it on the median and on the chance of loss: a typical outcome is a loss of most of the money, and fewer than one path in five ends higher.

The number

For Nebius's market value to be fair at our settings, the company would need to earn about $6.75 billion of free cash flow in 2036, which at a 20% margin means about $33.8 billion of revenue.

How it was calculated

We took the market value of $46.3 billion (271,855,218 shares at $170.47) and added net debt of $0.50 billion for an enterprise value of $46.8 billion. 6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate has a weakest assumption, and the honest way to test a sell verdict is to attack it. For the central case of $5.62, the weakest assumption is 95% volatility. For the probability-weighted value of $7.65, the weakest assumption is the 20% bull weight and the three-point shortcut. Fatal flaw 1: Nebius cannot fund its plan

Nebius guides to $20 billion to $25 billion of equipment spending for 2026 and has spent $8,130.3 million in six months against $981.3 million of revenue.[21][18] Analysts expect free cash flow of negative $17.54 billion for 2026.[38] It has raised about $19.2 billion in 2026, including a $3.5 billion bridge loan from Citibank that matures on October 16, 2027, carries a minimum liquidity covenant, and charges Term SOFR plus 2.75% rising to 3.50%.[26][19][29] If customers prepay less than the more than $9 billion expected for 2026, or lenders step back after more credit worries at CoreWeave, equity raises get harder and the stock falls first.[20][35] Nebius shares at $170.47 are also below the $183.22 and $180.31 conversion prices of the March notes, which means those holders are unlikely to convert and the debt stays debt.[31][13] A fall of 12.2% in one day on October 16 took the fund down 24.5%, and a similar day could come again.

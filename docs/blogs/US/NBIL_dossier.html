Before you read

NBIL is not a Treasury-bill fund, as the watchlist note said. It is a GraniteShares fund that aims to give twice the daily move of Nebius Group (NBIS), an AI cloud company.[1][2] We hold 50 shares worth $556.50, and the verdict is REPLACE_UNDERPERFORMER: sell all 50 by about November 6, 2026 (a provisional date until Nebius announces its report date), with a hard stop at $9.13. The chart is in a Stage 4 markdown, daily resetting costs about 63% a year at 100% volatility, and our scenarios average $6.34 against $11.13.[1] The call is wrong if Nebius volatility falls below 50% and the fund closes above $20.22, and at 100% volatility Nebius must rise about 66% in a year just for the fund to break even.

1. What the company does

NBIL is an exchange-traded fund, a basket-style product that trades on a stock exchange like a share. Its basket is unusual. It does not hold Nebius Group shares. It holds Treasury bills and one swap contract, and the swap pays it twice the daily gain or loss of NBIS.[2][5] A swap is a private contract in which one side pays the return of an asset and the other side pays a fee. At June 30, 2026 the fund's own report showed two holdings: Treasury bills at 71.55% of total investments and the Nebius swap with Clear Street at 28.40%.[3] The issuer is GraniteShares, which started the fund in early October 2025 at a net asset value (NAV, the value of one share of the fund) of $25.00.[2][5]

The word daily is the whole story. The fund resets its exposure at the close of every trading day, so each day starts with a fresh 200% bet on Nebius.[1] The prospectus says that for periods longer than one day the fund's return is each day's result multiplied together, and that this will very likely differ from twice the return of the stock.[1] FINRA, the broker industry regulator, has told brokers that daily-reset funds of this type typically are unsuitable for retail investors who plan to hold them longer than one trading session.[11] We use NBIL as a trading satellite, not as a savings holding, and the report judges it that way.

A buyer of NBIL pays in three ways. The first is the stated fee: a management fee of 1.30% plus other expenses of 0.20%, for a total of 1.50%, with a fee waiver that runs to December 31, 2026.[1] The second is the cost of the swap, which the prospectus estimated at 2.00% a year and which the June 30, 2026 schedule shows as a spread of 600 basis points over the overnight bank funding rate on a $187,542,014 notional amount.[1][5] The third is volatility decay, the slow loss that a daily-reset fund suffers when the stock swings up and down. The prospectus gives a table: at 100% annual volatility the fund is expected to lose 63.3% of its value over a year even when the stock finishes flat.[1]

The underlying business matters because it sets the swings. Nebius describes itself as the AI cloud company, building a full-stack platform for developers and companies.[16] Its capital spending goes mostly into GPUs (graphics processing units, the chips that train and run AI models) and data center hardware.[12] In 2025 its AI cloud unit reported $480.3 million of revenue in a group that reported $529.8 million, and the 20-F names Meta and Microsoft as customers on longer-term contracts.[12] The group also owns Avride, a self-driving and delivery robot developer, and TripleTen, an education company.[15] Nebius is spending far more than it earns to build capacity, which is why its share price, and therefore NBIL, moves so violently.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Leveraged ETF: a fund that aims for a multiple, here two times, of the daily move of one asset.
  • Daily reset: the fund restores its 200% exposure every trading day, so losses and gains compound along the path.
  • Volatility decay: the loss that a daily-reset fund suffers when the underlying rises and falls repeatedly, even if it ends where it started.
  • Swap: a private contract that exchanges the return of an asset for a fee; the other side is called the counterparty.
  • NAV: net asset value, the per-share value of everything the fund owns minus what it owes.
  • Stage 4: the markdown phase of the four-stage price cycle, with the price under a falling 50-day and 200-day average.
  • ATR: average true range, the typical size of one day's price swing, used here to place stops.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

A fund like NBIL has no moat of its own. Its competitive position is a question of price, size and safety against the other funds that sell the same bet. Three long funds track Nebius at twice the daily move: NBIL, Tradr's NEBX and Leverage Shares' NBIG.[8] NBIL charges 1.50% in its prospectus, against 1.30% for NEBX and 0.75% for NBIG.[1][6][7] Its actual expense ratio for the year to June 30, 2026 was 1.37%, below its own cap, which is a point in its favour.[3] Even so, NBIL is the most expensive of the three on the stated fee, and a buyer of the same exposure can pay about half as much at NBIG.

Size is the second test. At June 30, 2026 NBIL had net assets of $195,599,438 and 3,430,001 shares outstanding, up from $46,496,048.72 at December 31, 2025.[3][5][4] The June number is stale in one important way. The NAV per share was $57.03 on that date and the market price is $11.13 now, so assets are very likely much lower today unless buyers poured in. The issuer page we fetched did not show current assets, so we do not publish a number.[5][2] The risk is real. On October 9, 2026 Leverage Shares announced it was closing thirteen single-stock leveraged funds for their inability to attract sufficient assets.[9][10] NBIG was not on that list, and we do not claim NBIL is in trouble. We note only that small leveraged funds do get closed, and a closure forces a sale at a time the issuer picks.

Safety is the third test. NBIL's swap sits with a single counterparty, Clear Street, LLC, and the fund expects to post between 30% and 45% of its assets as collateral under its swap agreements.[5][1] If a counterparty failed, the fund could lose the gains the swap owes it. At June 30, 2026 those unrealized swap gains were $203,811,313, which is more than the fund's total net assets of $195,599,438, so the exposure to this one contract was large relative to the fund.[5] The swap runs to October 19, 2027, so the contract itself is long-dated.[5]

The underlying company is strong in some ways and fragile in others. It is growing at a very high rate: group revenue rose from $91.5 million in 2024 to $529.8 million in 2025, and $981.3 million arrived in the first half of 2026 alone.[12][15] Its direct rivals are other specialist AI cloud providers, including CoreWeave, Crusoe and Lambda, and general cloud companies such as Amazon, Google, Microsoft and Oracle.[12] Against CoreWeave it looks financially safer: about $0.50 billion of borrowings net of cash at June 30, 2026, versus roughly $29.54 billion at CoreWeave.[24] It is also fragile. Its 2025 annual report discloses two material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting, which are gaps in how it checks its own numbers.[12]

2B. Company specifics, operating KPIs, and management walk-the-talk

Layer 2 asks whether the product does what it says, using three to five measures chosen for this kind of fund. For a daily-reset leveraged fund the right measures are tracking accuracy, cost, volatility decay, counterparty and liquidity risk, and the strength of the underlying company. Each measure is scored PASS (1.0 point), WATCH (0.5) or FAIL (0.0).

The rule says a name qualifies at 3.0 points out of 5.0 with at most one FAIL, and fails with two or more FAILs. The cost measure is scored by a stated line: PASS needs a shortfall under 5% a year and a fee at or below both peers, WATCH means one of those two is met, and FAIL means neither is met. NBIL misses both, so cost is a FAIL. With volatility decay also a FAIL, NBIL scores 2.5 out of 5.0 with two FAILs, and Layer 2 fails. That agrees with the verdict, because the chart also fails Layer 1 in Chapter 3.

The number

We estimate the fund's one-year expected value at $6.34 per share, which is 43% below the $11.13 price, and its one-year breakeven, at 100% volatility and including the fee, at a Nebius gain of about 66%.

How it was calculated

We took three twelve-month Nebius prices, converted each to an NBIL price with the formula (1 + R) squared times the exponential of minus volatility squared, assumed 100% volatility and took off the 1.50% fee. We then weighted the three results by our probabilities of 20%, 45% and 35%.

The inputs

The 100% volatility sits between the 78% measured over 20 days and the 108% measured over the fund's life (our calculation)[27], and the prospectus quotes 80.07% for the five years to June 2025 and 117.79% at its worst 12 months.[1] The fee is the prospectus ceiling.[1] The Nebius prices are $281 (the Street mean target), $217.84 (the ledger 50-day average) and $84 (the lowest Street target).[25][24]

The range

At 80% volatility the three results would be $18.11, $10.89 and $1.62. At 108% volatility they would be $10.70, $6.43 and $0.96. The base case at 100% volatility is $7.6, the bull case $12.64 and the bear case $1.13.

If the number is off

If volatility is lower than 100%, the fund decays less and the expected value rises. At 80% it is still $9.09 on our weights, below today's price. At 40% the base case would be $17.59, a gain of 58%, and the weighted value $14.69, which is why a fall in volatility is a reassessment trigger. If Nebius volatility fell to 40%, the yearly decay would be about 15%, and the case would need a fresh look. If swap financing is higher than we assumed, the values fall further.

5. What could happen next

A reverse DCF asks what growth rate today's price already assumes, by working backward from the price to the cash flows a buyer must expect. It is not defined for NBIL, because a fund has no cash flow of its own: its value is the daily result of a swap. The ledger shows the same: no implied growth, no consensus earnings estimates and no analyst targets for NBIL, so the expectations table shows those as not available.

The underlying company does have numbers, and we looked at the ledger's own reverse DCF for NBIS. It solves for growth of -3.31% a year, but it starts from 75% of trailing operating cash flow of $5.258 billion. That cash flow includes customer prepayments: deferred revenue added $4,395.0 million to first-half 2026 operating cash flow.[15] Cash a customer pays in advance is a liability, not owner earnings, so we do not use that implied growth figure. The honest answer for the underlying is that free cash flow is deeply negative and a reverse DCF has no useful solution.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate has a weakest assumption. For the base case, the weakest assumption is 100% volatility. For the probability-weighted value, the weakest assumption is the 20% bull probability. A fast recovery in AI infrastructure stocks, with Nebius back above $281, would justify a 30% bull weight, and the expected value would then be $7.17, still below today's price. The date we would know is the Nebius third-quarter report. Red-team bear case: the strongest argument against selling

The best case for holding is that we are selling at the bottom. NBIL sits 0.15 ATR above a $10.67 floor, RSI is 30.56, Nebius is also near its swing low, and a recovery would be violent: the fund's best session in our data was +68.3% on August 12, 2026, the day of the Nebius second-quarter report (our calculation).[26][19] A holder with only $556.50 at stake gives up very little by waiting for a bounce. That is why the plan sells half at once and keeps half with a stop, and why we place the limit for the rest at $13.50.

Our answer is that the cost of waiting is not zero. The product loses value to decay every week in this volatility, the chart has not passed its recovery test, and a single session can take the whole position to the stop or through it: the fund's worst session since launch was -34.0% (July 1, 2026, our calculation).[26] We sell because the evidence for a turn is missing, not because we know the stock will fall.

Fatal flaw 1: Nebius cannot fund its plan

Nebius guides to $20 billion to $25 billion of 2026 capex and analysts expect $29.14 billion for 2027, against $981.3 million of revenue in the first half of 2026.[21][24][15] It has raised about $13.7 billion this year from convertible notes, share sales and a secured loan, and on October 22 it disclosed a $3.5 billion bridge facility from Citibank that matures October 16, 2027 at a rate starting at Term SOFR plus 2.75% and rising to 3.50%.[24][20] If customers pay less in advance than the $9 billion expected for 2026, or lenders step back after CoreWeave's bond yields rose above 15%, equity raises get harder.[21][25] A 30% fall in Nebius would take NBIL down about 51% before decay.

Fatal flaw 2: the fund breaks or closes

The prospectus says the fund could theoretically lose more than its net assets if the stock falls more than 50% in a day, and that it could limit or suspend creations and redemptions if it cannot get the exposure it needs.[1] The fund has one swap counterparty. Thirteen Leverage Shares funds were closed in October 2026 for lack of assets, so closure by an issuer is a live risk for small leveraged funds.[9] A closure forces a sale at the issuer's price and time, and may bring a taxable distribution.

Fatal flaw 3: a gap through the stop

Our stop at $9.13 is 17.97% below the $11.13 close, and the fund's typical daily swing is 28% of its price. A stop is only an order, and a gap can fill it far below the trigger. Stage 4 charts with RSI near 30 can fall fast, and on October 16 NBIL fell from $16.17 to $12.21 in one session (our calculation).[26] With 50 shares the most a total loss could take is the current $556.50, or 0.68% of net worth, which is why the position size is tolerable even if the stop fails.

7. What we would do, and when

The decision is REPLACE_UNDERPERFORMER: sell all 50 shares. The rule behind it is the technical routing rule, which makes a Stage 4 breakdown with the recovery test failing a Layer 1 disqualifier. NBIL is in Stage 4 and Test C fails on the short-term turn and on RSI of 30.56. The number that triggered it is the close of $11.13 under a falling 50-day average of $23.21. The decision becomes a hold only if the close rises above the 20-day average of $20.22 and the recovery test passes.

The distance to support is 0.15 ATR, which sits in the FULL_SIZE band of the support-distance ladder, the best band for a buyer. The ladder says how close a price is to a floor, but not whether to buy. Here the Layer 1 failure outranks it, so no new money goes in and the tier is TIER_0.

Portfolio heat is the share of net worth we would lose if every position hit its stop at once. Risk-unit sizing sets each position's weight so that a stop-out costs a fixed number of basis points (hundredths of a percent) of net worth, scaled to the conviction tier. For TIER_0 the budget is zero basis points, so the formula returns a 0% weight for new money. The existing 0.68% weight is a leftover, not a target.

Two terms matter for the stop. The 20-day and 50-day simple averages (SMA) are the average of the last 20 or 50 closing prices, and an exponential average (EMA) weights recent prices more. Structural invalidation is the price at which the setup is no longer true. For this fund it is a close under the $10.67 swing low, which is also the 63-day base low. The let-winners-run rule, which stops us trimming a strong Stage 2 holding on RSI alone, does not apply because NBIL is a loser and not a winner.

Here is the stop choice in numbers. The tactical rule for a trading vehicle sets the stop below the lower of the swing low ($10.67) and the 20-day average ($20.22), minus half an ATR of $3.08, which is $9.13. The core base-low rule would be the base low of $10.67 minus one ATR, which is $7.59. At the $11.13 close the base-case asymmetry is -1.77 to 1 with the $9.13 stop and -1.00 to 1 with $7.59, so neither stop gives a positive reward to risk. The stop at $9.13 implies a loss of 14.43% from $10.67 and 17.97% from $11.13.

Before you read

Palantir sells data software to governments and big firms, and its revenue rose 93% in the second quarter of 2026.[8] We still reject it as a new buy. Stock-based compensation (SBC) is the cost of paying staff in new shares, and it was 15.3% of 2025 revenue against a 5% limit; the dilution this causes is the fall in each owner's slice of the company.[2][22] The price of $209.05 sits on its yearly high, and the central target is below it.[20][17] We would be wrong if growth stays near 80% while share pay falls to 5%, which the report due 2 November 2026 will start to show[18].

What the company does

Palantir builds software platforms that connect to a customer's scattered databases, clean and link the data, and let analysts and AI models act on it. Its two older platforms are Gotham, used by defence and intelligence agencies, and Foundry, used by commercial firms. A newer layer, the Artificial Intelligence Platform, lets customers deploy language models on top of their own data.[1] The customer is a government department, a military command or a large corporation; it pays under multi-year contracts, usually for software access plus engineering help to get it running.

The company reports two segments. In 2025, Government revenue was $2,402.3 million (up 53%) and Commercial revenue was $2,073.2 million (up 60%), for total revenue of $4,475.4 million, up 56%.[1] In the second quarter of 2026 the mix moved further: Government was $989.5 million (up 79%) and Commercial $945.4 million (up 110%).[4] U.S. commercial revenue alone was $764 million, up 149% from a year earlier.[8]

The company had 954 customers at 31 December 2025, and its twenty largest customers paid an average of $93.9 million each in 2025, up from $64.6 million a year earlier.[1] By the second quarter of 2026 the twenty-customer trailing average had reached $124 million.[8] The current total customer count after 2025 was not found in the quarterly filing (research record customer_count_q2_2026 is not_found), so we show N/A rather than guess.

Revenue is recognised mostly as the software is delivered over the life of each contract. Signed but not yet recognised contracts are called remaining performance obligations (RPO), meaning signed contracts not yet recognised as revenue; they were $4.9 billion at 30 June 2026 against $4.1 billion at 31 December 2025.[10] Customer concentration matters: governments are the largest single customer type, and U.S. government revenue was $809 million in the quarter.[8]

Terms used in this report

Each term here is defined where it first appears. The 20/50/200-day moving average is the average closing price over the last 20, 50 or 200 trading days, a slowly moving line the price tends to bounce off. Stage 2 is the Weinstein stage that marks a confirmed uptrend. The relative strength index (RSI) is a meter from 0 to 100 of how fast the price has been rising. The average true range (ATR) is the average daily price swing in dollars. A base is a sideways stretch the price builds before a move, and the pivot is the top of a base. A breakout is a move above the pivot on heavy volume.

A stop loss is the price at which the thesis is wrong and the position is cut. The entry zone is the band of prices where buying is planned. Asymmetry (reward-to-risk) is the ratio of potential gain to potential loss from the entry: (target minus price) divided by (price minus stop). Gross margin is revenue minus the direct cost of the product, as a share of revenue, and operating margin is revenue minus all running costs, as a share of revenue. Free cash flow (FCF) is the cash left after running the business and buying assets. Guidance is the company's own published forecast for the coming period, and a consensus estimate is the average of analysts' forecasts for a number.

Why the business is strong or weak

The business is strong on every measure of growth and cash generation. Revenue growth accelerated from 56% in 2025 to 93% in the second quarter of 2026, and the company states a "Rule of 40" score of 155% (growth rate plus operating margin, where 40 is the usual bar for a healthy software company).[8] Government and commercial demand are both rising at the same time, which is unusual. Closed contract value in the quarter was $3.373 billion, up 49%, and U.S. commercial remaining deal value was $6.238 billion, up 124%.[9] There were 220 deals of at least $1 million, 98 of at least $5 million and 73 of at least $10 million.[9]

The profit structure has changed. Operating income was a loss of $411.0 million in 2021 and was $1,414.0 million in 2025, a 31.6% margin; in the second quarter of 2026 the operating margin under generally accepted accounting principles was 47%.[2][8] Gross profit was $3,686.3 million on $4,475.4 million of 2025 revenue, an 82.4% gross margin.[1] The company has no borrowings, holds $2,030 million of cash and $7,379 million of marketable securities, and has an undrawn $500 million credit line.[6]

The weakness is one number: stock-based compensation (see the terms above). SBC was $684.0 million in 2025, 15.3% of revenue, and $835.5 million over the twelve months to June 2026, about 13.6% of revenue.[2][5] Shares are issued to pay employees, so each existing owner holds a smaller slice. Diluted shares were about 2,565 million in 2025 and about 2,569 million in the latest period, so net dilution has been small in count terms because the company also withholds shares to cover taxes; the gate here, however, tests the expense against revenue, and the expense is large.[10]

Competitive position is strong in government (long accreditations, embedded in U.S. defence workflows) and still being proven in commercial markets, where hyperscale cloud vendors and workflow-software firms sell overlapping products. Net dollar retention, the usual proof that existing customers spend more each year, is not published in the filings we fetched (research record net_dollar_retention is not_found after four queries), so we mark it N/A. We use the substitute that is published: the top-twenty customer average revenue rising 67%.[8]

In short, the business is excellent. The cost of paying staff is the flaw. Growth is not the problem.

Why these estimates might be wrong

Fatal flaws. (1) SBC at 15.3% of revenue is three times the gate limit and is a standing cost to owners. (2) The reward-to-risk is below 1 to 1 on the base case and the stock sits in the NO_BUY band 4.8 ATR above the 50-day average. (3) The market price embeds growth far beyond any published guidance: a reverse calculation needs about $106 billion of revenue in 2035.

Weakest assumption in the price targets. Analyst targets lag a fast-moving price: they rose after the stock rose 60% in the third quarter, so the base target is a trailing figure.[18] Evidence of error: if third-quarter results (expected 2 November 2026 after the close; the company has not confirmed the date and some aggregators say 9 November) show revenue above the $2.160 to $2.164 billion guided, targets will move up.[8][18] The watchlist record's earlier "about 29 October" date is superseded.[23]

Weakest assumption in the DCF. The terminal growth and discount rate; we use judgemental ranges and a data-vendor discount rate of 13.05%, so the output is directional.

Red-team bear case, and the case against our own rejection

The strongest argument against REJECTED is that the gate is blunt. Palantir's SBC share fell from 50.5% to 15.3% in four years, its operating margin is the best in the peer set and its net share count rose only about 0.2% between the latest two annual and quarterly periods.[10] A strict rule rejects the best-run company in the group on a ratio that is improving fast. Against that, the rule is the portfolio's declared standard and the same rule archived NOW, SNOW, CRM and TWLO; relaxing it for the highest-priced name would be inconsistent.[23]

The bear case on the stock itself: at 81.6 times sales and a 0.67% FCF yield, any growth disappointment can cut the price by half or more; the consensus low target is $80 and the stock has moved from $106.37 to $209.58 in a year.[16][17][20]

Hinge claim: that 2027 revenue growth stays near 50% or better. If it slows below 35%, the base case becomes a loss; if it stays above 50%, the valuation debate shifts to margin and SBC rather than growth. This is first checkable at the 2 November 2026 report and again with the 2026 annual filing in February 2027.

Items not verified: net dollar retention, current customer count and recent insider Form 4 sales (record insider_form4_sales_recent is not_found); all are marked N/A and none changes the verdict, which rests on the SBC filing figure and the price arithmetic.

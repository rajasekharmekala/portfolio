1. What the company does

McKesson Corporation buys drugs and medical supplies from manufacturers. It sells them to pharmacies, hospitals, doctors' offices and cancer clinics. Its fiscal year ends on March 31, so fiscal 2026 means the twelve months to March 31, 2026. In that year revenue was $403.4 billion. All profit figures in this chapter are GAAP, short for generally accepted accounting principles, unless we say adjusted. Gross profit was $14.55 billion, or 3.6 cents per sales dollar. Operating income was $6.21 billion, or 1.54 cents[1]. Think of a toll road. Each trip pays a tiny fee, so profit depends on volume and on keeping costs flat as volume grows.

Since the second quarter of fiscal 2026 the company reports four segments[10]. North American Pharmaceutical moves branded, generic, specialty and biosimilar drugs through 27 US distribution centers[1]. Its customers include retail chains, independent pharmacies, hospitals and physicians. Oncology & Multispecialty supports community doctors who treat cancer and other complex diseases. It sells drug distribution, group purchasing, software and practice management. It includes the US Oncology Network, which had about 3,400 providers in the first quarter of fiscal 2027[1][18]. In June 2025 McKesson paid $2.5 billion in cash for a 70 percent controlling interest in Core Ventures, the business arm of Florida Cancer Specialists[1].

Prescription Technology Solutions sells software and services that connect patients, pharmacies, insurers and drug makers. It links to more than 50,000 pharmacies and has supported more than 650 biopharma brands[1]. Medical-Surgical Solutions sells supplies to physician offices, surgery centers and nursing homes. McKesson plans to separate it into a new company named Wellverse[1][3].

Who pays depends on the product. Pharmacies and providers pay McKesson for the drugs they receive. Manufacturers are customers too, for distribution and for programs that help patients get and afford therapy[1]. For branded drugs the contract pays a fee for service. Management said more than 95 percent of branded drugs work that way[18]. A list-price cut therefore lowers reported revenue far more than it lowers profit. The sales mix still matters. First-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue from GLP-1 medications, a drug class that management reports on its own line, was $15 billion, up 24 percent[18][27]. Cencora reports that these drugs carry lower gross margins than the rest of its mix[21]. Customer concentration is high. CVS was about 24 percent of fiscal 2026 revenue, and the next two customers were 11 percent and 10 percent[1].

Profit is spread differently from revenue. North American Pharmaceutical is 83 percent of sales but 48 percent of adjusted segment profit, because its margin is about 1 cent per dollar. Oncology & Multispecialty is 12 percent of sales and 20 percent of segment profit. Prescription Technology Solutions is under 1.5 percent of sales and 16 percent of profit, at a 19 percent margin[4]. So McKesson is a low-margin pipe plus two higher-margin service businesses. The Medical-Surgical separation matters for any forecast, because it removes 14.5 percent of segment profit.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Adjusted earnings per share (EPS) is McKesson's own profit-per-share measure. It removes items the company calls one-time, such as deal gains, restructuring and litigation charges. It can run above or below the audited GAAP figure.
  • Wholesale acquisition cost (WAC) is the list price a manufacturer sets for a drug. McKesson's revenue follows it, but its fee does not.
  • Noncontrolling interest is the slice of a subsidiary's profit that belongs to outside owners, such as Apollo's stake in Medical-Surgical Solutions.
  • Group purchasing organization is a buying club that pools many practices' orders to win lower drug prices.
  • Accelerated share repurchase is a deal in which a bank delivers shares to the company up front and the company pays for them over time.
  • Equity method is an accounting treatment in which a minority investment shows up as a share of the investee's profit rather than as consolidated sales.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The strongest part of the case is scale and position. The 10-K, the annual report filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, names Cencora and Cardinal Health as McKesson's primary competitors in distribution[1]. That tells us the field of scaled national rivals is short. We have no cited market-share figure. So the moat claim rests on renewals and margins, not on a share number. The renewal evidence is good. On October 1, 2026 McKesson signed an agreement in principle to extend its largest customer, CVS Health, through June 2032. That is five years beyond the old June 2027 expiry. The company also reaffirmed its fiscal 2027 guidance[17][1]. The definitive contract is still pending, and its terms are not public[26].

The second strength is a specialty and oncology platform that earns more per dollar than distribution. Oncology & Multispecialty grew adjusted segment profit 53 percent in fiscal 2026, to $1,435 million. Its adjusted margin rose from 2.54 to 2.96 percent[4][10]. Management said that, excluding Core Ventures, first-quarter fiscal 2027 segment revenue grew about 24 percent and profit about 15 percent[18]. That is in line with the segment's 13 to 16 percent long-term profit target[9]. Prescription Technology Solutions earns a 19.45 percent adjusted margin[4]. We cite no market-share figure for either segment, so we make no ranking claim.

The weaknesses are thin margins, concentration, policy exposure and an old legal bill. The consolidated adjusted operating margin has been flat at 1.56 to 1.60 percent for three years[10][4]. At that margin, a change of 0.1 point moves operating profit by about $400 million on $403 billion of revenue (our calculation). The top three customers were 45 percent of fiscal 2026 revenue, and CVS alone was 21 percent of trade receivables[1]. The company names the Inflation Reduction Act, tariffs and the 340B drug-discount program as policy risks[1][18]. Management said Part D price negotiation does not start until January 2028. It also said the 340B proposal is still in a comment period[18]. No source we read quantifies the effect of generic GLP-1 products. Management expects GLP-1 growth to continue through fiscal 2027, with quarter-to-quarter variability[18]. The opioid settlement liability was $5.7 billion at March 31, 2026. It is payable in annual installments through 2038, with $601 million due in the next twelve months[1].

The last point is the shape of the balance sheet. McKesson has a total deficit of $1,777 million. It bought back shares that sit in the accounts at a cost of $32,005 million. It funds its inventory with $59,973 million of payables to suppliers[1]. That float is a real advantage while sales grow. It also means working capital can swing operating cash flow by billions of dollars in one quarter. The first quarter of fiscal 2027 showed it. Free cash flow was an outflow of $372 million, against an outflow of $1,107 million a year earlier[3].

Customer concentration, in numbers

CVS was about 28 percent of revenue in fiscal 2024. It was about 24 percent in each of fiscal 2025 and fiscal 2026[11][13][1]. The ten largest customers were about 43 percent of trade receivables at March 31, 2026[1]. Pharmaceutical Commerce reports that CVS was 28 percent of Cardinal Health's fiscal 2026 revenue, so both large wholesalers depend on the same buyer[26]. A loss of CVS volume would show up first as lower revenue. Fixed distribution costs would stay, so operating profit would fall too. That is why the extension to June 2032 matters more than any quarterly number.

What the filings say about divestitures and discontinued operations

The Norway sale closed on January 30, 2026. The earlier sale of Canadian retail businesses closed on December 30, 2024. Neither was classified as a discontinued operation, so their gains and charges sit inside reported GAAP results[1][5]. Fiscal 2026 GAAP operating income includes a $480 million net pre-tax gain on Norway. Fiscal 2025 includes a $667 million charge on the Canadian retail sale[1]. Medical-Surgical Solutions is still a fully consolidated segment, not a discontinued operation. Every trailing number in this report still includes it. After a split-off or spin-off, trailing sales, profit and cash flow will be lower. So will the company's value. We therefore compare whole-company value with whole-company cash flow[1].

A moving average is the average closing price over a set number of trading days. We use the 20-day, 50-day and 200-day averages as slow lines that a price tends to respect. The relative strength index (RSI) is a 0-to-100 meter of how fast the price has been rising. Above 70 is called overbought. The average true range (ATR) is the typical daily price swing in dollars. We use it to measure distance in units of normal movement. A Stage 2 uptrend is the phase where the price is above a rising 50-day average. That average is itself above a rising 200-day average. Stage 4 is the mirror-image downtrend. A base is a sideways stretch the price builds before a move.

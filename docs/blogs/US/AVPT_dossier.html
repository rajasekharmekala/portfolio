Chapter 1

Core Investor Question: How a library basement utility became the primary data governance layer for 400 million enterprise cloud seats.

AvePoint began in July 2001 in Somerset, New Jersey. It was founded by Dr. Tianyi Jiang and Kai Gong. They started in a public library basement with $3,000 in savings. Microsoft had just released SharePoint Portal Server 2001. Employees loved it for sharing files and project lists. But Microsoft's early architecture had a major flaw. If an employee deleted a document library, recovery was painful. The administrator had to restore the entire SQL database from tape. That took the whole server cluster offline for hours.

Dr. Jiang held a PhD in data mining from Rutgers University. He had built high-speed data systems at Lehman Brothers. He and Gong built DocAve. It was the first tool capable of granular, item-level SharePoint backup. Administrators could restore a single missing file in minutes. The product was an instant hit. For a decade, AvePoint rode the growth of on-premises SharePoint. DocAve expanded into migration, compliance, and archiving. The founders funded global growth entirely from cash flow. They took zero outside venture capital for their first thirteen years.

The Cannibalization Crossroads: The 2014 Office 365 Existential Crisis

In February 2014, Satya Nadella became Chief Executive Officer of Microsoft and famously declared that Microsoft was pivoting to a 'Mobile First, Cloud First' corporate mandate. Microsoft began aggressively migrating enterprise workloads from on-premises Exchange servers and local SharePoint deployments into Office 365 (later rebranded Microsoft 365).

For AvePoint, this platform pivot was an existential crisis. In 2014, over ninety percent of AvePoint's revenue came from high-margin, upfront perpetual software licenses and annual maintenance contracts for on-premises SharePoint. Under Nadella's cloud architecture, enterprise customers were abandoning local server infrastructure. If AvePoint clung to its perpetual licensing cash cow, its enterprise customer base would steadily evaporate. Jiang and Gong made the high-stakes decision to cannibalize their existing business: they froze new perpetual product lines, redirected hundreds of software developers, and spent four grueling years re-architecting their entire codebase into a multi-tenant, cloud-native Software-as-a-Service platform hosted directly on Microsoft Azure.

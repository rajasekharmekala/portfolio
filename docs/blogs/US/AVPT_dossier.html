1. What the company does & day-to-day vertical operations

AvePoint sells enterprise software. It helps firms back up, move, govern, and manage data in Microsoft 365 and other cloud tools. It also lists Google Workspace, Salesforce, Dynamics, Azure, and AWS. IT, security, and legal teams pay for safer data, easier recovery, and better control as users and tools change. The company sells through its own sales team and a large partner network. In Q2, management said it served more than 28,000 organizations and had 6,000 partners. Those are company claims; this review did not check each count.[1][2]

The company reports total revenue, SaaS revenue, ARR, and client retention. It does not break revenue out by product. The map below shows what the products do, not how much each one earns. Backup and governance draw subscription fees. Migration and other help can add project work. Those services can carry lower gross margins.[1][2]

The customer workflow starts with the tenant, not a physical product

At setup, an IT or legal team lists key workspaces, user groups, data owners, and retention rules. The team picks workloads, links service accounts, sets user roles, and then chooses backup or data rules. Backups need a tested restore. A move needs a clean map of files and users. Data rules need owners who can act on them. These steps are based on product pages, not an inside view of AvePoint's own work.[12][14][18]

After setup, the software must keep pace with users, groups, licenses, access rights, and cloud changes. The client or a service partner checks for missed data, fixes faults, and adds workloads. This can help keep customers. It also adds support work and risk when cloud tools change.[13][16][17]

SaaS subscriptions anchor the economic model; services can dilute the blended margin

In Q2 2026, SaaS revenue was $98.5 million, or 79% of sales, and grew 27% year over year. The rest includes services and term licenses. AvePoint does not split that revenue by product. Management said software gross margin was 83%, while total non-GAAP gross margin was 73.7%. That gap shows why service mix and staff time matter.[1][2]

A client may start with a migration or backup, then add more workspaces, users, or data rules. This could lead to more subscriptions, but AvePoint has not shown a full account-by-account study. Investors can track net new ARR, client retention, large-account growth, and channel sales to see if this path works.[1][2]

2. Competitive moat, industry structure & external learning hub

AvePoint's edge is breadth. Its tools cover migration, backup, data rules, and managed services. It also sells through many partners. In Q2, it said 59% of ARR came through the channel. Two-thirds of new ARR came through partners. This can lower the cost to reach clients. It can also make the company depend on partners. It does not prove pricing power or exclusive access.[2]

Microsoft is also a rival. It sells built-in M365 backup. Veeam sells M365 backup and restore. Rubrik sells backup plus sign-in recovery, data checks, and AI controls. These are vendor claims, not lab tests. Buyers should test what each tool covers, how it restores data, what it costs, and who helps when it fails.[18][20][21]

An edge can form when a firm tests its restore plan, sets data rules, links many cloud tools, and trains staff. A switch then takes time and brings risk. Still, Microsoft owns M365 and can add more tools. Veeam and Rubrik seek the same budgets. We found no public data on win rates, price gains, or the time clients stay after a switch.[12][18][19][20][21]

More AI tools can give staff and agents new paths to company data. This raises demand for access rules and safe backup. AvePoint's own survey and Q2 call focus on this need. The survey is run by AvePoint, and the earnings release does not break out AI sales. AI is a lead to watch, not proof of new revenue yet.[2][23]

Evidence and limits

Useful proof comes from renewal, new ARR, large-client growth, channel sales, and customer cases. AvePoint reports 89% dollar gross retention and 111% net retention for Q2. It says migration tools lowered gross retention by about two points. These are company-defined measures. An 89% gross rate still means some customers cut or leave before growth inside accounts is counted.[1][2]

AvePoint picks the customers in its case studies. These pages show what a project did, but not the average time or return for all clients. 2B. Operational business KPIs, structural potential & CEO walk-the-talk

For a subscription software vendor, bookings and ARR create the growth narrative, but retention and delivery economics decide whether that growth compounds. Annual recurring revenue is a point-in-time contract measure, not recognized revenue or free cash flow. A healthy reading therefore checks the installed-base retention profile, the size and growth of enterprise cohorts, the mix of channel additions, and whether higher recurring revenue converts into cash without giving back margin.[1][2]

AvePoint reports ARR and retention in company-defined form. Gross retention measures the retained recurring base before expansion; net retention adds expansion and offsets reductions. The company discloses dollar-based rather than logo retention in the releases reviewed here. That helps assess spending inside existing accounts but does not reveal customer-count churn or concentration by account.

The business KPIs that actually matter

Five measures are most useful. ARR and net new ARR test demand; SaaS revenue tests conversion into recognized subscription revenue; gross and net retention test durability; large-customer counts and channel mix test distribution and enterprise expansion; software and consolidated margins plus operating cash flow test economics. Each metric is a check on a different link in the model.

Management has delivered revenue above the original annual range in each of the two completed years reviewed. FY2024 revenue of $330.5m exceeded the prior-year range of $308.6m–$316.6m. FY2025 revenue of $419.5m exceeded its original $380m–$388m range. FY2026 remains open. At Q2, management raised ARR guidance but lowered revenue and non-GAAP operating-income ranges from the initial FY2026 outlook. This is a coherent explanation, but the market still needs to see the spending produce retention, net new ARR, and sustainable margins. The Nov. 5 call is the next test.[1][2][4][9]

The product narrative has also moved from data protection and governance toward AI-agent governance. The potential is large, but the evidence needed to prove the strategy is commercial: paid product adoption, incremental ARR, lower customer-risk exposure, and cross-sell. The Q2 release did not break out AI revenue. Management's product language should therefore be treated as a thesis to monitor rather than a separate valuation premium.[1][2][23]

On the Q2 2026 earnings call, CEO Dr. Tianyi Jiang said, “AI agents are being created faster than organizations can manage them.” This frames a real governance problem, but the release did not break out AI-related sales; paid adoption and incremental ARR remain the tests. [2]

On the same call, the CFO said, “FX headwinds offset the ARR raise and the Q2 outperformance.” That caveat matters: stronger ARR does not ensure the same growth in reported revenue or operating income. 3. Financial trajectory & valuation sanity check

Revenue growth and ARR growth remain strong, though their rates are starting to moderate. Annual revenue rose from $271.8m in FY2023 to $330.5m in FY2024 and $419.5m in FY2025. SaaS revenue increased from $161.0m to $230.7m and $319.2m in those years. The current business retains a premium software profile, but investors should evaluate the price against growth durability and cash flow rather than use a broad software label.[4][5][6]

Profitability improved from a GAAP operating loss in 2023 to positive GAAP operating income in 2024 and 2025. Non-GAAP operating margin increased from 8.1% in FY2023 to 14.4% and 18.9%, then stood at 16.3% in Q2 2026. The Q2 margin is a single quarter; it should not be compared mechanically with full-year margins. Q2 management said higher investment in technology and go-to-market was planned for the second half.[1][2][4][5][6]

Cash merits a separate reading. FY2025 operating cash flow was $85.3m, below FY2024's $88.9m, even as revenue expanded. First-half 2026 operating cash flow improved to $40.2m from $20.8m. Cash and cash equivalents declined from $481.1m at December 2025 to $417.3m at June 2026, while the company repurchased $110.3m of common stock in the first half. These figures leave a substantial liquidity cushion, but buybacks consume the same cash that could fund product investment or acquisitions.[1][2][4][5]

The counterweight is 89% gross retention and a lower SaaS growth rate in Q2 than Q1. Watch those measures through the next two reports before paying for an AI-platform outcome.[1][2][3]

6. Key risks, thesis breakers & bear case

The strongest bear case is that AvePoint's products solve important but non-exclusive tasks. Microsoft owns the principal M365 platform and is expanding native backup. Veeam and Rubrik compete with specialist or broader resilience suites. If customers see enough value in built-in tools, AvePoint may need more sales effort or lower pricing to defend renewals.[18][19][20][21]

The current operating record is good, yet the valuation leaves less room for a routine slowdown. Q2 SaaS revenue growth eased to 27% from Q1's 35%, gross retention stayed at 89%, and management plans elevated H2 technology and go-to-market spending. If net new ARR fails to offset those costs, the growth multiple can reset before the long-term AI story is proven.[1][2][3]

Platform concentration and native competition

A large share of the product story touches Microsoft 365. Microsoft's native backup product gives customers a first-party choice and can shape API access and pricing. The multi-cloud portfolio reduces dependence, but the company has not reported the share of ARR outside Microsoft or the revenue contribution of non-Microsoft products. Track workload diversification and customer choice rather than assuming breadth has neutralized platform risk.[12][13][18][19]

Gross retention and migration mix

An 89% dollar gross retention rate is not a trivial leakage rate. Net retention of 111% means expansion more than offsets contraction among retained accounts in the reported cohort calculation, but this is not the same as low customer churn. Management said migration products lowered GRR by roughly two points; that explanation may be fair, yet migration can remain a structural source of project churn. If GRR falls or NRR approaches 100%, the ARR thesis weakens.[1][2]

Service margins and reinvestment

Consolidated non-GAAP gross margin was 73.7%, below the prior year's 74.8%, while software margins were 83%. Services and term-license mix can weigh on consolidated profitability. Management also raised second-half investment in engineering, partner enablement, sales capacity, and brand awareness. The spend has a clear stated purpose; the proof is faster product delivery, higher partner-sourced ARR, and better returns, not an AI narrative alone.[1][2]

Cash generation, capital return, and dilution

FY2025 operating cash flow declined from $88.9m in FY2024 to $85.3m. First-half 2026 operating cash flow improved, but cash fell from $481.1m to $417.3m as AvePoint repurchased $110.3m of stock. Repurchases can offset employee dilution, as management says, but buybacks also compete with investment and acquisitions. Reported first-half GAAP income includes a $19.9m tax benefit from releasing a valuation allowance; investors should not annualize that benefit.[1][2][4][5]

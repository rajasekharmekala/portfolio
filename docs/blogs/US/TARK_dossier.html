1. What the company does

TARK is an exchange-traded fund (ETF), which is a basket of investments that trades on a stock exchange like a share. It is run by AXS Investments LLC under the Tradr ETFs brand and sits inside Investment Managers Series Trust II, a Delaware statutory trust. [1] [2] The fund's stated goal is to deliver two times (200%) the daily performance of the ARK Innovation ETF, before fees and expenses, and it says plainly that it does not try to meet that goal over any period longer than one trading day. [1] Fund returns are quoted at net asset value (NAV), which is the value of the fund's assets minus its liabilities, divided by its shares. Nobody pays this fund for goods or services. Investors pay it fees, and what it earns comes from the price moves of ARKK.

The fund gets its two-times exposure through equity swaps. A swap is a contract in which a bank agrees to pay the fund the return of ARKK on an agreed dollar amount, and the fund pays the bank a financing rate. [1] On 30 June 2026 the fund had four swaps, with Clear Street, Goldman Sachs, TD Cowen and Wells Fargo, with notional amounts of $5,032,950, $3,126,441, $4,944,120 and $17,202,833, and financing rates from 3.63% to 4.18%, which is the Overnight Bank Funding Rate of 3.63% plus 0 to 55 basis points. [5] A basis point (bps) is one hundredth of a percentage point. Wells Fargo carried 56.8% of the notional (our calculation). Between 40% and 80% of the portfolio may sit in collateral such as Treasury bills, money market funds, short-term bond funds and commercial paper, depending on what the counterparties require, and the advisor expects to rebalance the fund's holdings every day to keep the two-times exposure. [1]

ARKK is an actively managed fund that holds between 35 and 55 companies chosen for a theme ARK calls disruptive innovation, and on 23 October 2026 it held 46 positions. [1] [20] The ten largest weights add up to 49.87% and the five largest to 31.70%: Tesla 10.43%, Space Exploration Technologies 6.46%, Circle Internet Group 5.25%, Tempus AI 5.00% and OpenAI Series C 4.56%. [20] Two positions, OpenAI and Kalshi, are private companies with no exchange price, and together they are 6.16% of ARKK (our calculation). [20] Because TARK doubles the daily move, a Tesla weight of 10.43% in ARKK means a Tesla exposure of about 20.9% of TARK's net assets.

Net assets have fallen from $60,367,741 on 31 December 2024 to about $11.77 million on 23 October 2026, a drop of about 80.5% (our calculation). [7] [22] A small fund matters for three reasons. It can be closed, it trades thinly, and its sponsor has a record of closing funds. We cover all three in chapters 2 and 2B. Position status: not held. The account record shows a purchase of 10 shares on 1 April 2026 for $362.61 and a sale of 10 shares on 23 October 2026 for $450.04, so the position is closed and the watchlist's holding label is out of date.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Leveraged fund is a fund that magnifies its target's moves by a fixed multiple. TARK aims for two times.
  • Daily reset means the fund rebuilds its exposure to two times at the end of every trading day, so its return over longer periods is a chain of daily results.
  • Volatility drag is the loss that comes from daily swings. A fund that falls 10% and then rises 10% ends below where it started, and two times leverage makes the loss larger.
  • Swap is a contract in which a bank pays the fund the return of an asset in exchange for a financing rate.
  • Net asset value (NAV) is the value of a fund's assets minus its liabilities, divided by its shares.
  • Capital gains distribution is a payout of gains the fund realised. It is taxable, and the share price falls by the same amount on the ex-date.
  • Reverse split exchanges several shares for one. It lifts the price and does not change the value of a holding.
  • Support is a price where buyers have stepped in before. Here it is the lowest price of the last 20 trading days, $44.26.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The fund does what it says on the label. Over the last 12 months to 23 October 2026, which is 252 closes and 251 daily returns, the daily returns of TARK and two times ARKK moved together with a correlation of 0.9978 and a slope of 0.992 (our calculation). [30] [31] Correlation is a measure from −1 to 1 of how closely two series move together, and a slope near 1 means the fund moved as much as the target says it should. The fund's own annual report states an average daily correlation of over 0.99 to two times ARKK for the year to 31 March 2026, after fees and the cost of leverage financing. [3] Our numbers agree, so tracking is a strength and not a risk to the thesis.

The weakness is what the daily reset does to a longer holding. From the first full day on 3 May 2022 to 23 October 2026, ARKK gained 64.9% on a total-return basis, and TARK lost 26.3% (our calculation). [30] [31] A fund that doubled each day's ARKK move with no costs would have gained only 10.2%, and simply doubling ARKK's total return would have suggested 129.8%. The gap between those two figures is volatility drag. The gap between the no-cost path and the real fund, 36.6 points, is the cost of fees, financing and trading. The prospectus says it in plain words: for periods longer than a single trading day the fund will lose money if ARKK is flat, and it may lose money even if ARKK rises. [1]

The second weakness is size. Net assets were $60,367,741 at the end of 2024, $26,624,643 at the end of 2025, $18,727,571 at 31 March 2026 and $19,500,392 at 30 June 2026, and a data vendor shows $11.77 million with 261,380 shares outstanding on 23 October 2026. [7] [6] [3] [5] [22] On the last session only 5,900 shares traded, and the median daily volume over the last 60 sessions was 9,250 shares, about $438,056 a day (our calculation). [30] A fund this small is cheap for the sponsor to close, and a sponsor that announced the closure of 11 funds in five months has shown it will close small ones. [16] [17] [18] If TARK were liquidated, shareholders of record would receive cash equal to net asset value on the liquidation date, which would avoid a loss beyond what the market has already taken but would force an exit at a time the owner did not choose. [19]

The third weakness is what sits underneath. ARKK's price is 47.6% below its February 2021 closing high of $156.58 (our calculation), and its five-year return to 23 October 2026 is -30.4% on price against +88.3% for QQQ, the Nasdaq-100 fund (our calculation). [31] [32] Of ARKK's weights, 46.45% sit in 21 companies with zero or negative trailing earnings per share, 46.69% in 20 companies with positive earnings, and 6.86% in five positions with no public earnings figure (our calculation). [20] [36] Chapter 5 turns this into the look-through valuation. The short version is that a whole-fund price-to-earnings ratio is not meaningful, so the case for owning TARK rests on price direction alone.

The competitive position: who else offers two times exposure

None of the comparison funds doubles ARKK. Its sibling SARK seeks the inverse of ARKK's daily move, and the large leveraged funds, QLD (two times the Nasdaq-100) and TQQQ (three times the Nasdaq-100), follow a different, more liquid index. [29] [27] [28] QLD has $14.57 billion of assets and an expense ratio of 0.89%, and TQQQ has $37.55 billion and 0.78%, against $11.77 million and 1.48% for TARK. [24] [25] [22] The Nasdaq-100 funds spread their swaps over banks such as BNP Paribas, Societe Generale, Bank of America, Barclays and Citibank. [25] TARK's niche is two times ARKK in particular. That niche is only worth something if the owner expects ARKK to trend upward over days or weeks, and the cost and size disadvantages against the larger funds are real.

The weakest assumption in each estimate

For the break-even return of 12.6% a year, the weakest assumption is that volatility stays near 37% and cost stays near 9.9%. The last three months show a shortfall of 19.1% a year, and a calmer ARKK would lower the break-even. Some terms first. Portfolio heat is the share of net worth that would be lost if every open position hit its stop at once, and the house limit is 12.0%. Risk-unit sizing sets each position so that a stop-out costs a fixed slice of net worth: the weight equals the risk budget in basis points divided by the stop distance in percent. A basis point (bps) is 0.01%. The tiers set the budget: TIER_1 is 100 bps, TIER_2 is 75, TIER_3 is 50, TIER_4 is 35, and TIER_0 means zero size. TIER_4 is the smallest tier with a risk budget, and it is the yardstick here because this is a speculative leveraged fund. CMP is the current market price, $45.06. An SMA is a simple moving average, which weights every day equally, and an EMA is an exponential moving average, which weights recent days more. Structural invalidation is the price that would show the thesis is wrong. Let-winners-run is the rule that lets a winning position grow while the stop trails.

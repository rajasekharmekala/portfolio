1. What the company does

Targa Resources is a midstream company. Midstream means the pipes, plants and ships that sit between a gas or oil well and the customer who finally uses the product. Targa runs two businesses. Gathering and Processing collects raw natural gas from producers' wells, cleans it, and pulls out natural gas liquids (NGLs), which are ethane, propane and butane. Logistics and Transportation then moves those liquids by pipeline to Mont Belvieu, Texas, splits them into separate products, stores them, and loads propane and butane onto ships at the Galena Park Marine Terminal near Houston.[1][7]

Producers pay Targa fees to gather, treat and process their gas. The Permian Basin of West Texas and southeast New Mexico is where most of the growth sits. Downstream customers, such as chemical plants and overseas buyers of propane and butane, pay for pipeline transport, splitting, storage and ship loading. Fees from midstream services were $2,624.8 million in 2025, which is 15.4% of the $17,028.3 million of total revenue. The other $14,403.5 million was sales of gas and liquids, and $10,507.8 million of cost went to product purchases and fuel.[1] Revenue is therefore large compared with profit, and this report judges size by gross profit (revenue minus product purchases and fuel, our calculation: $6,520.5 million, or 38.3% of revenue) and by adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization). The 10-K describes the contract mix as predominantly fee-based, with some Coastal contracts that pay Targa a share of the liquids.[1] Commodity prices still matter: in the second quarter of 2026 the realized natural gas price on Targa's own equity volumes was negative, at $2.48 per million British thermal units, because Permian pipelines were full and the local Waha price turned negative.[7]

The scale is large. In the second quarter of 2026 Targa's Permian plants took in 7,187.3 million cubic feet of gas a day, 14.5% more than a year earlier, split between Delaware at 3,793.8 and Midland at 3,393.5.[7] Downstream, fractionation volumes were 1,206.1 thousand barrels a day (up 24.5%), NGL pipeline transport was 1,098.9 (up 14.3%) and export volumes were 487.1 (up 15.1%).[7] The 10-K puts effective export capacity at about 14.0 million barrels a month at year-end 2025, and management reported record loadings of 14.8 million barrels a month in the second quarter.[1][16] The 10-K names Grand Prix Pipeline LLC, with Targa NGL and Targa Gulf Coast, as regulated common-carrier NGL pipelines, but the pages we read give no separate Grand Prix volume, so this report uses system-wide NGL transport volume.[1]

Growing this network costs a great deal. Management guides net growth capital expenditure, the spending on new plants and pipes, to about $4.5 billion in 2026, against about $250 million of maintenance capital expenditure, the spending that only keeps existing assets running.[7] Five plants in Permian Delaware (Copperhead, Yeti, Yeti II, Roadrunner III and Copperhead II) are under construction, as are the Train 12 and Train 13 fractionators and the Speedway NGL pipeline.[7] Speedway starts with about 500 thousand barrels a day of capacity, expandable to 1,000, and the Galena Park export expansion lifts capacity to about 19 million barrels a month. Both are due in the third quarter of 2027.[1] In January 2026 Targa also bought Stakeholder Midstream for $1.25 billion, funded with $650.0 million of commercial paper and $600.0 million from its receivables facility.[6]

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Midstream — the pipes, plants and terminals between a well and the end user.
  • NGLs (natural gas liquids) — ethane, propane, butane and heavier liquids separated from raw natural gas.
  • Inlet volume — the gas entering a processing plant, measured in million cubic feet per day.
  • Fractionation — splitting a mixed stream of NGLs into separate products such as propane and butane.
  • Waha — the natural gas pricing hub in the Permian Basin of West Texas; a negative Waha price means the pipes out of the basin are too full.
  • Egress — the pipeline capacity that carries gas out of a producing basin.
  • LPG — liquefied petroleum gas, mainly propane and butane, which Targa loads on ships for export.
  • Adjusted EBITDA — Targa's own measure of earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and certain other items; it is not an official accounting measure.
  • Adjusted cash flow from operations — Targa's adjusted EBITDA less cash interest and cash taxes; it is the nearest current figure to the older distributable cash flow measure.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

Targa is a strong operator in a business where position decides profit. The company owns the chain from the wellhead to the ship, which management calls a wellhead-to-water system.[16] A producer that signs with Targa to gather its gas also feeds Targa's pipelines, fractionators and export dock, so each new plant lifts volume across the whole network. The 10-K says the location and links between these assets are not easily replicated; that is the company's own claim, and the volume record that follows is the evidence we can check.[1]

Where the strength comes from

Adjusted EBITDA rose from $2,052.0 million in 2021 to $4,957.4 million in 2025. From 2022 to 2025 that is 19.6% a year (our calculation from $2,901.1 million to $4,957.4 million).[14][13][9] Fee revenue grew 12.1% a year over the same three years, from $1,863.8 million to $2,624.8 million (our calculation).[4][1] Net income attributable to Targa shareholders rose from $1,195.5 million in 2022 to $1,923.0 million in 2025, which is 17.2% a year, and diluted earnings per share (EPS), profit divided by the number of shares, rose from $3.88 to $8.49.[4][1]

The second quarter of 2026 shows the same pattern. Adjusted EBITDA was $1,603.1 million, up 37.8% from $1,163.0 million a year earlier, and management raised its full-year 2026 range twice: from $5.4 billion to $5.6 billion in February, to $5.7 billion to $5.9 billion in May, and to the top end of that range in August.[9][8][7] The chief executive said 2026 growth over 2025 may be close to $1 billion.[16] Credit ratings are investment grade, at BBB from Fitch, Baa2 from Moody's and BBB from S&P as of December 31, 2025.[1]

Where the weakness is

Targa spends almost all of its operating cash on growth. Free cash flow, which is cash from operations minus outlays for property, plant and equipment, was $584.1 million in 2025. That is 3.4% of revenue, down from 10.6% in 2021 (our calculation).[1][4] Over the twelve months to June 30, 2026 it was $740.9 million, or 4.4% of revenue (our calculation).[1][5] A forecast table built from S&P Global estimates shows negative free cash flow of $336.13 million for 2026.[25] Debt was $19,578 million at June 30, 2026, up from $17,433 million six months earlier, so growth is being paid for by borrowing as well as by operations.[7][9]

Part of 2026's strength was not planned. Management said its marketing businesses beat expectations by approximately $250 million in the first half, and it does not forecast material marketing margin in guidance.[16] President Jen Kneale said the strength of the marketing benefits would be a headwind for the third quarter compared with the second.[16] Volume risk is real too: 200 million to 400 million cubic feet per day of gas was shut in behind Targa's Permian systems on any given day in the second quarter because of weak Waha prices.[16] Spending also ran over plan in 2025. Net growth capital expenditure was $3,343.5 million, against an initial range of $2.6 billion to $2.8 billion.[9][12]

Customers, competitors, regulators and people

Targa's customers are oil and gas producers and downstream buyers. The 10-K lists customer credit risk as a risk factor and says falling commodity prices can hurt producers' finances.[1] We looked for a customer above 10% of revenue and found no named customer or percentage in the filing text we read, so customer concentration is not published in a form we can score.[1] The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) regulates Targa's interstate NGL and crude pipelines. In November 2025 it confirmed that the PPI-FG+0.78% rate index stays in place through June 30, 2026; the documents we read do not say what applies after that date.[1] The planned Forza gas pipeline in Permian Delaware still needs FERC approval, and the Stakeholder assets Targa bought in January include carbon capture activities that generate 45Q tax credits.[1][6]

Competitors are growing at similar or slower rates. Enterprise Products Partners reported second-quarter adjusted EBITDA up 17% and announced a new fractionator and two new gas plants in the Delaware and Midland basins, with the plants due in 2028 and 2029.[22] ONEOK reported 7% and Williams 6%, so Targa's 37.8% is the fastest in the peer set we checked, helped by the marketing gains described above.[21][20][7] Management is changing at the top of two functions. In August the board announced that the chief financial officer would retire effective September 1, 2026, and named the head of Logistics and Transportation to that job, with a former Enterprise Products executive taking over Logistics and Transportation.[18] Shareholders have backed pay, with say-on-pay support above 94% in each year from 2021 to 2025.[17]

For 2025 that is December 2024 and December 2025. For the trailing figure it is December 31, 2025 and June 30, 2026, a six-month span, because we did not collect June 2025 capital. Invested capital is total debt plus total owners' equity minus cash.[1]

The verdict is REJECTED, and the rule behind it is the reward-to-risk floor. Reward-to-risk, also called asymmetry, is the gain from the price to the base target divided by the loss from the price to the stop loss, which is the price at which we would admit the idea is wrong. A tactical momentum name needs at least 2.5. With a base target of $318.39 and a stop of $262.77, TRGP offers $16.02 of gain against $39.60 of risk at the current market price, or CMP, of $302.37.

1. What the company does

Tencent Holdings Limited was founded in Shenzhen in 1998 and has been listed in Hong Kong since 2004[2]. Most people meet it through Weixin, which the rest of the world calls WeChat. Weixin is a messaging app that also holds payments, small shops, short video, games and official accounts in one place. On 30 June 2026 it had 1,439 million monthly active users, up 2% in a year, and QQ, the older chat app, had 520 million[2]. Because so many people already use Weixin every day, each new service Tencent adds starts with an audience that costs almost nothing to reach.

Tencent earns money four ways. Value-added services, mostly games plus music, video and virtual items, brought in RMB 369.3 billion in 2025, which is 49% of sales[7]. Marketing services are the ads sold on Weixin, Video Accounts and Mini Programs, and they brought in RMB 145.0 billion. FinTech and business services cover payments, wealth products, consumer loans and Tencent Cloud, and brought in RMB 229.4 billion. Total revenue was RMB 751.8 billion, about $112 billion at 6.692 yuan per dollar[6][16]. Every figure in this report is in renminbi (RMB, the Chinese yuan) unless a dollar sign is shown, and the dollar conversions use the Federal Reserve rate of 6.6920 for 9 October 2026[16].

Games are the profit engine and advertising is the growth engine. Domestic games earned RMB 47.3 billion in the June 2026 quarter, up 17%, led by Delta Force, VALORANT and the new Roco Kingdom: World. International games, which include the Supercell titles, earned RMB 18.6 billion, down 0.8% in yuan and up 4% at constant exchange rates[2]. Marketing services grew 22% to RMB 43.6 billion, helped by AI models that choose which ad each user sees[2].

The new part of the story is artificial intelligence (AI). Tencent now builds its own Hy language models, sells AI work tools called WorkBuddy and CodeBuddy, and rents computing power through Tencent Cloud, which the company says ranks second in China for cloud platform services by revenue, citing IDC[4]. That is costly. Capital expenditure is money spent on long-lived assets such as servers and data centres, and it reached RMB 52.8 billion in the June 2026 quarter, up 176% in a year[2]. Chapters 3 and 5 follow what that spending does to cash and to the share price. How the US receipt works and what it costs

TCEHY is an American depositary receipt (ADR), a certificate that stands in for shares held abroad. Citi's receipt directory lists it on the over-the-counter market with a ratio of 1:1, which means one TCEHY equals one Hong Kong ordinary share[14]. The real shares trade in Hong Kong under code 00700, and the 16 October close there was HK$417.8[29]. At 7.8476 Hong Kong dollars per US dollar that is $53.24 a share[16], so the TCEHY close of $52.75 sits 0.9% below the Hong Kong price[30]. The gap is small and mostly timing, because Hong Kong closed twelve hours before New York[1].

Two costs belong to the receipt itself. The depositary takes $0.07 per receipt out of each dividend: on the June 2026 payment the gross dividend was $0.6749 and holders received $0.6049, so the fee took 10.4% of the cash[15]. The second cost is timing. The receipt trades only in New York hours, so news that arrives while Hong Kong is open shows up as a gap at the New York open, and no stop order can prevent that. Liquidity is adequate for this holding: 4.76 million receipts changed hands on 16 October, about $251 million[1], against 18.76 million Hong Kong shares, about $999 million[29].

Tencent is a foreign private issuer, a company based outside the US that reports under home-market rules. It files no 10-K or 10-Q. Everything in this report comes from its Hong Kong filings and investor site, in yuan and under IFRS accounting standards, and each conversion to dollars states the rate. The Citi pages do not say whether the programme is a sponsored Level 1 receipt, so that detail is N/A here.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

Tencent's strength is that its products feed each other. The company itself says its moats come from network effects, value added along the supply chain, game IP, low take rates, regulatory requirements and private data[4]. Weixin is the clearest case. With 1,439 million users, merchants, game studios and advertisers have to be there, and users stay because everyone else is. Video Accounts, the short-video feed inside Weixin, grew total time spent by more than 20% in the June 2026 quarter[2]. Management says it keeps ad load, the number of ads each user sees, below the industry level, which leaves room to add more later[4].

The numbers support the claim. Gross margin is the share of each sale left after the direct cost of delivering it, and Tencent's rose from 43.9% in 2021 to 56.2% in 2025 and 57.8% in the June 2026 quarter[7][4]. ROIC, or return on invested capital, measures the profit earned on the money put into the business, and it was 18.7% over the twelve months to June 2026 (Chapter 3 shows the working). Games carry the best margin, 60%, and FinTech and business services the lowest, 51%, because cloud services cost more to run[7].

The weakness is the price of staying ahead in AI. Total capital expenditure in the June 2026 quarter was 25.8% of revenue, compared with 10.4% a year earlier, and free cash flow, the cash left after running the business and buying assets, was negative RMB 13.8 billion[2]. That compares with positive RMB 34.0 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and RMB 56.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026[7][5]. Excluding prepayments for computing power, free cash flow would have been RMB 37.6 billion[2]. The company is telling us two things at once. The old business is getting more profitable, with non-IFRS operating profit excluding new AI products up 19% to RMB 86.1 billion and a 42% operating margin, which is operating profit divided by revenue, and the new AI business is absorbing that gain, which holds the total non-IFRS operating margin at 36.9%, down from 37.5%[2][4].

United States and China risks that sit outside the income statement

Ownership structure (VIE). Tencent is incorporated in the Cayman Islands[7], and its Chinese subsidiaries control the licensed operating companies that run games, ads and online services through contracts called Structure Contracts. This is the variable interest entity design. Tencent's own PRC lawyers say the contracts do not break existing law, and also advise that there are "substantial uncertainties regarding the interpretation and application of the currently applicable PRC laws, rules and regulations"[7]. An older rule, known as Circular 13, says foreign investors may not invest in online game operators through wholly owned or joint ventures[7]. A ruling against the structure would hit the shares an ADR holder owns, not the licences.

US Defense Department list. Tencent is on the Section 1260H list. The notice scheduled for 10 June 2026 says "Tencent is indirectly affiliated with the PLA"[17]. The first listing came in January 2025. Tencent called it a mistake, said it would seek reconsideration and would go to court if needed, and said the listing relates only to US defence procurement[18]. We found no filing in the sources fetched that shows a lawsuit or a removal. Alibaba, also on the June 2026 list, sued the Department on 23 June 2026[21][22]. Listing bars Pentagon purchases from 30 June 2026 and, from 30 June 2027, contracting for goods and services produced by a listed firm or any entity it controls[19].

The step that matters for an ADR holder. A different Treasury list, called NS-CMIC, bars US persons from buying or selling a listed company's publicly traded securities[19][20]. Tencent noted in 2025 that the two lists are distinct and that the 1260H listing does not stop anyone trading its shares[18]. An industry analysis calls the 1260H list a leading indicator of tighter designations, and lists Tencent among 188 named entities[21]. This is the tail risk with no date and no probability we can support. It is not a delisting, because the receipt already trades over the counter, but it could stop US persons from trading the receipt. The sources fetched do not say whether a wind-down window would apply.

China regulation and the receipt itself. Tencent names online gaming, internet finance, anti-money-laundering, data and foreign investment rules among its regulatory risks[7], and says FinTech growth depends on cooperation with licensed financial institutions[6]. The auditor, PwC, gave an unqualified opinion on the 2025 accounts[7], so there is no sign of an accounting or going-concern problem. The remaining receipt risks are the 10.4% dividend fee, New York-hours-only trading and the possibility that a sanctions step restricts trading in the receipt; the pages fetched give no ADR delisting notice. These risks feed the weakest-assumption review in Chapter 6.

4. What the price is doing

A price chart records what buyers and sellers have already agreed to pay. That makes it evidence about the crowd's mood. The portfolio reads it with fixed tests. A base is a sideways stretch where a falling stock stops making lower lows. It builds a floor. A pivot is the top of that stretch, the level a rise must clear. The Weinstein scale labels the trend from 1 to 4. Stage 1 is the floor-building phase after a fall. The relative strength index (RSI) is a 0-to-100 meter of how fast the price has been moving. A reading of 30 is called oversold. The average true range (ATR) is the typical daily swing in dollars, here $1.13. On 16 October TCEHY closed at $52.75. 6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate in Chapter 5 rests on a weakest assumption. This chapter names each one, says what evidence would show it wrong and when that evidence will be known. The red-team bear case comes first, because the portfolio's verdict is closest to it.

The strongest argument against owning more Tencent is that the price is leading the estimates down. First-half capital expenditure of RMB 84.7 billion already exceeds all of 2025, second-half spending is guided higher, free cash flow was negative in the June quarter, net cash fell 60% in one quarter, and the company issued dollar notes at 6.10% while spending rises[3][4][24][12]. Weakest assumption behind the target: that estimates stop falling at RMB 28.80

The base target multiplies today's consensus EPS by a median multiple. If consensus keeps falling, both parts of the arithmetic move against us. Evidence that this assumption is wrong would be a further cut of more than 5% in FY1 or FY2 EPS, or second-half capital expenditure above RMB 60 billion a quarter. Chapter 3 treats twelve-month free cash flow as the starting point, but management guides second-half capital expenditure above the first half. Tencent says the listing affects only US defence procurement and does not stop anyone trading its securities[18].

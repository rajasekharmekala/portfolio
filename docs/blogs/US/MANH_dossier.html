1. What the company does

Manhattan Associates, headquartered in Atlanta, sells software that tells a company where its goods are, where they should go and how to get them to a buyer on time.[11] Its customers are retailers, wholesalers, manufacturers and logistics firms.[11] Think of it as the air traffic control for a company's warehouses, delivery trucks and stores: one system sees every order and decides which building should ship each item. The main product family is Manhattan Active, which runs in the vendor's cloud and is sold as a subscription.[11]

The company earns money from five revenue lines. In the third quarter of 2026 total revenue was $306.2 million.[1] Cloud subscriptions were $134.4 million and services were also $134.4 million, each about 43.9 percent of the total.[1] Maintenance was $29.2 million, software license $3.6 million and hardware resale $4.5 million.[1] Cloud subscriptions are a recurring fee. Services are one-time projects in which consultants configure and install the software. Maintenance and licenses come from older customers who bought the software outright and run it themselves; that pool is shrinking as those customers move to the cloud.

The mix has changed fast. In 2025 cloud was 38 percent of revenue and services 47 percent.[11] In the third quarter of 2026 the two were level, and management called out that cloud revenue passed services revenue for the first time.[20] That matters because a subscription repeats every year while a services project ends. The backlog of signed work, called remaining performance obligations, reached $2.59 billion, about 2.2 times trailing revenue, and more than 99 percent of it is cloud contracts.[1][32] Management expects about 40 percent of it to turn into revenue within 24 months, and new contracts run five to six years.[21]

The business is mostly American. Americas revenue was $674.7 million of $886.2 million in the first nine months of 2026, about 76 percent, with EMEA at $166.0 million and APAC at $45.5 million.[1] The company had about 4,370 employees at the end of 2025 and cut about 6 percent of global headcount on 2026-06-01, with a charge of $8.3 million.[11][15][1] No customer exceeded 10 percent of revenue, and the five largest were 10 percent in 2025.[11]

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The competitive position rests on three facts. First, the 10-K says its solutions focus solely on enterprise commerce and supply chain operations, so engineering effort is not spread across unrelated products.[11] Second, it says every customer runs on one up-to-date version of its software, with new features delivered every quarter.[11] Third, the filings name the competition: ERP vendors Oracle, SAP and Infor, and supply chain vendors such as Blue Yonder, E2open and Infios.[11] Several of those rivals have more resources, and the company itself says they could gain a significant advantage if their products matched its own.[11] Manhattan competes on depth in warehouse, transport, order and store systems rather than on size.

The evidence for that depth is in the numbers management reported this quarter. Management says its win rate stayed above 70 percent and that new customers were about 35 percent of new cloud bookings.[21] It also said deal volume in the first nine months already passed the full-year 2025 total.[21] These are management statements, not audited facts, so the contract backlog is the harder proof: RPO rose 24.7 percent in a year to $2.59 billion, and cloud revenue growth sped up from 24 percent in the first quarter to 26.2 percent in the second and 28.2 percent in the third.[1][13][32] Faster growth with a long backlog suggests customers keep signing, not just renewing.

The weaknesses are real. The 10-K lists dependence on a single line of business among its risks.[11] Services revenue rose only 1.1 percent in the quarter and has fallen from 51 percent of sales in 2024 to 47 percent in 2025, so projects are not a growth source.[1][11] Old license and maintenance revenue is shrinking, which is why total growth is 9 percent while growth excluding those lines is 12 percent.[21] Sales and marketing spending rose 48.3 percent in the quarter, which, with higher stock pay, trimmed GAAP operating margin from 27.5 percent to 26.5 percent.[1][14] The warehouse management product also carries most of the remaining license revenue, which shows how much of the old business depends on one product group.[12]

Customer concentration is low, which helps. The five largest customers made up 10 percent, 12 percent and 11 percent of revenue in 2025, 2024 and 2023, and none exceeded 10 percent.[11] Ownership is widely held: Vanguard held 11 percent and BlackRock 10 percent in the proxy statement, and AllianceBernstein and FMR held 5 percent each.[17] The board named Juan Perez, a technology executive, as a director effective 2027-01-28.[3] The main outside risk is the retail and logistics cycle. In 2025, revenue growth slowed to 3.7 percent when services and old license revenue fell, and the 2026 recovery to a guided 9 percent shows how much the services line swings with customer budgets.[19][1][32]

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate in chapter 5 rests on a weakest assumption. This chapter names each one, the evidence that would show it wrong and the date we would see it. It also holds the red-team case: the strongest honest argument against our own verdict, written from the same facts.

Scenario 1: cash conversion is worse than the report shows

The weakest assumption behind the base case is that the third-quarter cash dip was timing. Days sales outstanding jumped from 67 to 81, receivables rose 24.9 percent since December and cash from operations fell 26 percent in the quarter.[1][32] Management says billing came late in the quarter and collections shifted.[21] If the delay is a collection problem, earnings are being booked but not received, and the 32.8 percent free cash flow margin could drop toward 20 percent. That would cut owner free cash flow roughly in half and take the owner margin to about 9.6 percent, below the 10 percent condition for the stock-pay exception. The evidence that would show it wrong is days sales outstanding of 76 or more on 2026-12-31, and a rejection if the figure is 85 or more, or nine-month 2026 cash from operations falling below 2025. The chapter 2B scorecard would keep the measure on WATCH unless the free cash flow margin also fell below 12 percent, so the score would stay at 3.5 and the exit rules in chapter 7 would do the work. We will know when the Q3 10-Q is filed, which was not yet on EDGAR at the as-of date, and at the fourth-quarter report, whose date is not announced.

Scenario 2: the multiple falls even if the business delivers

The base case holds a 38.9 times forward multiple while earnings growth is about 10 percent. That is a price-to-earnings-to-growth ratio near 4, which most buyers would call expensive. At 28 times the stock is $165, and the 52-week low of $119.06 shows how fast the multiple has moved.[30][32] Artificial intelligence is a risk the company itself lists: its 10-K names risks associated with its use of generative and agentic AI.[11] In our own view, a faster rival could use the same tools to narrow Manhattan's lead. Management says AI is helping bookings and that every AI pilot has converted to a subscription, which is a claim we cannot audit.[20][21] The evidence that would show our base case wrong is a close below the 50-day average with falling volume, or a forward multiple below 33 times on unchanged estimates. The Q4 report and 2027 guidance in late January settle the earnings side; the market settles the multiple every day.

Scenario 3: the cloud transition runs out of easy conversions

About one third of bookings come from customers converting from old on-premise licences, one third from new customers and one third from expansion.[20] The conversion pool is finite. Editions, the new packaging that management says is aimed at customers of all sizes, including smaller ones under an Essentials edition, had been live for one quarter, and management expects material impact in the second half of 2027.[20][21] Meanwhile, services revenue grew 1.1 percent, sales and marketing spending rose 48.3 percent and the 2026 adjusted margin guide is below 2025.[1][6][32] If cloud growth drops under 20 percent in 2027 while spending stays high, earnings growth falls to mid-single digits. The evidence would be cloud revenue below the $136 million Q4 target, RPO growth below 18 percent, or a 2027 adjusted operating margin guide that is flat or down. All three show in the Q4 report and the first 2027 guide.

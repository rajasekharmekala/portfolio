1. Origin, Corporate Evolution & Past Business Cycles

Core Investor Question: How did Cirrus Logic evolve from a 1984 PC graphics pioneer into a flagship mixed-signal audio and HPMS specialist, and what do its past revenue and M&A cycles reveal about its durability?

1.1 Origin: From Patil Systems to the 1990s PC Graphics & Storage Boom-Bust

Cirrus Logic began in 1981 in Salt Lake City. Former MIT and University of Utah professor Dr. Suhas Patil founded Patil Systems, Inc. to build structured logic array design tools [1]. In 1984, the firm moved to Silicon Valley and took the name Cirrus Logic, Inc. It shifted to fabless application-specific integrated circuits for the early personal computer boom [1]. Cirrus Logic went public on Nasdaq in June 1989 and grew into a top PC chip supplier [1]. By the early 1990s, it held leading share in hard-disk drive controllers, modems, and 2D VGA graphics chips [1]. In 1991, it bought Austin-based Crystal Semiconductor to add analog and mixed-signal chips. That deal quietly planted the core tech DNA that saved the firm a decade later [1].

That early PC boom hid a harsh structural trap. In 1994 and 1995, management left its fabless model to join costly wafer fab joint ventures with IBM (MiCRUS) and AT&T Microelectronics, including a $600 million pledge [1]. The timing was fatal. PC graphics shifted from 2D VGA to 3D chips and integrated chipsets right as those fixed costs hit [1]. Nvidia, 3dfx, and ATI outran Cirrus in 3D graphics while Intel folded peripheral logic into core chipsets. Revenue plunged, and heavy fab costs drove deep operating losses [1]. From fiscal 1996 to fiscal 2009, annual sales fell nearly 85% [1]. To survive, the firm exited PC graphics in 1998 and moved its headquarters to Austin, Texas in 2000—the home of Crystal Semiconductor. It also exited magnetic storage chips in 2001, shut down wireless networking in 2003, and sold its video unit into Magnum Semiconductor in 2005 [1].

1.2 The 2000s Audio Turnaround and the Flagship Smartphone Anchor

Under CEO David French (1999–2007) and successor Jason Rhode, the firm stripped back to a pure fabless analog and mixed-signal audio model [1]. Rhode became CEO in May 2007 after leading the Mixed-Signal Audio Division [1]. The turning point came in the late 2000s. Cirrus Logic replaced Wolfson Microelectronics inside Apple's iPod and later iPhones, winning the main low-power audio codec socket [1][24]. Its engineers built ultra-low-power, high-dynamic-range chips tailored to tight mobile battery and board-space limits [1][12]. That focus turned a small audio converter unit into a billion-dollar-plus engine [1][12]. It lifted the firm out of commodity PC parts and pushed gross margins past 48%–50%. It also built a light-asset cash machine, though at the cost of tying its fate to one giant buyer [1][12].

1.3 M&A Ledger: Wolfson ($467M), Lion Semiconductor ($335M) & INVENTVM (2026)

Over the past twelve years, Cirrus Logic made three key deals to deepen audio IP and enter nearby mixed-signal fields. First, in April 2014 (closed August 2014), it bought Edinburgh-based Wolfson Microelectronics for £291 million, or about $467 million [24]. Wolfson brought a deep Scottish design hub, Android flagship ties (notably Samsung), and MEMS and audio hub IP [1][24]. The deal expanded the design bench, even though Android phone audio later turned into a commodity as Samsung moved to built-in processor audio [1][24]. Second, in July 2021, new CEO John Forsyth bought Lion Semiconductor for $335 million in cash to enter switched-capacitor fast-charging and battery power ICs [9][14]. Management first aimed for about $60 million in partial-year FY2022 sales across Android OEMs [14]. Long China smartphone weakness hit Lion's broad-market demand instead. In Q4 FY2023, Cirrus took an $85.8 million ($85.76 million) intangible write-down plus $10.6 million in restructuring costs [9].

Third, on October 6, 2026—just after Q2 FY2027 closed—Cirrus Logic bought 100% of INVENTVM Semiconductor S.r.l., a Pavia, Italy-based fabless mixed-signal design house founded in 2021 [2][7][26]. Price terms were not shared, and management said the deal will not move near-term sales [2][7]. Still, INVENTVM adds piezoelectric amplifier DSP (PiezoDrive) and battery management (VoltaTek) talent aimed at AI wearables and next-gen consumer gear [2][3][7]. Overall, Cirrus Logic's M&A record is mixed. Wolfson built a lasting Scottish design hub that now anchors custom phone and PC silicon. Lion, by contrast, showed how hard it is to buy profitable non-Apple scale in cutthroat Android power markets [9][24]. That contrast guides our audit today. Management knows how to deepen engineering inside its core ecosystem, yet buying standalone scale outside Apple has proven far harder [9][24].

1.4 Past Revenue & Margin Cycles: High Gross Margin Floor, Sharp Volume Air-Pockets

Ten years of audited results show a clear cycle pattern [13]. Commodity memory and discrete analog rivals often see gross margins crash in downturns. Cirrus Logic did not. Its GAAP gross margin stayed tight between 49.2% and 53.7% in every fiscal year from FY2017 through H1 FY2027 [1][2][13]. Look at the steep FY2019 smartphone slump. Sales fell 22.6% from $1,532.19 million to $1,185.52 million, and GAAP EBIT fell 61.6% from $262.46 million to $100.77 million [12][13]. Even then, GAAP gross margin rose 75 basis points to 50.4% [12][13]. The same held in FY2024. Sales dipped 5.7% to $1,788.89 million amid post-pandemic phone softness and Android inventory cuts, yet gross margin climbed to 51.2% [1][9]. The cause is structural. Custom mixed-signal sockets avoid mid-cycle spot price wars, so slumps hit via lower unit volume and fixed R&D costs rather than gross-margin collapse [1][13].

What the 1990s Near-Death Experience Taught Management

Cirrus Logic's fortress balance sheet holds $1.15 billion in cash and marketable securities with zero financial debt at FY2026 year-end [1]. That cash pile is not an accident. It is scar tissue from the late 1990s, when IBM and AT&T wafer fab deals nearly broke the firm as PC graphics faded.

Every CEO since 2000 has kept two strict rules: stay 100% fabless and carry zero funded debt. That stance lets Cirrus absorb a 20% smartphone slump without diluting holders or cutting core R&D. Still, cash alone cannot remove single-customer risk.

2.2 Audio Products: 22nm Smart Codecs, Boosted Amplifiers & SoundWire PC Silicon

The Audio line rests on two main chip types: smart audio codecs and boosted Class-D speaker amplifiers. It also includes general-market DACs, ADCs, and pro-audio or auto codecs [1][3]. In flagship phones, Cirrus Logic recently moved its top buyer to a custom 22-nanometer smart codec paired with a new custom boosted amp [3]. Moving from 55nm to 22nm packs far more onboard DSP power into a tiny power budget. It runs always-on voice offload, active noise control, and spatial sound without waking the main processor [3]. Better still, custom phone audio sockets last a long time. In Q2 FY2027, management noted that prior custom codecs and boosted amps shipped for five and six years, giving multi-year sales visibility once qualified [3]. That long socket life matters for cash returns. Upfront R&D is paid back over five or six full product cycles rather than a single season [3]. It also gives Cirrus Logic time to co-develop the next chip node before the prior part rolls off [1][3].

Beyond phones, Audio leads Cirrus Logic's push into Windows and AI laptops [3][15]. For years, PC makers used legacy High Definition Audio (HDA) codecs led by Realtek Semiconductor. As thin laptops moved to the newer MIPI SoundWire (v1.2 / SDCA) bus to save board space and battery life, Cirrus launched its PC CS42L43 SmartHIFI codec and scalable CS35L56 boosted smart amps [15][16]. The CS42L43 fits in a 13 mm² chip-scale package and cuts power draw by up to 50% vs. older chips. It also plugs right into Microsoft's ACX audio framework and mainline Linux kernel drivers [15][17][19]. In Q2 FY2027, management noted a new six-speaker PC on NVIDIA's RTX Spark platform using Cirrus amps and codec. It also taped out its second-gen smart codec for AI PCs [3].

2.4 Concrete End-to-End Customer Examples: Inside a Smartphone and an AI Laptop

Two late-2026 devices show how Cirrus Logic works in practice. Example 1: A Flagship Smartphone Bill of Materials. Suppose you shoot 4K steady video, feel a silent haptic tap, and take a speaker call. Five or more Cirrus Logic chips run together behind the scenes [1][3]. Inside flagship phones, Cirrus Logic sells multi-chip bundles at massive volume [1][3]. The Core Paradox of Cirrus Logic's Product Mix

Product mix is broadening—HPMS rose from 33.4% of sales in FY2022 to 44.7% in H1 FY2027 [2][9]. Yet buyer mix went the other way. Apple grew from 79% of sales in FY2022 to 91% in FY2026 and 93% in Q2 FY2027 [1][2][10].

The reason is simple. Most HPMS wins (camera chips, haptics, and new 3D-sensing power ICs) sit inside the same top smartphone buyer. Investors must separate content-per-device growth (strong) from true customer breadth (still unproven at scale).

3. Distribution, Supply Chain & Unit Economics

Core Investor Question: How does Cirrus Logic manufacture, distribute, and convert every $100 of revenue into free cash flow, and where do its supply-chain and working-capital bottlenecks sit?

3.1 Fabless Manufacturing Architecture: GlobalFoundries, TSMC & OSAT Partners

Cirrus Logic runs a 100% fabless model. It owns zero wafer fabs and outsources wafer production, bumping, assembly, and final test to third-party partners [1]. Wafer supply centers on two top foundries: Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) and GlobalFoundries [1]. TSMC builds advanced, digital-heavy mixed-signal chips such as the 22-nanometer smart audio codecs. GlobalFoundries builds mature-node analog and mixed-signal wafers (55nm and 90nm-class) for boosted amps, haptic drivers, and power chips [1][3]. Assembly, packaging, and final test (OSAT) take place mostly in Asia via top contractors like ASE Technology and Amkor Technology [1].

Specialty mixed-signal wafers cannot shift quickly between fabs without months of re-testing. To guard supply and pricing, Cirrus Logic uses multi-year capacity deals [1][2]. In July 2021 (amended February 2025), it signed a Capacity Reservation and Wafer Supply Commitment Agreement with GlobalFoundries through calendar 2026. That deal required $195.0 million in upfront wafer prepayments, which amortized to $14.7 million at FY2026 end and $0 by September 26, 2026 [1][2][9]. It also gave temporary wafer-cost gains that helped lift Q2 FY2027 GAAP gross margin to 53.7% [2][3]. On June 30, 2026, Cirrus signed a new GlobalFoundries deal for calendar 2027 and 2028. Under it, Cirrus expects to buy at least about $600 million of wafers on a quarterly schedule, with shortfall terms [2]. This locks in U.S., European, and Singapore fab capacity outside Taiwan, cutting geopolitical risk—though it sets a binding take-or-pay floor if phone volume drops [1][2]. In short, foundry prepayments and wafer pledges act like an insurance policy. They protect autumn launch supply when capacity is tight, while adding fixed volume exposure if flagship phone shipments ever slow [1][2].

3.2 Direct Engineering-Led Go-To-Market & Contract Manufacturer Fulfillment

Broad analog peers like Texas Instruments or Analog Devices run huge field-sales and distributor networks for tens of thousands of buyers. Cirrus Logic does the opposite. Its sales model is tight and led by engineers [1]. Teams win custom sockets directly with OEM silicon architects 18 to 36 months before launch via joint design deals and tape-outs [1][3]. Once qualified, chips ship mostly to Apple's Asian contract makers—such as Foxconn, Pegatron, and Luxshare. Direct OEM and contract-maker shipments drive the bulk of billings, while distributors serve the smaller general-market audio, smart-meter, and industrial-imaging tail [1][2]. That lean setup kept FY2026 SG&A at just $159.84 million, or 8.0% of sales. It let Cirrus put 21.7% of sales ($433.95 million) into R&D while earning a 23.0% GAAP operating margin [1]. That cost gap is a key edge against broad analog rivals. Yet it also shows why breaking into thousands of smaller PC or industrial accounts would require higher sales spend over time [1].

3.3 Working Capital Discipline: DSO, DIO, DPO & Cash Conversion Cycle

Flagship phone launches cluster in the September and December quarters, giving working capital a sharp seasonal swing [2][3]. Inventory builds in Q1 and Q2 ahead of autumn ramps. As chips ship to contract makers in Q2 and Q3, accounts receivable jumps—rising from $220.15 million on March 28, 2026 to $365.75 million on September 26, 2026—before turning into cash in Q4 [1][2]. Full-year working-capital control improved sharply in FY2026 [1]. Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO) fell 28.1 days, from 121.3 days at FY2025 end ($299.09 million on $900.04 million COGS) to 93.2 days at FY2026 end ($240.87 million on $943.21 million COGS) [1]. Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) tightened from 41.6 to 40.2 days, while Days Payable Outstanding (DPO) rose from 25.6 to 31.2 days [1]. As a result, the Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) shrank 35.1 days, from 137.3 days in FY2025 to 102.2 days in FY2026 [1]. Paired with $53.34 million in prepaid-wafer burn-down, that cash release drove FY2026 operating cash flow to a record $650.60 million—157% of GAAP net income [1].

4. Industry Structure, Competitive Moat & External Learning Hub

Core Investor Question: How wide is Cirrus Logic's technical moat against analog giants and Asian rivals, why has Apple not in-housed audio codecs yet, and what do historical supplier parallels teach us?

4.1 Industry Structure: Precision Mixed-Signal vs. Integrated SoC Encroachment

Cirrus Logic operates in the global mixed-signal chip market, which splits into two very different tiers [1]. At the low end sit high-volume digital phone platforms (Qualcomm, MediaTek) and catalog PC audio codecs (Realtek Semiconductor). There, basic audio rides on the main SoC or sells at thin margins [15]. At the high end sit custom, ultra-low-power analog and mixed-signal firms. Their chips link right to physical parts—mics, micro-speakers, voice-coil camera motors, haptic engines, and battery cells [1][3]. Analog physics does not shrink well onto 3nm digital FinFET nodes, where voltage drops and noise rises. As a result, top hardware makers keep high-voltage boosted amps, fine ADCs/DACs, haptic drivers, and closed-loop camera chips on separate 22nm–90nm specialty dies [1][3].

4.2 Sources of Moat vs. Structural Vulnerabilities

Cirrus Logic's moat rests on three real tech pillars—and one clear commercial flaw [1][3]. First is deep co-design and patent depth. On March 28, 2026, Cirrus held about 4,340 issued and pending patents worldwide, including ~1,620 granted U.S. patents running through 2046 [1]. Its engineers co-design custom chips with Apple's hardware teams 24 to 36 months before launch. They embed custom DSP code, speaker limits, and closed-loop motor firmware that rivals cannot easily copy on a tight annual schedule [1][3]. Second is long socket life. Once qualified, a custom codec or boosted amp often ships across five to six years of flagship and cascade phone tiers, lifting R&D returns [3]. Third is scale trust. Shipping hundreds of millions of zero-defect custom chips each fall creates a high bar for smaller rivals [1].

Yet the bear case is just as clear and hard to ignore. In FY2026, Apple drove 91% of Cirrus Logic's net sales—up from 89% in FY2025, 87% in FY2024, and 79% in FY2017 [1][12]. That share rose to 93% in Q2 FY2027, or 92% for H1 FY2027 [2]. This single-buyer setup brings three lasting risks. First is in-housing tail risk: Apple has steadily brought CPUs, GPUs, main PMICs, and cellular modems in-house over the past decade [20][22][23]. Second is annual price cuts: in Q2 FY2027, 'previously anticipated pricing reductions' offset higher phone volumes and held YoY sales flat [3]. Third is weak pricing leverage: Cirrus has no big industrial or auto arm to cushion a lost phone socket [1]. Investors should therefore weigh both sides together. Technical switching costs protect Cirrus Logic across multi-year phone cycles, yet extreme single-buyer share puts a firm ceiling on long-term pricing power [1][3].

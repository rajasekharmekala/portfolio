Before you read

Cloudflare (NYSE: NET) runs a global network that sells internet security, speed and developer tools, and its revenue grew about 36% in the June 2026 quarter.[1] Our verdict is REJECTED because stock-based compensation (SBC, the cost of paying staff in shares) is 20.2% of revenue and free cash flow after that cost is negative, and because the $325.73 price offers a reward-to-risk of only 0.81 to 1 against our 12-month base value of $365.96, well short of the 2.5 to 1 floor.[1][3] Cloudflare has confirmed that it reports third-quarter results after the market closes on November 5, 2026.[12] We are not buying before then. We would reopen the case only if SBC falls below 15% of revenue with positive free cash flow after SBC, and the price pulls back into the $285.05 to $301.50 zone.

1. What the company does

Cloudflare describes its mission as helping to build a better internet. It has built a global network that gives businesses security, faster delivery of web traffic and a place to run software, so they do not have to buy and manage separate hardware.[2] A good way to picture it is a toll road with guards and fast lanes. Every request to a customer's website or application passes through Cloudflare's network. There it can be checked for attacks, sped up, and increasingly processed by code that runs on the network itself.

The company earns money from subscriptions to its network and products.[3] At the end of 2025 it had 332,466 paying customers, a figure that grew from 189,791 at the end of 2023,[3] but the money is concentrated in large accounts. The number of customers paying more than $100,000 a year in annualized revenue (the revenue of a quarter multiplied by four) was 4,698 in June 2026, up from 3,712 a year earlier.[2] No single customer accounted for more than 10% of revenue in 2023, 2024 or 2025, so the business does not depend on one buyer.[3]

A growing part of the story is developers. Cloudflare says more than 10 million developers now build on its platform, up from 7.4 million the quarter before, and that daily requests from AI agents on its network grew by more than 1,700% over one year.[23] Its developer products include Workers AI, which runs machine learning models on graphics processors spread across the network.[3] Cloudflare also released the first models trained by its own Workers AI team on October 1, 2026,[26] and on August 4, 2026 announced wallets that let AI agents hold an identity and pay for things within limits set by their human creators.[25]

Revenue is spread across the world, with the United States at 51% of the June 2026 quarter.[2] The table sums to the quarter's total revenue of $696.1 million.[1] The key fact for an owner is that the company sells a platform rather than one product: security, performance and developer tools share the same network, which is why growth has stayed above 28% a year for four years while the customer base moved up-market.[3][5]

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Annualized revenue is a customer's revenue in a quarter multiplied by four. It is how Cloudflare decides who counts as a large customer.
  • Dollar-based net retention compares the revenue from the same customers four quarters apart. Above 100% means existing customers spend more over time.
  • Non-GAAP figures leave out costs the company chooses to exclude, mainly stock-based pay; GAAP figures follow the official accounting rules and include them.
  • Convertible notes are loans that carry little or no interest and can turn into shares if the stock price rises above a set level.
  • Capped call is a contract the company buys so that part of the share dilution from a convertible note is offset, up to a price cap.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The strongest evidence for the business is the way big customers keep spending more. The dollar-based net retention rate was 120% in the June 2026 quarter, against 114% a year earlier. The count of customers above $100,000 a year grew 26.6%.[2] Remaining performance obligations (RPO), meaning signed contracts not yet counted as revenue, reached $2,732.0 million. The company expects to recognize 64% of that within twelve months.[2] In the fourth quarter of 2025 it closed its largest annual contract value deal ever, averaging $42.5 million a year.[7] No third-quarter 2026 deal figures exist yet because the quarter has not been reported.[12] These facts fit the company's own view that customers increasingly want one integrated vendor for security, performance and reliability rather than several point products.[3]

The weaknesses show up in profit and in cost of delivery. Under GAAP, the official accounting rules, the company lost $205.7 million from operations in the June 2026 quarter. Of that, $150.7 million was restructuring and other charges tied to a plan that cut about 20% of the workforce.[1][2] Cost of revenue rose 53% while revenue rose 36%. The drivers were $30.1 million more in third-party technology services, $20.9 million more in data-centre and bandwidth costs and $10.1 million more in depreciation.[2] As a result, GAAP gross margin fell to 71.8% from 74.9% a year earlier.[1] Gross margin is the share of revenue left after the direct cost of serving customers. A falling number means each new dollar of revenue is costing more to deliver.

Competition comes in three forms, according to the company's annual report: makers of on-site network hardware, vendors of single cloud products such as security, content delivery, DNS or email protection, and parts of the services sold by the large public cloud providers.[3] The filing groups rivals by category and does not name them. We therefore chose Zscaler, Akamai and Fastly as comparison peers in the matrix in chapter 3. We make no claim about which products they overlap on. Zscaler sells cloud security. Akamai sells delivery and security with a growing cloud infrastructure line. Fastly sells edge delivery and security.[17][19][18]

The risk that matters most for a network business is outages. The company's June 2026 quarterly report says that software updates to its global network caused widespread service unavailability in November and December 2025. It also says a failure in third-party storage behind Workers KV caused an outage of several hours in June 2025.[2] On February 20, 2026, a change to how it manages customer-owned IP addresses caused about 1,100 prefixes to be withdrawn. The full incident lasted 6 hours and 7 minutes, according to the company's own report.[24] The company states that these events did not have a material effect on its results,[2] but large customers are the group most sensitive to outages, which is a reason retention figures matter more than headline growth.

Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Walking the talk means checking whether management delivered what it said it would. Cloudflare guides on revenue, non-GAAP operating income and non-GAAP earnings per share, and it has beaten the top of its own revenue range in each case we checked. The 2024 and 2025 full-year outlooks given in February were beaten by 1.1% and 3.5% on revenue, and the profit beats were larger in percentage terms.[10][9][7]

The second half of the audit is the guidance for the current year. The 2026 outlook given in February has been raised twice, and the non-GAAP operating income range moved up by about 17% at the midpoint.[7][6][1] The May restructuring plan cut about 20% of the workforce, and the documents we read do not say how much of the raise comes from it. The same guidance implies slower growth ahead: the third-quarter revenue range of $736.0 to $737.0 million is about 31% above the $562.0 million of a year earlier, compared with 35.9% in the second quarter.[1][8] If the third quarter beats the top of its range by the 3.0% and 4.7% margins of the last two quarters, revenue would be about $759 million to $771 million, or 35% to 37% growth (our calculation).

On capital allocation, management said in May 2026 that it would settle the 2026 convertible notes with cash for the principal and shares for any excess, and it raised $2.5 billion of new 0% convertible notes due 2031 in August 2026 with a capped call that cost $259.5 million.[2][11] We found no stated target for SBC in the filings we read, so there is no promise to test on the cost that fails our Layer 1 gate.

Profit has two faces. Under GAAP, the official accounting rules, the company has lost money in every year shown. The operating loss was $207.2 million in 2025, or 9.6% of revenue.[3] Under non-GAAP, which leaves out stock-based pay, amortization of acquired intangibles and one-off items, it earned $303.9 million. In August 2026 the company issued $2.5 billion of new notes. It used part of the proceeds to settle the 2026 notes.[1][11]

Layer 1 underwriting: static levels, trajectory (rate of change), and archetype fit

It then checks which of three archetypes the company fits. A COMPOUNDER needs a high return on capital and low SBC, and it must clear a 2.0 to 1 asymmetry floor. A TURNAROUND_VALUE_INFLECTION misses a static floor but passes the hard red lines. It needs at least two improving signals and a cheap valuation. It must clear 2.5 to 1. A TACTICAL_BREAKOUT is a Stage 2 leader with accelerating results and a 2.5 to 1 floor. It may carry SBC below 15% of revenue if free cash flow after SBC is positive. Cloudflare fits none of them. It fails the COMPOUNDER ROIC and SBC floors. It has several improving signals. Growth rose from 34% to 36%, estimates are rising, and free cash flow margin rose from 10.0% to 12.5%. It also passes the hard red lines. But it is not cheap. The reverse DCF needs 45.8% a year, above the 34.69% analyst forecast. The sales multiple is 2.10 times its median. So it fails the TURNAROUND test. It is a Stage 2 leader with acceleration. But SBC of 20.2% and negative free cash flow after SBC fail the TACTICAL_BREAKOUT rule. The asymmetry is also 0.81 to 1.[1][15][16]

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate rests on an assumption that could be wrong. This chapter names the weakest assumption in each estimate, gives the bear case against our own conclusion, lists three ways the thesis could fail completely, and names the single claim everything depends on and the date we will know it.

The weakest assumption in the base value is that 2028 EPS equals the 2027 consensus grown at the vendor's three-year growth rate, because no published 2028 consensus was available to us. The weakest assumption in the reverse DCF is the starting cash flow, which depends on how capitalized software is treated. The weakest assumption in the net cash estimate is that the 2026 notes were settled as the company elected, which the third-quarter filing will confirm. The weakest assumption in the SBC test is that it stays near 20%: the workforce plan could lower it faster than we expect.

The bear case against our REJECTED verdict is that we are too strict. Growth is accelerating, retention is 120%, guidance has been beaten every quarter, and the company is now cutting costs. If SBC falls below 15% of revenue within two quarters and free cash flow after SBC turns positive, the quality gate would pass and the business would qualify for a pullback entry. That is a real possibility, and it is why we name a re-review zone rather than ruling the company out.

Fatal flaw 1: growth slows while the multiple is still high

The third-quarter revenue range implies growth of about 31%, and the full-year range implies about 29% for the fourth quarter (our calculation from the guidance midpoints).[1] If actual growth lands near those numbers rather than at 36%, a stock priced at 2.1 times its median sales multiple would be repriced. Our stress table shows that a base multiple of 137.8x on 2028 EPS, instead of 161.7x, would put the October 2027 value 4.26% below today's price.

Fatal flaw 2: an outage or security failure on a large customer's critical path

The company's own reports describe widespread unavailability from software updates in November and December 2025, a six-hour BYOIP incident on February 20, 2026 and a Workers KV outage in June 2025.[2][24] The company says none was material, but the filing warns that outages at large, high-profile customers carry added liability and can lead them to reduce usage.[2] Large customers drive growth, so a repeat could lower net retention from 120% and the multiple together.

Cost of revenue grew 53% against revenue growth of 36% in the June quarter, and gross margin fell 3.1 points.[2][1] If that continues, free cash flow could stall while SBC stays near 20% of revenue.

Before you read

Certara sells drug-simulation software and scientific consulting to drug makers. In 2026 it sold its regulatory and medical writing unit, so its sales total shrank to about $367 million a year and the history in its filings no longer matches the business that remains.[17] The shares rose 23% in a month, yet the company fails four of the six Layer 1 gates in this book: return on capital, recent cash generation, share-pay burden and the payoff minimum. This report therefore rejects it and does not allocate capital. The conclusion would be wrong if two straight quarters of continuing-business cash flow showed a cash margin above 12% and if the price fell back to about $7.80, where the payoff minimum is met.

What the company does

Certara helps drug developers decide how much of a medicine to give, to whom, and whether a trial is likely to work, before or during costly human testing. It does this with simulation software, mainly the Simcyp simulator and Phoenix, and with teams of scientists who run the models for clients. It reports more than 2,600 customers in 70 countries, and almost all are biotech and pharmaceutical firms, universities or regulators.[17] Customers pay in two ways. Software customers sign licences that renew each year. Services customers pay for projects, usually priced by effort and billed as work is delivered.

In the second quarter of 2026, software revenue was $48.8 million and services revenue was $44.5 million, so software was about 52% of $93.3 million in total sales.[17] No single customer exceeded 10% of 2025 revenue, and the ten largest customers were 24% of revenue, down from 27% in 2024.[1] The company was reorganized in May 2026 into two units, one for modelling in discovery and drug development and one for clinical evidence work.[17]

The business that remains is smaller than the one in the five-year history. On 2026-05-08 Certara closed the sale of its global medical writing and regulatory services unit to Veristat for $100 million in cash, of which $85.0 million was paid at close and $15 million sits in escrow, plus an earn-out of up to $35 million.[11][13] That unit earned about $17 million of 2025 adjusted EBITDA after overhead, or about $23 million before the overhead that stays behind, according to a correction the company filed on 2026-08-07.[9] In this paragraph, EBITDA means earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, which is a rough measure of operating cash profit.

The report uses a short list of terms. Each is defined here at its first use.

RSI is the relative strength index, a 0-to-100 gauge of how strongly a price has risen or fallen in the last 14 days.

ATR is the average true range, the typical daily price swing; for Certara it is $0.45.

ROIC is the return on invested capital, which is operating profit after tax divided by the money shareholders and lenders have put into the business.

FCF is free cash flow, the cash from operations left after capital spending and capitalized software.

SBC is stock-based compensation, the share awards paid to staff instead of cash.

NRR is net revenue retention, the share of last year's software revenue still paid this year after renewals, price rises and expansion.

EPS is earnings per share, profit divided by the number of shares.

The 20-day moving average, the 50-day moving average and the 200-day moving average are the mean closing prices of the last 20, 50 and 200 sessions.

Stage 2 is the markup phase of a share price, in which the price trades above a rising long-term trend.

A base is a quiet price range in which a share rests before a larger move.

A stop loss is a preset price at which a position is sold to cap the loss.

A limit order is an order to buy only at or below a stated price.

The entry zone is the price range in which a purchase is allowed.

Asymmetry is the ratio of the gain to the target to the loss to the stop loss, also called reward-to-risk.

Net debt is total debt minus cash.

The price-to-earnings ratio is the share price divided by earnings per share.

Guidance is the range of results that management expects for the coming period.

Book-to-bill is new orders divided by revenue in the same period, so a value above 1.0 means the order book is growing.

A buyback is a company's purchase of its own shares from the market.

Why the business is strong or weak

The strength is real but narrow. Certara's tools are used in filings to regulators. Management says all 13 new drugs the FDA approved in the second quarter came from its clients.[18][33][35] That gives the software a name that a rival cannot copy fast. Software bookings rose 9% to $50.7 million in the second quarter. The sales pipeline was up 27% from a year earlier.[17][18]

The weakness is that growth has almost stopped and leaders have left. Second-quarter revenue grew 1%. Services revenue fell 3%. Guidance for the year is 0% to 4% growth, which is low for this company.[17] The finance chief resigned effective 2026-07-14, and an interim chief took over on 2026-07-15. A search is under way. The president and sales chief left on 2026-08-01.[10][8] A new sales chief, Julien Perrier, began that day.[17] The sale to Veristat closed in May 2026, and the buyer says it adds more than 200 staff.[28][29][30][31] First-quarter revenue was $106.9 million, up 1%.[32] A July partnership with NVIDIA adds AI agents to the software.[34] On 2026-08-07 the company also fixed a figure it gave on its call about the sold unit's profit.[9]

How it was calculated

Guided 2026 revenue of $374.5 million times a 30% adjusted EBITDA margin is $112.4 million. At 14 times that is an enterprise value of $1,573.6 million. Subtracting net debt of $107.7 million leaves $1,465.9 million of equity value, and dividing by 156 million shares gives $9.40.

The inputs

Revenue and margin are the midpoints of company guidance, $367 million to $382 million and 29% to 31%.[17] Net debt is $291.8 million of debt minus $184.1 million of cash at 2026-06-30.[5] The 14 times multiple is an assumption: the market's own multiple today is about 13.3 times (an enterprise value of about $1.50 billion on $112.4 million), and Finviz reports 16.41 times on trailing figures.[21]

The range

At 12 times the value is $7.89 and at 16 times it is $11.40, so each turn of the multiple moves the answer by about $0.72.

If the number is off

Why these estimates might be wrong

Estimate 1 depends most on the 14 times multiple, because Certara has no stable earnings record to anchor a price-to-earnings multiple. The evidence that would show it wrong is the multiple at which large software and drug-services peers trade after the third-quarter results. The company has not announced the date of that report in the sources read, so the date is N/A until it is posted on the investor site.[17]

Estimate 2 depends most on the stop. A wider stop lowers the position size but does not change the ratio's direction, and a stop at the 200-day average of $7.19 would raise the risk to $1.94 and lower both ratios. The weakness in the numbers is the adjusted EBITDA definition, which adds back $33.1 million of stock pay and $56.2 million of amortization in 2025, so any reader who counts stock pay as a cost sees a much thinner profit.[1]

Three events could break the thesis in either direction.

  • Services decline accelerates. Services were 47% of second-quarter sales and fell 3%; if drug makers cut study budgets, a 10% decline would remove about $18 million of revenue, and a further bookings drop (services bookings fell 6%) would show it first.[17]
  • Leadership turnover leaves the plan unexecuted. The company needs a permanent CFO, a bedded-in commercial head and delivery of $13 million of savings. A missed guidance range in the third quarter would end the transformation story, and the 2026-08-07 correction of a figure given on the call already costs credibility.[9][10]
  • A short squeeze reverses. With 28.53% of the float sold short, a rally can overshoot, but the shares can fall just as fast when the squeeze ends. The share price is more fragile than fundamentals suggest, and the analyst average target of $8.71 sits below the market.[21]

The first hard check is the third-quarter report and its software bookings and net retention. The quarterly 10-Q filing that follows it gives the first clean free cash flow figure for a full quarter of the continuing business.

1. What the company does

Start with who pays. A Gallagher client is usually a business, a school, a city or a charity that needs insurance. Gallagher's staff study the client's risks, ask many insurers for prices and help the client choose. The insurer pays Gallagher a commission out of the premium. Clients also pay fees for advice, such as benefits design or risk consulting.[1] In 2025 revenue before reimbursements was $13,778 million. It came from $8,024 million of commissions, $4,195 million of fees, $466 million of supplemental revenues, $324 million of contingent revenues and $769 million of interest and other income.[1] Supplemental revenues are extra payments from an insurer for placing business with it. Contingent revenues are bonuses that depend on how profitable that business is for the insurer.

Two segments produce that revenue. The brokerage segment had $12,192 million of 2025 revenue. It sells retail and wholesale insurance, reinsurance and employee benefits advice. The risk management segment had $1,585 million. It runs claims for companies that insure part of their own risk, and its Gallagher Bassett unit charges fees for investigating and settling them.[1] About 72,000 people work across the group, and 47% of them are in the United States. The client list is spread thin. The largest client was about 1% of revenue and the ten largest together about 3%.[1] No one client can hurt the numbers much by leaving.

Growth comes from two sources, and the company calls this its two-pronged strategy. Organic growth is growth from existing clients, new clients and price changes, after removing acquisitions made in the last twelve months. Acquisition growth comes from buying other brokers, mostly small ones. In 2025 Gallagher closed 31 deals with $3,508 million of annualized revenue. Most of that came from two large purchases: Woodruff Sawyer for $1.2 billion and AssuredPartners for $13.8 billion. In 2024 it closed 46 deals with $363 million.[10][14][1] It raised $8.5 billion of new shares and $5.0 billion of new bonds for AssuredPartners in December 2024. It held that cash until the deal closed on 2025-08-18, so 2025 earnings include interest income that will not repeat.[1]

One accounting habit confuses many readers. Insurers pay premiums through Gallagher, and Gallagher holds the money until it passes it on. At the end of 2025 these fiduciary assets were $26,899 million, including $7,142 million of fiduciary cash. That money belongs to clients and insurers, not to Gallagher.[1] The company reports changes in these balances inside financing activities. So its operating cash flow is not swollen or shrunk by premiums in transit, and we can read free cash flow without a fiduciary adjustment.[1] Interest earned on the float lands in revenue, in the line for interest income, premium finance revenues and other income. Revenue therefore moves with interest rates as well as with insurance prices.[1]

Why a price cut in insurance does not cut Gallagher's revenue one for one

A broker's pay follows the premium. So people assume falling insurance prices must shrink broker revenue at the same speed. Management argues the link is looser. In the second quarter of 2026 property renewal premiums fell 10%. Casualty rose 3%, professional lines 1%, workers' compensation 2% and personal lines 3%. Every other line was up by 1% to 3%, and management put the total excluding property at +3%.[20] The company says only about one point of its organic growth now comes from price. It adds that clients who save on property often buy back cover they had dropped, raise limits or improve the structure of their program.[20]

That argument is plausible, but it is also what a broker would say, so check it against the numbers. Gallagher's brokerage organic growth was 5% in the second quarter of 2026. Base commissions and fees rose 4%, supplemental revenues rose 20% and contingent revenues fell 8%.[8] Brown & Brown, a smaller rival focused on mid-sized clients, reported 1.2% organic growth for its third quarter.[29] That shows how much slower growth can be when pricing softens. The fair reading is that Gallagher is cushioned, not immune. The cushion is why this report treats organic growth as the first operating measure in Chapter 2B. The next chapter asks how strong the cushion really is.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Broker: a firm that places insurance for clients with insurers and is paid commissions and fees. It does not take insurance risk itself.
  • Organic growth: revenue growth from the existing business, with acquisitions made in the last twelve months removed.
  • Tuck-in acquisition: the purchase of a small broker that folds into existing offices. Gallagher says these typically cost $1 million to $100 million each.
  • EBITDAC: earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization and the change in estimated acquisition earnout payables. The adjusted version also removes integration and workforce-termination costs.
  • Earnout: part of an acquisition price paid later if the acquired firm hits agreed targets. Its estimated cost is revalued each period.
  • Fiduciary cash: premiums and claim money Gallagher holds for clients and insurers before passing it on.
  • Run-rate synergies: the yearly cost and revenue gains management expects once two firms are fully combined.
  • Property and casualty (P&C): insurance on buildings and goods (property) and on liability, vehicles and workers (casualty).
  • Renewal premium change: the percentage change in the price of an insurance policy when it is renewed.
  • Excess and surplus (E&S): the market for unusual or hard-to-place risks that standard insurers decline.
  • Reinsurance: insurance bought by insurers. Gallagher brokers it through Gallagher Re.
  • Interest coverage: how many times operating profit before depreciation covers the interest bill. Chapter 2 turns from what the company does to how strong it is.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

Gallagher's competitive position rests on four things the filings let us check. They are scale, a spread of clients, a disciplined way of buying other firms, and a record of steady growth when insurance prices cool. It competes at the top end with Marsh, Aon and WTW, and in the middle market with Brown & Brown. All four report similar organic growth and margin measures, which makes the comparison in Chapter 3 possible.[28][31][30][29] This chapter tests each pillar against evidence. It then lists where the position is thinner than the headline suggests.

Where the moat is real

Scale and spread show in the filings. The company has about 72,000 employees. No client is above about 1% of revenue, and the ten largest are about 3%.[1] Management says Gallagher touches $200 billion to $250 billion of the roughly $7 trillion of premium written worldwide.[20] That is a management estimate, not an audited figure, but it suggests a long runway. The record backs the claim of steady execution. Brokerage adjusted EBITDAC margin rose from 33.9% in 2021 to 34.2%, 34.3%, 35.2% and 36.5% in the next four years.[5][4][3][2][1] Management says the second quarter of 2026 was the 25th straight quarter of double-digit adjusted EBITDAC growth.[20]

The acquisition machine is the second pillar, and the economics are simple. Gallagher says it paid about 9.4 times EBITDAC for businesses bought in the first quarter of 2026 and 11.3 times in the second. After the savings it expects, it says the effective price is nearer 10 times.[20] The shares trade at about 19.8 times this year's expected adjusted earnings per share. That is our calculation: $259.01 over the $13.10 consensus.[36] So each dollar of acquired profit is worth more once it sits inside Gallagher. Management calls the gap an arbitrage. It is real only while the share multiple stays above the purchase multiple. That is why the pace of deals matters in Chapter 2B: a lower share price narrows the gap.

The third pillar is steady organic growth through soft pricing. Brokerage organic growth ran 8.0% in 2021, 9.7% in 2022, 8.9% in 2023, 7.5% in 2024 and about 6% in 2025. It was 5% in each of the first two quarters of 2026.[5][4][3][2][10][9][8] Management says earlier soft markets saw prices fall across every line at once. This time the reset is concentrated in property.[20] The risk management unit, Gallagher Bassett, grew organically 12% in the second quarter of 2026 on new business and strong client retention.[20][8] A tenth of the company is therefore growing at twice the group rate.

Where the moat is thinner than it looks

The first weakness is direction. Organic growth has slowed in each of the last four years, from 9.7% in 2022 to about 5% now.[4][8] Brokerage organic revenue grew 4.6% in the first half of 2026 ($5,804 million against $5,549 million), so the 2026 outlook of 5.5% needs a stronger fourth quarter.[8] An analyst on the second-quarter call put the implied fourth quarter at about 7%, and management replied that the second quarter is low because property renewals weigh most then.[20] That is reasonable, but it is a forecast, not a result. The second weakness is the price cycle. Contingent revenues fell 8% organically in the second quarter, and property renewal premiums fell 10%.[8][20] Cantor Fitzgerald adds that AssuredPartners joins the organic calculation in 2027. It is growing about 4% against about 5% for the rest of brokerage, so it should be a modest headwind.[34][20]

The third weakness is the shape of the balance sheet. At 2026-06-30 goodwill was $23,026 million and amortizable intangible assets were $10,212 million. Together that is $33,238 million, or 40.6% of $81,808 million of total assets. It is more than the $23,747 million of equity, so tangible equity is negative.[6] That is normal for an acquisitive broker. But book value gives no cushion if an acquired book of clients leaves, and returns depend on getting purchases right. The fourth weakness is a complicated margin bridge. Adjusted compensation was 53.4% of brokerage revenue in the second quarter against 51.3% a year earlier. Part of that is because the 2025 revenue included $144 million of interest income.[8] Chapter 3 shows how to see through that.

The artificial intelligence question

On 2026-02-09 broker shares fell sharply when a new insurance-shopping tool built on ChatGPT launched. WTW closed 12% lower, Gallagher 9.9% lower and Aon 9.3% lower. A Bloomberg Intelligence analyst called the tools a force multiplier, not an existential threat, for brokers.[33] The fear is that software lets clients shop for insurance without a broker, which would cut commissions. The tool in question compares personal auto insurance rates, a consumer product. Gallagher's profit comes from complex business risks, benefits and reinsurance. There, advice, claims history and access to specialist insurers still decide the outcome.

Management has offered numbers. The chief financial officer said artificial intelligence could save about 5% of production-layer costs, 10% to 15% of support-layer costs and 20% to 30% of back-office costs over three to five years. On his arithmetic that could be 600 basis points of margin, and perhaps 400 after offsets. That would come on top of the 40 to 60 basis points of normal yearly gain.[20] We treat this as a possibility, not a forecast, for three reasons. None of it is in reported margins yet. Rivals make similar claims: WTW announced a plan targeting about a 30% adjusted operating margin by 2028.[30] And clients may demand part of the savings back as lower fees. So artificial intelligence cuts both ways in this report. It is a margin upside that is not in our targets, and a sentiment risk that has already moved the stock 10% in a day. 3. The numbers, in plain words

Chapter 3 shows five years of revenue, profit, cash and debt. It then applies the Layer 1 tests from our scoring file. Several of those tests were written for industrial and technology companies, so for a broker we state our substitutes openly. For return on capital we use return on invested capital: profit after tax and before interest, divided by equity plus borrowings minus cash. We show two versions. One is as reported. The other adds back amortization of acquired client lists and earnout revaluations, which are accounting charges, not cash costs. A tangible-equity measure is not usable here, because goodwill and intangibles exceed equity.[6]

For free cash flow (FCF) we use operating cash flow minus capital expenditure, which is the cash spent on equipment and software. Gallagher puts changes in fiduciary assets and liabilities in financing activities, so no fiduciary adjustment is needed.[1] For debt we use corporate borrowings minus cash. We ignore the premium-financing facility, which is secured by customer premiums and excluded from the company's covenant calculations.[8] We divide net debt, which is borrowings minus cash, by two profit measures. EBITDA means earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, and EBITDAC also adds back the change in acquisition earnouts. One measure is reported EBITDAC, computed from the income statement. The other is the company's adjusted EBITDAC, which also adds back integration, workforce-termination and acquisition-related costs.[8][10] One caution applies to 2021: it includes $1,141 million of revenue from a clean-coal business that has since wound down. Read growth from 2021 on revenue without it.[3]

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate in Chapter 5 rests on one assumption that is weaker than the rest. For the base case it is that the multiple stays near 19 times while earnings reach $14.81 for 2027. For the bull case it is that the market re-rates brokers to the 22.55 times median of the year-end multiples (2021 to 2025), which has not happened since the end of 2024. For the bear case it is that organic growth falls toward 3% to 4%. The third-quarter report and the first-quarter 2027 report will start to confirm or deny that path. The base case is also the most exposed to the multiple. With EPS unchanged at $14.81 and a multiple of 16.5 times, which sits inside the peer range, the value is $244.37 and the reward-to-risk at our $234.50 zone top is 0.54 to 1.[38][36][41]

This chapter builds the strongest honest case against buying. It has three scenarios, and each one alone would make the idea a mistake. It then names the single claim on which the whole report hinges and says when we will know. The aim is to make our own error visible before the market does. We also state in advance what evidence would change our mind.

Fatal flaw 1: organic growth keeps slowing toward the 1% to 3% range

The evidence for this case is already on the table. Gallagher's brokerage organic growth fell from 9.7% in 2022 to 8.9%, 7.5% and about 6% in the next three years. It is now 5%. Property renewal premiums fell 10% in the second quarter, and contingent revenues fell 8% organically.[4][3][2][10][8][20] Brown & Brown reported 1.2% organic growth for the third quarter on 2026-10-26, and its adjusted EBITDAC margin fell two points to 34.6%.[29] If Gallagher's own number fell to 3% or lower, our 2027 EPS of $14.81 would be too high. The 19.0 times multiple would look too generous, and the bear case would become the base case.

The counter-argument is that Gallagher's mix differs from Brown & Brown's. Gallagher has a risk management unit growing 12%, a reinsurance arm, a benefits business and international operations that grew faster than the United States in the second quarter.[20][8] Management also says only about one point of its organic growth comes from price.[20] We will not know which view is right until the third-quarter figures arrive on 2026-10-29, with the fourth-quarter outlook on the same call. Brokerage organic growth at or above 5%, with the full-year outlook unchanged, would show we are wrong to worry. A reading below 4%, or a cut to the 2026 outlook, would show the flaw is real.[19]

Fatal flaw 2: the acquisition engine stalls and the buying advantage shrinks

Acquisitions supplied about three quarters of second-quarter revenue growth, so a slowdown in deals matters more than a slowdown in organic growth. In the first half of 2026 Gallagher closed 14 brokerage deals with $107 million of annualized revenue, against 19 deals with $354 million a year earlier. Management said it was running at about 80% of its ten-year average.[8][20] The price paid rose from 9.4 times EBITDAC in the first quarter to 11.3 times in the second. The CFO said seller expectations are coming down, and that two second-quarter deals carried savings the multiple does not count.[20] Meanwhile the market gives Gallagher about 19.8 times forward earnings. The arbitrage, the gap between what Gallagher pays and what its shares are worth, is narrowing from both sides.

Cantor's cut to 2027 earnings, from $15.01 to $14.21, rests on exactly this slower pace of rollover revenue from acquisitions.[34] There is a balance-sheet side too. With reported leverage at 3.0 times, a series of large debt-funded purchases at 11 times earnings would push it up. Equity funding at lower share prices would dilute owners more than the December 2024 raise at about $274 a share did.[6][1] The evidence to watch is the deal count and annualized revenue acquired in the third-quarter release, and the rollover revenue table in the CFO commentary. If the quarter shows fewer than 5 deals or less than $50 million of annualized revenue, we would treat the engine as stalled.

Fatal flaw 3: adjusted profit is not cash profit, and automation or fee pressure compresses margins

Adjusted earnings per share of $11.96 for the twelve months to June 2026 is almost double the $6.03 of GAAP earnings. The gap is made up of amortization of acquired client lists ($1,105 million for the twelve months), integration costs, workforce termination and earnout changes.[8][10][6][1] Amortization is not cash. But the integration costs are cash, about $575 million over three years for AssuredPartners and $150 million for Woodruff Sawyer.[1] The risk is that investors start to price the stock on GAAP profit or free cash flow, not adjusted profit. On free cash flow the yield is 2.9% on enterprise value, which is low.

Margins face a second threat. If artificial intelligence lets clients buy simple advice from software, clients may push brokers to share the savings through lower fees. If the same tools raise competition, the 600 basis points of savings management describes could be competed away, not kept.[20] GAAP earnings per share rising toward the adjusted figure as amortization stops growing would show we are wrong. So would underlying margins expanding 40 to 60 basis points as guided. A repeat of the second quarter, when GAAP EPS was $1.25 against $1.40 a year before, with a falling underlying margin, would show the flaw is real.[6] We will know on 2026-10-29 for the quarter and in early 2027 for the year.

7. What we would do, and when

The decision is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT, with the gate status PASSED_CONDITIONAL. The rule is the support-distance ladder in our scoring file. A stock more than 1.6 average daily ranges above its nearest support sits in the NO_BUY band. AJG at $259.01 is 1.64 ranges above its 50-day average of $247.58.[42] The business itself qualifies. No hard red line is crossed. Free cash flow and leverage pass. The Layer 2 score is 3.5 of 5.0 with no failure, and the turnaround archetype fits. What fails is the price. With the stop placed where our rule puts it, below the $219.65 swing low, the reward-to-risk at the close is 0.52 to 1.

The seed idea from the 2026-10-27 market intelligence report was a limit order between $247.58 and $251.07 with a stop at $244.09. It claimed a reward-to-risk of 6.17 to 1. We do not accept it, and the reason is the stop. A stop at $244.09 sits just under the 50-day average and ignores the swing low. An ordinary 3% dip would hit it, and it would not protect against the failure of the October advance, which is the real invalidation. The rule for the tactical bucket puts the stop at the lower of the swing low and the nearer average, minus half a daily range. That is $219.65 minus $3.50, or $216.15. At that stop the seed zone yields only 0.87 to 1, so it fails our 2.5 floor.[42]

The entry zone we would use is $228.00 to $234.50. It is centered on the 200-day average of $231.92 and the cluster of closes between $232.70 and $237.04 from 2026-10-08 to 2026-10-14.[39][42] At the top of the zone the base value of $281.39 gives 2.56 to 1. At $228.00 it gives 4.51 to 1. We would place half the order at $234.50 and half at $230.00, and only after the 2026-10-29 report. If the report disappoints and the stock falls into the zone on bad news, we cancel the order instead of filling it. The zone assumes a healthy business. If the report is good and the stock rises, we do nothing and do not chase.[19] The printed zone is provisional, not an order to place unchanged. After the report we recompute the zone from fresh EPS, a multiple we can justify and a fresh stop. If that recomputation puts the zone top above the price at which reward-to-risk is 2.5 to 1, we lower it or cancel.

The reason for patience is the shape of the payoff. It is not a view that the stock must fall. In the base case the stock is worth $281.39 in a year. A buyer at $259.01 earns 8.6% with a stop 16.6% away. A buyer at $234.50 earns 20.0% with a stop 7.8% away. The probability-weighted value is $277.25. The multiple is the weak point: at 16.5 times with EPS unchanged the value is $244.37 and the reward-to-risk at $234.50 is 0.54 to 1. The order is cheap to leave in place and costs nothing if unfilled. Missing a rally costs less than buying near the top of a 23% rise in under three weeks, just ahead of a company report that decides the thesis.

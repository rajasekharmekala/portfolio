1. What the company does

Antero drills horizontal wells into the Marcellus Shale in West Virginia and sells what comes out of the ground. In the third quarter of 2026 it produced 4,234 million cubic feet of gas equivalent per day, a company record and 23 percent more than a year earlier[4]. “Gas equivalent” (Mcfe) is a way to add barrels of liquids to gas by treating one barrel as six thousand cubic feet of gas, so that one number describes the whole output[5]. Of that daily volume, 2,904 million cubic feet was natural gas, 124,109 barrels were propane and heavier liquids, 90,576 barrels were ethane and 6,924 barrels were oil[4].

The people who pay Antero are the buyers of those molecules. Gas goes to utilities, power plants, factories and liquefied natural gas (LNG) export terminals. The company says 2.3 billion cubic feet a day is sold at points along the Gulf Coast LNG corridor[6]. The liquids go largely overseas, and management calls Antero the largest US producer-exporter of natural gas liquids[6]. Liquids matter because they are priced off oil and global demand rather than off the US gas price: in the third quarter they were 43.7 percent of Antero’s commodity sales ($579.8 million of $1,327.3 million)[7][8].

Antero does not own the pipes it relies on. It pays other companies to gather, compress, process and transport its production, and those payments were $726.0 million in the quarter, which is 62 percent of all operating costs[7][8]. A large share goes to Antero Midstream, a separate listed company in which Antero holds an equity stake: gathering and compression fees paid to it were $715 million in the first nine months of 2026[9]. Antero has also signed long-term pipeline contracts: its firm transportation commitments total $7.46 billion of $10.66 billion in all contractual obligations, owed whether or not the gas flows[10].

A fixed-price sale protects cash flow when gas prices fall. Antero uses swaps, which are contracts that swap a floating price for a fixed one. For 2027 it has swapped 1,082,000 MMBtu a day (an MMBtu is a million British thermal units) at an average $3.82, plus collars (a price floor and ceiling) on a further 81,000 a day with a $3.52 floor[11]. Without hedges the third-quarter realized gas price was $2.63 per thousand cubic feet against an index price of $2.95, but the liquids lifted the blended price to $3.41 per Mcfe, 46 cents above the index[4].

What changed in the last year

Antero has been rebuilt by deals. On February 3, 2026 it closed the purchase of HG Energy for $2.8 billion in cash, which brought about 385,000 net acres in the West Virginia Marcellus[3]. The company says HG added about 400 drilling locations and raised net debt — total debt minus cash and liquid securities — by $1.5 billion from the year-end 2025 level, and that HG and the Utica sale together lift net production by about 700 million cubic feet equivalent per day[6]. It funded the purchase partly with a $1.5 billion three-year Term Loan A[12], and later sold its Ohio Utica assets for about $755 million in cash received in 2026[7][13].

Two older financing arrangements also ended. The Martica overriding royalty, sold in 2020 to Sixth Street, reached its return hurdle on May 1, 2026; Antero dissolved the entity on June 30, 2026, and 85 percent of those royalty cash flows reverted to Antero, worth about $60 million a year[14][15]. The volumetric production payment from 2020 ends on August 1, 2027, when about 35 million cubic feet a day of gas returns to Antero[15]. Michael Kennedy, the former finance chief, became chief executive on August 14, 2025, when the founder Paul Rady moved to chairman emeritus[16].

Reserves, the gas and liquids that can be recovered from known wells and locations, stood at 19.1 trillion cubic feet equivalent at the end of 2025, with 76 percent already developed and producing. This figure was set before the HG purchase and counts about 600 billion cubic feet equivalent of Utica reserves that were then held for sale[17][13]. The 4.7 trillion cubic feet equivalent of undeveloped reserves needs about $2.3 billion of development spending over five years[13]. The 2026 drilling and completion budget began at $1.0 billion and is now targeted at $1.2 billion, which management says supports high single-digit production growth in 2027[18].

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Henry Hub: the Louisiana pipeline hub whose price is the US benchmark for natural gas; Antero’s gas is priced off it plus or minus a regional difference called a differential.
  • Mcfe and MMBtu: Mcfe is a thousand cubic feet of gas equivalent; MMBtu is a million British thermal units, the energy unit gas futures use. One MMBtu is roughly one thousand cubic feet of gas.
  • NGLs: natural gas liquids, the propane, butane and ethane that come out of the ground with wet gas and are separated at processing plants.
  • Hedge, swap, collar: a contract that fixes or brackets the price of future production. A swap locks one price. A collar sets a floor and a ceiling.
  • Firm transportation: pipeline space the company has reserved and pays for whether or not it fills it.
  • Proved reserves: volumes of gas and liquids that engineering data show can be recovered with reasonable certainty under current economics.
  • EBITDAX: the company’s cash-profit measure, earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization and exploration expense, adjusted for items such as unrealized hedge changes. It is a company-defined measure, not an audited one.
  • Curtailment: deliberately holding back production when local prices are too low to justify selling.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The strongest part of the case is cost. Antero’s cash production cost, which is what it pays per unit to operate wells, gather, process and transport the gas and pay production taxes, fell to $2.05 per Mcfe in the third quarter from $2.44 a year earlier, a 16 percent drop that came in below the low end of guidance[19][20]. Management attributes much of this to folding in HG’s lower-cost wells and to renegotiating pipeline space, and it has set a goal of $0.70 per Mcfe lower cost than 2025 by the end of 2028[21]. Lower cost matters because a producer cannot control the price it sells at, only what it spends to get the molecule to market.

The second strength is product mix. About 44 percent of commodity sales come from liquids and oil, which are priced on global markets and are not tied to Henry Hub[7][8]. That helped in the second quarter of 2026, when the Henry Hub price fell 16 percent from the year before while Antero’s adjusted EBITDAX (the company’s cash-profit measure) rose 57 percent[21]. It also gives some exposure to the global supply shock this year: the NAGA review of 2026 describes a US–Israel–Iran war that began on February 28, damage to about 17 percent of Qatar’s LNG capacity, and Europe’s TTF gas benchmark up roughly 178 percent while Henry Hub futures rose only about 9 percent[22]. Liquids exports can earn part of that premium. Gas, limited by US export capacity, mostly cannot.

Third, the balance sheet is investment grade and the debt is moderate for the cycle. Antero reached investment-grade ratings from S&P and Fitch in 2024[23]. Net debt at September 30, 2026 was $2,781.8 million, which is 1.17 times the $2,374.9 million of adjusted EBITDAX earned over the last twelve months, and the only financial covenant (total debt to capitalization of 65 percent or less) is far from binding[24][25][8]. Hedges cover about two-thirds of fourth-quarter gas and 40 percent of 2027 gas at prices above the EIA forecast of $3.16 for 2027[11][2].

The weaknesses are equally clear. Antero is a price taker whose results swing with gas and liquids prices, so earnings are cyclical: net income was a loss of $186.9 million in 2021, then a profit of $1,871.6 million in 2022, $57.2 million in 2024 and $634.4 million in 2025[26][27][28]. Its fixed obligations are heavy, with $1.24 billion of firm transportation owed in 2027 alone[10]. Appalachian local prices are also weak right now: the company guided to a fourth-quarter gas price $0.15 to $0.25 below Henry Hub and expects to hold back 15 to 20 billion cubic feet equivalent of output because regional prices are too low[18].

How the position compares with Appalachian peers

Among the six listed Appalachian and gas-weighted producers we screened, Antero has the lowest forward price-to-earnings ratio at 8.22 on the vendor’s consensus basis, against 10.94 for Coterra, 11.76 for Expand Energy, 12.40 for CNX, 13.88 for Range and 18.14 for EQT[29][30][31][32][33][34]. A low multiple is only attractive if the earnings behind it hold, and Chapter 5 shows why we doubt the earnings figure the vendor is using. Antero’s return on invested capital of 10.36 percent on the vendor’s measure sits in the middle of the group (CNX 14.45 percent, Range 13.68 percent, Coterra 9.81 percent, Expand 9.10 percent, EQT 7.94 percent)[29][32][33][30][31][34].

The competitive map changed this week. On October 26, 2026 EQT agreed to buy Range Resources in an all-stock deal that creates a company with more than 9 billion cubic feet equivalent a day of production, an enterprise value over $50 billion and an unlevered free cash flow breakeven below $2 per MMBtu[35]. EQT shareholders would own about 73 percent of the combined company, and Range holders would receive 0.987 EQT shares for each Range share[36]. A scale competitor with a sub-$2 breakeven raises the bar for Antero, whose own advantages are liquids and cost reduction rather than size. It also shows the sector is consolidating, which is a possible source of upside for the next-largest Appalachian names, but we do not build a takeover into our numbers.

The one hard limit on the business is demand access. Appalachian gas must travel through a few large pipelines, and when regional demand is short, local prices fall below Henry Hub. Antero is trying to turn that weakness into a strength: it signed 265 million cubic feet a day of supply contracts for in-basin customers at Clarington, Ohio and agreed to resell about 400 million cubic feet a day of its pipeline space, targeting 1 billion cubic feet a day in 2027[19]. Those moves are worth about $20 million a year today and $50 million at the 1 billion level, so they help the margin rather than change the company[15].

Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Management’s record over the last two years is mostly good on what it controls (cost, volumes, spending) and weaker on what the market controls (price, leverage timing). In February 2025 it guided 2025 output to 3.35–3.45 billion cubic feet equivalent a day and drilling and completion spending to $650–$700 million[38]. It delivered 1,256 billion cubic feet equivalent of production, which is 3.44 a day, and $658 million of drilling and completion spending[17][13][8]. In 2024 the company also reported production 2 percent above its initial guidance midpoint and capital 8 percent below[38].

2026 has been busier. The company closed HG on February 3, which it described as ahead of schedule, then raised production guidance twice, to 4.15–4.2 billion cubic feet equivalent a day, and cut its cash cost guidance twice[37][6][21]. The misses are in realized gas price, which was guided to a premium of $0.10–$0.20 to NYMEX in February, lowered to $0.05–$0.15 in July and now expected to be a $0.15–$0.25 discount in the fourth quarter[37][21][18], and in leverage, which management said in February would fall under 1.0x during the year[37] but which we calculate at 1.17x on the last twelve months of adjusted EBITDAX[24][8]. The chief executive describes leverage as “approximately 1.0x”[19], so the gap is partly definition. We use the stricter trailing figure.

Two governance points are worth stating plainly. First, the 2024 annual report disclosed an error in how depletion (the accounting charge for using up reserves) was calculated in earlier quarters. The company judged the error not material, corrected 2022 and 2023, and the correction added $102.2 million to accumulated depletion at the end of 2023 and did not change operating cash flow[39]. Second, shareholders approved executive pay by 224.9 million votes for to 10.6 million against, but 33.5 million shares withheld support from one director nominee in the 2026 election[40]. Neither point is a red line, and the auditor, KPMG, has served since 2003[41].

Layer 1 asks whether the company clears the quality levels in the scoring rules, and which way each number is moving. A number that fails today can still be improving. For a gas producer we use ROIC, the return on invested capital or profit earned on each dollar put into the business, free cash flow margin through the cycle, net debt to EBITDA, which is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, stock-based compensation, and the red lines for distress and governance. Four chart terms also appear in the table. A Stage 4 breakdown is a falling trend and is the only chart-based disqualifier. Stage 2 is a confirmed uptrend. A stretched Stage 2 is judged with the average true range (ATR), the typical daily price swing, and the relative strength index (RSI), a 0 to 100 meter of how fast the price has risen. A breakout is a move above the top of a recent trading range. The result is that Antero passes every hard red line and four of five quality tests, and it misses the 12 percent return test on the cleaner measure.

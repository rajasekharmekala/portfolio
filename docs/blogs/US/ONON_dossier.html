Before you read

On Holding AG makes premium running shoes, apparel and accessories. It sells through retailers, stores and its website. Sales were CHF 3,014.0 million in 2025, with a 62.8% product margin and CHF 1,019.9 million of cash.[1] The conclusion is REJECTED: RSI means relative strength index, and it is above 70; the price is below its 200-day average; the frozen gate records Stage 2, the phase of a sustained uptrend, as absent; and reward-to-risk is below the tactical floor. The conclusion changes after a close above $37.70 with a rising 200-day average, or after a new base creates a target above $50 without weaker cash generation.

What the company does

50-day moving average means the mean closing price over 50 sessions. 200-day moving average means the mean closing price over 200 sessions. A base is a sideways price area. A pivot is the price where a price move may begin. A breakout is a close above that area. A retest is a later check that the old ceiling now holds as support. An entry zone is the planned buying range. Asymmetry compares possible gain with possible loss. Reward-to-risk is that gain divided by the planned loss. Net debt is debt less cash. Free cash flow is cash left after operating needs and capital spending. Guidance is management's stated outlook. Working capital is current operating assets less current operating liabilities.

On is a Swiss sportswear company whose products are built around running and everyday movement. A customer pays for shoes, apparel and accessories; a wholesale partner pays when goods are shipped or delivered; a direct customer pays at an owned store or online. The company outsources substantially all manufacturing, so it owns the brand, product design, distribution and customer relationship rather than large factories.[2] That model can grow without the capital burden of building factories, but it leaves On exposed to supplier lead times, freight costs, customs duties and inventory mistakes.

Two channels matter. Wholesale is the larger channel: 2025 wholesale sales were CHF 1,753.4 million, or 58.2% of sales. Direct-to-consumer, usually shortened to DTC, was CHF 1,260.5 million, or 41.8%, after growing 33.7% year over year.[3] The mix matters because an owned sale gives On more control over price, product presentation and customer data. It also carries store, payment and fulfilment costs, so channel growth is not automatically the same as profit growth.

Running shoes remain the economic centre. Shoes generated CHF 2,804.4 million in 2025, while apparel generated CHF 169.9 million and accessories CHF 39.6 million.[4] Apparel grew 68.2%, which expands the amount a customer can spend across a full outfit, but apparel is also more exposed to fashion, markdowns and size complexity. The company is therefore trying to move from a single-product running brand toward a broader premium sportswear brand.

Geography is another growth lever. The Americas generated CHF 1,740.1 million in 2025, EMEA CHF 762.7 million and APAC CHF 511.1 million. APAC grew 96.4%, while the Americas grew 17.6%.[5] Fast growth outside the home region raises the opportunity but also makes currency, local taste and distribution execution more important. The next chapter tests whether the operating evidence is strong enough to offset that execution risk.

Glossary of this report

Gross margin is the share of sales left after product cost; it is the first measure of pricing power. Adjusted EBITDA means earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization after the company’s specified adjustments; it is a way to compare operating performance before financing and non-cash charges. EPS means earnings per share, the net profit assigned to one share. ROIC means return on invested capital, or the profit earned for each dollar of long-term capital put into the business. FCF means free cash flow, the cash left after operating needs and capital spending. SBC means stock-based compensation, pay granted in shares rather than cash. ATR means average true range, a measure of the stock’s typical daily movement. RSI means relative strength index, an oscillator that compares recent gains with recent losses. A Stage 2 trend means price is above rising medium- and long-term averages; a Stage 1 base is a period of repair before that condition is established.

Why the business is strong or weak

On’s strongest evidence is the combination of growth and full-price execution. FY2025 sales rose 30.0% reported and 35.6% at constant currency, while gross margin rose from 60.6% to 62.8%.[6] A higher margin while sales expand says the brand has not needed to rely mainly on discounting. It does not prove that the margin will persist: the company sources most finished goods in US dollars and sells in several currencies, so exchange rates and freight can reverse part of the gain.[7]

The DTC KPI is a second strength. DTC sales grew 33.7% in 2025 and reached 41.8% of sales; in Q2 2026, DTC grew 26.0% and reached a record 45.7% of quarterly sales.[8] This earns a PASS because the channel is growing faster than wholesale and can improve control of pricing and launches. The caveat is that Q2 total sales grew only 13.5% reported because management deliberately moderated wholesale sell-in in the Americas. A channel transition can improve long-term economics while causing near-term revenue noise.

Product breadth is a third strength. Shoes grew 27.5% in 2025, apparel 68.2% and accessories 124.1%.[9] The apparel and accessory figures are from a smaller base, so their percentages should not be mistaken for equal profit contribution. They do show that On has more than one product route to grow the customer relationship. The underwriting score is PASS for category expansion, with a WATCH flag for the inventory and markdown risk that comes with more sizes and seasonal products.

International expansion is both an advantage and a stress test. APAC sales reached CHF 511.1 million and grew 96.4% in 2025, while Q2 2026 regional commentary continued to point to strong Asia demand.[10] The growth is material, but it is not yet proof of a durable local moat. The company must keep opening stores, building local demand and managing currency exposure without allowing distribution costs to consume the gross-profit benefit.

The consequence is mechanical: good operating KPIs cannot receive an allocation while both momentum tests fail. The stock needs either a controlled retest that holds support or a fresh base with a nearby pivot. Until then, the rebound is evidence to watch, not evidence to buy.

Why these estimates might be wrong

The first weak assumption is the company’s ability to maintain gross margin above 63%. The evidence that would disprove it is two consecutive quarters below 63% or an increase in promotions that reduces full-price sell-through. We would know at the next quarterly filing and earnings call. Tariffs, currency and product mix can change the result before the income statement looks visibly weak.

The second weak assumption is DTC growth. The evidence that would disprove the channel thesis is DTC growth below total sales growth for two quarters, or a fall in DTC share from the Q2 2026 level of 45.7%. The next quarterly release should disclose the channel split. A higher DTC percentage is not enough if store and fulfilment costs expand faster than gross profit.

The third weak assumption is inventory discipline. The company’s filings describe long production lead times and the need to commit to goods before final consumer demand is known.[18] The evidence that would show the risk is rising is an increase in inventory days, provisions or markdown language, but inventory turns were not published in the current record. The next 20-F or 6-K is the checkpoint; no estimate is inserted where the company has not published the metric.

The fourth weak assumption is the valuation target. A $45 or $50 target needs a price chart that confirms renewed demand, not only a spreadsheet. The evidence that would disprove the target is failure at $37.70, falling 200-day average, or a new low below $26.36. The market gives that evidence daily, but the underwriting decision should wait for a completed close rather than an intraday spike.

What we would do, and when

The action verdict is REJECTED, under the PIPELINE_CHALLENGER rule: a candidate is rejected when a Layer 1 gate fails. ONON fails the technical momentum gate, sits in the NO_BUY band because it is 2.79 ATR above the 50-day average, and offers only 2.4:1 base reward-to-risk against the 3.5:1 tactical floor. The company’s Layer 2 KPI score is 4 PASS and 1 WATCH, so this is a setup rejection rather than a claim that the business is impaired.

The decision changes to WATCH FOR SUPPORT if ONON closes at or below $31.96 and holds that level for two sessions without a new fundamental warning. It changes to a qualified tactical review only after a close above $37.70, a rising 200-day average and a new pivot within 1.0 ATR.

The entry zone is not active today. If the chart builds a valid base, a future entry would be considered near the verified 50-day average, currently $30.80, only after the support test holds. The tactical stop would sit below the new swing low and 50-day average by 0.5 ATR; no exact future stop is published because the next swing low is not known.

A purchase remains prohibited while RSI is above 70 or the price is more than 1.2 ATR above verified support. A close below $26.36 changes the decision to BROKEN STRUCTURE and requires a fresh company review.

The first target would be $45 only if earnings keep the gross margin above 63%, DTC remains above 40% of sales and the stock reclaims the 200-day average. The second target would be $50 only if the price clears the prior $51.08 area with volume and the company maintains its FY2026 sales and margin outlook. Those targets are conditional because the current asymmetry is below the tactical threshold.

The action changes from REJECTED to a new underwriting review at the earliest of the next earnings release, a close above $37.70, or a close below $26.36. Until one of those events occurs, capital remains available for a candidate that passes both the business and chart gates.

Chapter 1

ABM Industries Incorporated (NYSE: ABM) is one of North America's largest integrated facility solutions providers [1]. The company employs roughly 113,000 people to clean, engineer, power, and maintain mission-critical client infrastructure [1], [2]. Its service footprint covers commercial buildings, airports, manufacturing plants, schools, and enterprise data centers [1], [2]. For fiscal year 2025, ABM generated $8,745.9 million in revenue (+4.6% year-over-year) and $162.4 million in GAAP net income [1], [3]. Trailing-twelve-month sales expanded further to reach $9.15 billion through Q3 FY2026 [4]. A fundamental structural tension defines this $9 billion enterprise top line. ABM remains a century-old, unionized janitorial operator with thin contract margins. Simultaneously, management acts as an acquisitive contractor pushing into semiconductor cleanrooms, airport infrastructure, and turnkey microgrids.

The company's origin story explains its operating DNA [5]. In 1909, German immigrant Morris Rosenberg lost his San Francisco hotel and restaurant business during an economic downturn [5]. Armed with $4.50, he bought a bucket, sponge, mop, broom, and brush [5], [6]. He went door-to-door offering to wash merchant shop windows for whatever owners thought the work was worth [5]. He cleared a $3.50 profit on day one [5], [6]. The firm incorporated as American Building Maintenance in 1913 and adopted the name ABM Industries in 1985 [5], [7]. For more than a century, the business proved a simple economic thesis [5]. Property owners prefer outsourcing hourly facility labor and collective bargaining administration [7]. They also avoid direct exposure to workers' compensation claims and round-the-clock maintenance schedules [5], [7].

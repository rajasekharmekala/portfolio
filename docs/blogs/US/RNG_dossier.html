1. What the company does

RingCentral, Inc. is a software company in Belmont, California. It was founded in 1999, has about 7,300 employees and serves customers in more than 100 countries[30]. Businesses pay it a monthly or yearly fee to run their phone lines, text messages, video meetings and customer-service call centers over the internet instead of on equipment in their own building. That is called unified communications as a service, or UCaaS. Its call-center product is called CCaaS. The idea is simple: a clinic, a bank branch or a law firm gets a phone system that works on laptops and mobile phones, and the company keeps it running. The customer pays per user, and the fee repeats until the customer leaves.

In the quarter ended September 30, 2026, total revenue was $676.0 million, and subscriptions were $652.9 million of it, or 97%[1]. The rest, $23.1 million, comes from phones, hardware and services[1]. The company says more than 90% of subscription revenue comes from two products: RingEX, the main phone-and-meetings plan, and its contact-center products[3]. North America produced 87% of revenue in the quarter, down from 89% a year earlier, so overseas sales are growing a little faster than the rest[3].

The newer products are AI phone agents. AI Receptionist, called AIR, answers calls and sorts them. AI Conversation Expert, called ACE, reads call transcripts and coaches staff. Both are sold on either a usage basis or a per-seat basis, which means the customer pays for the calls handled or for the people using the tool[3]. On the October 22 call, management said AIR has more than 22,000 customers, ACE about 7,000, and a small-business contact-center bundle more than 14,000[22]. It also said customers using at least one paid AI product make up about 15% of annual recurring revenue, or $400 million, and that the figure doubled in a year[1][22].

The company reaches customers two ways. A direct sales team sells to larger accounts, and a channel of more than 6,000 partners and resellers sells to the rest[22]. The channel includes global service providers such as AT&T, British Telecom, Charter Communications, Optus, TELUS and Vodafone, which the CEO said are adding the AI products to their own product lines[22]. In June 2026 the company and Avaya changed their partnership so that Avaya Cloud Office customers move to a RingCentral-branded offer, and RingCentral booked a $15 million gain for releasing Avaya from certain duties[3]. The company has two classes of shares. Each Class B share carries 10 votes, and officers and directors as a group held 32.9% of the voting power at the December 2025 record date[16].

This is a mature subscription business. It sells new seats to new customers, adds AI products to customers it already has, and works to keep what it has. So the key measure of health is whether recurring revenue keeps growing after customers leave, and whether the cash it earns is real after paying staff partly in shares. Who pays, and what each side gets

The customer is a business that needs to answer calls and message its own customers. It pays RingCentral per user per month on a monthly, annual or multi-year plan[3]. RingCentral gets a recurring fee and a gross margin of 72.2% on a trailing 12-month basis, or 78.2% when stock pay and purchase-accounting costs are removed in the quarter[1][3]. The cost of that service sits in data centers it leases, in carrier fees and in support staff.

The company's own measure of recurring sales is annual recurring revenue, or ARR: the monthly value of all customer recurring charges at month-end, times 12. At September 30, 2026 it was $2.80 billion, against $2.63 billion a year earlier[3]. We read that as growth of about 6.5%, from rounded figures. The company also publishes a monthly retention rate. It has printed above 99% in each of the last five quarters, which means recurring revenue shrinks by under 1% in a typical month once upgrades and cancellations are netted[3].

2. Why the business is strong or weak

RingCentral's strongest asset is scale in voice. The CEO said the network carries about 40 billion minutes, 14 billion calls and 4 billion text messages a year, and that its carrier-grade network, scale, security and operating experience are a significant barrier to entry[22]. A competitor can copy software features. It is harder to copy a large phone network and the experience behind it. The second strength is distribution. More than 6,000 channel partners and resellers, and 17 global service providers, sell the product[22]. The CEO said the service providers tend to move a little slower, but that once they move, they move with very strong force[22]. The third is cash. Free cash flow rose from $104.9 million in 2022 to $530.2 million in 2025 and $603.7 million over the last 12 months, by our calculation[7][4][1][3].

The weaknesses are just as clear. Growth is slow: total revenue rose 4.8% in 2025 and 5.9% in the third quarter of 2026[4][1]. Competition is wide. The 10-Q names 8x8, Dialpad, GoTo, Microsoft, Twilio, Zoom, Amazon, Cisco, Alphabet, Five9, NICE, Genesys, Talkdesk and others, and says many have longer histories, more resources and larger customer bases[3]. Selling is costly. Sales and marketing took 42% of revenue on a GAAP basis in the third quarter[3]. And the balance sheet is thin: stockholders' equity was a deficit of $12.4 million at September 30, 2026 after being a deficit of $588.1 million at the end of 2025, and the company carries $199.4 million of convertible preferred stock[1]. Book-value measures such as return on equity are therefore not usable, so we use cash flow and debt instead.

One dependency matters more than the others. RingCentral resells NICE's contact-center platform, CXone, as RingCentral Contact Center, and the 10-Q lists NICE as a vendor whose dependence the company must manage[3]. In July 2026 the two firms signed a new multi-year deal under which NICE will resell RingCentral's phone product, and extended the existing contact-center marketing agreement for an additional year[23]. On the October 22 call an analyst asked what happens when that one-year extension ends. The CEO said he was not sure where the one-year figure came from, that no specifics had been disclosed, that the contract had multiple years left, and that RingCX, the company's own contact-center product, is getting stronger[22]. The press release gives the source of the analyst's figure. We therefore treat it as a real, open question about contract length, not as a threat that has happened.

What the AI evidence shows, and what it does not

The AI numbers are good. Customers using at least one paid AI product were about 10% of ARR at the end of 2025, over 10% in March 2026, 13% in June and about 15% in September. The company says the group doubled year on year in the March, June and September 2026 readings[8][10][11][1]. Management says this group has meaningfully higher revenue per user and retention above 100%[22]. It also says AI products earn gross margins close to the company average[22]. The CEO said half of growth comes from existing customers and half from new customers, and that about half of new customers take an AI product[22].

There is a catch in the arithmetic. The AI group is a group of customers, not a product line, so its ARR includes the ordinary phone seats those customers already paid for. As customers join the group, existing seat revenue moves into it. Using the rounded figures, the group grew by roughly $200 million in a year (from about $200 million to about $400 million), while total ARR grew by about $170 million ($2.80 billion less $2.63 billion)[1][22][3]. That leaves ARR outside the group about $30 million lower, by our calculation from rounded numbers. That gap is smaller than the rounding in the inputs, so the safe reading is that growth outside the group was close to zero, not that it fell. The company does not publish revenue for AI products alone, so we cannot split new AI sales from seats that changed label. Nearly all of the net growth appears to sit with customers who have adopted AI, and ARR growth of 6.5% is not yet the faster growth that a pure AI product line would show.

On the price side, the stock closed at $79.42 on October 22, opened at $90.98 on October 23, touched $98.14 that day and closed at $83.39. It then closed at $86.20 on October 28[33]. A market that pays for AI adoption has moved ahead of the recurring-revenue numbers, which is why Chapter 5 asks what the price already assumes.

Two promises are still open, and they are the harder ones. Management targets a GAAP operating margin of 20% and stock pay of 3% to 4% of revenue in steady state, in 3 to 4 years[22]. Layer 1 underwriting: static levels, trajectory (rate of change), and archetype fit

Layer 1 asks two things. Does the business clear the quality floors? Is the trend improving? The floors are a return on invested capital of 12% or more and a free cash flow margin of 12% or more. Net debt must be under 3.0 times EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization). Stock pay must be under 8% of revenue. As an alternative, it may be under 15% if owner free cash flow is 10% or more of revenue and diluted share growth is 3% or less. The third check is reward-to-risk, also called asymmetry. It compares the possible gain with the possible loss from the entry price, and the floor for this book is 2.5 to 1. A breakout is a move of the price above a level where it had stalled before, and one of our three archetypes is built around it. The red lines that send a name to REJECTED are net debt of 4.0 times EBITDA or more with interest cover under 2.0 times, a Stage 4 chart, or a going-concern or fraud warning. The debt is mostly a floating-rate term loan with an effective rate of 5.9%, plus $100.0 million of 8.5% senior notes due 2030 that cost 8.9% effective[3]. There are no debt maturities until 2030[22][3].

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Each estimate in Chapter 5 rests on a weakest assumption. This chapter names it, says what would show it wrong, and says when we will know. We also give the red-team bear case, which is the strongest honest argument against our own conclusion, and the one claim that decides everything, the hinge. Most dates are the next quarterly results, and that date has not been announced. The last two fourth-quarter releases were on February 20, 2025 and February 19, 2026, so the next is likely in mid to late February 2027, which is an estimate[9][8][30].

Fatal flaw 1: the cash flow is not as durable as the guide implies

Free cash flow is guided up 18% in 2026[22], and the 10-Q credits disciplined hiring and stock grants, offshore service providers, vendor consolidation, go-to-market changes and internal AI tools[3]. Those are lasting changes. But if part of the gain came from timing, for example when customers pay or when commissions are paid, it would not repeat. Sales and marketing still takes 42% of GAAP revenue[3], so a cost cut that goes too far could slow growth. If 2026 free cash flow lands under $620 million, the base, the reverse DCF and the buyback would all need review. We would know at the Q4 release in February 2027.

Fatal flaw 2: contact-center dependence on NICE and the AI label

The contact-center line sits inside the more than 90% of subscription revenue that comes from RingEX and contact-center products[3]. If NICE changes terms, competes harder, or lets the one-year extension lapse, the company would have to replace that line with RingCX before it could. The CEO says the contract has multiple years left, and that is the only evidence we have[22][23]. The second part of the flaw is the AI group. If customers use AI receptionists to cut seats faster than AI products add revenue, ARR growth could drop below 4% and move KPI 1 from WATCH to FAIL. Using the rounded figures, ARR outside the AI group was roughly flat to slightly lower than a year ago, a gap smaller than the rounding in the inputs[1][22][3].

Fatal flaw 3: a high stock after a big rally

The price is 76.7% above its 200-day average and has re-rated from 6.2 to 16.8 times forward earnings in 10 months[32][21]. The October 23 spike to $98.14 reversed to a close of $83.39[33].

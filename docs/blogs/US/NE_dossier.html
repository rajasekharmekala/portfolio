1. What the company does

Noble Corporation plc rents out drilling rigs. An oil company that wants to drill a well in deep water, or in the harsh weather of the North Sea, hires a rig and its crew from Noble and pays a fixed rate for each day the rig works. The legal entity is a public limited company incorporated in England and Wales; the SEC registry lists it as "Noble Corp plc" under central index key 0001895262, with the ordinary shares trading on the New York Stock Exchange as NE.[1][2] Every Noble filing used in this report sits under that key. Noble reports one business segment, contract drilling, and had about 4,500 employees at the end of 2025.[5]

The fleet is the product. When the 2025 annual report was filed in February 2026, Noble owned 31 rigs: 25 floaters, which are drillships and semisubmersibles that float and drill in deep water, and 6 jackups, which stand on the sea floor on three legs in shallower water.[5] Since then Noble has sold more jackups, so its third-quarter release measures activity on 28 marketed rigs, meaning rigs actively offered to customers: 23 floaters and 5 ultra-harsh-environment jackups built for the North Sea.[3] A rig that is not marketed is cold-stacked, which means parked with no crew and no contract.

Revenue comes from three numbers multiplied together: the number of rigs, the share of days each rig works, and the dayrate, the price per working day. In 2025 Noble earned $3,285.6 million of revenue, of which $3,107.2 million was contract drilling and $178.4 million was reimbursables, costs the customer pays back.[5] Floaters produced $2,568 million of the contract drilling revenue and jackups $540 million.[5] The average floater dayrate in 2025 was $402,703 and the average jackup dayrate was $185,337.[5] Because a rig costs money to crew and insure whether or not it works, a day without a contract hurts profit much more than a day at a slightly lower rate.

Customers are large and few. In 2025 ExxonMobil provided 19.7% of consolidated revenue, BP 13.2% and Petrobras 12.5%, and the US Gulf, Guyana and the North Sea provided 27.1%, 19.7% and 16.9%.[5] Noble signs multi-year programs: its Commercial Enabling Agreement with ExxonMobil reprices four drillships in Guyana on January 1 and July 1 each year to the projected market rate, and Petrobras extended the Noble Courage through December 2030.[5][9][12] The result is a business with good visibility on sales and a heavy fixed-cost base, which is why a few idle rigs can swing a quarter from profit to loss.

How a dayrate contract works, and what backlog means

Backlog is the dollar value of signed work still to be performed, counted at the full contract dayrate for the firm period, and it excludes mobilization fees and uncommitted option periods.[3] It stood at $6.7 billion on 26 October 2026, down from $7.5 billion in February and April and $6.8 billion in July.[3][9][12][11] Against 12 months of contract drilling revenue of $2,810.9 million (our calculation from the nine-month and annual filings), that is about 2.4 years of work.[3][5] Backlog shrinks every quarter by the revenue earned, and it only holds steady when new contract awards of about $680 million a quarter arrive.

The contract dayrate is not always the dayrate earned. Revenue can fall short when a rig is in the shipyard, suspended, or paid a lower standby rate, and some contracts include performance bonuses; Noble assumes about 40% of available performance revenue under certain long-term Shell and TotalEnergies contracts.[3] In the second quarter of 2026, the suspension of two rigs in Brazil alone cost about $43 million.[11]

Merger history and the portfolio changes of 2025 and 2026

Noble grew by acquisition. On 4 September 2024 it completed the purchase of Diamond Offshore, so Diamond no longer exists as a separate listed driller.[5] Revenue rose from $1,413.8 million in 2022 to $3,285.6 million in 2025 (a 32.5% yearly rate, our calculation), but that growth includes the Maersk Drilling combination and the Diamond purchase and is not organic.[6][5] Since late 2025 the direction has reversed: Noble agreed to sell six jackups for $424.0 million, of which $274.0 million is cash and $150.0 million is seller notes, and five of the six closed in the first quarter of 2026 for $360 million.[5][9]

The sector is also consolidating. Transocean agreed to buy Valaris on 9 February 2026 in an all-stock deal at 15.235 Transocean shares for each Valaris share, and on 23 October 2026 it said it expects to close on or about December 1 if the courts and shareholders approve.[18] That puts Noble in a market with one fewer independent competitor and one larger one.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

Noble's edge is a young, high-specification fleet with long programs in the world's best-funded basins. The 10-K calls it one of the youngest and highest-specification fleets of global scale, and it points customers at ultra-deepwater floaters and ultra-harsh jackups, the tightest part of the market.[5] Newbuild supply has "diminished materially", with very few new rigs left stranded in shipyards, which limits the risk of a flood of new capacity.[5] The backlog supports the claim: ExxonMobil, Shell, BP and TotalEnergies were 23.7%, 19.5%, 16.2% and 12.6% of the December 2025 backlog, and these are customers that plan wells years ahead.[5]

The trouble is that a good fleet does not guarantee good profit in a given year. Noble's adjusted EBITDA, the company's own profit measure before interest, tax and depreciation, fell from $277 million in the first quarter of 2026 to $212 million in the second and $201 million in the third, and the margin fell from about 35% to 28%.[12][11][4] Marketed utilization was 70% in the third quarter, which means about three rig days in ten had no contract.[3] Management says several rigs were "transitioning between contracts" in the quarter, which is the cost of a fleet whose long programs start in 2027 and not now.[3]

The oil price has not helped yet. Brent crude spot was $60.71 on 20 October 2025, reached $123.28 on 13 April 2026 and was $115.22 on 20 October 2026.[30][29] A near doubling of the price might be expected to bring a rush of drilling contracts. Instead the chief executive said fleet coverage and dayrates keep improving "despite limited apparent demand tailwinds arising from higher oil prices during 2026 thus far."[3] The 10-K says the near-term outlook for floaters and jackups carries "lingering utilization headwinds".[5] Oil companies approve rig contracts against multi-year plans, not against last month's price, so the price gain feeds into 2027 and 2028 drilling programs slowly if it lasts at all.

The cost side is a third weakness. In the third quarter Noble booked $133.0 million of income tax on $16.0 million of income before tax, and the adjusted tax charge was still $117.3 million.[3] Much of the tax is accrued in places such as Guyana, the United States, Switzerland and Luxembourg, and the 10-K calls $250.6 million of the 2025 charge recurring, so a weak group quarter does not reduce it.[3][5] That is why an adjusted EBITDA of $201 million produced an adjusted loss of $0.57 a share.[3]

Evidence for the strength: backlog quality and new contract rates

Since the July report Noble has won about $560 million of new work, including a long plug-and-abandonment program for ConocoPhillips on the Noble Interceptor of about 670 days and a three-year light well intervention agreement with TotalEnergies for the Noble Globetrotter I.[3] Management says leading-edge dayrates for top drillships are between the mid-$400,000s and $500,000 a day, against a floater fleet average of $403,113 in the quarter.[3] For ultra-harsh jackups the leading edge is $230,000 to $300,000, against an average of $207,651.[3] If those rates are real, rigs that roll off older contracts will reprice upward. The company also says about 20% more contracted capacity is in place for the second half of 2027 than the first half.[3]

Competitors say the same thing. Transocean expects industry utilization for deepwater and harsh-environment assets to move "well into the 90% range during 2027", and it announced two US Gulf drillship awards worth about $550 million in October.[16][19] We treat these as company statements, not facts. Noble and Transocean report the same pattern: strong awards for 2027 and later, with weaker current quarters.

Where the advantage is thinner

Fresh bookings did not keep up with work performed. New awards of $560 million in the third quarter compare with contract drilling revenue of $683.7 million, a book-to-bill ratio of about 0.82, and the second quarter added only about $200 million against $679 million of revenue (our calculation).[3][11] Book-to-bill is new orders divided by revenue, and a number below 1 means the backlog is shrinking. Over the two quarters it was 0.56, which is why backlog slid from $7.5 billion to $6.7 billion.[3][11][12]

Pricing is also mixed. The Noble Courage extension keeps the rig busy to December 2030 but cut the dayrate from $290,100 to $280,000 until the end of 2027 before it rises to $309,500, so a long contract can mean a lower near-term rate.[12] Noble's floater average rate was 4.8% lower than a year ago (our calculation from $403,113 and $423,489).[3] Customer concentration, the Middle East conflict that raised war-risk insurance costs for Valaris and a consolidating rival complete the picture of a strong but not safe franchise.[20][18]

Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Third-quarter 2026 results are public, so no report is pending for this plan. Noble filed its 8-K with the results on 26 October 2026, and the call is on 27 October at 8:00 a.m. Central time.[2][3] Revenue of $729 million beat the roughly $713.8 million consensus, but adjusted EPS (earnings per share) of -$0.57 missed the $0.23 consensus by $0.80, because $117.3 million of adjusted tax fell on roughly $26 million of adjusted pre-tax income (our calculation).[34][3] The release changes the case for the worse. Adjusted EBITDA of $201 million fell 5% from the second quarter, the midpoint of 2026 guidance fell, capital spending guidance rose and free cash flow was negative for the second quarter running.[3][4] Two things improved: about $560 million of new work and a utilization rise from 67% to 70%.[3] The price reaction supports a negative read. After the regular session NE traded about $45.41, down 5.0% from the $47.81 close, though thin overnight trading is only an indication.[31]

The full-year guidance trail is the cleanest test of whether management can forecast its own business. In February 2026 Noble guided 2026 adjusted EBITDA to $940 to $1,020 million, a midpoint of $980 million. In July it cut the range to $850 to $925 million, and on 26 October it narrowed it to $850 to $900 million, a midpoint of $875 million, which is 10.7% below the February midpoint.[9][11][3] Capital spending went the other way, from a $615 million midpoint to $692.5 million, 12.6% higher, though $40 million of the rise is spending that customers repay.[9][3] Management called 2026 a "transitional year" in February, so a weak 2026 was flagged; the size of the cuts was not.[9]

The 2025 record is better. Noble guided 2025 revenue to $3,250 to $3,450 million and adjusted EBITDA to $1,050 to $1,150 million in February 2025, and it delivered $3,285.6 million and $1,106 million, so revenue landed at the low end and EBITDA near the middle.[14][5][10] Capital spending is the weak spot: the first guide was $375 to $425 million net of reimbursements, and capital additions came to $500.1 million, or $475.2 million after $24.9 million of rebillable spending, about 12% above the top of the first range.[14][5]

Management delivered on financing and asset sales, which it controls, and missed on guidance and capital spending, and only partly delivered on the jackup turnaround. Guidance, capital spending and the turnaround depend on customers and timing. A record of hitting 2025 EBITDA and then cutting 2026 EBITDA twice tells you to value the 2027 inflection claim at a discount until contracts and quarterly results confirm it.

The dividend deserves its own explanation. Noble pays $0.50 a share a quarter, about $319 million a year on 159.6 million shares.[3][8] In the first nine months of 2026 operating cash flow was $561.9 million and capital spending was $500.4 million, leaving $61.6 million, while dividends were $243.1 million.[3] The gap was filled by $216.0 million of asset sale proceeds and by cash, which fell from $471.4 million to $337.2 million.[3][5] That is a choice management made openly, but it cannot last on 2026 cash flow.

On debt, the record is mixed. Net debt rose from $1,504 million at the end of 2025 to $1,551 million. The company's net debt ratio was 1.4 times twelve-month adjusted EBITDA at the end of 2025, is 1.7 times on the same adjusted basis now (net debt $1,551 million over $922 million), and is 1.9 times on its newer annualized-quarter basis, even though long-term debt fell by about $87 million.[10][4][3][5] We found no stated net debt target in the filings we cited, so there is no leverage promise to audit; the only promise is the capital return program, and the audit above shows how it is being funded.

Our variant perception

A variant perception is the point where our view differs from the consensus view, and the reason why. Our first difference from consensus is arithmetic. In 2025 adjusted EBITDA of $1,106 million produced adjusted EPS of $0.67.[10][31] Consensus FY2027 EPS of $1.98 is $1.31 higher. Each extra $100 million of EBITDA adds about $0.62 of EPS (we divide by 160.2 million diluted shares and hold tax constant), and lower depreciation and interest add roughly $39 million of cushion, so $1.98 needs EBITDA of about $1.28 billion (our calculation, which depends on our tax and interest assumptions).[5][3] That is 46% above the $875 million 2026 guidance midpoint and would be a 40% margin on the consensus revenue of $3.18 billion. Our second difference is the multiple. 6. Why these estimates might be wrong

A rejection is a bet that the price is too high for the facts, so the strongest case against it is the case for owning the stock. We also list what would show it, and when we would know.

Brent crude is at $115.22 against $60.71 a year ago, and Noble and Transocean both say 2027 utilization will be tight; Transocean expects industry utilization in the 90% range.[29][30][16] Noble says leading-edge floater rates are $450,000 to $500,000 a day, above its $403,113 average, and that contracted capacity is 20% higher in the second half of 2027.[3] If 2027 EBITDA reaches $1.2 billion and the market pays 10 times, the stock is worth about $64 on our model, or about $53 at 8.5 times. Fatal flaw 2: our multiple is too low because the sector is being re-rated

7. What we would do, and when

Layer 3 is the third check in our framework. It turns the chart and the risk rules into an order: where to buy, where the stop goes and how much to risk. The risk budget is the share of the portfolio we accept to lose if the stop is hit. It is measured in basis points, where 100 basis points is 1%. Each conviction tier has a budget, and TIER_0 is the no-position tier with a budget of zero. The initial weight is the smaller of the tier cap and the risk budget divided by the stop distance. The last row of the table, the let-winners-run rule, applies only to positions we hold, and NE is not held. Its 15% and 22% figures are limits in our scoring file (scoring.json) for a single holding that has grown, and they do not apply here.

The decision is REJECTED, at zero size, conviction tier TIER_0. The rule behind it is our reward-to-risk floor of 2.5 to 1 for a Turnaround or Tactical setup.

Under each swap, the counterparty bank pays FAS the daily total return of the Financials Select Sector Index. In return, FAS pays the counterparty a floating financing rate benchmarked to SOFR plus a negotiated spread [4]. Because swaps reset daily, FAS requires zero retail margin borrowing and carries non-recourse limited liability for shareholders [1], [2].

Creation/Redemption Supply Chain: Authorized Participants & 50,000-Share Units

Retail and institutional traders buy and sell FAS on NYSE Arca, where daily volume averages roughly 396.87K shares [10]. Behind that secondary tape sits a primary-market supply chain governed by Authorized Participants (APs). These APs are large broker-dealers and market makers such as Jane Street, Citadel Securities, Virtu, and major bank desks [3]. Unlike individual investors, APs deal directly with the Direxion Shares ETF Trust. FAS relies heavily on OTC total return swaps alongside physical equities and Treasury collateral. As a result, its creation and redemption mechanics differ from plain-vanilla stock ETFs. An AP can deliver an in-kind basket of Financials Select Sector Index equities for the physical sleeve. However, leveraged Direxion ETFs frequently settle creations and redemptions partially or wholly in cash through NSCC and DTC. Cash settlement lets Rafferty's portfolio managers scale OTC swap notional with counterparty banks at the 4:00 PM ET closing auction [3]. To protect existing shareholders from trading friction, APs pay fixed transaction fees plus variable cash-in-lieu execution spreads.

Swap Counterparty Panel, Collateral Custody & Hidden Financing Drag

Rafferty distributes FAS's ~200% incremental notional swaps across a diversified panel of global money-center dealers. This structure avoids single-bank concentration risk under ISDA Master Agreements and Credit Support Annexes (CSAs) [3], [4]. Daily mark-to-market variation margin sweeps between FAS's custodian and each dealer bank, minimizing uncollateralized credit exposure. If one swap dealer steps back or widens spreads, Rafferty can route notional to other panel banks [3], [4]. Meanwhile, FAS's cash and Treasury-bill collateral earns short-term risk-free yield. That collateral income partially offsets operating expenses and swap financing costs [1], [2].

Unit-economics analysis of FAS must separate explicit fund expenses from implicit swap financing drag. On the Statement of Operations, FAS charges a 0.75% contractual management fee to Rafferty Asset Management. Adding administrative, custody, legal, and Acquired Fund Fees and Expenses (AFFE, such as XLF or money-market shares) yields a 0.92% gross expense ratio. After Rafferty's Operating Expense Limitation Agreement, the net expense ratio is 0.88% [2], [10]. However, prospectus disclosures note that this 0.88% expense ratio excludes swap financing costs [2], [4]. Inside the daily total return swap settlement, the counterparty charges FAS roughly SOFR plus a dealer spread of 30–60 bps on the ~200% swap sleeve. When SOFR sits at 4.5%–5.0%, gross swap financing adds roughly 9%–10% of annualized drag before collateral offsets. Subtracting the Treasury yield on FAS's cash collateral leaves net financing drag equal to the dealer spread plus ~1x SOFR on the levered leg.

Consequently, an investor who only compares FAS's 0.88% net expense ratio against an unleveraged ETF's 0.08% fee underestimates total carry drag in high-rate regimes. The true all-in economic cost equals 0.88% explicit ER plus the dealer swap spread and net interest cost on the 200% borrowed notional.

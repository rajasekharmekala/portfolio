1. What the company does

Astera Labs makes the connective tissue of an AI data center. A modern AI cluster holds thousands of processors, called GPUs or accelerators, plus memory and network gear. Data has to move between all of them at very high speed, and signals degrade as they travel across circuit boards and cables. Astera sells chips, boards and modules that clean up those signals, share memory, and switch traffic, so the expensive processors are not left waiting for data.[2]

The company sells four product families built on one software layer. Aries retimers repair weak signals on the PCI Express links that join processors, storage and network cards. Taurus modules do the same job for Ethernet cables between servers and switches. Leo controllers let a server add and share extra memory over a fast link. Scorpio switches steer traffic between many chips, and the newest version, Scorpio X, links accelerators to each other inside a rack. The software, called COSMOS, is built into every product and lets customers monitor, tune and update the hardware.[2]

Who pays is a short list. The customers are large cloud operators (called hyperscalers), makers of AI accelerators, and server makers. Some buy directly and some through distributors or through the contract manufacturers that build their systems. In the June 2026 quarter four customers made up 82% of revenue: Customer A 29%, Customer B 25%, Customer C 15% and Customer D 13%.[6] The company notes that some of these are manufacturing partners buying for end customers, so the split can move between quarters without a change in end demand.[6]

The company is fabless, which means it designs chips but does not own factories. TSMC is its sole manufacturing partner for integrated circuits and no second source has been qualified. ASE and Amkor assemble and test the chips.[7] It earns money by selling those chips, and increasingly boards and modules, which carry lower margins. Gross margin, the share of each sales dollar left after the direct cost of the product, fell 250 basis points in a year to 73.3% in the June 2026 quarter because of that mix and because of warrants granted to Amazon, which reduce reported revenue.[8][9]

Revenue has grown from $34.8 million in 2021 to $852.5 million in 2025, and the first six months of 2026 brought in $700.8 million, up 99% on the same months of 2025.[8] Management says more than half of June-quarter revenue came from products that use the sixth generation of PCI Express, up from one third in the March quarter, and expects Scorpio to become the largest product family in the September quarter, one quarter earlier than planned.[10][1] The company is also adding optical links, through the $31.1 million purchase of aiXscale Photonics in November 2025 and a roughly $70 million equipment and licence purchase in February 2026.[11][12]

Why a data center needs these parts

Think of an AI cluster as a huge office where every worker needs documents from every other worker. Cables and circuit boards are the corridors. At the speeds used today, a signal sent down a long corridor arrives smeared, and a repair chip, a retimer, must read the smeared signal and send out a clean copy. As the speed doubles with each generation, the corridors get shorter in effect, so more repair chips are needed per system.[2]

That is the engine behind the company's claim about content per accelerator. Management said on the August call that Scorpio X alone should earn well beyond $1,000 per accelerator in future systems, and that total content per accelerator is heading to multiple thousands of dollars. These are management estimates, not audited results, and the number of accelerators sold is set by the customers' own plans.[10]

For you as an owner, the reading is simple. The company sells into the spending of a few very large buyers, and the spending is rising. The next chapters test how much of that is already in the price, and how much of it depends on one or two customers.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • PCI Express (PCIe): the standard data link that joins processors, storage, network cards and accelerators inside a server; each new generation doubles the speed.
  • CXL: a newer link standard, built on PCI Express, that lets servers share and expand memory.
  • Retimer: a chip that reads a weakened signal and re-sends a clean copy so a link can run faster or reach farther.
  • Hyperscaler: a very large cloud operator, such as the owners of the biggest public clouds.
  • Accelerator (GPU, XPU): a processor built for AI work; the company's products sell in proportion to how many accelerators are wired together.
  • Scale-up and scale-out: scale-up links accelerators inside one rack so they behave like one machine; scale-out links racks to each other over the network.
  • Fabless: a company that designs chips and pays a foundry such as TSMC to make them.
  • Contra-revenue: a cost booked as a reduction of revenue; here, the value of warrants given to a customer who buys products.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The competitive case rests on four things the filings support. First, the company was early: it says it created and commercialised first-to-market PCI Express, Ethernet and CXL products and has shipped millions of devices to the leading hyperscalers.[2][8] Second, it runs an interoperability lab where customers test its parts against other suppliers' parts before they build systems, which cuts the customer's integration risk.[2] Third, its software is built into the silicon and, on the August call, management said accelerators now talk to COSMOS directly to run workloads, which makes the switch harder to replace.[10] Fourth, and this is our inference and not a company statement, parts are chosen at the design stage of a system, so a win may last through the life of that platform. The turnover in customers described next shows that positions can still change quickly from one platform to the next.

The weak points are equally clear. The 10-K names Broadcom, Credo, Marvell, Microchip, Montage, Parade and Rambus as principal competitors, and the company says rivals compete on some but not all of its products.[7] The standards it builds on are open, so a rival can build to the same standard, and three of the five customers at 10% or more of revenue in 2025 were below 10% in 2024, and the largest customer of 2024, at 36% of revenue, fell below 10% in 2025, which shows that customer positions can change quickly.[13] The company publishes no audited market-share figure, so any claim of a dominant share is a claim we mark N/A.

Where the company is strong

The strongest evidence is financial. Gross margin was 75.7% in 2025 and 73.3% in the June 2026 quarter, and operating margin, the share of revenue left after all running costs, was 20.3% in 2025. Gross margin is far above the 50% line we use as the floor for chip businesses, which means customers pay for the engineering and not just the silicon.[14][1] Revenue beat the top of the company's own forecast range, which the company calls guidance, in each of the last six quarters, which suggests the company sees its orders clearly. Chapter 2B tests that record in detail.

A second strength is balance-sheet freedom. Cash and marketable securities were $1,253.0 million at June 30, 2026 and the balance sheet shows no borrowings, so a downturn in orders would not threaten solvency. Free cash flow (FCF), the cash left after paying for equipment, was $276.7 million over the trailing twelve months.[1][4] A third is the pipeline: management said over 10 customers are engaged on Scorpio X, with more expected to ramp by early 2027, although these are statements made on a call and not contracts.[10]

Where the company is exposed

Customer concentration is the largest exposure. Five customers were each at 10% or more of 2025 revenue, with Customers A and B at 20% each, and in the June 2026 quarter Customer A alone was 29%.[13][6] On the August call management said the September-quarter ramp of Scorpio X is led by its lead customer, with other customers to follow by year-end.[10] When a few buyers account for four-fifths of sales, a design change or an inventory pause at one of them can move quarterly revenue by tens of millions of dollars.

Supply is the second exposure. TSMC makes every integrated circuit the company sells, and no alternative foundry has been qualified.[7] The company also holds $181.7 million of purchase commitments, which is small beside its cash, so supply is a continuity risk more than a cash risk.[15]

Dilution is the third: the increase in share count that shrinks each share's slice. The company has granted Amazon warrants for up to 3,262,299 shares at $142.82 that vest as Amazon buys up to $6.5 billion of products, on top of earlier warrants for 1,484,230 shares at $20.34 and 831,945 more.[9][16][17] The accounting cost shows up as a reduction of revenue: $12.3 million in the first half of 2026 against $2.1 million a year earlier. That is a price paid for volume, and it also adds shares that do not appear in today's count.[17] The next chapter shows how the share count and stock-based compensation compare with the profit the company reports.

Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Management earns most of its credit on guidance. Since February 2025 it has issued a revenue range each quarter, and in all six quarters that followed the actual number came in above the top of the range, by between 2.8% and 9.8%. The pattern is consistent enough to be habit: ranges look cautious by design. The evidence that would show us wrong is September-quarter cash from operations. We would need to see it above about $150 million on revenue near $550 million. We will know when the company reports on November 3, 2026 and files its quarterly report in the days after.[32]

Central value: the multiple stays at 50.87

The weakest assumption is that investors keep paying 50.87 times next-twelve-month earnings while the 10-year yield is 5.10% and the stock has just been cut by one broker on valuation.[4][45][47][48] The multiple has already fallen from 76.61 to 50.87 in ten months, so it can easily fall further. It could also rise, because analysts expect 59% earnings growth in 2027 and the peers trade between 17 and 31 times next-year earnings.

The evidence would be the multiple after the November report. If the price falls while estimates rise, the multiple is compressing, and we would cut the central value in proportion. We would see this within a week of November 3, 2026. Hyperscaler spending signals arrive earlier, with Meta reporting on October 21 and Microsoft on October 28, and Meta has said it expects 2026 capital spending of $130 billion to $145 billion.[49][50][51]

Central value: adjusted EPS leaves out stock compensation

The weakest assumption is that adjusted earnings are the right yardstick. They exclude $63.99 million of stock compensation in the June quarter, which is 16.3% of revenue, and company guidance for September shows GAAP earnings of 0.755 times adjusted.[1] If investors move to GAAP earnings as the benchmark, the multiple on 2027 earnings is nearer 59 times than the 44.7 times on adjusted EPS, and our central value would fall by about a quarter.

The evidence would be a shift in how analysts talk about the stock: more GAAP-based targets, or the first Street estimates that exclude the awards. It is also visible in the share count, which we will see in the next quarterly report. Until then we state our central value both ways and keep the adjusted one because it is the way the market quotes the stock.

Bull value: the Scorpio X ramp broadens beyond one customer

The weakest assumption is that more than the lead customer ramps Scorpio X by early 2027. Management says it has over 10 customers engaged and expects additional customers to ramp by year end, but it also says most are in pre-production or qualification.[10] If only the lead customer ramps, 2027 revenue lands closer to the low estimate of $2.19 billion than the high of $3.68 billion.[5]

The evidence would be a second named hyperscaler in production by the March 2027 quarter, or the December-quarter forecast. The Street already expects $655.7 million of revenue in the December quarter, so we will know on November 3 whether management guides above that. A forecast below that figure would not break the business, but it would suggest that the second half of the ramp is slower than analysts hope, and it would lower the bull case.[5][32]

Bear value: both earnings and the multiple fall together

The weakest assumption is that low earnings and a low multiple happen at once. When earnings disappoint, multiples usually fall, but when the market is worried about rates the multiple falls first and earnings later. The bear case is a joint outcome that we give 25%, which could be too high if the company keeps beating.

The evidence against it would be two quarters in which revenue beats and the stock rises, showing that investors are paying for the beats again. The evidence for it would be a September revenue figure under $540 million, a gross margin below 70%, or a top-customer share above 35%. We will know on November 3, 2026.[32][6]

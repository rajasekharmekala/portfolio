1. What the company does

Avantor sells the everyday tools of science. A lab technician at a drug company, a university or a chip plant orders solvents, filters, tubing or freezers, and Avantor ships them. The company serves more than 300,000 customer locations in about 180 countries.[5] It reaches them in two ways. The first is VWR, a distribution business that also runs a private label under which more than 5,000 suppliers sell. The second is a set of manufacturing brands: J.T. Baker high-purity chemicals, Masterflex fluid handling and NuSil silicones for human implants.[5]

Since January 1, 2026 the company reports two segments. VWR Distribution and Services was about 72% of 2025 revenue at an 11.5% adjusted operating margin. Bioscience and Medtech Products (BMP) was about 28% at a 26.7% margin.[12] BMP is the profit engine: in the second quarter of 2026 it was 26.7% of net sales but 48.2% of segment adjusted operating income (our calculation from $117.6 million of $244.0 million).[2] Through 2025 the company reported Laboratory Solutions and Bioscience Production instead, and the old and new segments are not like-for-like.[4][12]

Customers fall into three groups: biopharma and healthcare, education and government, and advanced technologies and applied materials, such as chip makers.[5] The 10-K gives the approximate mix of each group in charts, and the chart values are not in the filing text, so we do not quote them.[5] More than 85% of net sales come from offerings the company calls recurring.[5] The United States produced $3,279.7 million of the $6,552.2 million of 2025 net sales, which is half.[5]

The company no longer reports growth by Americas, Europe and AMEA (Asia, Middle East and Africa). The last regional split we found is 2023 Adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization): Americas $912.6 million, Europe $449.5 million and AMEA $125.3 million.[7] Current releases split growth by segment, and the 10-K splits sales only between the United States and all other countries, so regional organic growth is not published in the filings we read.[2][5]

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Organic growth is sales growth after removing currency moves and the effect of businesses bought or sold. It shows whether the same business is selling more.
  • Adjusted operating income is operating profit before amortization of acquired intangibles and certain one-off items, as the company defines it. Avantor also uses it as its segment profit measure.
  • Net leverage is debt minus cash, divided by trailing twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA. The company adds back stock-based pay in its own version, which it calls adjusted net leverage.
  • Book-to-bill is orders received divided by sales shipped. A reading above 1.0 means the order book is growing.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

Avantor's edge is reach and habit. A scientist who has written a VWR catalog number or a J.T. Baker grade into an approved procedure rarely switches, and the 10-K says the company aims to develop solutions that become specified into customers' approved production platforms.[5] That is why more than 85% of sales are recurring, and it is also why the business kept generating cash through a long sales slump: operating cash flow was $953.6 million in 2021 and $623.8 million in 2025.[5][7] The edge is real, but it protects volume better than it protects price or margin.

The numbers show the cost of weak execution. Net sales fell from $7,512.4 million in 2022 to $6,552.2 million in 2025, a drop of 12.8%.[7][5] Adjusted EBITDA margin (profit before interest, tax, depreciation and certain one-off items, as a share of sales) slid from 20.9% in 2022 to 16.3% in 2025 and 15.0% in the second quarter of 2026.[7][4][2] Full-year 2025 organic sales fell 2.8%, and Laboratory Solutions, the old distribution-led segment, fell 3.2%.[4] Engine Capital, an activist shareholder, blamed 'share losses and margin challenges' in that segment in an August 2025 letter. That is the activist's claim, not a finding of ours.[23]

The balance sheet carries the scars. In the third quarter of 2025 the company wrote off $785.0 million of goodwill (the premium paid for past acquisitions) in the Distribution unit, because the share price and market value had fallen for a sustained period.[5] In the first quarter of 2026 a new test found the unit's estimated fair value above its carrying value only 'by a limited margin', so another write-off is possible if the share price or the unit's forecasts fall.[8] Goodwill and other intangibles together were 68.6% of total assets at June 30, 2026 (our calculation from $4,935.4 million and $3,014.2 million over $11,595.9 million).[2]

The peers set the bar. In their latest releases Thermo Fisher grew organic revenue 6%, Danaher grew core revenue 3.0% (5.5% excluding respiratory products) and Sartorius grew 8.1% in constant currencies over nine months, against Avantor's minus 0.4% in the second quarter.[28][29][30][2] Avantor's customers are not weak: the gap is partly Avantor's own commercial execution, which the new chief executive says he is repairing under a program called Revival.[2]

Customer funding and tariffs

Many customers are universities, government labs and foundations that depend on grants from agencies such as the National Institutes of Health (NIH), and the 10-K calls the level of government R&D funding 'unpredictable'.[5] On the February 2026 call the chief executive said customers 'remain hesitant to spend money even when it is committed and received', so he did not expect a noticeable increase in customer spending until funding was stable for an extended period.[12] On the July 2026 call he said education and Europe were stable 'at a lower level than we would love to see'.[13] The latest quarterly filing says recent US trade-policy developments reduced some tariff pressure but uncertainty remains; the company does not put a dollar figure on tariff exposure in the pages we read, so we do not either.[8] One peer, Sartorius, reported tariff refunds for levies that the US Supreme Court invalidated, which shows how much policy can move.[30] What this means for an owner: funding and tariff news can move the stock within days, and neither can be forecast from the filings.

How it was calculated

We took trailing twelve-month adjusted operating income, applied the company's 22.5% adjusted tax rate to get after-tax operating profit, and divided by invested capital, which is equity plus gross debt minus cash at June 30, 2026.

The inputs

Adjusted operating income $878.5 million (957.8 - 495.0 + 415.7); tax rate 22.5%; equity $5,630.2 million; gross debt $3,715.4 million; cash $306.8 million.[4][2]

The range

Some terms first. Layer 3 is our third test: the entry timing and sizing rules. The current market price (CMP) is the last close, $17.29. A stop loss is a standing order to sell if price falls to a set level, and a structural invalidation level is the price at which the reason for owning the stock is broken. A simple moving average (SMA) is the average of the last N closing prices, and an exponential moving average (EMA) is the same idea with extra weight on recent days. A basis point (bps) is 0.01%. Portfolio heat is the share of net worth that would be lost if every stop were hit at once, and risk-unit sizing sets a position so that hitting its stop costs a fixed number of basis points of net worth. Tiers set the budget: TIER_1 100 bps, TIER_2 75, TIER_3 50, TIER_4 35, and TIER_0 means zero size. We use TIER_0 now because the name is rejected. If it re-qualified, the yardstick would be TIER_4 (35 bps, a 1% to 2% weight) because three static floors fail and the next report is a binary event.

1. What the company does

Ecolab is a Delaware company founded in 1924 that sells water, hygiene and infection-prevention products and services. It reports $16 billion in annual sales, 48,000 associates and customers in more than 170 countries and 40 industries.[1] In plain terms, it sells a clean result rather than a chemical. A dairy plant buys sanitizer, dispensing equipment and a service visit. A chip factory buys ultrapure water. A restaurant chain buys dishwashing chemicals, leased machines and pest control. The customer pays for the program, and the chemical is one part of it.

Since January 1, 2025 the company reports four segments. Global Water, which was called Global Industrial before the rename, covers the Light & Heavy, Food & Beverage and Paper units and sold $7,679.9 million at fixed currency in 2025, about 49 percent of the segment subtotal. Global Institutional & Specialty sells cleaners and sanitizers to hotels, hospitals, restaurants and grocers. Global Pest Elimination sells pest detection and removal services, and Global Life Sciences sells cleaning and contamination control to drug and personal-care makers. The old Healthcare & Life Sciences segment no longer exists, because the healthcare unit moved into Institutional and Life Sciences became its own segment.[1]

Most revenue comes from selling products, and the rest from service and leases. In 2025 service and lease sales were $3,462.7 million of $16,081.2 million, or 21.5 percent.[1] Salespeople sell directly and through distributors, and the company leases dishwashing machines bundled with detergent and maintenance. It buys more than 10,000 raw materials, and the largest single one is about five percent of raw material purchases, so no one chemical decides the margin. Raw materials are generally bought on annual contracts, which means a sudden cost jump reaches the income statement before a price increase can catch up, and that is what happened in the second quarter of 2026, when energy and commodity costs rose and the company introduced an energy surcharge.[1][7][6]

Two purchases now matter. Ovivo Electronics, a maker of ultrapure water systems for semiconductor plants, closed on December 16, 2025 for $1,596 million in cash, net of cash acquired.[4] CoolIT Systems, a maker of direct liquid cooling gear for AI data centers, was bought from funds managed by KKR for about $4.75 billion in cash, closed on July 2, 2026, and is reported inside Global Water from the third quarter.[4][10][11] The company financed CoolIT by selling $5.0 billion of senior notes in May 2026, so it paid in cash and diluted shares were 282.2 million in the second quarter against 285.4 million a year earlier.[4][6] ChampionX is not one of these deals. The 10-K describes ChampionX as the company that took over Ecolab's former Upstream Energy business in a separation and split-off, so there is no ChampionX integration to track, only a possible indemnity claim.[1]

2. Why the business is strong or weak

Ecolab's moat is a service network that rivals find hard to copy. The company says the Global Water markets are led by a few large companies, with many small regional players. It says it competes on demonstrated value, technical expertise, digital technology, chemical formulations and global customer support.[1] Institutional, Specialty and Pest Elimination face a few large national competitors and many local ones. In Life Sciences the company names two large competitors in North America.[1] A technician on site, a dispenser that measures usage and a program tuned over years are costly to replace. That is why the company can raise prices when its costs rise.

The evidence for pricing power is in the margins. Gross margin is the share of sales left after the direct cost of making and delivering products. It was 40.2 percent in 2021, fell to 37.8 percent in 2022 when input costs surged, and rose to 44.5 percent in 2025 (our calculation from the income statements).[2][1] In the second quarter of 2026 reported volume grew 1 percent and pricing improved to 4 percent. Organic gross margin held at 44.9 percent against 44.8 percent a year earlier, even as commodity costs rose.[6] The company expects total pricing of 5 percent to 6 percent in the second half. That is the test of whether the energy surcharge sticks.[6]

Growth is concentrated in a few engines. Global High-Tech serves chip plants and data centers. It grew 29 percent organically in the second quarter and is approaching $1.5 billion of annualized sales. The company targets $4 billion by 2030 at a 25 percent operating margin.[6][11] Life Sciences grew 15 percent and earned a 26.5 percent organic operating margin, helped by a spike in bioprocessing, which the company named in its release. Pest Elimination grew 7 percent organically.[6] The weak spots are Heavy Water, Paper and basic industries. Customers disrupted by the Middle East conflict also cost about 1 percent of volume.[6] Institutional & Specialty reported sales fell 0.3 percent in 2025, because the 2024 figure still included the surgical solutions business that was sold. On an organic basis they rose 3.4 percent, from $5,761.9 million to $5,960.6 million (our calculation). Lower distributor inventories held back the fourth quarter, which the company said does not reflect end demand.[8]

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate rests on one assumption that could fail. This chapter names that assumption for each estimate, states the bear case in full, and describes three ways the thesis could fail outright. It then names the single claim everything hinges on and the date we will know. The bear case is the strongest honest argument against our own conclusion, so it is written as if by someone who wants the stock to fall.

Weakest assumption in the profit estimate

The 2027 estimate of $9.25 assumes that pricing of 5 percent to 6 percent in the second half sticks and that CoolIT adds to profit from 2027 as the amortization on the Nalco purchase rolls off.[6][11] The company said CoolIT dilutes by $0.20 a share in each of the third and fourth quarters, and that it expects to be accretive to adjusted EPS, excluding non-cash amortization, only in 2028.[11][10] The timing matters. CoolIT can add sales and operating profit in 2027, but the company dates EPS accretion, before non-cash amortization, to 2028, so our 2027 figure must not lean on a net CoolIT gain. The 2027 step-up rests on the base business, pricing and the end of Nalco amortization. As a sensitivity (our assumption), 2027 holds a full year of the $0.20 quarterly drag, $0.80, against $0.40 in 2026. If the extra $0.40 is not offset, 2027 EPS is about $8.85, which is $307.63 at 34.76 times and gives 2.80 to 1 at the $276.00 top, still above 2.5, and $263.82 at 29.81 times. Layer 3 is the last step of our method. The entry zone is the price range in which we would buy. Layer 3 turns the first two layers into an order: where to buy, where to stop, how much, and when to leave the order alone. The verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT. The rule behind it is the support-distance ladder in our scoring file, which sorts a stock by how many ATR it sits above its nearest verified support. A stock within 0.6 ATR gets full size (band FULL_SIZE), within 1.2 ATR standard size (STANDARD), within 1.6 a small starter size by limit order (LIMIT_ORDER_REDUCE), and above 1.6 nothing at market (NO_BUY), where we do not chase. Portfolio heat is how much of your net worth you would lose if the stop is hit. Risk-unit sizing sets the position so that loss stays inside a budget measured in basis points (bps), where 100 bps is 1 percent of net worth. Our scoring file gives each conviction tier a ceiling on the position size and a budget in bps; The largest tier (TIER_1) allows 7 to 10 percent and the smallest (TIER_4) 1 to 2 percent, and a zero-size tier (TIER_0) means no position. We use the third tier (TIER_3) as the yardstick for Ecolab, with a ceiling of 3.5 percent and a risk budget of 50 bps. The formula takes the smaller of the ceiling and the budget divided by the stop distance.

1. What the company does

Agilysys, headquartered in Alpharetta, Georgia, has spent more than 45 years building software for one industry: hospitality.[1] Its programs run the front desk of a hotel (who is staying, in which room, at what rate), the cash registers in restaurants, bars, spas and golf shops, and the back office that orders food and supplies.[2] The customers are hotels and resorts, casinos, cruise lines, corporate and campus dining operators, hospitals, stadiums and senior-living communities.[2] The company reports one business segment and employed about 2,413 people at March 31, 2026, roughly 70 percent of them in India, where most of the software is written.[1][2]

It gets paid in three ways. In the year to March 31, 2026 (fiscal 2026), revenue was $319.3 million.[1] Subscription and maintenance fees made up 64.5 percent of it. These are the recurring charges a hotel pays every month or year to keep using the software, the part investors prize because it repeats without a new sale.[1][9] Professional services (installation, training and consulting) were 22.6 percent, and products (hardware such as terminals, resold third-party equipment, and perpetual licences) were 12.9 percent.[1] In the September 2026 quarter, recurring revenue rose to 66.6 percent of the total and subscriptions alone were 71.2 percent of recurring revenue, so the mix keeps moving toward the repeating kind.[7]

A sale usually starts as a signed contract and turns into revenue as the software is installed. The company measures that signed-but-not-yet-recognised work as remaining performance obligations (RPO), which means contracts longer than one year whose goods or services have not yet been delivered.[5] RPO stood at $164.0 million on September 30, 2026, up from about $152 million six months earlier, and 43 percent of it should turn into revenue within 12 months.[5][2] The 10-K warns that some orders can be cancelled without penalty, so this backlog is a guide, not a promise.[2] The company puts its total addressable market (TAM), the full amount all hospitality customers could ever spend on this kind of software each year, at about $16 billion of recurring revenue, which is a company estimate, not an audited figure.[2]

The most important single customer relationship is Marriott International. In December 2022 Agilysys announced an agreement to deploy its cloud property management system across Marriott's U.S. and Canada luxury, premium and select-service hotels.[31] On the October 26, 2026 call, management said well over 2,000 Marriott properties are live, that select-service hotels are nearly done, that full-service hotels have started, and that the project is not yet halfway on properties, rooms or revenue.[20] The 10-K names Marriott only in a list of representative customers. It says the company's customers are highly fragmented and gives no percentage of revenue for any customer.[2] Management also leaves the Marriott subscriptions out of its sales and backlog statistics, so an outsider cannot size the dependence. That gap is why one of our five operating tests is scored as a watch item in chapter 2B.[20]

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The competitive position rests on focus and on how hard the product is to rip out. Agilysys sells only to hospitality, and its programs share one data model, so a hotel that runs reservations, restaurant sales, spa bookings and inventory on Agilysys sees one guest record across all of them.[2][20] Replacing a property management system or a restaurant register is like changing the operating system of a working business: staff must be retrained, hardware swapped and data moved while guests keep arriving. That cost is the main reason recurring revenue is sticky.[2] The 10-K names Oracle Corp., Shiji and Infor as larger full-service rivals, plus smaller specialists such as Maestro and the in-house systems of some big hotel chains.[2] Scale is lopsided: Oracle's last twelve months of revenue were $71.78 billion against $344.4 million for Agilysys, although Oracle's quarterly filing splits revenue into cloud, software, hardware and services and gives hospitality no line of its own.[40][41][25]

The evidence that customers stay and buy more is in the growth rates. Subscription revenue grew at least 23 percent in each of the last 20 quarters, and 32.6 percent in the September 2026 quarter.[20][7] Within that, subscription revenue from property management programs rose 53.8 percent and from register programs 18.4 percent.[20] The company added 14 new customers in the quarter (24 counting the Book4Time spa software it bought), and it recorded 119 cases of existing customers adding products, covering 331 new products, the fourth straight quarter above 100 such cases.[20] Management says each property could use 5 to 8 of its products, while the average outside the largest brands is about 2.8, so even without new customers the installed customer set offers room to grow.[20] The company does not publish a net revenue retention (NRR) figure, which is the share of last year's customer revenue that is still there this year; it only says churn is low, so we cannot test the stickiness claim with a number.[20][2]

The market backdrop is helpful but not the whole story. A third-party estimate puts the core hotel property-management software market at about $1.73 billion in 2026, growing about 7 percent a year, with 64.9 percent already in the cloud.[33] Agilysys is growing subscriptions more than four times faster than that market, which means it is winning share and selling POS, inventory and other modules, not just riding industry growth.[33][7] Hotel demand is also firm: U.S. revenue per available room rose 14.2 percent in the week to September 26, 2026, though the CoStar noted that a Rosh Hashanah calendar shift flatters the comparison.[32] Larger brands decide on their own technology cycles, so wins like Marriott come in lumps; management says no brand will move just because another rollout went well.[20]

The weak points are easy to name. The company is small, with trailing revenue of $344.4 million, and most of its engineering sits in one country.[25][2] Hospitality spending rises and falls with travel, and the 10-K says so directly.[2] Marriott is large enough to matter and is undisclosed in size. And the closest listed peers have been sold hard: over the 52 weeks to the data vendor's October 28 update Toast fell 18.26 percent, PAR Technology 53.41 percent, Shift4 51.28 percent and Oracle 53.92 percent, while Agilysys slipped only 1.31 percent.[34][36][38][40][25] That relative calm is good news about the business and a warning about the price: if the premium is not earned, the shares have further to fall toward peer-like valuations, and the rest of this report tests whether it is earned.

1. What the company does & day-to-day vertical operations

Agilysys builds software for hotels, resorts, casinos and other hospitality operators. Its products cover property management, point of sale, inventory and guest services. Customers also include cruise lines, dining providers, hospitals and entertainment venues. The company reports one operating segment because its CEO reviews consolidated results. Product-level profit is not disclosed, so investors must use sales and adoption indicators instead. [2][4][11]

Revenue comes from subscriptions, maintenance, professional services and products. In Q2 FY2027, recurring revenue was $62.2 million, or 66.6% of sales. Services were $19.2 million and product revenue was $12.0 million. Contracts often price software by room, property or point of sale. Subscription revenue is recognized over the service term. Implementation revenue is recognized as work is done, while hardware is recognized when shipped. This mix rewards live installs, but it also makes deployment speed important. [1][2]

The company’s Hospitality Cloud joins core systems with guest-facing modules. A hotel can connect room inventory and folios to dining, spa, golf, loyalty and payment workflows. A campus or casino can connect food service to purchasing and finance. Customers may buy modules separately, but the thesis depends on adoption across several products. More live modules can raise recurring revenue and make replacement harder. That advantage still depends on integration quality, staff adoption and reliable service. [11][14][15]

Hotels and resorts: connect the guest stay to property operations

A property management system records room supply, reservations, rates, arrivals, room assignments, housekeeping status and folios. Before arrival, staff control inventory and rate rules. At check-in, they identify the guest and assign a room. During the stay, teams coordinate requests and charges. At checkout, they settle the folio and close the stay. Agilysys adds mobile guest workflows, multi-property controls and connections to booking, spa, golf and dining. The operating gain is fewer hand-offs; investors need evidence of paid use and repeatable go-lives. [14][21]

Large resorts are a natural fit because one guest may create room, dining, spa, golf and event transactions. Rudding Park announced a plan to replace disconnected systems with an Agilysys platform across its hotel, spa, golf and dining operations. That is evidence of a customer selection, not proof of a completed rollout or measured return. New wins expand the installed base; successful activation and renewal create recurring revenue. [17]

Food and beverage: point of sale protects transaction flow

InfoGenesis supports orders and payments across fixed terminals, mobile devices, kiosks, self-service and tableside channels. Hotels use it in restaurants, bars, room service, poolside outlets and events. Campuses use it for menus and high-volume service. Offline processing matters where a network failure could stop orders or payments. The customer case is faster service, fewer failed transactions and better menu visibility. Agilysys has not disclosed a customer-level return series, so the savings cannot be quantified across the installed base. [15]

The Q2 call said domestic food-service management sales rose 42% year over year. POS subscription revenue grew 18.4%. Management also said hardware attachment per POS sale is lower as customers use their own devices. Broader POS sales can still lift product shipments, but hardware is not recurring revenue. Investors should track software attach, renewal and services margin. [1][3]

Inventory and procurement: translate menus into cost control

Eatec links purchasing, receiving, stock transfers, recipe costing, vendor records, invoice processing and inventory counts. A resort or campus can see what ingredients are on hand, what a menu item costs and what to reorder. Mobile counts and invoice tools can reduce manual reconciliation. POS data can link sales mix to food-cost decisions. These workflows create operating value, but Agilysys does not report separate Eatec revenue or retention. Cross-sell and subscription growth are the available indicators. [16][3]

Inventory systems also show the limits of the platform story. Operators may retain a specialist procurement or accounting tool even when replacing PMS or POS. Integration must prevent duplicate entry, delayed purchasing and reconciliation errors. The company should be judged by adoption across live properties, not catalogue breadth alone. [2][15]

Implementation, support and billing turn contracts into cash

Customer contracts can include subscriptions, maintenance, services and products. The latest 10-Q says subscriptions are generally priced by rooms or points of sale and recognized over the contract term. Maintenance is a stand-ready service. Most implementation work is recognized over time, and hardware when shipped. A backlog can grow before revenue is recognized. Services hours can also lag a sale. Q2 services gross margin was 30.9%, below the corporate gross margin, so implementation efficiency matters. [2][3]

The Marriott rollout tests this model at scale. Management said more than 2,000 properties were live and that select-service work was nearing completion. The project was moving toward full-service properties. The CEO said it was not near halfway by properties, rooms or revenue. Marriott subscriptions are also excluded from some sales and backlog measures but included in reported revenue. Those different scopes make simple comparisons misleading. [3]

2. Competitive moat, industry structure & external learning hub

The possible moat is workflow integration in a focused market. A hotel group can connect PMS, POS, inventory, guest profiles and amenity booking. A resort can link rooms with spa, golf, dining, events and loyalty. If data moves well across modules, each new product can add value and raise switching costs. Agilysys describes a unified data model and common APIs. Those are company claims, not independent performance measures. [1][11]

The fit is strongest where a property has several revenue centres and mixed cloud and on-premise needs. Agilysys says its modern software supports both deployment models. That may widen its customer set. It also adds engineering and support work across different environments. The moat is earned through implementation and service, not a product label. [14][15][2]

Competition differs by workflow. Oracle OPERA Cloud sells hotel PMS tools. Toast and PAR serve restaurant operations, while Shift4 has a payments-heavy venue business. These are not exact financial peers: Oracle is diversified, and the other vendors have different revenue mixes. Their presence shows why product count alone cannot prove pricing power. The test is whether customers choose Agilysys across a connected, multi-amenity estate. [23][25][26]

Why multi-product adoption can compound—and where the evidence stops

Q2 FY2027 added 14 new subscription customers excluding Book4Time, or 24 including it. New deals averaged 5.6 products. Deals with PMS averaged 9.3 products. Existing customers bought extra products in 119 instances, involving 331 modules. These figures support cross-sell, but the company does not disclose the eligible-property denominator or annual contract value per module. Adoption is promising; a retention cohort would make the case stronger. [3]

The 10-Q calls the customer base highly fragmented. That lowers ordinary customer concentration risk. A large PMS rollout can still create execution concentration. Management says Marriott is a huge project and remains short of halfway by several measures. This is a delivery risk, not proof that Marriott dominates current revenue. Track milestones, services margins and non-Marriott bookings together. [2][3]

The CEO’s market-size claim is a view, not a verified estimate. Evidence today is 32.6% subscription growth, 5.6 products per new deal and 331 add-on products. 6. Key risks, thesis breakers & bear case

The main risk is that growth does not turn into cash at the pace implied by the share price. Agilysys itself cites guidance delivery, sales momentum, backlog conversion and macro conditions as risks. Each maps to the investment case. [1][2]

The hinge is repeatable implementation. Subscription growth must stay high while service capacity improves and compensation costs remain controlled. The FY2027 guidance revisions support this case, but do not prove it. Slower conversion, weaker margins or lower module sales would expose the premium multiple. [3]

Backlog is a lead indicator, not recognized revenue

Management said product backlog was 90% higher year over year. It also said total backlog remained near a record after strong delivery. The Marriott project is excluded from some sales and backlog measures but included in reported results. The 10-K defines remaining obligations separately and excludes some usage-based and short-term contracts. These measures cover different populations. Delayed go-lives could reduce revenue even if bookings stay strong. [2][3][4]

Marriott is a material delivery test

Management reported more than 2,000 properties live, but said the rollout was not near halfway by properties, rooms or revenue. Select-service work was nearly complete; full-service work was starting. A delay can move subscriptions and service work between quarters. It can also use staff needed for other customers. Management says the project remains on plan, but no independent property-by-property schedule is public. [3]

Adjusted profit excludes a recurring share cost

The CFO expects stock-based compensation at 6%–7% of FY2027 revenue. The 10-Q reported $12.3 million of share compensation in the first half. Adjusted EBITDA excludes that cost. Valuation leaves little protection if growth normalizes

AGYS trades at 74.32x trailing earnings, 35.16x forward earnings and 9.49x sales. Finviz reports 14.86% TTM sales growth, below the latest quarterly subscription rate; the measures differ. Competition and missing retention data limit the moat claim

Oracle competes in hotel PMS, while restaurant and payments vendors compete in parts of the stack. Agilysys may win where groups need several connected modules, but rivals can lead in individual products. The company calls its customer base highly fragmented. It does not disclose net retention or churn cohorts in the cited filing, so lifetime value cannot be measured. [2][23][25][26]

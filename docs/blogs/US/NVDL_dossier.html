1. What the company does

NVDL is the GraniteShares 2x Long NVDA Daily ETF. An exchange-traded fund (ETF) is a basket that trades on a stock exchange like a share. GraniteShares runs NVDL, and its stated goal is to deliver 2 times (200%) the daily percentage change of NVIDIA Corp stock, before fees and expenses[1][2]. If NVDA rises 3% in a day, the fund aims to rise 6%. If NVDA falls 1.43%, as it did on October 16, 2026, the fund aims to fall 2.86%. NVDL actually closed down 3.03%, a shortfall of about 0.2 points against the target[8][33]. The fund began trading on December 13, 2022. It split its shares 3 for 1 on June 26, 2026, which cut the price per share to a third and tripled the share count, so every price in this book is split-adjusted[1][3][6].

The fund does not own NVIDIA shares. It gets its 2x exposure through total-return swaps, which are contracts with banks in which the fund receives the stock's gain or loss and pays a financing charge[1]. At June 30, 2026 the fund had swaps with nine counterparties. The largest was Clear Street, LLC, with $1.973 billion of notional exposure out of $3.897 billion in all, a financing spread of 625 basis points over the overnight bank funding rate, and an end date of October 19, 2027[6]. Most of the rest of the fund's assets are Treasury bills posted as collateral, 59.71% of total investments in the annual report summary[4]. The issuer's holdings file shows net assets of $3.243 billion, 98.45 million shares, a net asset value of $32.94 a share and swap exposure of 2.0 times net assets. The file name carries October 15 and the net asset value matches that day's close[7]. Each evening the fund resets its exposure to 200% of its net assets. A simple picture: a driver whose cruise control is reset every evening to twice the speed reached that day. That reset is what makes the fund useful for a one-day bet and costly for a long hold.

The people who pay the fund are traders who want a short, amplified bet on one stock. GraniteShares charges a management fee of 0.99% a year. With other expenses the stated total is 1.06%, and a contract holds total expenses at 1.15% or less until December 31, 2026[2][5]. The annual report puts the actual expense ratio for the year to June 30, 2026 at 1.02%, which is $119.74 on a $10,000 investment[4]. Two costs sit outside that table. The prospectus estimates the indirect cost of swaps at about 3.00% a year, a figure for the fiscal year to June 30, 2025[2]. Our own measurement from prices is larger: over the last 250 trading days the fund's actual return was about 9.7% a year below what a perfect daily 2x rule would have produced. That gap covers fees, swap financing and tracking gaps, and it is our arithmetic from the price history[12][13]. On $3.243 billion of net assets the 0.99% fee alone is about $32 million a year, also our arithmetic.

The engine behind the fund is NVIDIA Corporation, which designs chips and systems for artificial intelligence (AI) computing. It reports two segments: Compute & Networking, with $193.5 billion of fiscal 2026 revenue, and Graphics, with $22.5 billion[18]. In the second quarter of fiscal 2027, which ended July 26, 2026, revenue was $96.2 billion, up 106% from a year earlier, and Data Center revenue was $89.0 billion, up 117%[21]. NVDA closed at $214.13 on October 16, 2026, about 12.0% below its 52-week high of $243.37[33][41]. This book treats NVDL as the framework requires for leveraged funds: it studies the stock, the price trend and the decay risk. The positions replay of 2026-10-16 has no NVDL line and no NVDA line, so the fund is a pipeline challenger and the verdict applies to new money only[42].

The measures this book uses

Several measures come up again in later chapters, so here they are in plain words. Free cash flow (FCF) is the cash a business has left after paying for equipment. Earnings per share (EPS) is profit divided by the number of shares. Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA) is operating profit before borrowing costs, taxes and the wear on equipment. Return on invested capital (ROIC) is after-tax operating profit divided by the money put into the business. Stock-based compensation (SBC) is pay given to staff in shares. Net debt is debt minus cash.

The chart terms are these. The relative strength index (RSI) is a 0-to-100 gauge of how fast a price has been moving, and below 30 is called oversold. The average true range (ATR) is the typical daily price swing in dollars. A base is a flat floor that a price builds before a move, and a breakout is a move above the top of that floor. Stage 2 is the confirmed uptrend and Stage 4 is the downtrend. Gross margin is the share of revenue left after direct costs. Working capital is cash tied up in inventory and customer bills, less supplier credit.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Leveraged ETF — a fund that aims to return a multiple (here 2 times) of one day's move in something else.
  • Daily reset — the fund rebuilds its 2x exposure at every close, so gains and losses compound day after day.
  • Swap — a contract with a bank that pays the fund the stock's gain or loss in return for a financing fee.
  • Counterparty — the bank or broker on the other side of a swap; if it fails to pay, the fund can lose the gain it is owed.
  • Volatility drag — the slow loss a daily-reset fund suffers when the stock swings up and down, even if the stock ends where it began.
  • Net asset value (NAV) — the value of everything the fund owns, per share, calculated at the close.
  • NVDA — the Nasdaq ticker of NVIDIA Corporation, the stock NVDL follows.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

A fund has no brand or factory, so its strength is how well it does the one job it promises and how it compares with rivals doing the same job. On both counts NVDL is sound and unremarkable. Three large funds offer 2x daily exposure to NVDA: NVDL ($3.243 billion of net assets, 1.06% stated expenses), Direxion's NVDU ($471.11 million, 0.92%) and T-Rex's NVDX ($391.52 million, 1.05%)[7][5][14][15]. On October 16, 2026 NVDA fell 1.43%, so a perfect 2x fund would have fallen 2.86%. NVDL fell 3.03%, NVDU fell 2.93% and NVDX fell 2.81%[8][14][15][33]. NVDL is about seven times the size of its nearest rival, and its stated fee is the highest of the three by a small margin. Its edge over the others is size and trading volume, not price.

Strength: the fund does what it says each day

The strongest evidence is the daily match. On October 14, 15 and 16 NVDA moved -3.16%, -1.84% and -1.43%, so a perfect 2x fund would have moved -6.32%, -3.68% and -2.86%. NVDL moved -6.18%, -3.90% and -3.03%[9][13]. Over the last 250 trading days the average daily gap from the 2x target was -0.04 percentage points, the average absolute gap was 0.11 points and the worst gap was 0.72 points, on August 27, 2026, the day NVDL rose 16.76% after NVIDIA's results. These are our measurements from the closing prices[12][13][9]. The market price also stays close to value. The issuer's file gives a net asset value of $32.94, and the October 15 close was $32.98, a premium of about 0.1%[7][9].

Weakness: the daily reset eats value when the stock is volatile

The prospectus states the weakness plainly. Over a period longer than one day, the fund's return is the product of each day's compounded return, and the fund will lose money if the stock is flat[2]. Its chart shows a fund losing 6.0% over a year if the stock goes nowhere at 25% volatility, and 63.3% if the stock goes nowhere at 100% volatility[2]. FINRA warned in 2009 that daily-reset funds typically are unsuitable for retail investors who plan to hold them for longer than one trading session, and the SEC chair repeated the warning in 2021[16][17].

NVDA's volatility is moderate. Measured from daily closes it is about 37.3% a year over the last 250 trading days and 24.1% over the last 20[13]. Real prices show what that does. From October 17, 2025 to October 16, 2026 NVDA rose 16.87% and NVDL rose 7.51%. Simple doubling of the stock's gain would give 33.74%. Compounding each day's doubled return gives 19.11%, which is 14.6 points of drag. The fund's actual result was another 11.6 points below that, which is the cost of fees, swap financing and tracking gaps[12][13]. In plain terms, the fund captured about 22% of the doubled gain. The drag is mild when the stock trends and is large when it zigzags. Chapter 3 turns this into a break-even number.

The stock behind the fund: strong demand, large commitments

NVIDIA reported second-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $96.2 billion, up 106%, with a gross margin of 75.0% and Data Center revenue of $89.0 billion, up 117%[21]. Hyperscale customers, the largest cloud operators, bought $48.7 billion. AI clouds, industrial and enterprise customers bought $40.3 billion, up 138% from a year earlier[22]. Management guided third-quarter revenue to $108.0 billion, plus or minus 2%, and assumed no Data Center compute sales to China[21]. Shipments of older Hopper chips to China were under 1% of Data Center revenue in the quarter[22].

The strength has a price in commitments. NVIDIA's purchase and other commitments rose from $119 billion to $279 billion in one quarter, mostly memory[22]. The company also guarantees data center leases for customers. Its maximum gross exposure is $108.5 billion, of which $105.0 billion is the August 2026 guarantee for a site in Ohio where OpenAI is the tenant[22][28]. Receivables rose to $63.1 billion, with 60 days of sales outstanding, up from 45 days, because of extended payment terms on large multi-quarter deals[22]. Revenue is concentrated: in the second quarter one direct customer was 16% of revenue, and in the first half three were 16%, 15% and 13%[23]. NVIDIA issued $25.0 billion of senior notes in the quarter and agreed on September 2 to buy Hugging Face for about $11.9 billion[22][29].

None of this is a solvency problem. NVIDIA holds $56.6 billion of cash and marketable debt securities against $33.4 billion of debt, and it earned $127.0 billion of free cash flow over twelve months[21][18]. The point is that the stock's own growth now depends on customers who borrow to buy its chips, and the market is nervous about that. That is the link between a good business and a bad week, and Chapter 6 comes back to it.

The number

How it was calculated

A daily-reset 2x fund is worth about its starting value times the stock's price ratio squared, times a decay factor of e to the power of minus volatility squared times the years, times one minus the yearly cost times the years. With 37.3% volatility and 0.25 years the decay factor is 0.9658, and the cost factor is 1 - 0.0974 x 0.25 = 0.9757. Setting the product to one gives a price ratio of the square root of 1 / (0.9658 x 0.9757), which is 1.0302. This is the standard approximation and our simplification, not a number from the fund.

The inputs

Volatility of 37.3% from 250 daily closes of NVDA[13]. A yearly cost of 9.74%, measured as the gap between NVDL's actual 250-day return and the daily-compounded 2x return on NVDA[12][13]. The start price of $214.13[33].

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every number in Chapter 5 rests on a few assumptions. This chapter names the weakest assumption behind each estimate, the evidence that would show it wrong and when we will know. It then gives three fatal-flaw scenarios, the strongest case against our own conclusion and the single claim the conclusion depends on.

Estimates 1 to 4: the volatility and cost assumptions

We assumed NVDA volatility of 37.3% a year and a holding cost of 9.7% a year. Neither is stable. NVDA's volatility was 24.1% over the last 20 sessions, 37.3% over 250 and 43.8% over 500, all measured from daily closes[13]. At 50% volatility the three-month break-even rises from +3.0% to +4.5% and the base case falls from $34.22 to $33.28. The cost estimate comes from one year of prices and includes swap financing, which is quoted as a spread over short-term rates. The fund pays spreads from 2.00% to 6.25% over benchmark rates, so a rate hike, which the market is now pricing, would raise the cost[6][35]. The evidence that would show us wrong is realized volatility below 25% or above 50% for a month, or a visible jump in the daily gap. We will know within 20 trading days, and by the November 18 report.

Estimate 5: the scenario prices and weights

The $243.37, $228.18 and $190.01 prices come from chart levels in the ledger, not from a valuation, and the 25%, 50% and 25% weights are a statistical convention, not a forecast[41]. Analyst targets run from $180 to $515 with a mean of $328.72, and 59 of 62 analysts rate the stock a buy or strong buy, which is a real counterweight[30]. The evidence that would show our weights wrong is the November 18 report. Revenue above $110.2 billion, which is the top of the guided range, with gross margin at or above 74.0% would raise the bull weight. Revenue below $105.8 billion, the bottom of the range, or gross margin below 73.5% would raise the bear weight[21].

Fatal flaw 1: a gap through the stop

The $30.68 stop is $1.30 below the close, which is 0.69 of one normal day's swing. NVDL moved more than 5% in a day on 64 of the last 250 sessions, and its worst day was -12.37% on June 5, 2026[12]. A stop can be jumped, either overnight or inside one session, and then the loss is larger than planned. Treat the stop as a line where the idea is wrong, not as a guarantee of the exit price. This flaw would not change our verdict, because we are not buying, but it would change how any later entry is sized.

Fatal flaw 2: a counterparty or fund event

Clear Street, LLC holds 50.6% of the fund's swap notional and owes it $3.105 billion of unrealized gains, 73.6% of all swap gains, against net assets of $4.051 billion at June 30[6]. If a counterparty could not pay, the fund could lose part of what it is owed. The prospectus expects 35% to 45% of assets to be posted as collateral, which limits the loss but does not remove it[2]. Issuers also close funds. A data page headline says leveraged ETFs are being shut down at a record pace, and the fee cap runs only to December 31, 2026[8][5]. Nothing in the filings says any of this is imminent, and we cannot put a probability on it. It makes the downside worse than the formula in Chapter 5 shows.

Fatal flaw 3: the AI financing chain breaks at the source

NVIDIA now stands behind its customers' borrowing. It guarantees up to $105.0 billion of leases at a site where OpenAI is the tenant, and its commitments rose to $279 billion[22][28]. Receivables stretched to 60 days of sales, and one customer was 16% of second-quarter revenue[22][23]. With the 10-year Treasury yield at 5.10% and the sector down eight sessions in a row, a miss or a weak guide on November 18 could send NVDA down 10% in a day. A 2x fund would fall about 20%, through any stop[35]. This is the scenario in which the stock's good business does not protect the fund.

NVDA trades at 23.0 times fiscal 2027 earnings, against a median year-end forward multiple of 31.19, and the reverse DCF needs only 14.9% a year of cash flow growth[30][32]. Waiting for a close above $35.41 means giving up the first 10.7% of any rebound.

We weigh this and still hold the verdict, for four reasons. First, the technical floor is a rule, and the chart is in Stage 3. If you disagree, the cleaner way to hold the same view is NVIDIA stock itself, which has no daily reset and no counterparty. 7. What we would do, and when

Our decision for NVDL is REJECTED for new money. You do not hold the fund, because the positions replay has no NVDL line, so the question is whether to open a position and not whether to sell one[42]. The rule that decides it is Layer 1. The tactical technical floor needs a Stage 2 markup, and the ledger puts NVDL in Stage 3 with the close at $31.98 under its 50-day average of $35.41[41]. The reward-to-risk ratio is 1.72 to 1 against a floor of 3.5 to 1, and Layer 2 scores 3 of 5 KPIs against a bar of 4. Any one of those three would be enough for a rejection. NVIDIA's own (NVDA) financial floors all pass, so this is a verdict about timing and about the vehicle, not about the quality of the business.

The support ladder alone would allow a full-size entry. The close is 0.19 ATR above the $31.62 swing low, inside the FULL_SIZE band, which is any distance under 0.6 ATR[41]. The ladder only measures distance to support. It does not override a failed technical floor, a failed asymmetry floor and a momentum gate that reads NO_MOMENTUM. So there is no live entry zone today. The $31.62 to $31.98 band shown in the Layer 3 table is only the support area where the price sits today, and it has no standing as a buy zone. A real zone exists only after the chart confirms with a close above $35.41, and we would set it, with a new stop, when we re-underwrite.

The framework has two stop rules. The looser one, for long-term core holdings, puts the stop a full ATR below the lowest support. The tighter one, for short-term trades, puts it half an ATR below the lowest support. This is a short-term trade, so the tighter rule applies: the 20-day swing low of $31.62 less half of one ATR ($1.89) gives a stop of $30.68. The looser rule would give $29.71 and would cut the ratio to 0.99 to 1. The targets are $34.22 base, $38.93 bull and $23.73 bear. The stop is 4.07% below the close and 0.69 of one ATR, so one ordinary day can reach it.

Sizing is zero. A tier is a conviction level that caps position size, and tier 0 is our lowest, with a target weight of zero, so the sizing formula returns 0.0% by design. Risk-unit sizing sets the size of a position by how much of the portfolio you would lose if its stop were hit, so a wider stop means a smaller position. A basis point is one hundredth of one percent, so 50 basis points is 0.5% of net worth. If the gates later pass, the formula at 50 basis points of risk and a 4.07% stop would give about 12.3%, so the tier cap would bind instead, for example 3.5% for a tier 3. The leveraged bucket is at 24.89% of net worth with room of only $89.54 under the 25% target, so a purchase would first need a sale of a weaker leveraged fund. The let-winners-run rule would apply to a position that works: no mechanical trim just because the RSI passes 70, and the stop follows the rising 21-day or 50-day average. For a daily-reset fund we would still limit the hold to the three-month window used here.

1. What the company does

MSTX is an exchange-traded fund listed on Nasdaq that began trading on August 14, 2024.[8] Its stated aim is to deliver two times (200%) the daily percentage change in the share price of Strategy Inc, before fees and expenses, for a single trading day and not for any longer period.[1] The adviser is Tidal Investments LLC, and Defiance ETFs, LLC is the fund's sponsor under a fund sponsorship agreement.[1][2] The fund first launched at 1.75 times and raised its target to 2 times on October 29, 2024, which is why older data records still label it a 1.75X fund.[4] The total annual operating expense is 1.31% of assets, and net assets were $325.90 million on October 22, 2026.[1][8] The fee comes out of fund assets, so owners pay it through a lower fund value.[1]

The fund does not own Strategy shares. It signs swap agreements, which are contracts in which a bank pays the fund the price change on a stated dollar amount of Strategy stock and the fund pays the bank a financing charge.[1] It also holds Treasury bills and money market funds as collateral, which the prospectus expects to be 40% to 60% of assets, and it may use short-dated call options when swaps are hard to get.[1][7] At the end of each day the adviser resets the contracts so that exposure is again about 200% of the fund's assets.[1] The fund's April 30, 2026 shareholder report lists six swap counterparties: BMO Capital Markets, Cantor Fitzgerald, Clear Street, Jane Street Execution Services, Marex Capital Markets and National Bank of Canada Financial.[3] The fund page's holdings table shows six swap lines worth $625.68 million in total, about 1.92 times the page's net-asset figure; the table is stamped 'Data as of 10/26/2026' although it prices Strategy at $146.01, the October 23 close, so we treat the date stamp as a labelling oddity.[8][14]

The stock behind the fund is Strategy Inc, which runs two businesses. One is an enterprise analytics software business with revenue of $477.2 million in 2025.[19] The other, which drives the share price, is a bitcoin treasury: the company reported 848,517 bitcoin held on October 18, 2026 at an aggregate cost of $64.02 billion, an average of $75,446 per bitcoin.[16] It pays for the bitcoin with money raised from selling common stock and preferred stock, and its preferred stock carried a liquidation preference of $15.46 billion on June 30, 2026.[17] So a move in bitcoin reaches the fund in three steps. Bitcoin moves. Strategy's shares have moved about 1.45 times as much as bitcoin day to day (our calculation). The fund then moves by twice the stock's move each day.[14][15]

The fund's own history shows how large the swings are. It returned 175.98% from inception to April 30, 2025, then lost 91.79% in the fiscal year to April 30, 2026.[4][3] Net assets fell from $1,666.4 million on April 30, 2025 to $377.4 million a year later and $325.90 million on October 22, 2026.[4][3][8] A reverse split of 1 share for 10 took effect on March 19, 2026, which cut the share count by 90% and raised the price tenfold without changing the value of anyone's holding.[6][3] All prices in this report, including the session data used for the price tests, are adjusted for that split; the price source lists it as a 1:10 event.[13]

Why a daily reset changes the maths

A daily-reset fund is a fund whose leverage is restored to the same level at the end of every trading day. That is the opposite of a margin loan, where the loan stays fixed and the leverage changes with the price. After a fall the fund must shrink its dollar exposure, and after a rise it must grow it, so it buys after gains and sells after losses. In a market that swings up and down, those trades lock in losses. The prospectus calls the result compounding and states that the fund will lose money if the underlying security is flat over a period longer than one day.[1] The prospectus table shows the size of the effect: with a flat underlying stock over one year, the fund returns -22.1% at 50% volatility, -43.0% at 75% and -63.2% at 100%.[1] Chapter 3 recomputes this with Strategy's actual volatility, and Chapter 7 turns it into a plan.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Daily-reset fund — a fund that rebalances every day to hold a fixed multiple of one day's price change, here two times.
  • Swap — a contract in which a bank pays the fund the return on a stated amount of Strategy stock in exchange for a financing fee.
  • Counterparty — the bank or broker on the other side of a swap; if it fails to pay, the fund can lose the gain it is owed.
  • NAV — net asset value, the value of everything the fund owns minus what it owes, divided by the number of shares.
  • Volatility decay — the loss a daily-reset fund suffers when the underlying price moves up and down, even if it ends where it began.
  • Reverse split — a change that merges old shares into fewer new ones, here ten old for one new, which raises the price and cuts the share count.
  • Preferred stock — shares that pay a fixed or set dividend ahead of common shares; Strategy's series are called STRF, STRC, STRE, STRK and STRD.
  • ATM (at-the-market) program — a way for a company to sell new shares gradually into the open market.
  • USD Reserve — a cash reserve Strategy keeps to pay preferred dividends and interest on its debt.
  • mNAV — here, the market value of Strategy's shares divided by the market value of the bitcoin it holds; below 1.0 means the shares trade for less than the bitcoin behind them.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The fund does its mechanical job well. Over the last 252 trading days, our regression of MSTX daily returns on MSTR daily returns gives a slope of 1.9875 and an R-squared of 0.99927, so about 99.9% of the fund's daily movement is explained by two times the stock's daily movement.[13][14] The slope is the number of percentage points the fund moves for each 1 point in MSTR, and R-squared is the share of day-to-day variation explained by the line. The daily tracking error, the standard deviation of the fund's daily return minus twice MSTR's, is 0.29 percentage points (our calculation).[13][14] Shares also trade close to value: the fund page shows a 0.03% discount to NAV and a median 30-day spread of 0.11%.[8] For a trader, that is a clean instrument. For a holder, the problem lies in what the daily reset does over weeks, which is the next paragraph.

The weakness is cost and decay. Strategy's shares have moved at about 82% annualized volatility over the last year (our calculation), close to the 90.46% five-year figure that the prospectus reports.[14][1] At that volatility the prospectus formula implies a loss of about 49% in a year on a flat stock.[1] The fund's real result was worse than the formula. In the year to April 30, 2026 the fund lost 91.79%, while a perfect, cost-free 2x daily copy of MSTR's closing prices would have lost 88.10%, so the fund finished with about 69% of the value that copy would have kept (our calculation).[3][14][13] The annual report names fund expenses and financing rates as drivers of multi-day performance but does not give the financing rate, so we cannot split the gap between the 1.31% fee and swap financing.[1]

Among funds that give leveraged exposure to Strategy, MSTX is not the largest or the cheapest. REX Shares' MSTU holds $714.15 million against MSTX's $325.90 million, so MSTX has about 46% of MSTU's assets, and MSTU's total expense ratio is 1.05% against 1.31%, a gap of 0.26 percentage points (our calculation from both pages).[23][8] MSTU describes itself as the first 2X ETF for MSTR in the United States.[23]

The sponsor record is mixed. The sponsor's adviser has closed several other Defiance 2X funds in 2026, for example the 2X Long B and CVNA funds in June, the 2X Short OKLO and HOOD funds and the 2X Long LMND and ZETA funds in August, and five Leveraged Long + Income funds in January.[9][10][11][12] None of those is MSTX. Still, MSTX's own assets fell by 80% from April 30, 2025 to October 22, 2026.[4][8] Its 1-for-10 reverse split in March 2026 took effect when the fund's NAV was $2.44 a share; the notice gives no reason.[6][3] A reverse split does not change the value of an owner's holding.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate in Chapter 5 rests on one assumption that could fail. This chapter names the weakest assumption in each estimate, states the strongest honest bear case against our own verdict, describes three ways the position could fail outright and names the one claim the whole conclusion depends on. It ends with the date on which we will know.

Fatal flaw 1: a gap that the stop cannot catch

Our stop works on closing prices and executes at the next open, so a gap can skip it. In our sample of daily data, Strategy's worst one-day fall was 17.1% on 2026-02-05, which was a 34.5% fall for the fund (our calculation).[14][13] Bitcoin trades through the weekend while Strategy's shares do not, so news arrives on Saturday and shows up in the stock on Monday. Across 91 Mondays, the stock opened more than 5% lower than the prior Friday close on 8.8% of them, and the lowest opening gap was -11.4% on 2025-04-07, which would be about 23% for the fund (our calculation).[14]

The money at stake after our trim, 82.480594 shares, is $1,299.07, which is 1.58% of net worth. A repeat of the worst day in our sample would cost 54 basis points of net worth, which is 1.6 times the 35 basis point budget for a TIER_4 name. A one-day fall of 50% in the stock would take the fund to zero, because 2 times -50% is -100%, and the prospectus says an investor could lose the full principal within a single day.[1] That total loss would cost 158 basis points. The stop limits a slow decline; it does not limit a gap. The trim cuts the worst-day loss from about 118 bps on the full 3.41% position to 54 bps, which is still above the 35 bps budget, and we accept that overshoot for the 1.58% we keep because the budget is sized to the stop and not to a gap. If the stop fails and Strategy falls 35% in one session, the fund falls about 70% to about $4.73 and the 82.480594 shares lose about $909, or 110 bps. If you cannot accept a loss of that size overnight, sell the remaining shares as well.

Fatal flaw 2: a failure at the fund, a bank or the sponsor

The fund depends on six swap counterparties, and on April 30, 2026 they owed it $160.2 million, with no collateral shown against that amount in the offset table.[3] The prospectus lists counterparty risk and says the fund may obtain only a limited or delayed recovery from a counterparty in distress.[1] If a bank failed or terminated its swap, the fund would have to replace it with another bank or with listed options, which the prospectus calls less efficient and likely to make the 2x goal harder to meet.[1][7] The sponsor's adviser has also closed other Defiance 2X funds this year, and a closure of MSTX would give holders a set date and a payout at NAV, which could land at an inconvenient price.[9][10][11][12] Closure of MSTX is not announced, and we have no evidence that it is planned.

Fatal flaw 3: Strategy's financing stops working

Strategy's shares trade at 0.90 times the value of its bitcoin by our calculation, so the market values the shares below the bitcoin behind them. The company needs cash to pay about $1.76 billion a year in preferred dividends and interest and raises it by selling shares, selling preferred stock and, after June 30, selling bitcoin.[17] If bitcoin fell far enough that the company could not sell shares and had to sell bitcoin into a falling market, the stock would fall faster than bitcoin and the fund twice as fast. There is a cushion on the dates we can see: the reserve covered 16 months on June 30 and 26 months on July 24, and bitcoin at the October 23 close is worth about 3.1 times the June 30 debt and preferred stock combined. Those balances are about four months old, so this is not a verified current figure.[17][15] But the cushion shrinks as the share price falls, because selling shares to raise cash costs more shares.

The bear case against our own verdict

The bear case against a trim is simple: bitcoin may be at the start of a rise, and a trim sells part of the fund after a 40.55% gain and before a larger one. The fund returned 145.84% in the three months to September 30, and Strategy's shares rose 48% in the last three months.[8][14] If that continues, selling 96 shares at about $16.50 would give up upside. The bear case against keeping the remaining 82.480594 shares is the opposite: our central estimate for the fund is $12.27, almost exactly at the $12.26 stop, so in a flat market the remainder ends the quarter at or just above the stop, and any extra decay would trigger the exit. We keep it because Layer 2 qualifies, because the leveraged bucket sits at 16.93% of net worth under its 20% to 25% range, and because a small position keeps the bull-case upside, not because the arithmetic favors it.

1. What the company does & day-to-day vertical operations

Veracyte sells test information at different points in a cancer patient's care. In the United States, it offers laboratory-developed tests through centralized, CLIA-certified labs in South San Francisco and San Diego. Cytopathology experts in Austin support those labs. After the physician receives a patient report, Veracyte generally bills the third-party payer. It takes assignment of benefits and the risk of collecting payment. The business needs doctors to order its tests and payers to pay for them.[2]

Two tests dominate sales today. In Q2 2026, Decipher brought in $91.9 million and Afirma $51.2 million. Together, their $143.1 million was about 98% of testing revenue of $145.7 million. Total revenue was $150.3 million. Veracyte launched Prosigna LDT and TrueMRD in the same quarter. Their future potential is not the same as the proven contribution of the two main tests.[1][3]

Afirma: classifying a thyroid nodule

Afirma starts with a thyroid fine-needle aspiration (FNA) sample. Its Genomic Sequencing Classifier analyzes expression across more than 21,000 genes in that sample. It classifies thyroid nodules and gives molecular information, including for Bethesda III nodules. It works on a sample already taken; it is not a general cancer-screening test. The lab sends a report to the prescribing physician. The physician can use that classification in care decisions.[21][2]

Veracyte's provider material cites a 2018 validation study. It reported 91% sensitivity, 68% specificity, 96% negative predictive value and 47% positive predictive value. These results do not guarantee the same outcome for every patient or setting. Predictive values depend on the population tested. Afirma is an established business: it had about 18,600 tests and $51.2 million in revenue in Q2 2026. The supplied excerpts do not show how many patients avoided surgery or quantify Afirma's unit economics.[33][1]

Decipher: measuring prostate cancer risk

Decipher Prostate asks a different question. How aggressive is a prostate tumor, and what is its risk of progression? Its genome-wide transcriptome assay measures expression of 22 genes to produce a Decipher score. The test uses tissue already taken during a biopsy or surgical removal. The physician receives information about biological risk, not a new tissue diagnosis from a fresh procedure.[22][2]

Decipher was Veracyte's largest disclosed product in Q2 2026. It had about 29,700 tests and $91.9 million in revenue. Those figures rose 17% and 20%, respectively, from a year earlier. The score's value depends on how it is used in a patient's treatment discussion. The excerpts do not show that the test alone determines treatment or guarantees an outcome.[1][22]

TrueMRD: looking for recurrence after treatment

TrueMRD for muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) looks beyond the first tissue assessment. It uses whole-genome sequencing to create a signature unique to the patient's cancer. It then uses that signature to look for recurrence after definitive therapy. The tissue comes from archived, formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded material taken during a transurethral resection of bladder tumor. A blood sample is collected two to four weeks after definitive therapy. That sample is used for circulating tumor DNA analysis.[23]

The archived tumor material supplies the patient-specific signature. The later blood draw supplies the sample checked for recurrence. Management said TrueMRD launched in Q2 2026 and secured Medicare coverage. The supplied excerpts do not establish its own test volume, revenue or repeat-testing economics. Its contribution is not yet shown to be comparable to Afirma's or Decipher's.[23][1]

Prosigna: a new U.S. laboratory offering

Management said it launched Prosigna LDT in Q2 2026. The company calls its U.S. product the Prosigna Breast Test. It adds a U.S. breast-cancer test to Veracyte's established thyroid and prostate tests. A launch alone does not prove broad adoption or a mature revenue stream.[3][1]

The test uses existing biopsy or surgical tissue and a PAM50 gene-expression classifier. It produces a recurrence-risk score and report for use in a treatment discussion. Medicare coverage took effect August 19, 2026. It applies to qualifying postmenopausal patients with early-stage estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer. This is a defined group, not every patient or payer. Product-level revenue remains undisclosed in the supplied excerpts.[26][32]

Convergent and UroAmp: acquired, with milestones still ahead

After the June quarter, Veracyte completed its acquisition of Convergent. It paid $150 million in cash at the merger's effective time, subject to customary purchase-price adjustments. It may pay up to $30 million more if specified UroAmp publication and reimbursement milestones are met. The deal makes evidence development and reimbursement explicit tasks. Neither outcome can be assumed in advance.[18]

The SEC filing identifies Convergent as a bladder-cancer diagnostics company. It calls UroAmp proprietary urine tumor DNA technology. The filing does not establish test sales, payer coverage or integration economics. The acquisition is complete, but the milestones remain potential. Ordering and payment are separate events. Veracyte generally bills third-party payers after it sends a patient report to the prescribing physician. It takes the risk of collection from payers and patients. Coverage and payment terms matter as well as clinical adoption. A test count alone does not tell us how much cash Veracyte will collect.[2]

Payer concentration matters. Medicare made up 33% of total revenue in Q2 2026, and UnitedHealthcare 13%. The Medicare figure excludes Medicare Advantage plans. The labs also depend on suppliers. Several collection-kit components, CLIA-test reagents, IVD products and related systems and service kits come from single-source suppliers, the company says. 2. Competitive moat, industry structure & external learning hub

Doctors have choices beyond a Veracyte test. For thyroid nodules, they can use other molecular tests. For prostate cancer, they can also use clinical methods and nomograms. Veracyte names ThyroSeq and Interpace as thyroid rivals. It names Myriad Genetics and MDxHealth as prostate rivals. The moat depends on evidence, use and payment working together—not just a distinctive assay.[15]

Afirma and Decipher are established businesses. That does not mean either test is clinically superior to every rival. In Q2 2026, their volumes were about 18,600 and 29,700 tests, respectively. TrueMRD and Prosigna LDT launched that quarter. They still need to show adoption and payment before they can claim the same maturity.[1][3]

Afirma: competing to inform the thyroid decision

Veracyte names ThyroSeq and Interpace Diagnostics as thyroid rivals. Sonic Healthcare markets ThyroSeq. Afirma GSC analyzes the transcriptome of a fine-needle aspiration sample to classify thyroid nodules. Doctors must weigh the evidence for their patients and the consequences of each result. The supplied record does not compare these rivals head to head.[15][21]

Afirma's 2018 validation study found 91% sensitivity and 68% specificity. It found 96% negative predictive value and 47% positive predictive value. These measures answer different questions. Neither a reassuring nor a suspicious result is certain. Predictive values depend on the population tested. The study does not guarantee results for each patient, prove that operations were avoided or establish an edge over ThyroSeq. The learning hub links to a real-world meta-analysis. The supplied excerpts do not quantify its findings.[33][24]

In Q2 2026, Afirma volume grew 10% year over year and revenue grew 18%. That shows use and sales growth, not market share or superiority. A rival could win with better evidence, access or fit for a doctor's decision. The supplied records do not show that a named rival does so. In prostate cancer, doctors also have clinical risk tools.[1][15]

Decipher: added evidence against other choices

Decipher measures the expression of 22 genes in existing prostate cancer tissue. Its score conveys tumor aggressiveness and progression risk. The company names Myriad Genetics and MDxHealth as rivals. Both offer genomic tests for prognosis in localized prostate cancer. Doctors also use clinical methods and nomograms for similar purposes. The test must add useful information for the patient, whether used with those tools or in place of them.[22][15]

An NRG/RTOG 0815 analysis examined tumor samples from 395 trial participants. Median follow-up was 10.2 years. The biomarker cohort was reported as balanced between treatment arms. This adds context to the prostate evidence. But the excerpt does not quantify the score's interaction with treatment, outcomes by risk group or performance against a rival test. It cannot show that test-guided decisions improve outcomes for every patient.[20]

Decipher delivered about 29,700 tests and $91.9 million in Q2 2026. These rose 17% and 20% year over year, respectively. The business is large and growing. The figures do not show its share of eligible patients or a head-to-head clinical edge. A newer monitoring test must now prove its place against an established rival.[1]

TrueMRD and Natera: a new contest, not an established moat

Veracyte names Natera as its primary rival in minimal residual disease testing for muscle-invasive bladder cancer. TrueMRD uses whole-genome sequencing to create a signature for each patient's cancer. It then looks for recurrence after definitive therapy. The process uses archived tumor tissue and a blood sample. The blood is collected two to four weeks after therapy. This differs from classifying a thyroid nodule or scoring prostate cancer risk.[15][23]

The sequencing method alone does not prove that TrueMRD detects recurrence better than Natera's test. Nor does it prove better outcomes. Management said TrueMRD launched in Q2 2026 with Medicare coverage. Its comparative sensitivity, commercial volume and revenue remain unquantified here. The potential edge needs clinical evidence, physician use and collections to become an established one. Payment matters across all these tests.[23][1]

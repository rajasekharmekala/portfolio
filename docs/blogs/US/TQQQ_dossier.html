1 Product Overview & Fund Structure

The ProShares UltraPro QQQ (NASDAQ: TQQQ) is the world's largest leveraged exchange-traded fund, issued by ProShares Trust and managed by ProShare Advisors LLC[1]. The fund seeks daily investment results, before fees and expenses, that correspond to 300% (3x) the daily performance of the Nasdaq-100 Index (NDX)[2]. The Nasdaq-100 includes 100 of the largest domestic and international non-financial companies listed on The Nasdaq Stock Market based on market capitalization[3].

TQQQ creates its 300% exposure by allocating fund assets into a combination of physical Nasdaq-100 constituent equities, index futures contracts (E-mini Nasdaq-100 Futures traded on the CME), and equity swap agreements with major global financial institutions (including Goldman Sachs, Citibank, Bank of America, and UBS)[4]. The fund actively rebalances its swap notional and cash collateral daily to maintain an exact 3:1 leverage ratio relative to end-of-day net assets[5].

As of September 2026, TQQQ manages approximately $23.8 billion in net assets with an average daily trading volume exceeding 45 million shares, providing exceptional liquidity, minimal bid-ask friction (one cent wide), and an active institutional options chain[6]. The fund's net expense ratio stands at 0.86% per annum, making it one of the most cost-efficient instruments for capturing high-beta upside in verified technology bull markets[7].

3 Daily Reset Mechanics, Swap Structure & Convexity

TQQQ's 3x leverage reset delivers positive compounding convexity in persistent trending bull regimes. When the underlying Nasdaq-100 trends upward with low intraday volatility, the compounding formula $\prod_{t=1}^N (1 + 3 r_t)$ produces returns that exceed 3x the benchmark's simple cumulative return[11]. For example, during sustained Stage 2 expansions (such as 2020–2021 and 2023–2024), TQQQ delivered multi-hundred percent gains that outpaced the theoretical 3x benchmark return due to daily geometric compounding[12].

Conversely, in volatile sideways regimes or sharp market corrections, daily reset compounding accelerates downside losses. In a 33.3% single-day index crash, a 3x fund mathematically faces total liquidation ($3 \times -33.33\% = -100\%$)[13]. However, SEC-mandated market-wide circuit breakers (Level 1 at 7%, Level 2 at 13%, and Level 3 at 20% S&P 500 decline) halt trading before single-day index declines can reach fatal liquidation levels, protecting fund solvency[14].

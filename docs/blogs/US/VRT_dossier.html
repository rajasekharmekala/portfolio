Chapter 1

Vertiv Holdings Co. traces its engineering roots to Liebert Corporation, founded in Columbus, Ohio in 1965 [1]. Liebert built precision air conditioning for mainframe computer rooms before Emerson Electric absorbed it into Network Power [1]. For decades inside Emerson, Network Power operated as a steady supplier of uninterruptible power supplies (UPS), thermal chillers, and server racks [1]. Its core customers were enterprise data centers and telecom closets [1]. That industrial era ended in December 2016 when Emerson divested the division to Platinum Equity for over $4 billion and named it Vertiv [3], [46]. Under private equity ownership, Vertiv carried heavy debt and ran as a fragmented group of regional plants rather than one unified global platform [3], [46].

The modern public company emerged on February 7, 2020, via a merger with GS Acquisition Holdings Corp [3]. Goldman Sachs sponsored the SPAC, and former Honeywell CEO David Cote led the transaction [3]. Cote stepped in as Executive Chairman alongside legacy CEO Rob Johnson [3]. They pitched public investors on an industrial self-help plan: apply Honeywell-style operating discipline, expand margins toward the mid-teens, and pay down Platinum Equity's debt load [3]. Barely a year after listing, however, the thesis collided with the post-pandemic supply-chain shock [4], [46].

The 2021–2022 crisis exposed a structural flaw in Vertiv's legacy commercial contracts [4], [46]. As hyperscale and colocation orders surged in 2021, Vertiv locked in multi-quarter delivery schedules at fixed prices [4], [46]. Meanwhile, copper, steel, semiconductor components, and ocean freight costs spiked sharply [4], [46]. When Q4 2021 results arrived in early 2022, full-year 2022 gross margin fell to 28.4% [4]. Free cash flow swung to negative $263.8 million as working capital ballooned [5]. Net leverage climbed to 5.80x EBITDA, triggering shareholder class-action lawsuits and intense scrutiny over Platinum Equity's secondary share sales [6], [41]. In October 2022, CEO Rob Johnson stepped down [7]. The board elevated Giordano Albertazzi, who had previously led margin turnarounds in EMEA and the Americas, to Chief Executive Officer [7].

Albertazzi and Cote executed a rapid commercial and operational reset across 2023 [7], [8]. They instituted strict price-cost governance and inserted inflation-indexing and escalation clauses into long-lead backlog [7], [8]. They also rationalized SKUs and deployed the Vertiv Operating System (VOS) across global factories [7], [8]. Operating profit jumped from $223.4 million in 2022 to $872.2 million in 2023 [4]. Free cash flow swung by more than $1 billion to positive $765.9 million [5]. Crucially, this turnaround coincided with the generative AI cluster inflection, where rack power leaped from 10–15 kW to 100–130+ kW [9]. Traditional air cooling could no longer handle that heat density [9].

Rather than relying solely on organic R&D, Vertiv deployed its cash flow into targeted capability acquisitions [10], [11]. In December 2023, it bought UK-based CoolTera Ltd. for Coolant Distribution Units (CDUs) and manifolds [10]. In August 2025, it paid $203.5 million for Great Lakes Data Racks & Cabinets and acquired Waylay NV [12], [13]. In December 2025, Vertiv closed PurgeRite for $1,003.5 million in cash plus up to $250 million in 2026 earnouts [11]. In October 2026, Vertiv added King Environmental Services in EMEA and closed UtilityInnovation Group (UIG) for $1.45 billion in cash plus up to $1.15 billion in earnouts [43], [44]. UIG moves Vertiv upstream into microgrid controls and behind-the-meter power architecture [43]. By Q3 2026, quarterly revenue hit $4,312.8 million (+61% YoY), and 9M 2026 sales reached $10,236.6 million [14], [41].

To see how Vertiv's equipment works in practice, consider three concrete end-to-end deployments across its core product and service lines:

1. Thermal Management & Liquid Cooling (CoolTera CDUs & Liebert Chillers): When a North American hyperscaler or neocloud operator deploys a 100+ kW per rack NVIDIA Blackwell GB200 or Vera Rubin cluster, air conditioning alone fails [9], [16]. Vertiv supplies the full thermal chain from roof to chip [10], [16]. Roof-mounted Liebert free-cooling chillers reject heat to the outside atmosphere [10], [16]. Indoors, Vertiv XDU/CoolTera Coolant Distribution Units (CDUs) pump, filter, and regulate ultra-pure glycol-water coolant through manifolds directly to server cold plates [10], [16]. Vertiv's engineering team co-designs the facility thermal loop and delivers factory-tested skids [11], [16]. PurgeRite technicians then mechanically flush, air-purge, and chemically stabilize the closed-loop piping before a single GPU powers on [11], [16]. Hardware is priced on an engineered project contract, usually paired with a 3-to-5-year preventive maintenance and fluid-hygiene service agreement [1], [11].

2. Critical Power Management (Trinergy UPS, Switchgear & Busways): AI training clusters draw massive, rapidly fluctuating electrical loads that can destabilize utility feeds [17]. For a multi-megawatt colocation campus in Virginia or Europe, Vertiv supplies medium-voltage switchgear, busway power distribution, and modular Trinergy UPS units [17]. These systems are engineered to ride through grid sags without dropping compute jobs [17]. Regional factories pre-integrate the units before Vertiv field engineers commission them on site [1], [17]. Customers also sign multi-year battery monitoring, thermal imaging, and 4-hour emergency SLA service contracts [1], [17].

3. Integrated Rack Solutions & Vertiv 360AI / OneCore: Enterprise and edge AI buyers, such as financial institutions or regional hospitals, often lack hyperscale internal engineering teams [18]. Vertiv packages Great Lakes heavy-duty racks, in-row cooling, rack PDUs, and Waylay predictive monitoring software into Vertiv 360AI or SmartRow reference architectures [12], [13], [18]. At larger scale, it offers prefabricated Vertiv OneCore modular blocks for Vera Rubin DSX [16], [18]. The customer buys one validated SKU bundle with predictable lead times [1], [18]. Vertiv technicians perform turnkey installation and ongoing remote diagnostics [1], [18].

Chapter 3

Vertiv operates a hybrid go-to-market model tailored to customer scale [1]. For hyperscalers, cloud providers, and major colocation operators, Vertiv uses a direct enterprise sales and solution-architect force [1], [14]. Those teams engage 12 to 24 months ahead of site commissioning to lock in technical specifications [1], [14]. For mid-market enterprise, telecom, and commercial-industrial accounts, Vertiv sells through value-added resellers (VARs), IT distributors, and electrical OEM partners [1].

Vertiv manufactures across global facilities in North America, EMEA, and APAC, yet its production footprint still trails its geographic revenue shift [1], [41]. Per the FY2025 Form 10-K, 58.8% ($6,013.6 million of $10,229.9 million) of net sales shipped to United States destinations, up from 51.0% in 2024 [1]. By contrast, Q3 2026 Form 10-Q Note 8 shows only 43.6% ($577.8 million of $1,324.6 million) of net property, plant, and equipment sits in the United States [41]. Because 56.4% of net fixed assets sit outside the United States, cross-border tariff policy remains an active cost friction [1], [41]. After the U.S. Supreme Court invalidated IEEPA tariffs in February 2026, U.S. Customs launched its CAPE refund portal in April and June 2026 [41]. Vertiv has not recognized any refund gain contingencies under ASC 450-30, and prior Mexico SAT customs audits ($10.1 million paid in 2024) underscore cross-border compliance risk [1], [41].

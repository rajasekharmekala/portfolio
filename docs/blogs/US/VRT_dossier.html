Chapter 1

Vertiv Holdings Co. traces its engineering roots to Liebert Corporation, founded in Columbus, Ohio in 1965 [1]. Liebert built precision air conditioning for mainframe computer rooms before Emerson Electric absorbed it into Network Power [1]. For decades inside Emerson, Network Power operated as a steady supplier of uninterruptible power supplies (UPS), thermal chillers, and server racks [1]. Its core customers were enterprise data centers and telecom closets [1]. That industrial era ended in December 2016 when Emerson divested the division to Platinum Equity for over $4 billion and named it Vertiv [2]. Under private equity ownership, Vertiv carried heavy debt and ran as a fragmented group of regional plants rather than one unified global platform [2].

The modern public company emerged on February 7, 2020, via a merger with GS Acquisition Holdings Corp [3]. Goldman Sachs sponsored the SPAC, and former Honeywell CEO David Cote led the transaction [3]. Cote stepped in as Executive Chairman alongside legacy CEO Rob Johnson [3]. They pitched public investors on an industrial self-help plan: apply Honeywell-style operating discipline, expand margins toward the mid-teens, and pay down Platinum Equity's debt load [3]. Barely a year after listing, however, the thesis collided with the post-pandemic supply-chain shock [2].

The 2021–2022 crisis exposed a structural flaw in Vertiv's legacy commercial contracts [2]. As hyperscale and colocation orders surged in 2021, Vertiv locked in multi-quarter delivery schedules at fixed prices [2]. Meanwhile, copper, steel, semiconductor components, and ocean freight costs spiked sharply [2]. When Q4 2021 results arrived in early 2022, full-year 2022 gross margin fell to 28.4% [4]. Free cash flow swung to negative $263.8 million as working capital ballooned [5]. Net leverage climbed to 5.80x EBITDA, triggering shareholder class-action lawsuits and intense scrutiny over Platinum Equity's secondary share sales [2], [6]. In October 2022, CEO Rob Johnson stepped down [2], [7]. The board elevated Giordano Albertazzi, who had previously led margin turnarounds in EMEA and the Americas, to Chief Executive Officer [2], [7].

Albertazzi and Cote executed a rapid commercial and operational reset across 2023 [2]. They instituted strict price-cost governance and inserted inflation-indexing and escalation clauses into long-lead backlog [7], [8]. They also rationalized SKUs and deployed the Vertiv Operating System (VOS) across global factories [7], [8]. Operating profit jumped from $223.4 million in 2022 to $872.2 million in 2023 [4]. Free cash flow swung by more than $1 billion to positive $765.9 million [5]. Crucially, this turnaround coincided with the generative AI cluster inflection, where rack power leaped from 10–15 kW to 100–130+ kW [9]. Traditional air cooling could no longer handle that heat density [9].

Rather than relying solely on organic R&D, Vertiv deployed its restored balance sheet into targeted capability acquisitions [10], [11]. In December 2023, it acquired UK-based CoolTera Ltd. for Coolant Distribution Units (CDUs), manifolds, and secondary fluid networks [10]. In August 2025, it paid roughly $200 million for Great Lakes Data Racks & Cabinets to bolster custom high-density white-space enclosures [12]. That same month, it acquired Belgium-based Waylay NV for AI-driven predictive monitoring software [13]. In December 2025, Vertiv closed an approximately $1.0 billion cash acquisition of PurgeRite, plus up to $250 million in 2026 earn-outs [11]. PurgeRite locked down specialized mechanical flushing, purging, and filtration field services for closed-loop liquid cooling [11]. By Q3 2026, quarterly revenue hit $4.31 billion (+61% year-over-year), and nine-month 2026 sales reached $10.24 billion—surpassing all of FY2025 in three quarters [14], [15].

To see how Vertiv's equipment works in practice, consider three concrete end-to-end deployments across its core product and service lines:

1. Thermal Management & Liquid Cooling (CoolTera CDUs & Liebert Chillers): When a North American hyperscaler or neocloud operator deploys a 100+ kW per rack NVIDIA Blackwell GB200 or Vera Rubin cluster, air conditioning alone fails [9], [16]. Vertiv supplies the full thermal chain from roof to chip [10], [16]. Roof-mounted Liebert free-cooling chillers reject heat to the outside atmosphere [10], [16]. Indoors, Vertiv XDU/CoolTera Coolant Distribution Units (CDUs) pump, filter, and regulate ultra-pure glycol-water coolant through manifolds directly to server cold plates [10], [16]. Vertiv's engineering team co-designs the facility thermal loop and delivers factory-tested skids [11], [16]. PurgeRite technicians then mechanically flush, air-purge, and chemically stabilize the closed-loop piping before a single GPU powers on [11], [16]. Hardware is priced on an engineered project contract, usually paired with a 3-to-5-year preventive maintenance and fluid-hygiene service agreement [1], [11].

2. Critical Power Management (Trinergy UPS, Switchgear & Busways): AI training clusters draw massive, rapidly fluctuating electrical loads that can destabilize utility feeds [17]. For a multi-megawatt colocation campus in Virginia or Europe, Vertiv supplies medium-voltage switchgear, busway power distribution, and modular Trinergy UPS units [17]. These systems are engineered to ride through grid sags without dropping compute jobs [17]. Regional factories pre-integrate the units before Vertiv field engineers commission them on site [1], [17]. Customers also sign multi-year battery monitoring, thermal imaging, and 4-hour emergency SLA service contracts [1], [17].

3. Integrated Rack Solutions & Vertiv 360AI / OneCore: Enterprise and edge AI buyers, such as financial institutions or regional hospitals, often lack hyperscale internal engineering teams [18]. Vertiv packages Great Lakes heavy-duty racks, in-row cooling, rack PDUs, and Waylay predictive monitoring software into Vertiv 360AI or SmartRow reference architectures [12], [13], [18]. At larger scale, it offers prefabricated Vertiv OneCore modular blocks for Vera Rubin DSX [16], [18]. The customer buys one validated SKU bundle with predictable lead times [1], [18]. Vertiv technicians perform turnkey installation and ongoing remote diagnostics [1], [18].

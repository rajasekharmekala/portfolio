1. What the company does

ICE owns markets and sells the data and software that surround them. Its customers are banks, asset managers, energy producers and buyers, bond investors, and mortgage lenders and servicers. It reports three businesses. Exchanges trades and clears futures and options on oil, gas, interest rates and farm goods, runs the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) and its options markets, lists companies, and sells market data and connections. Fixed Income and Data Services prices bonds, runs bond indexes, clears credit default swaps and sells a global data network. Mortgage Technology sells the loan origination, closing and servicing software that U.S. lenders use to make and manage home loans. [1][4]

Think of ICE as a toll road with three on-ramps. When customers trade, ICE earns a small fee on each contract or share. When customers pay for data, software or a listing, ICE earns a subscription that repeats. In 2025 net revenue was $9,931 million, split about 54% Exchanges, 24% Fixed Income and Data, and 21% Mortgage Technology (our calculation from the 10-K). Recurring revenue, the part that repeats at regular intervals, was $5,056 million, about 51% of net revenue. The other half rises and falls with market activity. We use a few plain measures below. Operating margin is the share of net revenue left after operating costs. Free cash flow is operating cash left after capital spending. Net debt is borrowings minus cash. [1]

One feature makes ICE's balance sheet look far larger than the company. Its clearing houses stand between the buyer and the seller of every futures contract, so members must post cash as margin, a security deposit against losses. At 2026-06-30 that customer money was $114.6 billion of the $174.2 billion in total assets. It is held for members and does not belong to ICE shareholders, so we leave it out of every debt and cash figure below. [4]

The business gains when markets are busy and worried. Energy price swings, interest-rate repricing and equity volatility all raise trading, and the contracts customers keep open (open interest, the count of contracts still outstanding) show how much risk they keep on ICE's books. Mortgage Technology is different: it depends on how many U.S. home loans are made, which depends on mortgage rates. Management calls the mix an all-weather model. Chapters 2B and 3 test that claim against the numbers. [13][4]

Two newer moves shape the case. ICE agreed on 2026-07-29 to buy MarketAxess, a bond trading platform, for $167.00 per share in cash, a price that values the equity at about $6.0 billion. It also invested about $1.64 billion in Polymarket, a prediction market: $1 billion in October 2025, $600 million in March 2026 and up to $40 million of share purchases. [9][12][18]

2. Why the business is strong or weak

ICE's strongest asset is liquidity, the ease of buying or selling without moving the price. Traders go where other traders are, so each market feeds itself. ICE says its Brent crude contract is the price barometer for more than three-quarters of the world's internationally traded crude. In the third quarter of 2026 a record 28 million Brent options contracts traded, average daily volume rose 63% year to date, and open interest in Brent options was up 18% to 4 million contracts. [16] That kind of lead is hard for a rival to copy, because a new venue must attract both sides at once.

The second strength is stickiness. Total futures and options open interest was 118.1 million contracts at 2026-06-30, up 20% in a year, with financial contracts up 46% and energy up 6%. [4] Customers who hold positions for months, margin them at ICE and run their risk systems on ICE data are unlikely to leave for a small saving. Management reports that rates open interest set a record in June and that the number of its market data users grew 10%. [13]

Third, the data business grows steadily. Fixed Income and Data Services recurring revenue was $1,956 million in 2025, up 6%, and it rose 9% in the first half of 2026. Management reports that ICE's bond index business reached a record $922 billion of fund assets, up 29%. [7][4][13] Data has high margins and few customers who cancel, so it steadies the total when trading slows.

The weaker spots are real. Mortgage Technology is the least healthy piece: 2025 recurring revenue grew only 1% ($1,574 million against $1,555 million) and the first half of 2026 grew 2%, with the adjusted operating margin flat at 41%. [7][4] Part of the issue is loan counts shrinking as mortgage lenders merge. Energy is the second weak spot in the latest quarter: energy futures revenue fell 13% in the second quarter ($518 million against $595 million) even though it was up 16% for the half. [4] Revenue from trading can fall as fast as it rose.

How ICE compares on profit margins

Margins show how much of each revenue dollar stays as operating profit. On a trailing twelve-month basis, ICE kept 51.8% of net revenue as operating income (our calculation). CME Group kept about 65.7%, Cboe about 64.1% and Nasdaq about 47.2% (our calculations). [1][4][28][29][32][33][30][31] ICE sits in the middle because its mortgage software business earns a much lower GAAP margin (GAAP means standard accounting rules) than a pure exchange does, and because ICE carries about $961 million a year of amortization from past acquisitions.

That gap is the honest answer to the question of why ICE trades at a lower multiple than CME or Cboe on trailing earnings: 22.0 times against 24.1 and 25.0 (our calculation on GAAP earnings). The market pays less for a mixed business with a large acquisition history. If ICE proves that the mortgage and data businesses earn their cost of capital, the discount should narrow. What regulators and rivals could change

ICE lists several changes in progress. The European Union's rules for clearing euro rates could pull some euro interest-rate clearing away from ICE Clear Europe. The U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) is writing rules for prediction markets, where ICE has invested $1.64 billion. The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) proposed changes to the rule that forces trades to the best quoted price, which could change how equity venues compete. [4][18] None of these has a number attached yet, so we treat them as risks to watch and not as costs in our estimates.

On the competitive side, CME is the main rival in rates and has been growing revenue about 9% in 2026. In bond trading, MarketAxess, which ICE is buying, reported on 2026-10-23 flat third-quarter commission revenue and a 16% fall in third-quarter EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization). [28][25] ICE is therefore paying a full price for a franchise whose growth has slowed, which is the main question in the acquisition.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

An estimate is only as good as its weakest assumption. For each of our estimates we name that assumption, the evidence that would show it wrong, and the date we will know. We then build the strongest bear case against our own conclusion and name the single claim on which the whole case hangs.

The weakest assumption in each estimate

Free cash flow of $4,641 million assumes that the first-half tax refunds and the timing of Section 31 fees do not flatter it. ICE's own adjusted figure is higher, but the 10-Q credits part of the first-half improvement to tax refunds and to Section 31 timing [4]. The evidence would be a second-half cash flow far below the first half, and we will know when the 2026 10-K is filed in February 2027 (the 2025 filing came on 2026-02-05 [1]).

The 8.7% return on invested capital assumes that invested capital is measured fairly. It would look better if goodwill from old deals were ignored, but those deals are the reason the business exists, so we do not. The pro forma leverage of 3.28 times assumes that MarketAxess earns about $438 million before synergies. Its third-quarter EBITDA fell 16%, so the true figure at closing could be lower, and leverage higher [25][12]. We will know from MarketAxess's results and the closing date, expected in the first half of 2027.

The Reverse DCF of 4.83% assumes that the 18 times exit multiple and the 10% discount rate are fair for an exchange. They are our fixed settings, so the number is for comparison with growth, not a price forecast. The base value assumes 2027 EPS of $8.88 and a 21.0 times multiple. The weakest part is the EPS, because consensus relies on a second half that is 15% above the previous year after a second quarter that grew 5%. The third-quarter report on 2026-10-29 is the first test [21][4].

Bear case 1: the volume was borrowed from a volatile year

Total volume in September was up 51% and in the third quarter up 31%, but that rested on rate repricing and oil swings that may calm [15]. In the second quarter, energy revenue fell 13% even though open interest rose, which shows that open interest does not guarantee fees [4]. The evidence would be energy revenue below $570 million in the third quarter and a lower 2027 expense and revenue outlook in February.

Bear case 2: the MarketAxess deal costs more than it earns

ICE is paying 10.6 times EBITDA that already includes $100 million of synergies, for a platform whose commission revenue was flat in the third quarter and whose EBITDA fell 16% [12][25]. It bid $175 and agreed on $167, so it did not overpay on the first offer, but the target's own projections show only $440 million of EBITDA after share pay in 2026 [26]. If the synergies slip, accretion is near zero, and the $6.0 billion of debt keeps leverage above 3.3 times into 2028. ICE would also owe a $327.4 million regulatory termination fee in specified regulatory circumstances, and the outside date is 2027-07-29 with two possible six-month extensions [9]. The MarketAxess stockholder vote is on 2026-10-29, a day on which three lawsuits and 15 demand letters are pending [27][26]. The evidence would be a failed vote, a regulatory delay, or ICE's leverage ratio staying at or above 3.0 times for a year after closing.

Bear case 3: a rule change or a rival takes away part of ICE's edge

1. What the company does

ICE runs three businesses. Exchanges operates regulated markets where customers trade and clear futures, options and stocks, and it sells the data and connections that come with those markets. Its flagship Brent crude oil contract is the cornerstone of a global oil network that includes more than 800 related crude and refined products, and it offers natural gas benchmarks across North America, Europe and Asia.[1] The segment also holds the New York Stock Exchange listings business, which earned $129 million in the second quarter of 2026.[8] In 2025 Exchanges produced $5,411 million of net revenue, which is revenue after the fees ICE passes through to regulators and liquidity providers.[10]

Fixed Income and Data Services sells bond pricing, indices and analytics, runs clearing for credit default swaps, and sells data delivery technology. It earned $2,419 million in 2025, and $1,956 million of that was recurring, meaning subscription-style revenue that renews.[10] Mortgage Technology sells software that US lenders use to start, close and service home loans, plus mortgage data. It earned $2,101 million in 2025, which is 21% of ICE's net revenue.[1] ICE completed the Black Knight acquisition on September 5, 2023, and the cash paid for acquisitions that year was $10,198 million. The mortgage segment's revenue jumped in the first full year after that deal, from $1,317 million in 2023 to $2,022 million in 2024.[3][12][1]

Recurring revenue was $5,056 million of the $9,931 million total in 2025, or 50.9% by our calculation, and the rest was transaction revenue of $4,875 million that rises and falls with trading and lending activity.[10] The second quarter of 2026 looks the same: recurring revenue was $1,353 million of $2,666 million, or 50.8%.[8] This mix matters because half of the revenue arrives whether or not markets are busy, while the other half gives the earnings their upside in volatile quarters.

ICE's clearing houses also hold customers' margin deposits, which are cash and collateral that members post to cover the risk of their open trades. The balance was $114,599 million at June 30, 2026 against $76,789 million at December 31, 2025, and it sits on both sides of the balance sheet because it belongs to members.[8] Changes in these deposits are booked in financing activities, not operating cash flow, so they do not inflate the cash flow we use later.[1] The only part ICE keeps is the interest and fees earned on the deposits, which was $66 million in Exchanges and $54 million in Fixed Income and Data Services in the first half of 2026.[5]

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The strength starts with network effects. A trader who hedges oil on Brent needs the deepest pool of other traders, and the pool is already there: the flagship contract anchors more than 800 related products, and gas benchmarks cover three continents.[1] The financials show how well this earns. Exchanges kept a 74% operating margin in 2025 on $5,411 million of net revenue, and its data and connectivity revenue grew 12% in the second quarter of 2026 to $287 million.[10][8]

Activity data point the same way. Open interest across all futures and options reached 118.1 million contracts at June 30, 2026, up 20% from 98.6 million a year earlier, and energy open interest was 67.6 million, up 6%.[5] In the September 2026 statistics ICE reported total average daily volume up 51% from a year earlier and open interest up 13%, with record futures open interest of 17.5 million lots in financial products on September 10.[20] Average daily volume means contracts traded per trading day. For the third quarter as a whole, energy volume was up 18% and Brent volume was up 38%, and ICE reported a new quarterly volume record for Brent options.[20] That is a strong set-up for the October 29 report, but it is volume, and revenue also depends on the fee ICE earns per contract.

The weaknesses are specific. Energy revenue is lumpy: it rose 46% in the first quarter of 2026 to $814 million and then fell 13% in the second quarter to $518 million.[9][8] ICE's own explanation is that oil volume rose after the outbreak of the U.S.-Iran conflict, which mainly affected the first quarter, and then fell as that shock faded.[5] Mortgage Technology grew only 5% in the second quarter and carries an operating margin of 8% on a GAAP basis, though 43% on an adjusted basis, because acquisition amortization is large.[8]

Three outside pressures deserve a plain statement. First, mortgage volume depends on loan rates, and the 30-year fixed rate was 7.32% for the week ending October 22, 2026, against 6.19% a year earlier.[28] ICE's 10-K says higher mortgage rates cut demand for mortgages and hurt transaction revenue in this segment.[1] Second, bourse stocks were hit this year by fears that perpetual futures, which are contracts with no expiry date, could pull trading away from traditional exchanges, and ICE shares were down nearly 5% for 2026 at July 30.[29] Third, regulation: ICE Futures U.S. is regulated by the CFTC, ICE Futures Europe by the Financial Conduct Authority, and ICE Endex by Dutch authorities, and the 10-K flags EU proposals that could regulate prices and fees for data providers.[1] The 10-K text we fetched does not name FERC, the US energy regulator, so we mark that item as not found.

Where the evidence is thin

We did not find a filing sentence that gives ICE's share of Brent or natural gas trading, so we make no market-share claim. We also have no cited measure of how much of the 2026 volume gain comes from customers who joined after the Iran conflict began. The practical effect is that the energy franchise looks durable on open interest, which is the stock of positions customers hold, but we cannot size the part of the second-quarter fall that is permanent.

3. The numbers, in plain words

Over five years ICE grew net revenue from $7,146 million to $9,931 million and operating income from $3,449 million to $4,929 million, a rise in operating margin from 48.3% to 49.6% even after absorbing the lower-margin mortgage business.[3][1] The growth is a mix of acquisition and organic gains. Earnings quality needs a caution. Cash conversion is the best part of the story. Cash flow from operations is the cash the core business collects, and free cash flow is what remains after capital spending. Here is the bear case, stated as strongly as the evidence allows. ICE trades at 21.2 times trailing adjusted EPS. The exchange segment earns most of the group's profit. Its energy line is the most volatile part. Energy revenue was $814 million in the first quarter of 2026 and $518 million in the second. Fatal flaw 2: the MarketAxess debt arrives into a hostile rate market

ICE has already issued $3.75 billion of notes at 4.700% to 5.400%. It has also arranged a $2.0 billion term loan. We estimate about $187 million a year of new interest on the notes alone, from the four coupons.[17][18] If the purchase is delayed or blocked, ICE would carry that debt without the MarketAxess earnings. If the deal closes on time and energy volumes fall, net debt to EBITDA could stay near 3.3x for longer than the 18 to 24 months the company has targeted.[19]

Before you read

Datadog sells software that warns engineers when their apps or cloud servers break, and its revenue rose 36% in the June 2026 quarter.[4] We reject it for the shortlist today because it fails three hard tests: pay in shares is 21.9% of revenue against a 5% limit, the return on invested capital is under 3% against a 12% floor, and the payoff ratio at the current price is 0.04 against a floor of 3.5.[1][15] The price also sits 7.63 daily ranges above its nearest support, so a buy now would chase a rally. We would change our mind if growth holds above 30% and pay in shares falls fast.

Terms used in this report

  • RSI (relative strength index) is a 0-to-100 gauge of how fast the price has risen lately.
  • ATR (average true range) is the normal daily price swing in dollars.
  • EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) is a rough measure of operating profit before those costs.
  • ROIC (return on invested capital) is the yearly profit earned on the money put into the business.
  • EPS (earnings per share) is profit divided by the number of shares.
  • FCF (free cash flow) is cash from operations minus capital spending and capitalized software; it is the cash left for owners.
  • SBC (stock-based compensation) is employee pay in shares, a real cost to owners.
  • ARR (annual recurring revenue) is the yearly value of subscriptions in force today.
  • NRR (net revenue retention) compares this year's revenue from last year's customers with what they paid before.
  • RPO (remaining performance obligations) is contracted revenue not yet delivered.
  • 20-day moving average is the average close of the last 20 sessions; the 50-day moving average and the 200-day moving average are the same over 50 and 200 sessions.
  • Stage 2 is the advancing phase of a stock's four-phase life cycle, with price above a rising 200-day average.
  • Base is a rest period in price before the next move.
  • Pivot is the price level that ends a base. A breakout is a close above that level on heavy volume.
  • Entry zone is the price range where we would be willing to buy.
  • Limit order is an order that buys only at a set price or lower.
  • Stop loss is a pre-set exit price that caps the loss.
  • Asymmetry is the possible gain divided by the possible loss; reward-to-risk means the same thing.
  • Gross margin is the share of revenue left after direct costs.
  • Net debt is borrowings minus cash.
  • Price-to-earnings is the share price divided by earnings per share.
  • Guidance is the forecast a company gives for its next quarter or year.
  • Dilution is the fall in each owner's share of the company when new shares are issued.
  • Buyback is a company's purchase of its own shares.

What the company does

Datadog runs a software platform that collects signals from a customer's servers, applications, databases, logs and security tools, and shows them on one screen so engineers can find and fix problems quickly. At 2025-12-31 it had about 32,700 customers in over 160 countries, up from about 30,000 a year earlier, and about 603 customers paying at least $1.0 million of ARR (annual recurring revenue, meaning the yearly value of subscriptions in force today), up from 462.[1] Customers pay a subscription plus usage-based fees: the more data a customer sends and the more products it turns on, the higher the bill. That structure explains both the fast growth and the main risk, because a customer can cut usage quickly.

The company competes product by product. In application performance monitoring its filings name Cisco, New Relic and Dynatrace; in log management, Cisco and Elastic; in cloud monitoring, the built-in tools of Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud.[1] Revenue concentration is the part new readers miss: the filing says an "AI-native" group of customers, which includes the largest customer, added about seven percentage points to year-over-year growth in the December 2025 quarter and high single digit points in the June 2026 quarter.[1][4] In the same 10-Q the company says it saw a reduction in usage from its largest customer starting in the third quarter of 2026, which may slow revenue growth.[4]

Control sits with a small group. Class B shares carry ten votes each against one for Class A, and the 2026 annual meeting re-elected Olivier Pomel, the chief executive, with 503,784,971 votes for.[11][10] The board grew from ten to eleven members on 2026-02-26 with the appointment of Dominic Phillips.[12] Shareholders also approved moving the legal home from Delaware to Nevada at a 2026-04-21 special meeting.[13] The company employs about 8,100 people.[16] At 2026-07-31 the company had 334,904,614 Class A and 24,170,410 Class B shares outstanding.[4]

Why the business is strong or weak

The business is strong on growth and cash, and weak on what it costs shareholders to produce that growth. Revenue was $1,121.5 million in the June 2026 quarter, up 36% from $826.8 million, and the company guides to $4.45 to $4.47 billion for fiscal 2026.[4][7] Cash from operations was $315.9 million in the quarter, and free cash flow for the first half was $567.8 million.[4] Cash and marketable securities were about $4.99 billion against $985.5 million of convertible notes due 2029, a net cash position of about $3.71 billion.[4][15]

The weak points are three. First, stock-based compensation was $750.7 million in fiscal 2025, which is 21.9% of revenue, and 19.6% of revenue in the first half of 2026.[1][4] Second, GAAP operating income, which counts that pay as a cost, was a loss of $44.4 million in fiscal 2025 and only $5.5 million in the June 2026 quarter.[1][4] Third, the 10-Q describes a usage cut by the largest customer that began in the third quarter of 2026, while the stock jumped 32% in a month.[4][25] The tiering below applies our software indicators to the latest filings; the scores are informational because the company already failed Layer 1.

Why these estimates might be wrong

The weakest assumption in all three cases is that fiscal 2027 EPS is close to $2.96 and that investors keep paying a high multiple for it. Three scenarios would break that, each with a date or a number that would show it early.

Fatal flaw 1: the largest customer cuts further

The June 2026 10-Q says usage from the largest customer fell starting in the third quarter and that this may slow revenue growth, and that the AI-native group added high single digit points of growth.[4] If that group's spend is cut by half, growth could fall from 36% to the mid-20s within two quarters. The evidence arrives with third-quarter results, which StockAnalysis estimates for 2026-11-05 before the open; the company's own date is unconfirmed.[15] The third-quarter guidance was revenue of $1.135B to $1.145B, only about 1% above the second quarter's $1.121B, which already carries a hint of slowing.[7]

Fatal flaw 2: the multiple compresses even if results are good

At 110.55 times forward earnings and 26.55 times sales, the stock is priced for growth that stays above 30%.[15] Dynatrace trades at 32.21 times forward earnings with 8.90% ROIC, so a re-rating toward even 60 times (our bear multiple) cuts the price by roughly 40% with no change in the business.[17] Ten-year Treasury yields at 5.22% make long-duration growth stocks more sensitive to this risk.

Fatal flaw 3: the share-pay drag keeps free cash flow from reaching owners

Free cash flow of $914.7 million in fiscal 2025 sounds comfortable, but stock pay was $750.7 million. If the company chose to buy back shares to offset dilution, free cash flow after that spending would be about $164 million, or 4.8% of revenue.[1] The 1.66% annual rise in share count shows owners are already paying the cost.[15]

One more error source is the data itself: our ROIC is a published trailing figure (2.91%, Finviz 3.17%), not a number we computed, because GAAP operating profit is close to zero and any invested-capital computation would be dominated by the $4.99 billion cash pile.[15][28] The next test of all of these is the third-quarter report and the 10-Q that follows it.

If third-quarter revenue growth is 33% or higher and the company says the largest customer's usage has stabilized, raise the bull probability from 20% to 30% and re-run the gates; if growth is below 30%, lower the base-case multiple to 80 and re-test.

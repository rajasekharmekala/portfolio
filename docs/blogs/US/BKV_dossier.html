1. What the company does

BKV Corporation, based in Denver, Colorado, produces natural gas and natural gas liquids from the Barnett Shale in north Texas and from a smaller position in northeast Pennsylvania. It also owns a gas-fired power business near Temple, Texas, and a set of carbon capture projects.[1][4] The company calls itself the largest natural gas producer by gross operated volume in the Barnett Shale and one of the top 15 gas-weighted producers in the United States.[1] It went public in 2024, and its controlling shareholder is Banpu, a Thai energy group that indirectly owned about 67.6% of the shares on February 27, 2026.[3][4] In the second quarter of 2026 it produced 978.3 million cubic feet of gas equivalent a day, up from 811.0 a year earlier.[1]

Gas is measured in thousand cubic feet, written Mcf. BKV also sells natural gas liquids (NGLs, such as ethane and propane) and a little oil, so it converts everything to gas equivalent: Mcfe is a thousand cubic feet equivalent, MMcfe a million and Bcfe a billion. Who pays BKV? A small group of buyers. The 10-Q says the company sells the significant majority of its gas, liquids and oil to fewer than five customers. In the second quarter three customers made up 31%, 14% and 10% of revenue from contracts with customers, and at December 31, 2025 one customer made up 62% of that kind of receivable.[2] Until the second quarter a third party marketed all of its gas, and the company now says it has moved to market all of its production itself.[4][1] Gas sells at market prices, so BKV is a price taker.

The benchmark is Henry Hub, the Louisiana pipeline hub whose price sets the US reference for natural gas, quoted in dollars per million British thermal units (MMBtu). BKV's gas sells below it because the gas must be moved to buyers. In the second quarter Henry Hub averaged $2.90 and BKV's gas sold for $2.14 before hedges, a gap of $0.76. A year earlier the gap was $0.77, and for 2025 as a whole it was $0.65.[1][3] Sales of gas, liquids and oil were $221.9 million in the quarter: gas $156.1 million, NGLs $61.0 million and oil $4.9 million. Pennsylvania supplied $16.0 million of that, about 7%, so this is mainly a Barnett company.[2]

Each unit of gas carries a price and a cost. In the second quarter BKV's upstream sales came to $2.49 per Mcfe before hedges (our arithmetic: $221.9 million over 89.0 Bcfe of production). Its cash operating cost was $1.39: $0.50 for lease operating and workover, $0.13 for taxes and $0.76 for gathering and transport. A year earlier the total was $1.49.[1] Realized gains on gas and liquids hedges added about $0.42 per Mcfe, from $31.6 million on gas and $5.9 million on liquids (our arithmetic).[2] The upstream segment also paid $14.6 million of net interest, and general and administrative cost of $0.20 to $0.25 per Mcfe is guided on top of the $1.39.[1] Chapter 3 turns these figures into a break-even gas price.

Two newer businesses sit beside the gas wells. The first is power. BKV owns 75% of the BKV-BPP Power joint venture, which runs the Temple I and Temple II combined-cycle plants with about 1.5 gigawatts of capacity in the Texas grid operated by ERCOT. It raised its stake from 50% on January 30, 2026 by paying an affiliate of Banpu $115.1 million in cash and 5,315,390 shares, and it now consolidates the venture.[2][32] The plants earn the spread between the power price and the gas cost: in the second quarter the average spark spread was $22.31 per megawatt hour against $24.27 a year earlier, and the Power segment's operating income was $5.8 million against $29.7 million.[1] Management is negotiating a power purchase agreement (PPA), which is a long-term contract to sell electricity at an agreed price, for part of Temple's output, and says it is in discussions with a select group of counterparties.[1] The second is carbon capture. BKV injects carbon dioxide underground for fees and for federal Section 45Q tax credits, which brought in $3.0 million in the quarter. The Barnett Zero project has sequestered about 375,800 metric tons since 2023, and two more projects started in the second quarter.[1][2]

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Stage 2 and Stage 4 are labels from the Weinstein stage scale: Stage 2 is a confirmed uptrend, and Stage 4 is a confirmed downtrend with the price under a falling 50-day and a falling 200-day average.
  • RSI stands for the relative strength index - a 0 to 100 meter of how fast the price has moved, where under 30 is called oversold and over 70 overbought. ATR stands for average true range - the typical daily price swing in dollars.
  • A base is a sideways trading range that a price builds before a move, and a pivot is the top of it. In this report the 63-day low is the floor of the base.
  • A stop loss is the price at which the thesis is wrong and a position is cut. A limit order is an order that fills only at your price or better, and the entry zone is the band of prices where buying is planned.
  • Asymmetry, also called reward-to-risk, is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop, measured from the entry price. The method's floor is 2.0 to 1 for a core holding and 2.5 to 1 for a tactical or turnaround idea.
  • EPS means earnings per share, which is profit divided by the share count. The price-to-earnings ratio is the share price divided by earnings per share. A consensus estimate is the average of analysts' forecasts for a number.
  • EBITDA stands for earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization - profit before those accounting and financing charges.
  • FCF stands for free cash flow, which is cash from operations minus spending on assets. Cash flow from operations is the cash the business itself generates before financing, and capital expenditure is spending on wells, plants and equipment.
  • ROIC stands for return on invested capital - after-tax operating profit divided by the money tied up in the business (equity plus debt minus cash). Operating margin is operating profit as a share of revenue.
  • Net debt is borrowings minus cash. Debt-to-equity is borrowings divided by shareholders' money. Working capital is current assets minus current liabilities, the cash tied up in day-to-day operations.
  • SBC stands for stock-based compensation - the cost of paying employees in shares. Dilution is the rise in the share count when new shares are issued, and a buyback is the company buying its own shares back.
  • Guidance is the company's own published forecast for a coming period. A hedge is a contract that fixes or brackets a future price, such as a swap (a fixed price for a floating one), a collar (a floor and a ceiling) or a put (a floor).

2. Why the business is strong or weak

BKV's strength is a long, low-decline gas position in the Barnett and the ability to buy more of it. Proved reserves were 5,920,986 MMcfe at December 31, 2025, of which 3,899,227 MMcfe, or 66%, were already producing, and 1,713,878 MMcfe, or 29%, were undeveloped.[4][5] Against 2025 production of 304,975 MMcfe that is a reserve life of 19.4 years, and against the second quarter's annualized 356 Bcfe it is 16.6 years (our arithmetic).[4][1] The reserves grew from 3,131,909 MMcfe a year earlier partly because the gas price used for the report rose from $2.13 to $3.39 per MMBtu and partly because BKV bought 742,978 MMcfe in the Bedrock deal.[4] The company paid $266.5 million in cash and issued shares worth $124.3 million for Bedrock, and it has now added another Barnett package in September 2026.[4][3][33][15]

The second strength is cost. Cash operating cost per Mcfe fell from $1.49 to $1.39 year on year in the second quarter, and management says drilling and completion changes and offset-well effects in the Barnett raised output while development capital stayed below the midpoint of guidance. It also reports that one Upper Barnett well de-risked about half of its Upper Barnett locations and cut the expected break-even gas price from $3.75 to $3.25 per MMBtu.[1] That break-even is the company's own claim and we could not test it. Output beat the top of the second-quarter guide of 925 to 975 MMcfe/d, and the fourth-quarter 2025 figure of 939.7 also beat its 885 to 935 range.[1][7][6][8]

The weaknesses are price, cash and control. BKV cannot set its price, and its gas sells further below Henry Hub than it did, at a $0.76 gap in the second quarter and a guided $0.90 to $1.00 for the full year, against $0.65 in 2025.[1][3] The business is also spending more than it earns. Operating cash flow was $181.7 million in the first half, cash capital spending $193.4 million, and BKV also paid $158.5 million of deposits on power equipment.[2][1] Banpu holds about two thirds of the shares, so minority holders have limited say, and several of its ventures involve Banpu affiliates, including the 25% power stake that it bought from one of them in January 2026.[4][2]

The integrated plan is the part we cannot yet price. BKV wants to produce gas, burn it in its own power plants and bury the carbon dioxide, which management calls a closed loop. So far the evidence is thin. The power segment earned $5.8 million of operating income in the second quarter against $29.7 million, no long-term PPA has been signed although an advisor had been engaged by February 2026, and the carbon capture business has reached about 375,800 metric tons in total, against a stated target of 1.5 million tons a year of injection by 2028.[1][6] That does not mean the plan fails. It means the stock price rests on gas wells that cover their costs, and on power and carbon capture options that still need a customer and funding.

Where the position is strong

Three facts carry the strong case. The reserves are mostly producing (66% proved developed producing) and the reserve report values them at a PV-10 of $2,788 million at SEC prices, up from $672 million, where PV-10 is the present value of future net revenue discounted at 10% before tax.[4] Hedges are large and priced above the current market: the June 30 book has 98.96 million MMBtu of 2027 swaps at $3.99, 37.66 million MMBtu of 2027 collars with a $3.57 floor and a $4.00 ceiling, and 36.5 million MMBtu of 2027 puts at $3.00, against an EIA forecast of $3.16 for 2027.[2][30][31] And liquidity was $836.7 million at June 30 before the convertible notes.[1]

Where the position is weak

The weak points are leverage that has risen, funding that depends on capital markets, and a thin analyst base. Net leverage on the company's measure was 0.65 times at the end of 2024, 0.92 times at the end of 2025 and 1.78 times at June 30, 2026, and the first two numbers excluded the power debt that was consolidated from the first quarter of 2026.[10][6][1] The lenders have loosened the leverage thresholds for dividends, buybacks and investments twice, from 1.75 to 2.00 times in October 2025 and from 2.00 to 2.25 times in May 2026.[19][18] Share count rose from 84.6 million at the end of 2024 to 109.4 million at June 30, 2026, a rise of 29% in 18 months.[3][2] Debt now includes $500 million of 7.50% senior notes due 2030 and the new 1.625% convertible notes, whose low coupon is paid for with $64.7 million of capped calls and the option of 18.0 million new shares at $31.93.[4][33][13] Finally, the 2026 per-share estimate on the consensus page rests on two analysts.[29]

Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

A good test of management is to compare what it said one or two years ago with what happened. On the operating promises BKV's record is good. It guided 2025 capital spending to $320 million to $380 million in February 2025, cut the range to $290 million to $350 million in August, and spent $318.5 million. It also beat its fourth-quarter 2025 production range and its second-quarter 2026 production range.[10][9][6][8][7][1]

On the financial promises the record is weaker. In February 2025 management set a long-term net leverage target of 1.0 to 1.5 times. The company ended 2024 at 0.65 times, 2025 at 0.92 times on the narrower basis, and reported 2.02 times at March 31, 2026 and 1.78 times at June 30 after it consolidated the power joint venture.[10][6][7][1] The 2026 capital plan was raised twice, from $410 million to $560 million in February to $570 million to $740 million in May and $690 million to $875 million in August, and the increase is almost all power.[6][7][1] The full-year gas discount was guided at $0.65 to $0.85 until August, when it became $0.90 to $1.00.[6][1]

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate above rests on assumptions that can fail. We list the three we think are weakest, say what evidence would show each is wrong and give the date we expect to know. This is our red-team case, the strongest honest argument against the central value and against buying. Because the verdict is already REJECTED, these flaws also explain what would have to change before we looked again.

Fatal flaw 1: the funding gap forces more shares or more debt

The weakest assumption is that BKV's roughly $230 million of sustaining cash flow is what owners keep. The company plans $690 million to $875 million of capital spending in 2026, the midpoint of which, $782.5 million, less $135 million of partner contributions is $647.5 million, against operating cash flow that we estimate near $462 million for the year (the first half's $226.9 million before working capital plus twice the second quarter's $117.6 million), a gap of about $185 million before the undisclosed Barnett price.[1] The company has funded 2026 so far with $185.5 million of equity in the first half and about $455 million of retained convertible proceeds, and the revolver had $684.5 million of room at June 30 before the Barnett borrowing.[2][13][1] The turbine contract adds $720 million of payments after the $80 million fee credit through December 2028, of which about $200 million including the credit falls due by March 31, 2027 and about 90% of that is backstopped.[16] Lenders have raised their leverage thresholds twice in seven months, and the convertible can add 18.0 million shares, which suggests the funding need is already pressing on the structure.[19][18][13]

The evidence that would show this wrong is a third-quarter report in which company net leverage, pro forma for the Barnett purchase, stays at or below 2.25 times (the level of the latest bank basket), the 2026 capital range does not rise above $875 million, and no new equity is sold below the current price. We expect to know on November 5, 2026, and again when the 2027 plan comes with the fourth-quarter report, which we expect in February or March 2027 (the date is not announced; the 2025 annual results came out on February 25, 2026).[26][6]

Fatal flaw 2: the power business does not turn into contracted cash flow

The second weak point is the power contract itself. The equity story and the spending both lean on one arriving. Power operating income was $5.8 million in the second quarter against $29.7 million a year earlier, the spark spread narrowed to $22.31 from $24.27, and wholesale prices of $41.59 per megawatt hour were below management's expectation.[1] First-half Power Adjusted EBITDAX of $51.9 million compares with a full-year guide of $135 million to $175 million, so the second half must supply $83 million to $123 million, which depends on summer demand.[1] An advisor had been engaged by February 2026 and no contract has been announced, although in October a hyperscaler agreed to reimburse about 90% of equipment payments due by March 31, 2027 and is the intended customer.[6][17] The turbine contract can be ended by BKV if no offtake is agreed by March 31, 2027, and the 8-K does not say whether the $80.0 million reservation fee already paid is refundable, so we treat it as at risk.[16]

A signed offtake agreement with disclosed price, term, start date and funding would be evidence against this flaw. A decision to keep buying turbines without an agreement would not be, because it only raises the exposure. The date passing with no agreement, or a termination notice, would confirm the flaw. We expect updates on the November 5 call and a firm answer by March 31, 2027.

Fatal flaw 3: 2027 earnings and the gas price are lower than the Street assumes

The third weak point is the earnings estimate. Consensus has 2027 earnings per share rising 38% to $2.14 while the EIA forecasts a 9% lower Henry Hub price than in 2026.[29][30] The 2026 estimate rests on two analysts and the count for 2027 is not published, the 2026 estimate was cut 8.47% in 30 days, and BKV's gas discount has widened to $0.76 with $0.90 to $1.00 guided for the year.[29][34][1] About 53% of 2027 gas is not covered by swaps or collars, and each $0.10 per MMBtu on that volume moves earnings by about $0.11 a share (our arithmetic: 154.5 million MMBtu, 23% tax, 108.0 million shares).[2] The hedge book also thins after 2027, with 94.1 million MMBtu of swaps in 2028 at $3.79 and 35.6 million in 2029 at $3.60.[2]

We would treat the estimate as wrong if we saw consensus 2027 earnings below $2.00, a third-quarter gas discount outside the guided $0.70 to $0.80 range, or the EIA moving its 2027 Henry Hub forecast below $3.00. We expect to know on November 5, from the EIA's next outlook on November 10, 2026, and from the fourth-quarter report.

What the company does

A supplier of data-moving chips

Marvell is a fabless chip company, meaning it designs semiconductors and pays outside factories to make them. Its chips connect, move, store and process data inside cloud data centers, carrier networks and enterprise equipment.[2] In the fiscal year ended January 31, 2026 Marvell reported $8.19 billion of revenue, of which $6.10 billion (74%) came from data center and $2.09 billion (26%) from communications and other markets.[2] A year earlier data center was 72% of a much smaller $5.77 billion, and two years earlier only 40%.[2][3] The company has changed what it is in about two years.

The two ways Marvell earns money from AI

The first is interconnect, the chips and optical modules that carry signals between servers, racks and buildings. The second is custom silicon, chips built to one customer's design, such as an AI accelerator for a cloud giant. At its October 6, 2026 Investor Day, management said custom products brought in about $1.5 billion in fiscal 2026 and are shipping to all four large U.S. cloud companies.[16] Management also said its addressable market could reach roughly $400 billion by 2030, a company estimate that this report cannot verify independently.[16]

Who buys

Sales are concentrated. In the quarter ended August 1, 2026 one distributor accounted for 44% of net revenue and one direct customer for 16%.[4] A distributor is a reseller that sits between Marvell and the end buyer, so the 44% does not name the end customer, but it still means a few relationships carry most of the sales. By destination of shipment, China was 36% of fiscal 2026 revenue and Taiwan 20%, though the filing warns these places are not where the final customer sits.[2]

Recent deals that changed the share count

Three deals matter for an owner. In March 2026 NVIDIA bought $2.0 billion of Series A convertible preferred stock that converts into about 21.8 million common shares at roughly $91.84 each and shares in dividends on an as-converted basis.[10] On August 18, 2026 Marvell issued Google a warrant, a right to buy shares at a fixed price, for 58,970,907 shares at $206.58, expiring in 2033; only 1,360,867 vest on a calendar basis and the rest vest as Google-linked custom revenue accumulates in $500 million steps.[9] And Marvell bought Celestial AI, an optical-interconnect start-up, with an earnout of up to 24.4 million more shares if revenue milestones are met through fiscal 2029.[4]

Plain-English glossary

ROIC (return on invested capital) (return on invested capital) is after-tax operating profit divided by the money tied up in the business. SBC (stock-based compensation) (stock-based compensation) is pay handed out in shares, which dilutes existing owners. FCF (free cash flow) (free cash flow) is operating cash flow minus capital spending. Asymmetry is the possible gain divided by the possible loss on a trade.

Why the business is strong or weak

The underwriting rule

The pipeline judges every name in three layers. Layer 1 is a financial floor: ROIC at or above 12%, free cash flow margin at or above 12%, net debt under 3 times EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization), stock-based pay under 5% of revenue, and, for a tactical name, a Stage 2 chart and an asymmetry at or above 3.5 to 1 against a target anchored to earnings, a cash-flow model or a measured chart move.[26][27] A single failure at Layer 1 ends the review as REJECTED, whatever the chart looks like.

Why stock-based pay is the sharper problem

Stock-based pay was 10.3%, 9.3%, 11.1%, 10.4% and 7.2% of revenue in fiscal 2022 through fiscal 2026, so it has never been under 5% in the five years on record.[14] It was $326.2M in the second quarter of fiscal 2027, 11.9% of revenue, and guidance for the third quarter implies about $310M on $3.15B of revenue, or 9.8%.[7] The percentage is falling because revenue is rising faster than pay, but it remains almost twice the ceiling.

Debt and cash

At August 1, 2026 Marvell held $3,932.8M of cash against $4,962.9M of debt, a net debt of about $1.03 billion.[4] In April 2026 it issued $1 billion of 5.30% notes due 2036 to refinance earlier debt.[13] The company also has $8.52 billion of foundry and test purchase commitments, a large promise to chip makers that becomes a burden if demand slows.[4]

Why these estimates might be wrong

Where the rejection could be too harsh

The two failing gates use GAAP figures, which count acquisition amortization of $892.7M TTM and share-based pay. Marvell's non-GAAP operating margin was 36.6% in the second quarter.[7] If revenue reaches the $20 billion plan, GAAP return on capital would likely clear 12% within two years even with the same costs. The stock-pay ratio is also falling: from 11.9% of revenue in the second quarter to about 9.8% implied in the third.

Where the bullish story could be too generous

Sales depend on a distributor with 44% of revenue and a direct customer with 16%.[4] The Google warrant ties up to 58.97 million shares to as much as 240 tranches of $500 million of revenue, which Reuters reads as up to $120 billion of revenue.[15] A deal like that can also signal that Marvell is paying in equity to win business. The $8.52 billion of foundry commitments and the 36% China revenue share add supply and policy risk.[4][2]

Gaps in this report

Filing lists were checked on SEC EDGAR for new insider or ownership filings, and none was analysed in detail.[17][19] We did not verify customer names behind the distributor, insider selling, a three-year median P/E, fourth-quarter guidance or the date of the next earnings call. Where a figure was unavailable we wrote N/A instead of guessing. The FY2031 targets come from a single news summary of the company event, not the company's press release.[16][18]

The hinge claim

If one claim fails, the whole case changes: that GAAP ROIC and stock-pay ratios will improve as revenue scales rather than stay stuck as new deals add share issuance. A third-quarter stock-pay ratio near 9.8% with new warrant costs would show the claim is too optimistic.

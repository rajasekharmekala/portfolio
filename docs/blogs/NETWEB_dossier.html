Executive Summary & Investment Pitch

Netweb Technologies India Limited (NSE: NETWEB, BSE: 543945) represents India's preeminent domestic original equipment manufacturer (OEM) and architectural integrator of High-End Computing Solutions (HCS), spanning high-performance computing (HPC) supercomputing clusters, artificial intelligence (AI) GPU server systems, enterprise private cloud hyperconverged infrastructure (HCI), and high-throughput data center storage systems[1]. As sovereign nations and global enterprises pivot from general-purpose CPU compute to accelerated parallel GPU architecture, Netweb functions as the indispensable Indian hardware integration bridge. Through its premier Tier-1 OEM manufacturing alliance with NVIDIA—covering the GH200 Grace Hopper Superchip, NVIDIA MGX modular enterprise architecture, and upcoming Blackwell GB200/B200 platforms—Netweb operates at the epicenter of India's multi-billion dollar AI infrastructure expansion[2].

Over the past 36 months, Netweb has executed a profound operational transformation from a niche scientific supercomputer provider into a hyper-growth enterprise AI systems powerhouse. Audited annual revenues have surged from ₹247.34 crore in FY22 to ₹724.21 crore in FY24, ₹2,184.22 crore in FY26, and ₹2,702.40 crore on a trailing-twelve-month (TTM) basis, compounding at a 3-year revenue CAGR of 69.9%[3]. Concurrently, net profit has expanded from ₹22.45 crore in FY22 to ₹206.12 crore in FY26 and ₹261.20 crore TTM, yielding a 3-year PAT CAGR of 63.6% alongside a Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) of 37.5% and Return on Equity (ROE) of 32.8%[3]. 1. History, Founding Origins & Corporate Evolution

Netweb Technologies India Limited was incorporated on September 22, 1999, in New Delhi by four first-generation entrepreneur brothers—Sanjay Lodha, Navin Lodha, Vivek Lodha, and Niraj Lodha—under the initial vision of designing, assembling, and deploying specialized, mission-critical computer systems for academic research institutes and scientific laboratories[7]. During the early 2000s, when India's domestic hardware ecosystem was entirely reliant on multinational imports from IBM, Hewlett-Packard, and Sun Microsystems, the Lodha brothers established the proprietary Tyrone brand name. Tyrone was conceptualized as a domestic architectural alternative engineered to deliver high-performance computing clusters tailored to the rigorous computational requirements of scientific simulations, seismic modeling, computational fluid dynamics (CFD), and defense research[7], [8].

A pivotal strategic milestone occurred in 2008 when Netweb designed and deployed one of India's earliest high-density supercomputers for the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bangalore. Recognizing that commoditized server assembly offered minimal long-term economic moat, management made the strategic decision to invest heavily in in-house architectural design, mother-board level thermal optimization, custom server chassis fabrication, and proprietary clustering software stacks[7]. This focus on deep engineering allowed Netweb to secure major contracts under the Government of India's National Supercomputing Mission (NSM), spearheaded jointly by the Department of Science and Technology (DST) and the Centre for Development of Advanced Computing (C-DAC)[9]. Netweb was selected to architect and deploy flagship supercomputers across India's premier academic institutions, including Param Yuva II at C-DAC Pune, Param Brahma at IISER Pune, Param Ananta at IIT Gandhinagar, and Param Seva at IIT Hyderabad[10].

Between 2018 and 2022, as global computing paradigms underwent a structural inflection toward deep learning and artificial intelligence, Netweb initiated a decisive capital expenditure cycle to transition from traditional CPU-dominated clusters into heterogeneous GPU-accelerated computing systems[1]. In May 2023, India's Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) and C-DAC unveiled AIRAWAT (AI Research Analytics and Knowledge Assimilation platform), an indigenous AI cloud supercomputer built and installed by Netweb at C-DAC Pune. Powered by thousands of high-performance GPUs and Netweb's proprietary Tyrone server architecture, AIRAWAT achieved a peak computing capacity of 13,170 teraflops, earning rank #75 on the global TOP500 list of the world's fastest supercomputers at the International Supercomputing Conference (ISC 2023) in Germany—marking India's fastest and highest-ranked AI supercomputer in history[11].

Capitalizing on this operational inflection, Netweb completed its Initial Public Offering (IPO) in July 2023. The issue, which sought to raise ₹631 crore at an upper price band of ₹500 per equity share (face value ₹2.00), was subscribed an extraordinary 90.36 times, driven by Qualified Institutional Buyers (QIB) subscribing 128.74 times and Non-Institutional Investors (NII) subscribing 81.64 times[12]. The stock listed on the NSE and BSE on July 27, 2023, at ₹947—an 89.4% premium to its issue price. Net proceeds were earmarked for the construction of a state-of-the-art Surface Mount Technology (SMT) manufacturing line in Faridabad, Haryana, funding working capital requirements for multi-megawatt server deployments, and scaling software R&D for its proprietary Kubyts cloud orchestration platform[7], [12].

2. Operations, System Architecture & Revenue Engines

Netweb Technologies operates through an integrated, vertically synchronized hardware design, architectural integration, and software deployment model. Unlike contract electronics manufacturers (EMS) that operate purely on build-to-print assembly margins, Netweb functions as an Original Design Manufacturer (ODM) and full-stack solutions architect. It designs custom multi-layer printed circuit boards (PCBs), engineers high-density server chassis with precision airflow and direct-to-chip liquid cooling loops, embeds proprietary firmware, and delivers complete turn-key computing clusters integrated with high-speed InfiniBand networking and parallel file systems[1], [13].

2.1 High Performance Computing (HPC) & Supercomputing

HPC systems constitute Netweb's historic foundation, contributing approximately 25%–30% of consolidated revenues. These systems comprise multi-node clustered servers engineered to process massively parallel scientific workloads, molecular dynamics simulations, weather forecasting, and defense aerodynamics[1]. Netweb markets these solutions under the Tyrone Camarero and Tyrone Skylus product families. The company provides complete architectural stacks, including server nodes, high-bandwidth inter-connects (NVIDIA Quantum InfiniBand operating at up to 800 Gb/s), job schedulers (SLURM, PBS Pro), and optimized MPI libraries. Gross margins in this division range from 20% to 26%, supported by deep technical tailoring and long-term annual maintenance contracts (AMC)[8], [13].

2.2 AI Systems & GPU Supercomputing Infrastructure

AI Systems represent Netweb's fastest-growing and highest-margin revenue engine, expanding from less than 10% of revenue in FY21 to over 48% of consolidated turnover in FY26 and Q1 FY27[3], [4]. This division encompasses specialized enterprise servers engineered explicitly for training and inferencing Large Language Models (LLMs), foundational generative AI models, computer vision neural networks, and sovereign AI cloud platforms. Key product offerings include:

  • NVIDIA GH200 Grace Hopper Superchip Servers: Netweb was among the first global OEMs to qualify and manufacture NVIDIA GH200 systems domestically in India. The GH200 combines an energy-efficient 72-core ARM Neoverse CPU with an NVIDIA H100 Tensor Core GPU linked via a high-speed 900 GB/s NVLink-C2C interconnect, providing unified memory architecture essential for trillion-parameter AI models[2], [14].
  • NVIDIA MGX Architecture Systems: Modular server platforms that allow rapid customization of accelerated computing across diverse GPU configurations (H100, H200, L40S, and upcoming B200) across standard 1U, 2U, and 4U form factors, slashing enterprise deployment lead times from months to weeks[2].
  • Direct-to-Chip Liquid Cooling Clusters: As modern AI accelerators exceed 700 to 1,000 Watts of thermal design power (TDP) per GPU, conventional air cooling becomes physically unviable. Netweb has developed proprietary liquid cooling distribution units (CDUs), cold plates, and manifold systems capable of dissipating high heat loads while slashing data center Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) to below 1.15[13].

2.3 Enterprise Private Cloud & Hyperconverged Infrastructure

To address the data sovereignty, latency, and cost-predictability demands of Indian enterprises, Netweb provides turnkey Private Cloud and Hyperconverged Infrastructure (HCI) solutions (accounting for 15%–18% of revenue)[1]. This division integrates computational compute nodes, software-defined storage (SDS), and software-defined networking (SDN) into a single unified management fabric. Netweb deploys its proprietary Kubyts cloud management platform—a Kubernetes-native software platform that automates workload orchestration, multi-tenancy provisioning, GPU resource virtualization, and hybrid cloud bursting, generating recurring software subscription and licensing revenues[1], [7].

2.4 Advanced SMT Manufacturing & R&D Footprint

Netweb's manufacturing headquarters is located in Faridabad, Haryana, housing a cutting-edge surface-mount technology (SMT) facility, environmental stress screening (ESS) chambers, robotic automated optical inspection (AOI) lines, and a high-voltage burn-in testing facility[7]. Unlike pure traders who import pre-assembled chassis, Netweb's Faridabad facility qualifies under the Government of India's Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme 2.0 for IT Hardware, which provides tiered financial incentives of 3% to 5% on incremental net sales of domestically manufactured servers and computing equipment[15]. The company employs over 120 dedicated R&D engineers, hardware architects, and firmware specialists, representing approximately 30% of its total white-collar workforce[7].

3. Indian AI Infrastructure Megatrends & Sovereign Cloud Policy

The macro tailwinds underpinning Netweb's multi-year structural compounding are governed by three converging sovereign and industrial secular imperatives:

  1. The ₹10,372 Crore IndiaAI Mission: In March 2024, the Union Cabinet chaired by the Prime Minister approved the comprehensive IndiaAI Mission with a multi-year budget allocation of ₹10,372 crore[5]. A central pillar of this mission is the establishment of an accessible, subsidized sovereign AI compute infrastructure comprising over 10,000 graphics processing units (GPUs) available to Indian startups, researchers, and public enterprises. Netweb is uniquely positioned as the foremost domestic OEM qualified to bid, manufacture, and integrate these high-density clusters, which explains the explosive 135% surge in its commercial bidding pipeline from ₹4,432 crore to ₹10,410 crore between March and June 2026[4], [5].
  2. Data Sovereignty & The Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act: Statutory enforcement of data localization for critical financial, healthcare, and governmental data has forced Indian enterprises, banks, and public institutions to migrate sensitive artificial intelligence model training from foreign public cloud hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud) into on-premise, air-gapped private sovereign cloud environments—driving demand directly into Netweb's Tyrone HCI and Kubyts platforms[16].
  3. Make in India Public Procurement Mandate (Class-I Local Supplier): Under revised Public Procurement (Preference to Make in India) Orders issued by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), government entities, defense organizations, and public sector undertakings (PSUs) are statutorily mandated to award tenders exclusively to Class-I local suppliers possessing over 50% domestic local value addition[17]. Multinational server giants such as Dell Technologies, Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE), and Lenovo frequently fail to meet these local content thresholds on advanced custom server designs, establishing an effective regulatory moat for Netweb in all public and defense compute contracts.

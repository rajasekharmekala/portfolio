Before you read

This dossier reviews Netweb Technologies Limited, which trades on the National Stock Exchange as NETWEB and which the fund already holds. It asks two questions: is the business still sound enough to keep owning, and is the price reasonable enough to add to? The answer to the second question is no, because the reward to the target does not meet the rule for a Tactical name. The answer to the first is mixed: returns on capital are high, but cash conversion over three years fails the rule. Chapter 1 explains the terms and rules used to reach these answers.

1. What the company does

Netweb designs and builds high-performance computers. These include supercomputers, artificial intelligence (AI) servers, private cloud systems and storage machines, sold to universities, research labs, data centres, government bodies and large companies. The company says it has installed more than 500 supercomputing systems and describes itself as one of India's largest makers of them.[12] The rating agency CRISIL puts the count of made-in-India high-performance units at more than 300, which is a narrower and independently stated figure.[18]

The company was founded in 1999 and listed on the exchanges in July 2023. Its offer documents were filed with the registrar on 25 July 2023.[25]

How the three layers work

Every candidate passes three layers in order. Layer 1 is a set of hard gates, each pass or fail with no room for judgement. Layer 2 is a scorecard of three to five operating measures chosen for the business. Layer 3 is timing, which compares the price with a verified support level. The run's rules say that if Layer 1 fails, Layer 2 and Layer 3 are not scored, and this dossier follows that rule.

The verdict words

For a holding, the verdict is one of four words. KEEP_COMPOUNDING means the position stays at full size. ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP means add on a pullback to support. TRIM_PROFIT_BOOKING means sell 30 to 50 percent because the stock is stretched. REPLACE_UNDERPERFORMER means the trend or the thesis has broken and the capital should move elsewhere. Provisional means the verdict stands on today's evidence and is re-read as soon as a test that cannot yet be run becomes runnable.

The rules behind the numbers

The stop for a Tactical name is set by a formula: the lowest of the recent 20-day swing low and the 20- and 50-day averages, minus half of one ATR (average true range). ATR is the typical daily price swing over the last 14 sessions, so dividing a distance by ATR tells you how many normal days of movement separate the price from the level. The asymmetry ratio compares the distance to the target with the distance to the stop, and a Tactical name needs at least 3.5 to one.

The target has to be anchored. An anchor is a number built from something measurable: forward earnings multiplied by a three-year median price-to-earnings ratio (P/E), which is the share price divided by the earnings per share, or a measured move, which is a target built from a documented price pattern. A broker's target that is not built this way does not count. Every figure should also be no older than 60 days, so older figures are listed as exceptions in the run notes.

What the stage words mean

Stage 4 is the phase in which a stock has broken down, usually below a falling long-term average. The stage label comes from the run's price ledger, which is the authoritative source for every price in this dossier. Here the ledger reads Stage 2 with a rising 50-day average and a 200-day average that is also rising, so the technical test is passed. Under the rules in this dossier, a Stage 4 breakdown on heavy volume would start a replacement review.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

A business is strong when it earns high returns on the money invested in it, turns those earnings into cash, and does not need heavy borrowing to grow. It is weak when one of those three is missing. Netweb passes the first and the third comfortably. The second, cash, is where the gate fails, and the reasons are set out below.

Returns on capital and leverage

The ROCE (return on capital employed) measures how much operating profit a company earns for each rupee tied up in the business. Netweb's ROCE for the year to March 2026 is 36.70 percent in the StockAnalysis series.[1] The StockFin page shows 37.44 percent, and the Screener header shows 37.5 percent, both without a stated period.[13][9] The gate uses the lowest figure, 36.70 percent, which clears the 15 percent floor by a wide margin.

Return on equity, which measures profit against shareholders' money, was 32.87 percent for the same year.[1] Leverage is light. The debt-to-equity ratio is borrowings divided by shareholders' equity, and it was 0.39 for the year to March 2026.[1] One aggregator shows 0.23 on a different basis, and the gate uses the higher figure, 0.39, so the test is passed on the more cautious number.[14]

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

An estimate is only as good as the assumptions under it. This chapter sets out where the numbers in this dossier could be wrong, and what would show that.

The forecast rests on four analysts

The consensus estimate is the average of analysts' forecasts, and it puts earnings per share at ₹67.71 for the year to March 2027, against ₹36.30 for the year to March 2026.[3] The same source puts revenue at ₹4,168 crore for that year, an increase of 91 percent, and it shows four analysts with a strong-buy rating.[3] Four analysts is a thin sample, and the average can move sharply when one of them revises. The forecast also assumes that the profit growth of the last year holds, which the cash series does not yet show. The forward figure is not traced to contracted revenue in the cached evidence: the order book and the government procurement figure were not found, so the step-up is an analyst assumption.

Cash conversion may be a timing problem, or it may not

There are two readings. The first is timing: fast growth ties up cash in stock and receivables that is collected later, and the 2026 ratio of 0.83 supports that. The second is structural: the company sells to a few buyers who pay late, and the 2025 fall and the Q1 build fit that better. The cached evidence cannot separate them, which is why the verdict is provisional.

Margins: one good quarter is not a trend

The operating margin rose to 14.30 percent in the quarter to June 2026, against 11.99 percent in the quarter before.[7] For the year to March 2026 the margin was 12.40 percent.[2] One quarter is a point, not a line. A margin that falls for two quarters in a row would be the first sign that growth is being bought with price.

Fatal flaw scenarios

Three scenarios would make the case wrong. A slowdown in orders from the largest customer would cut revenue while the new capacity's fixed costs stay. The working-capital build could persist into the next audited year. A fall in the multiple would take the price below the stop even if the business performs, because a stock priced at 73.9 times forward earnings has little room for disappointment. The stop protects against the first two only partly, and not against a gap on results day.

The share count and the fund raise

The share count is the denominator of every per-share figure. StockAnalysis shows 56.94 million shares, up 0.29 percent over the year.[5] Per-share earnings and the target both depend on it, so the anchored target should be re-run once the count is confirmed. The target of ₹5,804.78 is pre-dilution. Each 1 percent of new shares would cut forward earnings per share by about 1 percent, and the target by about ₹58.

What this means for an owner: the estimates are thin, the margin trend is short, and the cash gate depends on one aggregator. None of these overturns the decision today, but each is a reason to re-read the holding at the next results date.

7. What we would do and when

The decision is to hold without adding. This chapter sets out the rule for that decision, the levels that would change it, and the dates to watch.

The verdict and its rule

The verdict is KEEP_COMPOUNDING, marked provisional. The full keep rule requires every Layer 1 gate to pass and at least three operating KPIs to pass. Both Layer 1 floors fail: the cash-conversion floor of 0.70 and the asymmetry floor of 3.5. Layer 2 is not scored, so the full rule is not met. When no exit or trim rule fires, the default is to hold and not add, so this is a weaker keep, and the provisional label and the do-not-add instruction mark it as such. The position is held because no exit trigger has fired. REPLACE_UNDERPERFORMER requires a close below the stop, a Stage 4 breakdown, two or more operating KPIs at fail, or an impaired thesis. The close is above the stop, the trend is intact, and the KPI test cannot be run under the run's rules. The judgement is that the cash result is a gate failure, not yet an impaired thesis. The reported profit is growing and FY2026 cash was strong, with a ratio of 0.83. Against that, the June 2026 balance sheet shows a new build of about ₹426 crore net, which is why the verdict is provisional and the next results are the test. The thesis would count as impaired if a second cash window also falls below the floor, or if receivables rise again after the next results.

Both fails rest on the same aggregator, so they are not independent evidence. If the cash gate were treated as unresolved rather than failed, the keep would not change: the asymmetry floor alone still blocks new money, the full keep rule is still not met, and no exit rule would change. The cache holds no second source for forward earnings per share.

The number

The asymmetry ratio for NETWEB is 1.13 to one. It is the reward to the anchored target divided by the risk to the formula stop. The anchor is verified, so the ratio is not N/A. It fails the 3.5 floor by a wide margin, and the failure is the reason the position is not added to.

How it was calculated

The formula is (target minus close) divided by (close minus stop). The close is ₹5,001.00 and the formula stop is ₹4,289.78, so the risk is ₹711.22, which is 3.6 ATR. The target is forward earnings per share of ₹67.71 multiplied by the three-year median P/E of 85.73, which gives ₹5,804.78. The reward is ₹803.78, and the ratio is 803.78 divided by 711.22, or 1.13.

The range

The ratio moves with the stop and the target. If the stop were set at the 20-day swing low without the half-ATR buffer, the risk would be ₹613.00 and the ratio about 1.3. Under the ledger's ₹4,830.13 stop, the risk would be 5,001.00 minus 4,830.13, or ₹170.87, and the ratio would be 803.78 divided by 170.87, or 4.70, which clears the 3.5 floor. Under that stop the target needed for 3.5 would be 5,001.00 plus 3.5 times 170.87, or ₹5,599, which is below the anchored target of ₹5,804.78. The asymmetry fail is therefore stop-rule dependent. The scoring-file formula governs, because the scoring file sets the Tactical stop as the lower of the swing low and the averages, less half an ATR, and the ledger note uses the 50-day average instead. The keep verdict does not change on this basis: the cash gate still fails, and no add is supported on the formula basis. A bear scenario that cuts forward earnings by 15 percent, an assumption and not a forecast, gives ₹57.55 (57.5535) times 75.44, or ₹4,341.84, a fall of 13.2 percent. That sits just above the formula stop, which is 14.2 percent below the close, so a stop-out would be the likely outcome in that case.

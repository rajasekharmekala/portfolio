Before you read: the terms we use

This chapter defines every technical term once, at the point where it first matters. If a word looks unfamiliar later, come back here. Each definition uses a plain comparison, because the numbers only mean something once the terms are clear.

A 20-day moving average is the average closing price over the last 20 sessions, a bit like a smoothed trend line. A 50-day moving average does the same over 50 sessions, and a 200-day moving average over 200 sessions, so a longer average shows the slower trend.[29] Each average is only as good as the history behind it, and that matters for this stock.

Stage 2 is the uptrend phase in the four-stage cycle, when a stock trades above a rising average. Stage 4 is the downtrend phase, when the stock falls below a falling average. A base is the sideways stretch a stock rests in after a fall, like a car idling before it pulls away.

ATR (average true range) is the typical daily price swing of the stock, measured in rupees.[27] Dividing a distance by ATR tells you how many normal days of movement sit between the price and a level, which is why the report uses it for stops and entry bands.

RSI (relative strength index) is a speed gauge from 0 to 100. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen fast, and readings below 30 mean it has fallen fast. It describes momentum, not value.[28]

ROCE (return on capital employed) is the profit a business earns on all the money tied up in it. A high ROCE means the company turns each rupee of invested capital into a lot of profit.

EPS (earnings per share) is the company's profit divided by its number of shares. EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) is operating profit before the costs of borrowing, tax and wear on machines.

Operating margin (OPM) is the share of each rupee of sales left after running costs. An operating margin of 20 percent means 20 paise of every rupee of sales remains before interest, tax and depreciation.

Debt-to-equity is the borrowed money a company uses for each rupee of its owners' money. A ratio of 0.16 means 16 paise of borrowing for each rupee of equity.

The price-to-earnings ratio (P/E) is how many rupees an investor pays for one rupee of yearly profit. A P/E of 120 means the price is 120 times the yearly profit, so the buyer is paying for years of future growth.

Asymmetry means the ratio of the possible gain to the possible loss, measured from the stop. Our rules need a target that sits at least 3.5 times the distance from the price to the stop for a Tactical Momentum name.[4] A verifiable target is one that can be traced to a named method, such as forward earnings times a historical median P/E, or a measured-move from a chart base.

An entry zone is the price band in which a new buy is allowed. The band is set by distance from a support level, measured in ATR, so the buy happens near a level where the stock has found buyers before.

A pivot is the price level a stock must close above to confirm a new trend, like a gate that opens only above a set height. A retest is a return to a level that was just broken, to see whether it now holds as support. A limit order is an order to buy only at a stated price or better, so it may not fill at all.

The Layer labels follow our three-layer framework. Layer 1 is a set of hard pass-or-fail gates. Layer 2 is a traffic-light scorecard of operating measures. Layer 3 is the entry-timing rule.

With these terms in hand, the next chapter describes the company itself, so that the numbers that follow have a business to attach to.

What the company does

Sedemac Mechatronics makes electronic control units, which are the small computers that run one part of a vehicle. The company describes itself as a Pune-based technology company specializing in control electronics, incorporated in 2007.[5] In plain terms, it sells the brain for a motor, and its sales follow the number of vehicles that use that motor.

An electronic control unit is an embedded computer that controls one or more of a vehicle's electrical systems.[24] The company's products are of this kind, and they sit inside two-wheelers and three-wheelers as well as industrial equipment.

The products, and why one of them matters most

The company's largest product line is the integrated starter generator, or ISG, control unit. An idle-stop system shuts the engine off at a red light and restarts it when the rider moves, which the Wikipedia overview describes as a way to reduce idle time and fuel use.[22] Management describes the ISG electronics as a motor controller for an internal combustion engine, and it names the Honda Activa among the two-wheeler models that carry the feature.[15]

The company also makes motor controllers for electric two- and three-wheelers, a category that is growing quickly in India.[26] A brushless DC motor needs its electronics to know where the rotor is, and controllers that do this without a physical position sensor are often called sensorless controllers.[23] The alternative is a Hall effect sensor, a common way to read rotor position.[25]

The bridge from products to money is volume. In FY26, the company's Mobility segment grew 61 percent to ₹911 crore, and Industrial grew 57 percent to ₹148 crore.[8] In the June 2026 quarter, Mobility revenue rose 53 percent to ₹281.04 crore while Industrial fell 16 percent to ₹28.73 crore.[9] That split matters because the growth engine is the two-wheeler and electric business, not the factory-automation side.

The next question is how the business is financed and whether its customers are spread widely enough to survive a loss of one of them.

Listing, plants and the customer base

The company listed on the exchanges on 11 March 2026, after an initial public offer whose final offer document the SEBI filing shows was filed with the registrar of companies on 6 March 2026.[9][21] That short listing history is the reason several long-run measures in this report cannot yet be computed, a point we return to in the gate table.

On 25 September 2026 the company began limited commercial production at its new Manufacturing Facility 3 (MF3) in Pune.[5] It also reported record Q2 FY27 ECU sales, up 37.5 percent, with cumulative sales of one million ISG and EFI ECUs.[5] The MF3 facility is the new capacity that the next two years of volume growth will need.

The customer base is narrow. The ICRA rating filing states that 77 percent of revenue comes from the largest client, and that 75 percent of raw materials are imported.[7] Management does not give that single number on the call. It describes a different measure, the largest customer's revenue share minus the sum of the next four, and says the measure has been falling over three years.[15] The two statements do not contradict each other directly, but neither gives us a clean figure for the top customer, so we use ICRA's 77 percent and flag the definition gap.

Customer concentration is the first weak point, and it sits next to real strengths in returns and cash, which the next chapter weighs.

Why the business is strong or weak

A company can be strong on its numbers and still be fragile in its structure. This chapter sets out both sides, using the measures the Layer 1 and Layer 2 frameworks name. Strength here means high returns on the money the business uses, low borrowing and cash that arrives with profit. Weakness means concentration, cost exposure and expectations that already assume a lot.

The strengths, in the company's own numbers

Returns are the strongest part of the case. ROCE stands at 35.3 percent and ROE at 26.9 percent on the current Screener reading.[5] A company that earns 35 paise on every rupee of capital it uses can fund growth from its own earnings more easily than a company that earns less.

Cash backs the profit. Over the last three fiscal years operating cash flow totalled ₹280 crore against profit after tax of ₹157 crore, a ratio of 1.78.[5] The company's own release reports FY26 PAT of ₹104 crore, more than double the prior year, and revenue of ₹1,058 crore, up 61 percent.[8] The ICRA rating action in September 2026 upgraded the bank loan rating to [ICRA]A (Positive), citing growth and an interest coverage of 26.5 times in FY26.[7]

Strength is clear in returns and cash. The weak points are in the concentration and the cost base, which the next paragraphs take in turn.

The weaknesses, and why they matter more than they look

The first weakness is the one already described: 77 percent of revenue from one client.[7] A single customer that moves volume to a rival, or that changes its model line, can remove a large part of sales in one quarter. The second is cost exposure, with 75 percent of raw materials imported, so a currency move or a freight shock flows straight into margins.[7]

Management has already warned about the margin direction. On the May 2026 call it said mild margin pressure is expected from commodity and supply-chain factors, and it would not give product-level margins.[15] The June 2026 quarter shows the first sign of this: the operating margin was 19 percent against 20 percent a year earlier.[5] The quarter's profit also included a ₹2.98 crore reversal of an old tax provision, so the underlying profit growth was nearer 78 percent than the 95 percent reported headline.[9]

Cash tied up with customers is also growing. Debtor days, the average number of days it takes customers to pay, rose from 24 in March 2025 to 46 in March 2026.[5] That is not a crisis at 46 days, but it doubled in a year, and a growing customer book with slower payers is how a profitable company runs short of cash. Capex planned for FY27 of ₹150 to 200 crore is also above FY26 operating cash flow of ₹128 crore.[7][5]

The weaknesses are real but are not yet showing in the cash flow. The Layer 2 scorecard below turns these points into traffic lights.

ROCE and ROE: how well the money works

ROCE tells you how much profit the business makes on all the capital it has tied up, including borrowed capital. A business that earns 35.3 percent on capital is generating far more than a bank deposit would, and it can fund growth without asking shareholders for money. ROE is the same idea measured against the owners' equity only. On the Screener page, ROE is 26.9 percent now, 22 percent over three years, and 27 percent in the last year.[5] The consequence is that the company does not need heavy outside funding to build a plant, which is consistent with the MF3 start.

The caution is that the company's own FY26 summary says RoCE improved to over 40 percent, which is higher than the Screener figure.[8] The definitions differ, and we use the Screener figure because it is the one we can recompute from the balance sheet. That difference does not change the verdict, because the gate passes at either number.

Returns say the business uses its capital well. The next measure asks how much borrowed money sits behind that capital.

Debt-to-equity: how much is borrowed

Debt-to-equity compares borrowings with the owners' equity. At March 2026 the company had borrowings of ₹72 crore against equity of ₹449 crore, which is equity capital of ₹44 crore plus reserves of ₹405 crore.[5] That gives 0.16, well below the 0.70 limit in our rules. ICRA reports gearing of 0.2 times, which is the same conclusion on a slightly wider debt definition.[7] The consequence is that a sales slowdown would not threaten the company's ability to pay its lenders.

Low debt means the business can absorb a shock. The cash test shows whether the profit is real money.

Cash conversion: is the profit real money?

Cash conversion asks whether the reported profit turns into cash. Our test sums three years of operating cash flow and divides by three years of profit after tax. Operating cash flow was ₹61 crore in FY24, ₹91 crore in FY25 and ₹128 crore in FY26.[5] Profit after tax was ₹6 crore, ₹47 crore and ₹104 crore over the same years.[5] Adding them gives ₹280 crore of cash against ₹157 crore of profit, so the ratio is 1.78, far above the 0.70 floor.

A ratio above 1 means the company turned more than its profit into cash, which can happen when depreciation is large or when customers pay faster. That is a good sign for the cash picture but a warning about the future: if capex rises faster than profit, cash left over after investment can fall even while profit rises. The rating filing puts FY27 capex at ₹150 to 200 crore, which will test this.[7]

Cash conversion passes, and it shows the profit is backed by money. The growth rates that follow show whether that money is growing fast enough to justify the price.

Growth: the compound rates over three years

A compound annual growth rate, or CAGR, is the steady yearly rate that would take a number from its start value to its end value. Screener shows sales growing at 36 percent a year over three years and profit at 128 percent a year over the same period.[5] The sales figure is close to what the printed endpoints give: sales of ₹423 crore in March 2023 and ₹1,058 crore in March 2026 imply about 36 percent a year.[5] The profit figure starts from a small base of ₹9 crore, so it is high but should be read as a recovery from low profit, not as a permanent pace.

The forward view is still strong. Analyst consensus sees net sales of about ₹1,507 crore in FY27, which is roughly 42 percent above FY26.[13][12] If that is delivered, the growth story holds, and the open question is margin, not volume.

Growth is strong, but the margin determines how much of that growth reaches shareholders. The operating margin test follows.

Operating margin: how much of each rupee of sales is kept

The operating margin is the share of sales left after running costs such as raw materials, wages and factory overheads. The Screener row shows 12, 10, 11, 15, 18 and 20 percent across the last six years, and the margin dipped from 12 to 10 percent in the second year, then rose every year to 20 percent.[5] That rise is a large part of why profit grew faster than sales. The FY26 company summary puts EBITDA margin near 21 percent, and ICRA reports an operating margin of 21.5 percent, which are definitional variants of the same story.[8][7]

The June 2026 quarter is where the squeeze appears. Operating margin was 19 percent, against 20 percent in June 2025.[5] One quarter is not a trend, but management has warned of pressure, so we watch it closely. An operating margin below 13.5 percent for two quarters is the level the earlier shortlist row set as an invalidation, and we keep it as a stop-type test.[3]

The margin is the weak link in the earnings path. The last measure is what the market is paying for that earnings path.

Price-to-earnings: what the market pays for each rupee of profit

The price-to-earnings ratio divides the share price by earnings per share. On the Screener page the trailing ratio is 121, meaning investors pay ₹121 for every ₹1 of profit earned over the last twelve months.[5] The forward figure is much lower, because earnings are expected to rise. At the current price the analyst consensus FY27 EPS of ₹41.70 implies a ratio of about 77 times.[11][1] That is still high, and it is a bet that growth continues.

The historical comparison is missing. A three-year median of this ratio would be the usual yardstick for a target, and it does not exist yet for this stock, because it listed in March 2026.[9][16] That gap is the reason the target gate below cannot be passed.

The stop and the money at risk

The working stop is a pre-decided exit: if the stock closes below it, we sell. The number that matters is how much of the account would be lost if the stop were hit, and that is what the estimate below works out.

The number

If the stop at ₹2,950 is hit, the loss on the 200 shares is ₹54,640, which is 1.16 percent of the account.[2][3]

How it was calculated

The loss is 200 shares multiplied by the distance from the close of ₹3,223.20 down to the stop of ₹2,950, which is ₹273.20 a share.[1] That is 200 times 273.20, or ₹54,640. The stop is 1.72 ATR below the close, or 8.5 percent.

The inputs

The inputs are the 200-share holding from the positions file, the close and ATR from the ledger, and the working stop from the watchlist row.[2][1][3] The account total is ₹46,95,955 including cash of ₹3,678, so the percentage uses that base.

The range

The stop-placement rule for this bucket puts the stop below the lower of the 20-day swing low and the 20- or 50-day average, minus half an ATR. That rule gives ₹2,798.59. At that stop the loss would be ₹84,920, or 1.81 percent of the account.[4][1] The working stop is tighter, which we accept because it protects more of the profit already made.

If the number is off

The number assumes the stop is filled at ₹2,950. A gap down through the stop, which can happen on a bad announcement, would fill lower and widen the loss. Brokerage, the exit tax and slippage are not included, and they are small against this loss but real. The account total also moves each day with the market, so the percentage is a snapshot, not a fixed figure.

The money at risk is modest for the account, but the profit at risk is large. The next chapter tests the assumptions that could make the whole path wrong.

The verdict and the rule behind it

The taxonomy has four labels for holdings. KEEP_COMPOUNDING needs all gates passed and at least three of four operating KPIs healthy. ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP needs a high-conviction compounder pulling back to verified support, with room in the allocation. TRIM_PROFIT_BOOKING needs an extended price, a reached target or a weight over the cap. REPLACE_UNDERPERFORMER needs a broken structure, two or more failed KPIs, or an impaired thesis.[4] Measured against those conditions, the stock meets none of them in full.

No taxonomy label fits the position, so we do not assign one. KEEP_COMPOUNDING needs all gates passed and at least three of four operating KPIs healthy, as the taxonomy words it; our scorecard scores five measures, of which two pass, and neither condition holds. ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP is blocked because any add needs a verified target. TRIM needs an extended price, a reached target or a weight above the cap, and none applies: the weight is 13.73 percent, under 15.[2] REPLACE needs broken structural support, two or more failed KPIs or an impaired thesis. Support is intact and one measure fails. The advice is therefore HOLD, NO ADD: keep the 200 shares at the current size, add nothing, and exit on a close below ₹2,950.[3] The judgement is sensitive to the KPI count.

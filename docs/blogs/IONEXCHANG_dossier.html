1. Executive Summary & Institutional Dilemma

Ion Exchange (India) Limited (IONEXCHANG) is an established franchise in the Indian environmental infrastructure ecosystem. Through its integrated triad of water engineering projects (58% of revenue), specialty chemical resins (29% of revenue), and consumer purification systems (13% of revenue), the company possesses genuine technological capabilities in Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD), sea water reverse osmosis (SWRO), and nuclear-grade ion exchange resins[1][3].

However, an institutional audit under the 3-Layer Underwriting Framework reveals that Ion Exchange is currently ensnared in a severe profitability and cash conversion down-cycle. Consolidated operating margins (OPM) collapsed from historical highs of 13.0–14.0% to just 6.0% in FY26, with TTM net profit dropping 53.6% from ₹208 Cr in FY25 to ₹98 Cr in FY26 due to cost overruns in municipal engineering packages (notably UP Jal Nigam / SWSM water supply contracts) and delays in high-margin international project billings (Sri Lanka)[4][5]. Consequently, 3-Year cumulative CFO/PAT plunged to 0.25x (cumulative CFO of just ₹134 Cr vs PAT of ₹546 Cr), and ROCE fell to 14.2%, breaching Layer 1 Hard Gating Floors. Action: Rejection and archival to Archive_Rejected[6].

2. Corporate Evolution & Ownership Structure

Incorporated in 1964 as a subsidiary of the UK-based Permutit Company, Ion Exchange became a wholly Indian-managed enterprise in 1977 following foreign equity dilution under FERA regulations[2]. Under the longtime stewardship of Chairman & Managing Director Rajesh Sharma, the company developed comprehensive indigenous chemical synthesis capabilities at Ankleshwar and Roha, producing the renowned INDION brand of ion exchange resins and water treatment chemicals[7].

A structural governance vulnerability is the company's low promoter shareholding: promoters hold only 25.43% of outstanding equity (with zero pledge), while the public holds 38.21%, domestic institutions hold 15.32%, and foreign portfolio investors hold 4.86%[1]. This dispersed ownership structure leaves management relatively insulated while offering limited insider skin-in-the-game during operational downturns.

3. Operating Architecture & Tri-Divisional Model

3.1 Engineering Division: Industrial & Municipal Water EPC

The Engineering division (58% revenue share) designs and executes turnkey water and effluent treatment plants for heavy industries (oil & gas refineries, thermal power, steel, chemicals, textiles) and municipal infrastructure bodies (Jal Jeevan Mission, Delhi Jal Nigam, UP SWSM)[3][8]. While the unexecuted engineering order book stands at ₹2,664 Cr with a massive ₹9,200 Cr bid pipeline, execution has been severely hampered by state government billing delays, bureaucratic clearance bottlenecks, and inflationary raw material costs on fixed-price contracts[5][9].

3.2 Chemicals Division: INDION Resins & Specialty Water Treatment

The Chemicals division (29% revenue share) is the company's highest-margin jewel, manufacturing cation, anion, and chelating resins for water demineralization, pharmaceutical purification, food & beverage processing, and nuclear power plant coolant circuits[10]. The new state-of-the-art greenfield resin manufacturing complex at Roha (Maharashtra) is slated for full commissioning in FY27, designed to expand high-margin specialty resin exports to Europe and North America[11].

3.3 Consumer Products: ZeroB & Commercial Purification

The Consumer division (13% revenue share) markets commercial water purifiers, water softeners, and residential ZeroB water purification appliances across urban institutional and household channels[12].

3.4 International Operations: Sri Lanka, Middle East & Portugal

Ion Exchange operates overseas assembly and manufacturing facilities in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Indonesia, Bangladesh, and Portugal[13]. Its marquee international contract is a multi-million-dollar municipal water supply and treatment project for the National Water Supply and Drainage Board (NWSDB) in Sri Lanka. While this contract bolsters export order visibility, revenue recognition has faced periodic delays due to foreign exchange restructuring and project milestone sign-offs in Colombo[14].

4. Sector Dynamics: Industrial Zero Liquid Discharge & Municipal Capex

With India facing acute industrial water scarcity and Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) mandating Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD) for heavily polluting industrial clusters (textiles, tanneries, pharmaceuticals, distilleries), industrial water recycling demand is compounding at 12–15% annually[15]. However, municipal EPC contracts under government schemes remain fraught with payment delays, retention money lockups, and bureaucratic disputes, creating an asymmetric risk profile for pure-play EPC contractors[16].

5.2 Working Capital Bleed in Long-Cycle EPC Contracts

Working capital days ballooned from -48 days in FY17 to +53 days in FY26 as debtor days reached 135 days and trade receivables expanded to over ₹1,150 Cr[1][17]. Free cash flow was heavily negative in FY25 (-₹287 Cr) and FY26 (-₹280 Cr) due to the combination of negative operating cash flows and ongoing capex for the Roha resin facility (CWIP of ₹176 Cr and Net Fixed Assets expanding to ₹753 Cr)[18]. To fund this shortfall, borrowings rose from ₹59 Cr in FY23 to ₹482 Cr in FY26[1].

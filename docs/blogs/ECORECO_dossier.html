1. Corporate Identity, History & Pioneering E-Waste Mandate

Eco Recycling Limited (ECORECO) is India's pioneer BSE-listed electronic waste management and environmental engineering enterprise, headquartered in Mumbai, Maharashtra.[1] Founded in 1994 by social entrepreneur and environmental pioneer Mr. B.K. Soni, the company established India's first formal, government-authorized e-waste dismantling and recycling facility in Maharashtra under the premise that "the greatest mine is already above the ground."[2]

The company listed on the BSE (Scrip Code: 530643) and operates as a recognized provider of comprehensive environmental compliance, data security, and circular resource recovery solutions.[3] As of September 2026, the promoter group holds a dominant 73.35% equity stake, FIIs hold 0.25%, DIIs hold 0.19%, and public shareholders hold 26.21% across 37,205 retail and HNI accounts.[4]

2. Business Architecture & Specialized Service Verticals

ECORECO provides an integrated suite of electronic lifecycle services spanning four core commercial verticals:[5]

  • E-Waste Recycling & Material Recovery: Environmentally compliant dismantling, shredding, and mechanical separation of End-of-Life (EOL) IT, telecommunications, medical, and consumer electronics into ferrous metals, non-ferrous metals (copper, aluminum), plastics, and precious metal concentrates.[6]
  • IT Asset Disposition (ITAD) & Data Destruction: On-site and off-site magnetic degaussing, physical shredding, and cryptographic data erasure certified to DoD 5220.22-M and NIST 800-88 compliance standards for enterprise servers, hard disk drives, and mobile devices.[7]
  • Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) Services: End-to-end collection, channelization, fulfillment, and generation/trading of verified EPR recycling credits on the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) portal for consumer electronics OEMs.[8]
  • Lamp & Specialized Waste Recycling: Hazardous mercury extraction and fluorescent tube recycling using proprietary closed-loop recovery technology.[9]

3. Processing Infrastructure & Recycling Capabilities

The company's primary processing campus is located in Tarapur and Navi Mumbai, Maharashtra, licensed by the Maharashtra Pollution Control Board (MPCB) with an authorized annual recycling capacity exceeding 31,000 metric tonnes per annum (MTPA).[10]

The facility features automated sorting conveyors, high-capacity shredders, eddy-current separators, optical color sorters, and dedicated hydrometallurgical and chemical recovery blocks for printed circuit board (PCB) valorization.[11] ECORECO holds ISO 9001 (Quality), ISO 14001 (Environmental), ISO 45001 (Occupational Health & Safety), and ISO 27001 (Information Security) certifications.[12]

4. Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) & Regulatory Framework

India generates an estimated 1.7 million tonnes of e-waste annually, ranking as the world's third-largest e-waste producer, with less than 20% handled through formal, authorized recycling channels.[13] Under the revised E-Waste (Management) Rules 2022 enacted by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC), electronics manufacturers, brand owners, and importers face escalating mandatory recycling targets (rising to 80% of historical sales volume).[14]

These regulations established a market-driven EPR certificate mechanism where registered recyclers generate electronic credits on the CPCB portal upon processing audited waste, creating a high-margin service revenue stream for authorized formal players like ECORECO.[15]

7. Cash Conversion Forensics & Extreme Working Capital Lock-Up

Despite robust reported accounting profitability, forensic cash flow analysis uncovers an alarming structural failure in cash realization. Across FY24–FY26, cumulative Cash Flow from Operations (CFO) was only ₹27 Cr (FY24: −₹3 Cr; FY25: ₹17 Cr; FY26: ₹13 Cr) compared to cumulative reported PAT of ₹64 Cr (FY24: ₹18 Cr; FY25: ₹23 Cr; FY26: ₹23 Cr), yielding a severely deficient 3-year CFO/PAT ratio of 0.422× (far below the institutional minimum hurdle of ≥0.70×).[19]

Working capital diagnostics reveal that operating profits are trapped in receivables and unsold inventory:[20]

  • Debtor Days: Stretched from 58 days in FY24 to 63 days in FY25 and 119 days in FY26.
  • Inventory Holding Days: Expanded to an extreme 443 days (and was 652 days in FY25), representing stockpiled untreated e-waste and unrefined precious metal slimes.
  • Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC): Stands at 395 days (and 232 Working Capital Days), indicating that the company requires over a full year to turn operational outlays back into realized cash.

8. Precious Metal Recovery & Refining Economics

E-waste printed circuit boards contain gold, silver, palladium, and copper at concentrations 10 to 50 times richer than naturally mined ores.[21] ECORECO's business model relies on mechanical enrichment of PCB dust followed by chemical hydrometallurgical refining.[22]

However, the lack of an integrated domestic precious metal smelting ecosystem forces Indian recyclers to export enriched concentrates to global smelters (e.g. Umicore, Boliden) under long settlement cycles, creating severe inventory holding drag and working capital lock-up.[23]

9. Enterprise Client Franchise & 'Book My Junk' Retail Initiative

ECORECO serves enterprise clients across banking, IT/ITeS, telecommunications, and government PSUs (including TCS, Infosys, State Bank of India, and IBM) for secure ITAD and corporate e-waste disposal.[24]

To access the unorganized consumer retail segment, the company launched its proprietary digital mobile application, "Book My Junk," enabling households and small businesses to schedule doorstep collection of discarded electronics.[25] While strategically innovative, retail collection volumes remain sub-scale, with over 85% of feedstock still sourced through institutional enterprise tenders.[26]

10. Informal Sector Competition & Policy Enforcement Risks

The primary structural impediment facing formal recyclers is India's entrenched informal recycling ecosystem (the Kabadiwala network and unorganized dismantling clusters in Seelampur and Moradabad), which handles >80% of national e-waste using hazardous, low-cost acid-leaching and open-burning methods.[27] Because informal operators pay cash upfront and bypass environmental taxes, formal recyclers often struggle to procure raw feedstock at economically viable yields unless stringent CPCB EPR audit enforcement penalizes non-compliant producers.[28]

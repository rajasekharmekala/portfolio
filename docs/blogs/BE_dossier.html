Bloom Energy sells equipment and services that turn fuel into electricity without combustion, and it also sells electrolyzers intended to produce hydrogen. That combination gives the company a credible answer to a real problem: data centers and industrial customers need power before the grid can deliver new transmission capacity. The investment question is narrower than the technology story. At the 8 October ledger close, BE was above both moving averages and classified as Stage 2, yet the core underwriting ratios were not deterministically available in the run's verified evidence. Missing evidence is not a neutral score; under the rules it is a failed gate. [1] [2] [3]

What the company does and why customers buy it

Bloom's Server is a distributed power system: it is installed near a customer and generates electricity on site. That matters when a campus is waiting for a utility interconnection, when reliability is more valuable than the last cent of fuel cost, or when water and local emissions constraints narrow the available choices. The company's electrolyzer is a related platform aimed at hydrogen production, but the two products have different adoption curves and should not be valued as one mature recurring business. [1] [8] [10]

The data-center use case is the clearest commercial proof point in the source record. Bloom announced that its Equinix relationship exceeded 100 MW across 19 data centers, and an earlier Intel agreement described fuel-cell power for a Silicon Valley high-performance-computing site. Those announcements establish customer interest and deployment context; they do not, by themselves, establish recognized revenue, return on invested capital, or the margin after service obligations. [12] [13]

That distinction drives the underwriting. A signed capacity or supply arrangement is like a restaurant reservation: it signals intent, but the investor still needs to see the meal's price, cost and cash settlement. The 2025 annual report and subsequent quarterly filing are the right evidence for that bridge. Until the hard-gate values are extracted and reconciled, the business remains interesting but unqualified. [1] [6]

Operating model, customers and supply chain

Bloom's economics combine product shipment, installation, service and fuel-cell replacement cycles. Product revenue can arrive in large projects, while service revenue can smooth the installed base but also carries warranty, performance and replacement responsibilities. The annual report describes a business exposed to component availability, manufacturing execution, customer concentration, permitting, fuel prices and policy support. Each exposure can turn a high-growth quarter into a low-cash year if working capital expands faster than gross profit. [1] [3]

AI infrastructure is a demand catalyst, not a moat by itself. Competitors can offer grid connection, turbines, batteries, solar-plus-storage, conventional generators or other fuel-cell architectures. Bloom's differentiators are the footprint, modularity, electrical output, deployment speed and potential emissions profile; the durability of those advantages depends on delivered cost per megawatt-hour and uptime after service costs. The EPA and DOE explain why fuel cells can provide stationary power, but those public descriptions do not verify Bloom's project-level economics. [18] [19] [20]

Supply-chain diligence should focus on ceramic cells, stacks, power electronics, specialty metals, contract manufacturing and customer financing. The company must convert a growing backlog into profitable shipments without pulling forward inventory or receivables. Because the run's source ledger does not include a verified backlog-to-revenue bridge or customer concentration percentage for BE, those values are marked N/A rather than inferred from press coverage. [1] [3] [9]

Financial record and forensic cash test

The FY2025 release reported record full-year revenue of $2.02 billion, record gross margin, 2.5-times year-over-year product-backlog growth and a second consecutive year of positive operating cash flow. Those are meaningful improvements because they address scale and cash conversion at the same time. They are not enough to pass the current screen: the US framework requires ROIC at least 12%, free-cash-flow margin at least 12%, net debt/EBITDA below 3.0x and SBC dilution below 5% of revenue. Fatal flaws and red-team cases

Case one — AI demand is real but not durable. Data-center customers may diversify across grid, turbines, storage and other generation technologies. If utility interconnections accelerate or fuel prices move against Bloom's delivered cost, the urgency premium can shrink. The invalidation is not a headline; it is two consecutive quarters of backlog conversion or gross-margin deterioration alongside weaker new orders. [1] [12]

Case two — accounting growth outruns cash growth. Large shipments can raise revenue while inventory, receivables, guarantees and service obligations absorb capital. The red-team test is cumulative free cash flow after capex, not operating cash flow alone. The thesis remains disqualified until that calculation is positive and the margin clears 12%. [1] [6] [23]

Case three — valuation leaves no execution margin. A price-to-sales ratio reported at 30.25 and EV/EBITDA reported at 213.82 make the stock sensitive to any miss, even if the underlying market expands. The red-team conclusion is that a good company can still be a poor entry when the expected return depends on perfect delivery. A verified DCF or measured move is required; this dossier deliberately supplies neither because the cash inputs are incomplete. [15] [22]

Research ledger and measurement discipline

The run separates three kinds of evidence. The first is the frozen market ledger, which supplies the close, moving averages, RSI, ATR, volume multiple and trend label for 8 October 2026. The second is regulatory evidence: the 2025 Form 10-K, the 2025 earnings exhibit and the Q2 2026 quarterly filing. The third is commercial evidence, including customer announcements and the investor presentation archive. Mixing these layers would make a customer announcement look like a realized financial result, so each claim stays attached to its source class.

The ledger is useful because it gives a reproducible technical state. It is not a substitute for a filed income statement. Likewise, a 10-K is authoritative for reported history but does not provide today's price or a forward target. The dossier therefore leaves several cells as N/A. That is a deliberate control: the portfolio can re-run the same formulas when the next verified evidence arrives, rather than inheriting a number that was only plausible at the time it was written.

Definitions used in the gate

ROIC measures operating profit after tax against the capital required to produce it. Free-cash-flow margin divides cash left after capital expenditure by revenue. Net debt/EBITDA tests whether the balance sheet can carry its obligations from operating earnings. SBC/revenue measures how much equity compensation dilutes the economics of each dollar of sales. Each ratio answers a different question, so a pass on one cannot compensate for an unknown or fail on another.

Support is also defined mechanically. The 50-day average is a moving estimate of the intermediate trend; ATR is a volatility unit. A price 1.41 ATR above the 50-day average is not automatically expensive, but it is outside the configured standard entry band. The rule prevents the analyst from converting excitement about a theme into a market order. The position can be reconsidered after a retest, but the retest must hold.

Scenario map for an owner

Upside path. AI and high-performance-computing customers continue to need fast, reliable on-site power; Bloom converts its backlog into revenue while gross margin expands and service costs remain controlled. Positive free cash flow becomes repeatable, the four hard-gate ratios clear their floors, and the stock builds a new base above its long-term average. That is the path that would support a tactical re-entry, but it requires filed evidence rather than extrapolation.

Middle path. Demand remains healthy, yet projects ship unevenly and the stock moves sideways while investors wait for cash conversion. In that path BE can remain a useful research name without deserving a full-size allocation. The right response is patience: preserve the 50-day and 200-day references, monitor quarterly cash flow, and do not pay a premium for a backlog whose margin has not been demonstrated.

Downside path. A customer delays deployment, the utility grid catches up, input costs rise, or warranty and service requirements absorb the operating improvement. The market then compresses the multiple before the income statement visibly breaks. A close below the 200-day average, followed by weak fundamentals, would turn the monitor into a structural exit for an existing owner. The current run does not make a holding-specific sell recommendation because the user asked for the single-company underwriting dossier, not an account action.

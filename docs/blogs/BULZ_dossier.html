Before you read

BULZ is not an operating company. It is an unsecured note from Bank of Montreal linked to a technology index. It seeks three times the index return for one day, before costs. This report rejects it until price support, momentum, and contract checks all pass.

What the company does

The instrument, not a company

BULZ is an exchange-traded note, or ETN, which is an unsecured debt obligation whose value is linked to a reference index rather than a pool of assets held in a trust. Bank of Montreal is the issuer; an owner of BULZ is a creditor of the bank and does not own the fifteen index constituents directly.[4] The note trades on NYSE Arca under ticker BULZ and has a stated maturity of June 28, 2041, but the issuer may redeem or accelerate it under the legal terms.[5] That distinction matters: an equity fund can hold securities for its shareholders, while an ETN depends on the issuer's promise to pay the calculated amount.

The reference index is the Solactive FANG Innovation Index. Its stated design combines eight core names—Apple, Amazon, Meta Platforms, Alphabet, Microsoft, Netflix, NVIDIA, and Tesla—with seven additional liquid US-listed technology or innovation companies selected under the index rules.[2] Constituents are intended to be equally weighted, with scheduled rebalancing and reconstitution. The basket gives exposure to mega-cap platforms, semiconductors, software, automobiles, and internet businesses; it is therefore a concentrated growth and technology factor, not a broad US market portfolio.[3]

BULZ seeks a positive three-times multiple of the index's return for each trading day. The daily reset means that the result over a month or year is not three times the index's result over that period. If the index rises 10% one day and falls 10% the next, it ends down about 1%; a three-times daily note rises about 30% and then falls about 30%, ending down about 9%. This is path dependency, meaning that the order and size of daily moves affect the compounded result. The prospectus warns that this effect can be material, especially when volatility is high.[6]

Who pays, and what the holder receives

The holder pays the market price to a seller, not a subscription fee to a fund manager. The note's indicative value is calculated from the index move, the leverage factor, an investor fee, financing costs, and other adjustments described in the pricing supplement.[7] The published materials describe an investor fee of 0.95% per year and a financing charge linked to the Federal Funds Effective Rate plus 2.25%; the exact effective deduction can vary with the note's terms and market conditions.[8] A wide bid-ask spread, a premium or discount to indicative value, or a trading halt can add another cost that is not captured by the simple three-times slogan.

There is no operating revenue, recurring revenue, customer retention, backlog, profit margin, or cash left after operating needs generated by BULZ itself. Index constituents generate those measures, but their results belong to the underlying companies and are not a claim on the note holder. The note's economic engine is daily index exposure less financing, fees, rebalancing effects, and issuer credit risk. The correct question is not “is BULZ a good company?” but “is a short-term, three-times daily exposure to this volatile basket, at this price and with this contract, worth the risk?”

Glossary

A 50-day moving average is the average closing price over the last fifty sessions; a 200-day moving average averages two hundred sessions and is a slower trend reference. RSI, or relative strength index, is a bounded momentum measure comparing recent gains with recent losses. ATR, or average true range, estimates a typical daily trading range and helps express support distance in comparable units. Stage 2 means a markup phase in which price is generally above rising long-term averages; Stage 4 means a declining phase below weakening averages. A stop loss is a pre-set exit level used to limit damage, while a retest is a return to a broken resistance or support level to see whether it holds. Asymmetry is the ratio of potential gain to potential loss; the rule here is (target minus entry) divided by (entry minus stop).

BULZ combines daily leverage, financing cost, issuer credit, and market-liquidity risk. A daily objective cannot be treated as a long-term return promise, and a basket of stocks is not the same as owning those stocks. For a new investor, the practical advice is simple: do not buy this note merely because its chart is above its averages; wait for a fresh support test and a passing gate.

The issuer owes the payoff. The index sets the reference. The daily reset sets the path. Fees reduce value. Credit risk remains. Liquidity matters. This is a trading tool, not a savings product.

Why the business is strong or weak

Layer 1: binary hard gates

The US Layer 1 financial health floor uses return on capital, cash-flow margin, debt burden, and share-based pay impact for operating companies. BULZ cannot pass those tests because it has no operating income statement, no corporate cash flow, no operating-earnings measure, and no equity capital base. The issuer's balance sheet is not the note's balance sheet, and a holder cannot use Bank of Montreal's bank ratios as if they were BULZ's return on capital. The appropriate result is FAIL_INSTRUMENT_FINANCIAL_FLOOR (the note cannot be tested with company financial ratios), not an invented company ratio or a borrowed bank ratio.[9]

The technical floor is also not passed. The frozen ledger shows a close of $43.24, a 50-day average of $39.20, a 200-day average of $32.71, and a rising 50-day average. That is a Stage 2 shape. Yet the note sits 8.35 ATR above the 63-day base pivot, against a momentum-gate maximum of 1.2 ATR for Test A, the “in momentum now” test, and 1.0 ATR for Test B. The gate therefore records Test A fail on extension and Test B fail on distance from base, despite positive relative strength.[10]

The asymmetry floor is unverified and therefore fails. A target anchored to forward earnings or a three-year median valuation multiple cannot be applied to a debt note without earnings per share. A measured move from a valid, current base is also unavailable because the price is 8.35 ATR from the pivot and the gate requires a confirmed retest or breakout condition. Under the scoring configuration, an unanchored target is a rejection, so a speculative target would not cure this gate.[11]

The practical advice is to wait for a fresh bar, a support test, and a passing gate rather than chase a recent gain.

Three fatal flaws

Fatal flaw one—daily reset decay: a volatile sideways index can lose value in a three-times product even if the index finishes near its starting level. The investor bulletin on leveraged products describes this as a reason they are generally designed for short-term objectives rather than passive holding.[12]

Fatal flaw two—issuer credit: BULZ is unsecured debt. If Bank of Montreal cannot meet its obligation, the index's gains do not remove the credit claim. This risk is different from tracking error and different from a loss caused by the underlying stocks.[13]

Fatal flaw three—price and liquidity gap: a market quote is not always the same as indicative value. A volatile note can show a wide spread, a premium, or a discount, and early redemption or a market disruption can change the cash outcome. No current spread observation was verified in the research record, so the report treats the unknown as a risk rather than assuming it is small.[14]

For the reader, the result is practical. A good index can still produce a poor note return. A strong chart can still be too late. A low fee cannot remove daily reset risk. These points lead to the price test.

What the price is doing

Frozen momentum-gate result

The verified session date is 2026-10-08. BULZ has no verified 2026-10-09 instrument bar in the frozen data, so its technical readings use the 2026-10-08 bar and are disclosed as one session stale. The delay does not change the rejection; it means a fresh bar must be checked before any later decision.

The frozen snapshot for 2026-10-08 gives Test A, the test for being in momentum now, a fail. Its structure checks pass: $43.24 is above $39.20, which is above $32.71; the 50-day average is rising; the 200-day average is higher than it was 63 sessions earlier; and 63-day relative strength is +9.13 percentage points versus SPY. Test A still fails because the support-distance measure is 8.35 ATR, far beyond the 1.2 ATR limit.[16]

Test B, “entering momentum,” also fails. The 50-day average is rising 9.74% over 21 sessions, relative strength is rising 15.24% over 28 sessions, volume conditions pass, but the base-pivot distance is 8.35 ATR against a 1.0 ATR maximum. The gate's consequence is explicit: no allocation, however attractive the underlying basket, until a confirmed breakout of a base pivot or a retest that holds.

The chart would confirm the idea if price formed a new base, returned toward support without a structural break, then held a retest with improving volume and a passing gate. It would reject the idea if price closed below the selected stop structure, if the 50-day average rolled over below the 200-day average, or if the note's indicative value and market price separated materially. RSI at 53.13 is neutral; it neither rescues the failed gate nor signals an oversold bargain.

Why these estimates might be wrong

The bull assumption is that the underlying innovation basket trends upward in a sufficiently smooth path and that the note remains liquid near indicative value. That can be wrong even when the basket ends higher: alternating gains and losses compound poorly under a three-times daily reset. The evidence that would show the assumption wrong is a sequence of volatile reversals, widening spreads, or a persistent discount. We would know from the next daily indicative-value and market-price observations, plus the issuer's current product notice.[17]

The base assumption is that the 50-day and 200-day averages remain useful support references. It can fail because a leveraged note can gap through a moving average, and because the reference index may rebalance. The evidence is a close below the 200-day average accompanied by a falling 50-day average or a failed retest. We would know at the next verified exchange session and at the next scheduled index review.

The bear assumption is that a decline will reach the 200-day average before a more severe event occurs. It can fail because issuer credit, market disruption, or a trading halt changes the payoff independently of chart support. The evidence is a new prospectus supplement, issuer credit event, suspension notice, or a large indicative-value/market-price divergence. We would know when BMO, the exchange, or the SEC publishes the notice; the report does not assume that a stop can always be executed.

There is also a data gap. The current research pass verified the product documents and the frozen price ledger, but did not establish a five-year index return series, a real-time bid-ask spread, or a current credit spread for the issuer. Those are unresolved gaps, not inputs to be filled with estimates. A future review should fetch each item before changing the verdict.

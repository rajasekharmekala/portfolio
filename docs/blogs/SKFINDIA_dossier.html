Executive Summary & Rejection Synthesis

SKF India Limited (NSE: SKFINDIA, BSE: 500472) is a leading manufacturer of precision bearings and rotating equipment solutions for the automotive sector. In October 2025, SKF India completed a massive corporate restructuring, demerging its highly profitable, high-margin Industrial business into a separate entity—SKF India (Industrial) Limited—in a 1:1 share entitlement scheme.[13] Following the demerger ex-date, SKF India's share price adjusted from ~₹4,900+ down to ₹1,539.80, leaving the listed entity as a pure-play automotive bearing supplier.[14]

An institutional underwriting audit of the residual automotive entity reveals significant fundamental and technical deterioration:

  1. ROCE Margin Dilution Below Institutional Hurdle: Prior to the demerger, SKF India delivered consolidated ROCE of ~20–21%, propelled by high-margin industrial applications (wind energy, railways, metals, mining). The standalone automotive business delivers a significantly lower ROCE of 13.3%, failing our Layer 1 Financial Quality Floor (minimum 15.0% absolute, preferred >18.0% for Core Alpha).[15]
  2. Broken Technical Structure & Death Cross: At CMP ₹1,539.80, the stock is trapped beneath its falling 200-DMA (₹1,619.68). The 50-DMA (₹1,535.87) trades below the 200-DMA, exhibiting a structural Stage 1 consolidation / Stage 4 hangover with an anemic Relative Strength Rating of 34/100 against the Nifty 500.[16]
  3. Elevated Valuation for Commoditized Auto Portfolio: Trading at 36.7x trailing P/E for a business growing mid-single digits with sub-14% ROCE, the risk-reward asymmetry is completely distorted.

Under our rigorous adversarial underwriting protocol, SKF India fails Layer 1 Gating Hurdles. We issue a formal 🔴 REJECTED / ARCHIVE verdict, archiving the candidate to Archive_Rejected and withholding capital until the demerged Industrial entity lists and establishes independent price discovery.

1. Corporate History & The 2025 Demerger Separation

SKF India was established in 1961 as Associated Bearing Company Limited, a joint venture between Aktiebolaget SKF (Sweden) and Tata Group, later becoming a consolidated subsidiary of AB SKF.[17] Over six decades, the company built an extensive presence across automotive (2W, passenger cars, commercial vehicles, tractors) and industrial (heavy engineering, power transmission, wind turbines, railways) markets.[18]

In December 2023, the board announced a comprehensive Scheme of Arrangement to demerge the Industrial business into SKF India (Industrial) Ltd, approved by the NCLT Mumbai bench in 2025 with an ex-date of October 15, 2025.[19] While intended to unlock shareholder value by creating two focused entities, the immediate operational consequence for the remaining listed entity (SKF India Ltd) was the loss of its highest-margin, moat-protected industrial business units.[20]

2. Standalone Automotive Operations & Product Portfolio

The post-demerger SKF India Ltd operates manufacturing facilities in Pune, Chinchwad, and Haridwar dedicated exclusively to automotive applications:[21]

  • Two-Wheeler & Three-Wheeler Bearings: Deep groove ball bearings (DGBB) and steering bearings for Bajaj Auto, Hero MotoCorp, and Honda.
  • Passenger Car Wheel Hub Units: Integrated Hub 1, Hub 2, and Hub 3 bearing units for Maruti Suzuki, Hyundai, and Tata Motors.
  • Commercial Vehicle & Tractor Applications: Tapered roller bearings and cylindrical roller bearings for Tata Motors, Ashok Leyland, and Mahindra Tractors.

3. Auto Bearing Industry Economics & OEM Margin Pressures

Unlike industrial replacement bearings which command 22–28% gross margins and pricing power, automotive OEM supply is characterized by fierce pricing pressure:

  • Annual Cost Reduction Mandates: Auto OEMs enforce mandatory 2–3% annual price downs.[22]
  • Special Bearing Steel Inflation: Bearing steel (SAE 52100 / 100Cr6) constitutes over 50% of raw material cost; price pass-through to auto OEMs is subject to a 90–120 day lag.[23]
  • EV Transition Threat on Drivetrain Bearings: Electric vehicles eliminate internal combustion engine bearings, transmissions, and multi-speed gearboxes, reducing total bearing count per vehicle by 35–45%.[24]

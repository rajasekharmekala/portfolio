0. Executive Summary & Investment Pitch

GRP Limited converts end-of-life tyres, tubes and rubber waste into reclaim rubber, and also develops upscaled polyamide and engineered die-cut products.[1][2] The company reports relationships with eight of the ten largest global tyre manufacturers. The customer qualification asset is meaningful, but does not guarantee pricing power when scrap costs rise faster than customer resets.

Consolidated sales were ₹461 Cr in FY24, ₹550 Cr in FY25 and ₹535 Cr in FY26, while PAT moved ₹23 Cr, ₹31 Cr and ₹3 Cr.[3] FY26 operating profit was ₹41 Cr and OPM 8%; borrowings were ₹208 Cr. At CMP ₹1,922 and P/E around 150×, the stock discounts a recovery not visible in current earnings. Action verdict: REJECTED.

Conclusion: The circular-materials theme is investable; GRPLTD at this evidence point is not. Reconsider only after ROCE exceeds 15%, OPM normalizes above 12%, debt falls and a Stage 2 advance is confirmed.

1. History & Milestones

Established in 1974, GRP built its identity around recycling rubber waste into industrially usable reclaim rubber.[4] It expanded into engineered rubber, polyamide, custom die forms, composites, crumb rubber, pyrolysis and recovered carbon black.[5] FY25 commissioning of crumb assets and capex for pyro/crumb projects created a vertical-integration thesis, but also introduced depreciation, start-up losses and debt. Management has described a roadmap from roughly 88 KTA toward 110 KTA reclaim-rubber capacity.[6]

2. Operations & Revenue Model

Reclaim rubber: size reduction, fibre/steel separation, devulcanisation and finishing. The core vertical contributes about 89% of revenue and serves tyre, conveyor, component and industrial-rubber customers in 65+ countries.[7] Engineering plastics: upscaled polyamide and die forms broaden the addressable market; FY25 commentary indicated 23% volume growth.[8] Pyrova/ELT: tyre-derived oil, recovered carbon black and steel provide a higher-value option, but utilization and offtake—not installed capacity—determine returns.[9]

Tyre qualification is slow because compound changes affect durability, rolling resistance and warranty performance. Once approved, customers may be sticky; however, raw-material inflation and delayed price corrections in synthetic reclaim can compress margins.[10] FY26 debtor days were 75 and the cash-conversion cycle 105 days.[11]

3. Sector Operating Dynamics

Reclaim rubber intersects tyre demand, commodity spreads and environmental regulation. Indian reclaim-rubber consumption has been reported to grow faster than overall rubber consumption, while EPR rules encourage documented recycled content.[12][13]

8. Fatal Flaws & Invalidation

  1. ROCE collapsed from 18% to 7% while assets expanded.
  2. Input inflation and pricing lag can keep synthetic-reclaim spreads negative.
  3. Borrowings ₹208 Cr, CCC 105 days and FY26 FCF negative create balance-sheet risk.
Invalidate on OPM <7% for two quarters, CFO/PAT <.50, ROCE <10%, debt >₹225 Cr without EBITDA growth, close below ₹1,480, loss of Tier-1 homologation, tariff disruption, or Pyrova/GCSL failure to reach 75% utilization.

9. Portfolio Allocation

Allocate 0% new capital until gates clear. Existing exposure, if any, is an event-driven monitoring position, not a core compounder. A replacement should have ROCE >15%, D/E <.70, positive FCF and a Stage 2 chart. Waiting has low opportunity cost because valuation already assumes normalization.

Methodology note 1

This dossier uses consolidated audited tables, explicit source links and conservative underwriting rules.

Reading the framework

The framework runs in three layers, and a company must clear each one before the next is read. Layer 1 holds the hard floors. They are a return on capital of at least 15 percent, borrowing below 0.70 times the owners' capital, three-year cash from operations at least 0.70 times reported profit, a chart that shows a real uptrend, a momentum test that passes, and an expected gain at least 3.0 times the expected loss on a verified target. Layer 2 would score three to five operating measures that drive this particular business. Layer 3 would decide the entry posture from how far the price sits above a support level. A failure at Layer 1 ends the review, which is why this dossier does not score Layers 2 and 3.

The momentum gate is a code-based test that reads the frozen price file for the session. It asks two questions. Test A asks whether the stock is already in momentum, meaning a Stage 2 uptrend with rising averages and relative strength against the benchmark. Test B asks whether the stock is entering momentum from a quiet base, with a pivot that is close and volume that is drying up and then expanding. A candidate must pass one of the two. Failing both means no allocation, however good the business. The thresholds are marked in the configuration file as needing calibration, so the gate is applied as written and the result is reported as it is.

The support measure uses the average true range, shortened to ATR. ATR is the typical daily price swing over the last 14 sessions, and it works like a ruler. A distance from support of 1.2 ATR means the price has moved about one and a fifth typical days' swings away from the level. Under the support ladder in the configuration, a stock within 0.5 ATR of support is at full-size band, within 1.0 ATR is at standard band, and beyond 1.2 ATR is in the no-buy band, where the rule is to wait. The entry posture is only read once Layers 1 and 2 pass, so a stock in the no-buy band is not a buy for this report.

The verdict labels follow from these rules. IMMEDIATE_BUY is given to a Layer 1 and Layer 2 pass with the price at support. ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP is given to a pass with the price near support, and WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT to a pass with the price extended. REJECTED is given when any Layer 1 floor fails or support has broken. QUALIFIED_DEFERRED is given to a candidate that passes every layer but has no free slot in its book. Each verdict in this dossier names the rule that produced it and the number that triggered it.

Two rules govern the dates. First, every figure carries its as-of date, which is the period the figure describes. A figure is not moved to a newer date to pass a check. Second, a research item older than 60 days is a stale as-of exception. The exception is disclosed in the run notes, and the figure is kept at its true period. Most of the company's latest figures are older than 60 days because the last annual report closed in March 2026 and the last quarter closed in June 2026, so the exceptions are listed in full at the end.

Terms used in this report

This chapter explains each technical and financial term once, with an everyday comparison, so the later chapters can use the words without stopping to define them. The terms appear in the order the framework needs them.

ROCE, or return on capital employed, asks how much operating profit a business earns for each rupee tied up in its plants, machines and working stock. A figure of 21.8 percent means about 22 paise of operating profit a year for every rupee the business uses (Mar 2026) [1]. ROE, or return on equity, is the shareholder's version of the same question: profit after tax against the owners' money. It was 17.7 percent for FY26 [1].

Debt-to-equity is borrowings divided by the owners' capital. Borrowings were ₹55 crore and equity capital plus reserves were ₹572 crore at March 2026, so the ratio is about 0.10 [1]. The framework's limit for a business that is not a lender is below 0.70. Cash from operations is the cash the business brings in from its activity before spending on equipment. Comparing it with reported profit shows whether profit has arrived as cash, and the three-year ratio is the framework's cash test.

OPM, or operating profit margin, is the share of each sales rupee left after the costs of making and selling the product. It was 19 percent in FY26 [1]. P/E, or price-to-earnings, divides the share price by profit per share. A P/E of 32.0 means investors pay ₹32 for each rupee of a year's earnings (13 Oct 2026) [1].

ATR, the average true range, is the typical daily price swing over the last 14 sessions. The ledger ATR is ₹10.63, about 3.8 percent of the close. RSI, the relative strength index, is a gauge from 0 to 100 of how fast a price has recently risen or fallen. The 14-day reading is 53.8, which is neither overbought nor oversold.

Stage 2 is the rising phase of a stock's cycle, when the price holds above averages that are themselves rising. Stage 3 is the topping phase that often follows, when the rise stalls and the averages flatten. Stage 4 is the falling phase, when the price breaks below averages that are heading down. Base is a sideways stretch in which a stock trades in a narrow range before its next move, like a car idling at a junction. The base low used here is the lowest price over the last 63 sessions, ₹248.80.

Stop loss is the price at which a holding would be sold to cap the loss. It sits below the level at which the trend counts as failed. Measured move is a target found by adding the height of a base to the level it breaks out from, which chart readers use to estimate how far a move might run. Asymmetry is the ratio of expected gain to expected loss, so a ratio of 3.0 means three rupees of gain for each rupee at risk to the stop.

Pledge means a promoter has offered shares as security for a loan. If the loan is not repaid, the lender can sell them, so a high pledge is a risk to the shareholder even when the business is sound. Relative strength compares a stock's return with the benchmark index over the same window. The benchmark here is the Nifty 50. The 50-day and 200-day averages are the average closing prices over 50 and 200 sessions. A price above a rising line is in an uptrend, and a price below a falling line is in a downtrend.

How the framework works for this company

The framework is a set of hurdles that a company must clear in a fixed order, and the order is the point. Layer 1 holds the hard floors. A failure at any of them ends the review with a REJECTED verdict, and the later layers are not read. This is deliberate. A good business with a broken chart is not a good allocation, and a rule that let the business quality excuse the chart would let almost any stock through, because almost every business looks sound when its story is told well.

Each candidate is tested against a strategy bucket. CORE_ALPHA is for businesses expected to compound over three to five years. TACTICAL_MOMENTUM is for shorter moves of three to twelve months. It needs a Stage 2 trend above rising 50-day and 200-day averages. ADF Foods is tested under CORE_ALPHA, which matches its profile as a cash-generating business with a multi-year growth record, and it is also tested under TACTICAL_MOMENTUM for completeness.

Asymmetry and target anchoring

The upside-to-downside ratio compares how far the price could rise with how far it could fall before the stop is hit. The formula is the target minus the close, divided by the close minus the stop, and it is taken from the configuration file. A target without an anchor is treated as unverified, and an unverified target fails the floor. The rule is strict because a target picked to look attractive can make almost any stock appear to have room to run.

The framework accepts two anchors. The first is a forward earnings forecast multiplied by a three-year median price-to-earnings ratio. The second is a technical measured move from a base. This run could not verify the first. Three searches for a forward earnings-per-share figure, and three for a three-year median P/E, returned only conflicting snippets, and none was a stated forecast with a basis that could be read [1]. The forward-earnings anchor is therefore recorded as not found, and the measured move is used.

The measured move is built from the ledger. The 52-week high is ₹346.95 and the 63-session base low is ₹248.80, so the height of the advance is ₹98.15 and the base target is ₹445.10. That is 58.3 percent above the close. The working is simple, but the anchor needs a caveat. The ledger does not record the top of the base, so the 52-week high is used as the nearest recorded level from which the base could resolve. The anchor is therefore unverified: it is a chart judgement rather than a measured fact, and the price has not yet broken out of its range, so the ratio is not counted as a clean pass. If the target were set at the 52-week high itself, the ratio would be 1.45, which fails the floor. The result therefore depends on the choice of anchor, and the dossier says so.

Fatal-flaw red team

The red team asks what would make the verdict wrong, and the answer should be sharp. Three fatal flaws were tested against the evidence. Each is a reason the rejection could be wrong, or a reason a buy might fail even after the stock passes the gates.

Flaw one: the recovery is a bounce inside a falling average

The first flaw is that the recent recovery is the start of a new uptrend, and the momentum gate is simply early. The price is 3.8 percent above its 50-day average, has risen 1.06 percent over the month, and the 28-session relative strength is positive at plus 9.69 points. If that continues, the stock would pass Test B on relative strength and might clear the structure test later. The evidence against it is the same as the gate's reading. The 50-day average is still falling, having lost 4.69 percent in 21 sessions, and the market breadth in the ledger reads Downtrend, with 21.5 percent of stocks above their 50-day averages. A bounce inside a falling average is the case the gate was built to refuse, so the flaw is real as a possibility and does not change the present verdict.

Flaw two: the profit growth is flattered and the cash is thin

The second flaw is that the earnings are weaker than the headline. StockWatch reports that the June quarter's reported profit of ₹17.29 crore, up 13.4 percent, includes a US import-tariff refund of ₹7.29 crore recognised in the quarter, and that the underlying profit is closer to ₹11.9 crore, about 31 percent below the reported figure, a fall of about 22 percent year on year [2]. A further ₹9.28 crore of the refund remains unresolved, pending customer arrangements, and could still flow through the accounts or be passed back. The FY26 cash ratio is 0.64, and the three-year figure of 0.71 clears the floor by ₹1.9 crore [1]. The answer to this flaw is that the three-year cash test still passes and that the company's own FY27 guidance band of ₹800 to 850 crore is the lower case. Those points carry weight, but they do not rescue a stock that fails the chart.

Flaw three: the overseas book and the litigation

The third flaw is that the overseas business, which is now about 28 percent of revenue, earns little. StockWatch reports that the overseas subsidiaries were roughly breakeven to loss before the tariff refund, and that the auditors' review flagged a combined net loss of ₹1.16 crore across three unreviewed overseas entities in the June quarter [2]. On 10 October 2026 the company disclosed under the listing rules that a US subsidiary had settled judgments of US$2.31 million for US$1.75 million, with US$450,000 received by 9 October [1]. The settlement is a cost the business has now recognised, and its size is small against the revenue base, but it shows that the export arm carries legal exposure. The ownership picture is steadier. The promoter group holds 36.13 percent as at June 2026 and has no pledged shares in the last three reported quarters, against 1.26 percent in September 2025 [3]. That is a point in the company's favour, and it is the reason the pledge is not the flaw here.

What would make the bear case wrong is a clean run of Stage 2 signals: a 50-day average that rises above ₹284.20, relative strength turning positive, volume expanding on a session, and a quarter in which the overseas book breaks even. The record does not show any of these yet, so the flaws stand and the verdict stands with them.

0. Executive Summary & Investment Pitch

Tinna Rubber and Infrastructure Limited (NSE: TINNARUBR) is Asia's largest integrated processor and recycler of End-of-Life Tyres (ELT) and a domestic market leader in engineered crumb rubber modifier (CRM), Crumb Rubber Modified Bitumen (CRMB), Polymer Modified Bitumen (PMB), reclaimed rubber, and recycled steel wire shots[1]. Operating advanced recycling facilities across Pan-India (Panipat, Wada, Haldia, Gummidipoondi, Kala Amb) and an international joint-venture facility in Oman, Tinna converts hazardous scrap tyres into high-performance industrial circular feedstocks for the road construction, automotive tyre compounding, and conveyor belting sectors[2].

The investment thesis is driven by statutory regulatory tailwinds: the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (MoRTH) mandate requiring 10%–15% modified bitumen in National Highway construction, and the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) tyre recycling regime, which creates a lucrative secondary market for EPR credit certificates[3]. Tinna has driven a 5-year sales CAGR of 33% and 3-year PAT CAGR of 36.2%, expanding TTM revenue to ₹572 crore and net profit to ₹62 crore, while lifting EBITDA margins to 17%–19% through higher value-added reclaimed rubber and international geographic expansion[4].

1. History & Corporate Milestones

Founded in 1977 by the Sekhri family, Tinna Rubber initially began operations in footwear manufacturing and leather goods before strategically pivoting into crumb rubber manufacturing from discarded truck tyres in the late 1990s[6]. In 2002, the company pioneered the commercialization of Crumb Rubber Modified Bitumen (CRMB) in India, collaborating with state-owned oil refiners (IOCL, HPCL, BPCL) to blend rubber additives for resilient, crack-resistant highway asphalt.

Over the last five years, Tinna has transformed its business model from a commodity rubber grinder into an engineered materials platform. Milestones include commissioning a fully automated devulcanization reclaimed rubber plant in Wada, Maharashtra, entering into an international joint venture in Sohar, Oman to tap Middle Eastern and European ELT feedstock, and establishing automated steel radial tyre wire cleaning lines to recover high-tensile steel wire for foundries and shot-blasting industries[7].

2. Operations & Revenue Model (How the Business Operates)

2.1 Product Lines & Value-Add Cascade

Tinna achieves 100% material utilization of waste radial tyres through a zero-waste recycling cascade:

  • Modified Bitumen & Emulsions (45% of Revenue): CRMB, PMB, and micro-surfacing bitumen emulsions sold directly to national highway EPC concessionaires (NHAI projects, L&T, Dilip Buildcon, GR Infra) and oil marketing companies. Extends road pavement life by 40%–50% and reduces thermal cracking[8].
  • Reclaimed & Micronized Rubber (32% of Revenue): High-tensile devulcanized reclaimed rubber sheets and ultrafine rubber powder supplied to major tyre manufacturers (Apollo Tyres, CEAT, MRF, Bridgestone) to replace virgin natural rubber in non-critical tyre carcass components at 30% lower cost[9].
  • Recycled Steel Wire & Cut Wire Shots (15% of Revenue): High-tensile bead steel and cord steel extracted during shredding, processed into abrasive steel shots for metal casting, shot peening, and blast cleaning in the automotive forging sector[10].
  • EPR Certificates & Environmental Credits (8% of Revenue): Pure margin credit trading under CPCB's statutory EPR portal, generated through verified ELT recycling volumes[11].

2.2 Unit Economics & Raw Material Sourcing

Tinna sources waste radial truck tyres globally (importing ~60% from the Middle East, US, and Europe, with ~40% domestic collection). ELT scrap is purchased at ₹14–₹18/kg. Through mechanical shredding, ambient grinding, and chemical devulcanization, the output is sold at a blended realization of ₹38–₹75/kg (with specialized reclaim fetching ₹80–₹110/kg), generating gross margins of 38%–44% and conversion EBITDA of ₹8,000–₹12,000 per MT of processed tyre[12].

3. Sector-Specific Operating Dynamics

India generates over 2.0 million metric tonnes of scrap tyres annually, while global ELT generation exceeds 1.5 billion tyres per year. The statutory ban on polluting pyrolysis oil plants and strict CPCB enforcement of EPR regulations has eliminated unorganized scrap processors, consolidating feedstock supply into organized compliance majors like Tinna Rubber. Moreover, national highway expansion (Bharatmala Pariyojana) and higher bitumen usage intensity provide structural multi-year volume visibility[13].

8. Adversarial Red-Team: 3 Fatal Flaws & Invalidation Triggers

  1. Fatal Flaw 1: Raw Material Scarcity & Global Feedstock Inflation: Surging global ocean freight or competition for scrap radial tyres could inflate imported tyre purchase costs. Mitigation: Tinna has built captive domestic collection networks across 5 regional hubs and established the Oman JV close to Middle East tyre waste supplies.
  2. Fatal Flaw 2: National Highway Tender Slowdowns: Monsoon seasonality or pauses in MoRTH highway contract awarding could temporarily defer modified bitumen dispatches. Mitigation: Reclaimed rubber sales to global tyre majors and steel wire sales to foundries provide steady non-highway revenue buffers.
  3. Fatal Flaw 3: Policy Reversal on Crumb Rubber Blending: Any dilution in MoRTH's circular bitumen blending standards. Mitigation: Modified bitumen improves road lifespan by >40% with proven lifecycle cost savings, making reversal economically irrational.
Hard Invalidation Triggers:

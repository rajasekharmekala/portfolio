1 Executive Summary & Underwriting Decision

Constellation Energy Corporation (NASDAQ: CEG), headquartered in Baltimore, Maryland, is the largest producer of clean, carbon-free commercial electricity in the United States, operating a premier commercial nuclear generation fleet of 21 reactors across 12 operational sites producing over 22,000 megawatts (MW) of baseload capacity.[1] For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025, Constellation reported consolidated revenue of $25,533.0 million, generating GAAP operating income of $3,086.0 million and GAAP net income attributable to common shareholders of $2,319.0 million ($7.40 diluted GAAP EPS; $9.39 Adjusted Operating EPS).[2] The company generated $4,125.0 million in operating cash flow and $3,010.0 million in Free Cash Flow (11.79% FCF margin; 3-year cumulative FCF conversion of 14.0%).[3]

Constellation has emerged as a central pillar of the artificial intelligence infrastructure boom following its landmark September 2024 announcement of a 20-year Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) with Microsoft Corporation to restart the 835 MW Three Mile Island Unit 1 nuclear reactor, renamed the Crane Clean Energy Center (CCEC), scheduled for commercial operation in 2028.[4] The company also benefits from the Section 45U Nuclear Production Tax Credit (PTC) established under the Inflation Reduction Act, which establishes a federally guaranteed gross margin floor of $15.00/MWh for eligible nuclear generation through 2032.[5]

2 Corporate History & Leadership Governance

Constellation Energy Corporation was established as an independent publicly traded entity on February 1, 2022, following its spin-off from Exelon Corporation (NASDAQ: EXC), separating Exelon's regulated utility businesses from its merchant power generation and customer supply operations.[9] The company inherited the largest privately owned nuclear fleet in the world, with facilities located primarily across the Mid-Atlantic, Midwest, and New York competitive wholesale power markets (PJM, NYISO, and ERCOT).[10]

The management team is led by President and Chief Executive Officer Joseph Dominguez, who previously served as CEO of ComEd and has been a prominent national advocate for valuing nuclear power's carbon-free reliability in wholesale power market design and federal tax policy.[11] Management has executed a disciplined capital allocation strategy focused on fleet life extensions (securing 20-year Subsequent License Renewals from the Nuclear Regulatory Commission), power uprates, and long-term bilateral clean energy contracts with technology hyperscalers.[12]

Corporate governance is characterized by strong board oversight, robust nuclear safety review committees, and alignment of executive compensation with operational reliability (capacity factor >94%), financial performance, and shareholder capital returns (dividends and share repurchases).[13]

3 Business Model, Unit Economics & Ecosystem Monetization

Constellation operates an integrated energy generation and competitive retail marketing model, organized across two core operational segments:

  1. Generation Segment: Owns and operates approximately 32,400 MW of generating capacity, comprising 22,100 MW of nuclear capacity, alongside natural gas, oil, hydroelectric, wind, and solar assets.[14] The nuclear fleet operates with industry-leading capacity factors averaging 94.6% to 95.2%, generating ultra-reliable 24/7 baseload power at marginal production costs below $30.00/MWh.[15]
  2. Commercial & Industrial (C&I) Customer Supply: Supplies electricity, natural gas, and clean energy management products to approximately three-fourths of the Fortune 100, structuring long-term fixed-price power contracts, hourly carbon-matching PPAs, and co-located data center solutions.[16]

The unit economics of nuclear power have undergone a structural paradigm shift driven by AI datacenter demand. Hyper-scalers building multi-gigawatt compute clusters require round-the-clock, zero-carbon electricity that intermittent wind and solar cannot provide without prohibitive battery storage costs.[17] Under the 20-year Microsoft PPA for the Crane Clean Energy Center, Microsoft is contracted to purchase 100% of the 835 MW output at a substantial premium to prevailing PJM wholesale power prices, providing guaranteed long-term cash flows with zero merchant commodity risk.[18]

RNIT AI Solutions Limited (BSE: 517286, Scrip ID: RNITAI, formerly Autopal Industries Limited) is an Indian enterprise artificial intelligence, biometric authentication, and e-governance software architecture enterprise headquartered in Hyderabad, Telangana.[1][5] The company represents a court-mandated corporate restructuring executed under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC, 2016). Formerly an insolvent automotive lighting producer in Jaipur, the listed shell was revived through a resolution plan approved by the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), Jaipur Bench, on September 23, 2024.[2][3] The restructuring cancelled 100% of legacy promoter equity, enforced a 75% capital reduction on public shares, and executed the reverse amalgamation of Hyderabad technology firm RNIT Solutions & Services Limited (established in 2019 by IIT alumnus Raja Srinivas Nandigam).[4][6][28]

Rebranded as RNIT AI Solutions in February 2025, the company operates across public-sector digital identity infrastructure.[5] Its proprietary software suite—anchored by Faceify (AI workforce attendance and access control), RNIT FRS (high-throughput facial recognition engine), and modular domain architectures including RIMS (Real-time Identification Management System), EMS (Education Management System), and HDMS (Health Department Management System)—has processed over 2.5 billion biometric authentications across Indian state government departments.[7][13][15][32] The company recently secured a state-wide mandate from the Andhra Pradesh State Skill Development Corporation (APSSDC) for a Generative AI conversational skill assessment platform.[12]

In FY 2026, RNIT AI Solutions delivered net sales of ₹51.50 crore, operating profit margins of 30.72%, and net profit of ₹12.02 crore, generating a 20.2% ROCE supported by a net cash treasury of ₹38.65 crore.[7][11] However, within the portfolio, RNITAI represents 22.58% of total portfolio equity (10,000 shares @ ₹55.33 avg, CMP ₹105.42, +90.54% unrealized gain), violating the 15.0% single-stock risk ceiling.[27] Trading at 72.4x P/E on BSE with a reward-to-risk asymmetry of 1.72:1 (failing the ≥3.5:1 tactical hurdle), fiduciary discipline mandates an immediate TRIM_PROFIT_BOOKING prescription of 40% to 50% of the holding to harvest ₹4.24L to ₹5.30L and redeploy into Core Alpha compounders resting directly AT_SUPPORT.[34][35]

1. Founding Vision, Corporate Transformation & History

RNIT AI Solutions Limited reflects an NCLT-sanctioned corporate restructuring under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC).[2][5] Predecessor entity Autopal Industries Limited was incorporated in November 1985 in Jaipur, Rajasthan, producing halogen bulbs and auxiliary lighting for automotive OEMs.[2]

By the mid-2010s, the automotive transition to LED systems rendered Autopal's glass and filament manufacturing lines obsolete. Facing Asian imports, operational losses, and debt, Autopal's production halted, its net worth eroded, and the company defaulted.[3][31] On September 15, 2022, the NCLT Jaipur Bench admitted Autopal into CIRP under Section 7 of the IBC.[2] A consortium led by tech entrepreneur Raja Srinivas Nandigam (founder of Hyderabad-based RNIT Solutions & Services Ltd) and Vivek Kumar Ratakonda submitted a resolution plan to merge RNIT's profitable biometric software business into the listed corporate shell.[3][4]

2.2 Edge-to-Cloud Biometric Processing Pipeline & Data Security

Rather than transmitting raw facial photographs across congested networks, RNIT deploys lightweight deep convolutional neural network (CNN) models onto Android devices and Face-Terminals.[7] The device detects landmarks, verifies liveness against spoofing, and extracts a 512-dimensional vector hash. 3. Sector Dynamics: Generative AI, Cloud Infrastructure & Indian Enterprise Adoption

The operating backdrop for Indian enterprise software is driven by national digital public infrastructure expansion, cybersecurity regulations, and the rapid deployment of domain-specific artificial intelligence.[16][17]

3.1 The Sovereign Cloud & Public Sector AI Mandate (Digital India & DPDPA 2023)

Enacted in August 2023, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA 2023) mandates that critical citizen biometric records must be stored within domestic sovereign borders, prescribing fines up to ₹250 crore for unauthorized cross-border transfer.[19] This framework prevents foreign public cloud APIs from handling unvetted state biometric verification, directing demand toward accredited domestic providers like RNIT that offer localized cryptographic hashing and MeitY-compliant storage.[16][19]

3.2 Enterprise AI Market Trajectory & Hardware vs Software Spend

According to Gartner and IDC India, enterprise AI expenditure in India is expanding at a 31.5% CAGR (2024–2028), projected to exceed $6.4 billion by 2028.[17][18] While the initial investment wave concentrated on hardware and compute assemblers (Netweb Technologies, E2E Networks), enterprise capital is increasingly pivoting toward high-margin software application architects who address specific governance and workforce bottlenecks.[17][23][24]

3.3 The Secular Shift from Fingerprint Scanners to Edge Facial Recognition

Indian biometric programs historically relied on optical fingerprint sensors under early Aadhaar implementations. However, manual labor degrades fingerprint ridges, dust fouls optical glass, and hardware maintenance on millions of fragile USB dongles drained state budgets.[13][32] Contactless facial recognition software operating on existing camera smartphones and tablets has emerged as the replacement technology, cutting total cost of ownership by over 60% and increasing verification speed from 4–5 to over 25 citizens per minute.[15][32]

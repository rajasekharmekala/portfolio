What the company does

This chapter explains the business before any number is tested. The company describes itself as the largest AI-based facial recognition software provider to the Indian government[1]. It sells that software as a subscription, which is called software as a service, or SaaS. The buyer rents the software on a recurring basis rather than buying a one-time licence, which means revenue repeats as long as the contract runs. The company says it has processed more than 3 billion facial templates across more than 300 departments[12].

Recurring fees are a good model if the government keeps renewing. The demand comes from identity checks that the state already runs. UIDAI, which runs India's Aadhaar identity system, describes face authentication as one step in an identity flow on its website[22]. The national AI and digital government portals describe the wider programme that funds this kind of work[23][24]. These pages confirm the category, not the company's share of it.

Profit after tax (PAT) for FY26 was ₹12.02 crore[3][12]. We could not verify the split between government and private clients, because no retrieved filing gives that breakdown.

The company's investor presentation for the year ended March 31, 2026 is indexed on Trendlyne[9], and StocksSena's filings index covers the company's annual reports and earnings material[11]. The company has a corporate history that matters for this read. Eduinvesting says the company was until recently Autopal Industries Limited, and that the name changed alongside a restructuring[12]. Brickwork's rating note uses the same former name[15]. The second tranche was issued under an NCLT plan, and the National Company Law Tribunal is the forum that handles insolvency and restructuring cases[12].

Why the business is strong or weak

This chapter sorts the company's strengths from its weaknesses, using the numbers that the gates and the scorecard will test. The first measure is ROCE (return on capital employed), which shows how much profit the business earns on the money tied up in it. A higher ROCE means the company needs less money to grow.

What is strong

The company's Screener header prints ROCE of 20.2 percent[1]. The Value Research annual table gives 14.29 percent for FY26[6], and that is the published figure we use. Our own rebuild from Value Research's EBIT of ₹16.77 crore against equity plus borrowings gives 10.7 to 13.7 percent, depending on whether closing or average capital is used[6]. The second measure is operating margin, which is the share of each rupee of sales left after the running costs of the business are paid. For this company, the cleanest version is EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) divided by sales. EBITDA for FY26 was ₹20.77 crore on sales of ₹51.50 crore, which is about 40 percent[6]. Screener's FY26 operating margin of 40 percent agrees[1].

The latest quarter is the best test of whether that margin is holding. On Mint's after-depreciation basis the June quarter would fall below the 25 percent test, and that is disclosed rather than hidden.

Net cash is the third strength. Cash and bank balances were ₹28.68 crore, against borrowings of ₹18.04 crore at the end of FY26[12]. The company is not carrying a heavy debt load on those figures, whatever its history.

The record does not show how much of the earlier fall came from sales and how much from reclassifications. Pledged shares are reported at 0.0 percent[8].

The third weakness is credit. Brickwork Ratings reaffirmed the bank loan rating at BWR D, which is its default category, for ₹14.44 crore of facilities[15]. Value Research gives ROE of 11.13 percent for FY26[6], and StocksSena gives 11.5 percent[10]. Screener's header prints 16.6 percent[1]. ROE is not a gate, but it confirms that the returns are weaker than the header suggests.

Debt: borrowings against equity

debt-to-equity is the company's borrowings divided by its shareholders' equity. A value below 0.70 is the gate. Borrowings were ₹18.04 crore and equity was ₹139.04 crore at the end of FY26[6][12], which gives a debt-to-equity of 0.13[6]. Layer 1: the hard gates

Layer 1 is a set of pass-or-fail tests that must all pass before a name can be bought. Four terms appear in the technical test. A Stage 2 is the uptrend phase, when a stock rises above rising moving averages. A Stage 4 is the downtrend phase, when the price falls below falling averages. A 50-day moving average is the average closing price over the last 50 sessions, and a 200-day moving average is the same measure over 200 sessions. A death cross is when the 50-day average falls below the 200-day average. The target test uses an asymmetry ratio, which is the reward from the target divided by the risk to the stop. The risk is measured from a stop loss, which is a price set in advance at which the position is sold to cap the loss.

At 23.0 percent, the position must fall to 15 percent or less. That needs a sale of at least 3,478 shares[31][32]. A minimum sale leaves no room: any rise in the price would put the weight back over the cap, because a 3,478-share sale sits at the cap. We therefore recommend 5,000 shares, which is 50 percent and the top of the band[32]. The reasons are the thin tape and the unresolved rating. The table shows the position before and after the sale.

The number: stop level at 83.57

The stop level is the price at which the remaining holding is reviewed for sale. It is set at 83.57, which is the swing low of 85.69 less half an ATR of 4.24, or 2.12[30].

How it was calculated

The tactical stop rule places the stop below the lowest of the swing low and the 20-day and 50-day averages, minus half an ATR[32]. The lowest of those levels here is the swing low at 85.69[30]. So 85.69 less 2.12 gives 83.57[30].

The inputs

The swing low of 85.69, the ATR of 4.24 and the moving averages come from the facts ledger built on Yahoo Finance daily bars[30]. The rule is in the scoring configuration[32].

The range

Because the ATR is 4.24, a normal day's swing can move the price by about 4 rupees[30]. The level of 83.57 sits 24.43 rupees, or 22.6 percent, below the close[30]. On a thin tape a sale at that level is not guaranteed, so the level is a review point. A gap through it would lose more than the 2.6 percent of net worth shown above, and the de-rating case in the outcomes section is the worse-case figure to read against it.

If the number is off

If the swing low is wrong, the stop moves with it. If the ledger's 52-week high of 130.20[30] is an error, the stop does not change, because we believe the swing low is built from closing prices, but the distance-from-high reading does. If a close below 83.57 happens on heavy volume, the remaining shares are reviewed for sale that day, and the verdict moves to REPLACE_UNDERPERFORMER.

The plan, step by step

Place one limit sell for 5,000 shares at 108.00 or better, valid for five sessions[16]. If the order has not filled after five sessions, cancel it and re-price it once at the close less one ATR, which is about 103.76[30]. If the price closes below 92.17 before the sale is complete, move the limit to the close and review the remaining shares under the same rules[30]. Do not add shares while the gate fails. Set the tax on the sold lots aside, since the lot-level data is not in the file[31].

What would change this view

A daily close below 83.57[30] on heavy volume sends the remaining shares for review and sale that day, and the verdict moves to REPLACE_UNDERPERFORMER.

A daily close below the 50-day average of 92.17[30] on heavy volume cancels any plan to hold the rest and forces a fresh review of the stop.

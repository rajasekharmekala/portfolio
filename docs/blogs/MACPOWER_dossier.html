0. Executive Summary & Investment Pitch

Macpower CNC Machines is a Rajkot-based Indian machine-tool manufacturer supplying computer numerical control (CNC) equipment. CNC machines translate digital tool paths into repeatable cutting, turning and milling operations; their economic value comes from accuracy, uptime, application engineering and service rather than from the steel enclosure alone. The company reports a broad catalogue spanning turning centres, vertical machining centres (VMCs), horizontal machining centres (HMCs), vertical turning lathes (VTLs), grinders and related solutions, with 380-plus models and more than 13,389 installations cited in public materials.[1][7]

The operating setup is strategically relevant because Indian automotive, defence, aerospace, rail, general engineering and subcontracting customers are increasing domestic machining capacity. FY26 standalone sales were approximately ₹333 crore, versus ₹202 crore in FY23, while profit after tax rose from roughly ₹13 crore to ₹34 crore. TTM sales were shown at ₹367 crore and TTM operating margin at approximately 17%.[4] The company is nearly debt-free, with FY26 borrowings of about ₹1 crore against net worth near ₹175 crore; ROCE and ROE screens were 29.1% and 21.3%.[4]

The adverse underwriting fact is cash conversion. CFO was ₹17 crore in FY24, ₹7 crore in FY25 and ₹14 crore in FY26, against PAT of ₹24 crore, ₹25 crore and ₹34 crore: cumulative CFO/PAT is 38/83 = 0.46x, below the 0.70 hard floor.[4] Inventory days rose to approximately 259 and debtor days to 51 in FY26, producing a cash-conversion cycle near 182 days. This is not a balance-sheet solvency crisis, but it is a working-capital intensity warning in a capital-goods cycle. At ₹2,195, the stock is close to its ₹2,226 52-week high and carries a screen P/E near 56.4x.[4] Accordingly, the action is WAIT FOR SUPPORT, not an outright business rejection.

1. Corporate Overview & History

Macpower’s corporate history is rooted in Rajkot’s engineering cluster, where machine-tool companies developed around foundries, component suppliers, tool rooms and automotive ancillaries. The company’s public materials describe a long operating history and a national service footprint built around application support. Its public-market identity matters because the customer buys a production system: machine, tooling compatibility, commissioning, operator training, spares and process validation. A supplier able to shorten installation time and keep a machine productive can win despite a higher initial quote.[1][7]

The company’s evolution has followed the typical Indian machine-tool ladder: basic turning and lathe products first, followed by more complex VMC/HMC configurations, larger work envelopes, live tooling, multi-axis capability and engineered solutions. The portfolio’s breadth helps the sales force address a customer’s first machine purchase while offering an upgrade path as that customer moves from job work into higher-precision components. That ladder is a commercial moat only when service response and application engineering remain reliable; catalogue breadth by itself is not a durable barrier.[1][2]

1.1 Governance and ownership

Promoters held approximately 73.22% in June 2026, with the public holding approximately 25.82% and foreign and domestic institutional ownership comparatively small.[4] High promoter ownership aligns long-term incentives but also concentrates voting control and liquidity risk. The September 2026 AGM disclosure and trading-window closure are relevant governance markers; investors should reconcile every related-party, pledge, remuneration and capital-allocation note to the latest annual report before increasing exposure.[33][34]

2. CNC Machine Product Portfolio

Macpower’s product set spans metal-cutting platforms with different levels of automation, rigidity and precision. A turning centre rotates the workpiece while cutting tools remove material; a VMC holds the workpiece stationary while a vertical spindle cuts along multiple axes; an HMC uses a horizontal spindle and often supports palletised production; a VTL handles large-diameter or heavy workpieces; grinding systems perform high-precision finishing. The buyer’s selection is driven by workpiece geometry, tolerance, batch size, cycle time and automation requirements.[1][7]

The institutional question is therefore whether higher-end mix and repeat customers increase gross contribution faster than complexity increases fixed cost.

3. End-User Industry Exposure

Machine-tool demand is derived demand. A customer orders a machine when expected utilisation, product demand and financing conditions justify incremental capacity. Automotive and auto-component demand can provide volume, but it also creates pricing pressure and concentrated cycle exposure. Defence and aerospace offer longer qualification periods, stringent traceability and potentially higher value per machine; they also require certifications and process capability. General engineering and job-work customers broaden the addressable market but can be more price sensitive and financially heterogeneous.[2][20][25]

4. Manufacturing Facility & Capacity Expansion (Rajkot)

Rajkot provides a dense supplier ecosystem for castings, fabrication, machining, motors, controls integration and field service. The manufacturing model is best understood as a combination of fabricated and cast machine structures, purchased control systems, spindles and drives, in-house assembly, testing and application engineering. This structure creates operating leverage when utilisation rises, but it also leaves the company exposed to imported electronics, currency movement, lead times and quality-control bottlenecks.[1][2]

Public Screener extracts show fixed assets rising from approximately ₹47 crore in FY24 to ₹55 crore in FY25 and ₹61 crore in FY26, with capital work-in-progress of roughly ₹1 crore in FY26.[4] That pattern suggests measured expansion rather than a large disclosed greenfield capacity shock. Capacity utilisation, installed machine capacity and order-book values are not fully visible in the free data extract; these must be confirmed from the latest investor presentation before underwriting an aggressive volume step-up.[2][13]

Capacity conclusion: The balance sheet can fund incremental manufacturing, but the evidence currently supports a gradual throughput expansion thesis—not an assumed multi-year capacity doubling. A quarterly increase in machines manufactured and sold, alongside stable inventory days, would materially strengthen the thesis.

5. Unit Economics & Machine Realization

Machine-tool unit economics are governed by average realisation, bill of materials, configuration, installation cost, warranty provisions and the time between dispatch and customer acceptance. A basic turning centre may be sold into a competitive tender; a configured VMC or multi-axis cell earns more engineering value but can require application trials, tooling coordination and acceptance milestones. Revenue recognition therefore need not map neatly to shipment dates, and a high-growth quarter can consume cash before profit is collected.

FY26 working-capital metrics are the central forensic issue: debtor days near 51, inventory days near 259 and a cash-conversion cycle near 182 days.[4] A machine maker can legitimately carry long inventory because castings, sheet metal and bought-out components must be staged before assembly; however, the burden must convert into cash through dispatch, acceptance and collection. Management should disclose ageing, advances from customers, cancelled orders, warranty provisions and inventory provisioning with enough granularity for investors to distinguish growth investment from demand slippage.

10. Risk Factors & Cyclical Machine-Tool Risks

  1. Cycle and valuation: At roughly 56.4x screen P/E, the stock discounts sustained growth. A normalisation in capex or OPM can compress the multiple before the business thesis changes.[4]
  2. Working-capital absorption: Inventory of approximately 259 days and CCC near 182 days can force supplier financing or customer advances. The risk is not merely accounting; it can limit growth during an upcycle.
  3. Imported component exposure: Controls, drives, spindles and precision components may be sourced internationally. Currency depreciation, sanctions, logistics delays or supplier concentration can impair delivery and margin.
  4. Customer concentration: Automotive and large engineering customers can negotiate aggressively, defer acceptance or require customisation. A broad catalogue does not eliminate buyer power.
  5. Higher-complexity execution: 5-axis and aerospace machines require process validation and field engineering. Failed commissioning can create warranty expense and delay cash collection.
  6. Promoter and liquidity: High promoter ownership supports alignment but can reduce free float; a momentum reversal may therefore be sharper.
Hard invalidation triggers: daily close below ₹1,875 on heavy volume; rolling CFO/PAT below 0.50x after FY27; OPM compression greater than 300 bps without a documented temporary cause; inventory above 280 days; debtor days above 60; or material order cancellations / receivable impairment.

12. Capital Allocation Verdict

Verdict: 🔴 REJECTED FOR FRESH CHASE; 🟡 WAIT FOR SUPPORT. A disciplined portfolio can keep a small research placeholder or paper position, but should not convert momentum into an oversized allocation. The base target of ₹2,850 is anchored to a conservative combination of forward earnings growth and a normalised premium multiple; the bull target of ₹3,350 requires evidence rather than narrative.

Methodological Notes and Evidence Discipline

The financial extraction uses the standalone series displayed by Screener.in because the accessible table clearly labels the yearly profit-and-loss, balance-sheet and cash-flow rows. Where consolidated figures differ, the annual report and exchange filing should prevail. The three-year cash-conversion calculation deliberately uses the latest three complete reported fiscal years rather than a favourable subset: FY24 CFO ₹17 crore plus FY25 CFO ₹7 crore plus FY26 CFO ₹14 crore equals ₹38 crore; corresponding PAT of ₹24 crore, ₹25 crore and ₹34 crore equals ₹83 crore. The quotient is 0.4578x, rounded to 0.46x. This transparent arithmetic is the reason the security fails the cash-quality gate even while leverage and return ratios pass.

Sales CAGR is calculated from FY23 ₹202 crore to FY26 ₹333 crore across three intervals: [(333 ÷ 202)^(1/3) − 1] × 100 = 18.1%. PAT CAGR is calculated from FY23 ₹13 crore to FY26 ₹34 crore: [(34 ÷ 13)^(1/3) − 1] × 100 = 37.7%. OPM is reported at approximately 17% TTM in the fetched ratio table. ROCE of 29.1%, ROE of 21.3%, market capitalisation of ₹2,196 crore and P/E of 56.4x are reproduced as screen values, not independently restated audited ratios. The dossier avoids treating screen rounding as precision.

Technical indicators require a full daily OHLC history. The public pages available during this run supplied the closing price, 52-week range, ticker confirmation and a market-commentary pivot near ₹2,007, but did not expose a machine-readable daily series for exact DMA, RSI and ATR recomputation. Accordingly, the figures labelled “proxy” are execution placeholders, not fabricated tick data. Before any order is placed, the analyst must refresh a daily series and recalculate 20/50/200-DMA, RSI(14), ATR(14), relative-strength rating and support distance. The conservative decision—do not chase a new 52-week high—does not depend on false technical precision.

Operational KPI status follows an evidence hierarchy. A green score requires a current primary filing or presentation with a clearly defined numerator and denominator. “Order-book-to-bill” means new orders divided by recognised revenue over a comparable period; “capacity utilisation” means output divided by practical installed capacity; “high-end mix” means the share of revenue or units from VMC, HMC, five-axis and other engineered platforms; “debtor days” measures trade receivables relative to revenue; and “export share” is export revenue divided by total revenue. Because the free table did not disclose the first, second, third and fifth items with enough granularity, those rows are watch items rather than optimistic assumptions.

The entry architecture is intentionally support-aware. ₹1,930–2,030 is a proposed accumulation band around the ₹2,007 pivot and the proxy 20-DMA, not a promise that the stock will trade there. ₹1,875 is a hard invalidation floor selected below that support cluster with room for volatility. The 12–18 month base target of ₹2,850 is a scenario anchor, not a guaranteed price; it assumes revenue growth, stable-to-improving margin and a lower but still premium earnings multiple. The bull target of ₹3,350 additionally assumes higher-end product mix and a normalisation of cash conversion. If those assumptions do not appear in primary disclosures, the target is withdrawn.

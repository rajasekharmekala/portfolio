The ideas behind the verdict

This chapter defines the terms the rest of the report uses. Each term is marked where it first appears, and the glossary at the end repeats the definitions. A moving average is the average closing price over a fixed number of sessions. It works like a trend line through recent prices.

The RSI, or relative strength index, is a speedometer from 0 to 100. A reading above 70 means the stock has moved fast, and a reading below 30 means it has fallen fast. The ATR, or average true range, is the average daily price range over fourteen sessions. It measures the normal wobble of the price. Here the ATR is ₹67.07, which is about 3.3 percent of the price.

Stage 2 means the stock is in a markup phase. The price sits above a rising 50-day average, and that average sits above a rising 200-day average. Stage 4 is the decline that follows, when the price falls below falling averages. A base is a sideways range where a stock pauses. A pivot is the price that marks the top of that range. A breakout is a close above the pivot on heavier trading. A retest is a return to the broken pivot that holds.

A stop loss is a set price at which you sell to limit a loss. The framework's stop for this holding is ₹1,459.37. An entry zone is the price range in which the framework allows a new buy, here ₹1,944.96 to ₹2,025.44. An asymmetry ratio is the reward you could make divided by the loss you could take. A reward-to-risk ratio is the same comparison stated in words, so a ratio of 3.0 means three rupees of possible gain for each rupee of possible loss.

The ROCE, or return on capital employed, is operating profit divided by all the money the business uses, borrowed or owned. EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. It is a rough measure of operating profit before financing and accounting charges. EPS, or earnings per share, is profit divided by the number of shares. The price-to-earnings ratio, or P/E, is the share price divided by earnings per share.

A gross margin is revenue less the direct cost of making the product, as a share of revenue. An operating margin also deducts the running costs of the business. A debt-to-equity ratio is borrowed money divided by owners' money. Net debt is borrowings less cash. Free cash flow is the cash left after a company pays for its capital spending.

A capital expenditure, or capex, is money spent on plants and equipment. Working capital is the cash tied up in stock and unpaid customer bills, net of unpaid supplier bills. Dilution is the fall in each share's claim on profit when new shares are issued. Guidance is what management says it expects. A measured move is a target equal to the height of a trading range, added to the range's top.

What the company does

Manorama Industries makes specialty fats and butters from tree-borne seeds, such as sal and mango kernels, which are processed into cocoa butter equivalents, or CBE. A CBE is a fat that behaves like cocoa butter in chocolate and confectionery but is cheaper and more stable, so chocolate makers buy it as an input. Think of it as a substitute ingredient that a large food company qualifies once and then buys for years. The company describes its model as turning waste into value: it buys forest seeds from collectors, extracts the oil, fractionates it into hard and soft parts, and refines the result into products for food, cosmetics and pharmaceutical customers [2]. The reported business is consolidated, which means it includes the overseas subsidiaries that now source raw material in West Africa and build processing capacity there.

What this means for an owner is that revenue depends on two things. One is the volume the plants can process, which is the capacity measured in tonnes a year and how full the plants run. The other is the mix, meaning how much of the output is high-value CBE and specialty fat rather than commodity product. In the June 2026 quarter, management said the company used about 80 percent of its capacity [8], and it guided to 80 to 85 percent for the full year FY27 [9]. Current fractionation capacity is 47,500 tonnes a year, and debottlenecking is expected to take it to about 52,000 tonnes by Q3 FY27 [9] [10].

The sourcing network is a central part of the story. The company buys raw seed through a network of collectors and has extended it to Africa, where it has subsidiaries in several countries and a land purchase in Burkina Faso for a shea processing plant [11] [9]. Management describes these as backward integration moves, which means they bring a step of the supply chain under the company's own control. The moat is therefore partly in the sourcing and partly in the qualifications the company holds with large confectioners. We could not verify the specific number of village collectors that some secondary write-ups quote, so this dossier does not repeat it.

Products and sourcing

The product line has two parts that carry different economics. Commodity fats and oils have lower margins and sell on volume. CBE and specialty fats carry higher margins and are sold on qualification and specification, which means a customer who approves the product tends to keep buying it. The mix matters because the margin difference drives the gap between a 25 percent operating margin and a 17 percent one. Management said in the Q1 FY27 call that the value-added share had risen and that this, along with the better utilisation of the expanded plant, drove the margin [5]. The gross margin, which is revenue less the direct cost of product, fell from 48 percent to 43 percent in the fourth quarter as new overseas subsidiaries carried start-up costs [12]. So the margin story has two strands that pull in different directions, and the next two quarters will show which one dominates.

What this means for you is that the product mix is the variable to watch, more than the headline volume. A reader who wants to check the thesis can compare the gross margin figure in each quarterly result with the 43 percent level reported for the fourth quarter. Staying above that level, while utilisation rises toward 85 percent, would support the compounding view. Falling below it while volumes grow would suggest the growth is coming from lower-value product.

Glossary

ATR. average true range: the average daily price range over fourteen sessions.

RSI. relative strength index: a momentum measure from 0 to 100.

EBITDA. earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization.

ROCE. return on capital employed: operating profit divided by capital employed.

EPS. earnings per share: profit divided by the number of shares.

P/E. price-to-earnings: the share price divided by earnings per share.

Stage 2. the markup phase, with the price above a rising 50-day average that is above a rising 200-day average.

Stage 4. the decline phase, with the price below falling averages.

Base. a sideways trading range where a stock pauses.

Pivot. the price that marks the top of a base.

Breakout. a close above the pivot on heavier trading.

Retest. a return to a broken pivot that holds as support.

Stop loss. a set price at which a position is sold to limit a loss.

Entry zone. the price range in which the framework allows a new buy.

Asymmetry. the reward divided by the risk, measured from today's price.

Reward-to-risk. the same comparison stated in words.

Gross margin. revenue less the direct cost of the product, as a share of revenue.

Operating margin. operating profit as a share of revenue.

Debt-to-equity. borrowed money divided by owners' money.

Net debt. borrowings less cash.

Free cash flow. cash left after capital spending.

Capital expenditure. money spent on plants and equipment.

Working capital. cash tied up in stock and customer bills, net of supplier bills.

Dilution. the fall in each share's claim on profit when new shares are issued.

Guidance. what management says it expects.

Measured move. a target equal to the height of a trading range, added to its top.

Distribution day. a session in which the index falls on heavier trading than the session before.

CFO. cash from operations: cash a business generates from trading.

Moving average. the average closing price over a fixed number of sessions.

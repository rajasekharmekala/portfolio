Terms used in this report

The framework uses a small set of market terms. ROCE is return on capital employed, which measures the profit a business earns on the money tied up in it. A moving average is the average closing price over a set number of sessions, like a smoothed trend line; the 50-day and 200-day averages are the two the rules use. Stage is the Weinstein picture of a stock's season: Stage 1 is a flat period after a fall, Stage 2 is a rising markup, Stage 3 is a top or pullback where the rise has stalled, and Stage 4 is a decline. A base is a period of sideways trading that comes before a new move, and a pivot is the price level above which a base is considered to have broken out. A breakout is a close through that level on enough volume to be meaningful.

RSI, the relative strength index, is a 14-day reading of how fast a price has risen or fallen; values above 70 are read as stretched and below 30 as oversold. ATR, the average true range, is the typical daily swing of the stock over 14 sessions, and the rules use it as a ruler for distances. Asymmetry is the ratio of the possible gain to the possible loss, measured against a verified target and a stop. An operating margin is operating profit as a share of sales, before interest and tax.

The momentum gate is a code check that runs on the same price file for every name. Test A asks whether the stock is in an upward trend now, and Test B asks whether a new uptrend is starting from a base. A name that fails both gets no allocation, however good the business.

How the framework works for this company

The framework has three layers, applied in order. Layer 1 has three financial floors, a technical floor, a momentum test and an asymmetry floor. The financial floors are return on capital employed at or above 15 percent (with 18 percent or more preferred for a core name), debt to equity below 0.70, and three-year cumulative cash from operations at least 0.70 times three-year profit. The technical posture is tested next, then the momentum gate, and the asymmetry floor is the last test in Layer 1. A company that fails any one is rejected, and the verdict names the first failure.

This company is a core name in the watchlist, so the technical rule for it is a Stage 1 base breakout or a Stage 2 markup, with no Stage 4 breakdown. The momentum gate is applied to every candidate in any bucket. Both the core rule and the momentum gate are read from the same closing-price file as of 13 October 2026, so the technical test is the same for every name in this batch.

What this means for you: a company can be excellent on the balance sheet and still be rejected. The framework does not rank quality against price. It asks two separate questions and requires a yes to both before any money is allocated.

Asymmetry and target anchoring

The asymmetry floor compares the possible gain with the possible loss, using a target that must be anchored to a forward earnings multiple or to a technical base measure. The core floor is 3.0. The ratio is the distance from the close to the target divided by the distance from the close to the stop, as set out in the scoring configuration.

This chapter is not evaluated. Layer 1 stopped at the technical gate, and the framework says a failed gate ends the sequence. No target has been anchored and no ratio is recorded for this run. A ratio computed from an unanchored target would be a number without a basis, and the rules forbid it. The reference stop in the estimate that follows is a level for reading the risk, not an order.

Fatal-flaw red team

The red team asks what would make the case for the company wrong, not what makes it right. Three flaws are worth stating.

First flaw: the growth engine is narrow. Sales compounded at 7 percent over three years and profit at 10 percent, according to Screener. Investorstack's summary of the June 2026 quarter puts the Almond Drops franchise at about 80 percent of sales Investorstack, Bajaj Consumer Care earnings call summary. If a competitor takes share in hair oil, margin and return on capital are both exposed. The operating margin fell from 30 percent in March 2019 to 13 percent in March 2025 before recovering to 19 percent in March 2026, which shows how sensitive the margin has been to input costs and promotion.

Second flaw: the one-year profit jump is not yet a trend. A quarter with 84 percent profit growth can reflect a low base and price actions. The ledger shows the stock down 2.53 percent over the past month, so the market's reaction to the result has not lasted. Until the averages reflect the growth, the market is not yet convinced it will last.

Third flaw: a large one-year move carries reversal risk. The 52-week range runs from ₹235.15 to ₹692.00. A stock that has moved this much in a year is exposed to a sharp reversal, and the Stage 3 reading is consistent with that risk.

What this means for you: none of these flaws decides the verdict alone. Together they support the rule. The company is worth watching, and the trend has to answer the flaws before a purchase makes sense.

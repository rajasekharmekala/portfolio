What the company does

Before the numbers, a few terms the rest of the dossier uses. ROCE means return on capital employed, the operating profit a business earns for every rupee of capital it uses. RSI means relative strength index, a 0 to 100 gauge of recent momentum that reads below 30 when a stock has fallen unusually fast. ATR means average true range, the typical daily price swing, which we use as a ruler for distances. Stage 2 means a stock in a sustained uptrend, and Stage 4 means a stock in a sustained decline. An asymmetry means the ratio of the possible gain to the possible loss, which is the test that decides whether a buy is worth the risk.

This part answers a simple question: how does the business make money? Sanghvi Movers rents out cranes. A crane rental company owns a large fleet of heavy machines and hires them, with an operator, to contractors who need heavy lifts on construction and industrial sites. The company describes itself as providing medium to heavy duty cranes on rental basis to private and public sector undertakings, with a lifting capacity ranging from 20 MT to 1000 MT, where MT means metric tonnes.[5] That matters for you because the business earns money only when the cranes are working, so the share of the fleet that is rented out at any moment is the first number to understand.

The company's profile says it is the largest crane hiring company and Asia's largest crane rental operator, with a fleet of 485+ cranes as of FY26, 15 depots across India and 175 acres of freehold crane parking.[5] These are company claims repeated on the Screener page, which cites a credit-rating document and an exchange filing. We could not read those source documents at the time of research, so we treat the fleet count as the company's own figure and not as independently checked. The fleet is the main asset of a business like this, so its size and how much of it is working drive the rest of the numbers.

The business is a capital business. Each crane costs money up front, earns rent for years, and then needs replacing. The useful test is whether the company earns a good return on the money tied up in those cranes, which is what ROCE measures. A company whose ROCE stays high for years usually has a real advantage, such as a hard-to-copy fleet or customers who keep coming back. A company whose ROCE falls is one where the advantage is fading, or where new capacity earns less than old capacity.

The price of the stock is a separate question. The market prices the stock every day, and it also changes its mind about the business. For that reason this dossier keeps two lines of evidence apart: the business evidence from the filings and the price evidence from the exchange bars. When the two disagree, we say so.

With the business model clear, the next question is whether it is a strong business or a weak one, and that depends on the quality of the returns and the cash.

Why the business is strong or weak

This part weighs the strengths against the warning signs. A crane fleet is a moat only if the company can keep the cranes busy at good prices. The consolidated profit history shows a company that grew quickly and then slowed in its margins, which is the central tension in this file.

Growth has been fast, and sales are still growing

Sales rose from ₹456 crore in FY23 to ₹1,070 crore in FY26.[5] That is a compound growth rate of 32.9 percent a year over three years, which means sales grew a little more than two and a third times in three years, or about a third more each year. The quarter to June 2026 kept the pace: sales were ₹380 crore against ₹273 crore a year earlier, a rise of 39 percent.[5] The Moneycontrol headline for the same quarter reports consolidated net sales of ₹379.67 crore, up 38.89 percent, which agrees with the Screener figure.[15] Growth of this kind is good news, but only if the profit kept from each rupee of sales holds up. That is the next part.

Profit is growing more slowly than sales, and the margin is falling

The operating profit margin, shortened here to OPM, is the share of each rupee of sales left after the direct costs of running the cranes. It was 37 percent in FY26.[5] It was 56 percent in FY23 and 42 percent in FY25. In the quarter to June 2026 it dropped to 33 percent, the lowest figure in the quarterly series on the Screener page.[5] The standalone figure for the same quarter is 42 percent, so the consolidated figure is lower than the standalone one. The pages we read do not explain that gap, and we could not confirm its cause.[6] Net profit for the June 2026 quarter was ₹65 crore against ₹50 crore a year earlier, a rise of 30 percent, but down from ₹69 crore in the March 2026 quarter.[5] The Business Standard headline for that quarter describes the same drop as a fall of 5 percent on the previous quarter.[15] So sales are rising faster than profit, and profit has slipped in the latest quarter. That is the first real weakness.

How to read the margin line: a higher OPM means the company keeps more of each rupee. Read the quarterly values in sequence, not one at a time. Compare like with like: the June 2025 quarter was 36 percent, so the year-on-year fall is three points, from 36 to 33. Fair value by earnings

The number

How it was calculated

We multiply the trailing twelve-month earnings per share by the three-year median P/E ratio. The earnings per share is ₹23.03.[5] The three-year median of the year-end multiples is 13.50 times, taken from the FY24, FY25 and FY26 values of 31.17, 13.50 and 11.00.[10] Multiplying the two gives about ₹311.

The inputs are the trailing earnings per share of ₹23.03, the three-year median multiple of 13.50, and the present close of ₹392.05.[5][1] A forward earnings estimate would be better, but none was available in a readable source, so the trailing figure stands in for it.

The range

The price would need earnings per share near ₹29 at the three-year median multiple to reach today's ₹392, which is roughly 26 percent above the trailing figure.

If the number is off

We weight the second risk more heavily, because the margin trend is the one the reported figures support.

Where the evidence is thin

Three gaps matter. First, the reason for the 14 percent fall over two weeks is not confirmed. The Google News feed lists headlines for the governance lapse on 23 September, a share-price fall of 8.32 percent on 1 October, and the promoter gift on 6 October, but the publisher pages would not open at the time of research.[15] We therefore treat the fall as coinciding with the governance headline, not as caused by it. Second, the FY26 annual report is the primary source for ROCE, the audit opinion and the receivables. The BSE copy returned binary data, so the annual numbers come from the Screener and stockanalysis aggregators, which repeat the audited statements.[5][8] Third, the promoters' pledge position is not on any page we could read, so the pledge question stays open.

A third page, Groww, shows June 2026 revenue of ₹281 crore, which conflicts with the Screener consolidated figure of ₹380 crore. We do not use the Groww figure, because the consolidated filing figure is the one the framework asks for.[16]

These gaps do not change the verdict, but they limit how strongly we can state it. The governance point is the one that could make the case worse, and it is the one we can least verify. Where we say the stock fell because of the headline, we have not checked the article. Where we give a number, we give the source, and where the source is an aggregator, the number is repeated from audited statements rather than read from the filing itself.

With the weak points named, the decision follows from the rules, not from the hope that the stock recovers.

What we would do, and when

This part is the decision. It applies the holding taxonomy to the facts above and names the levels that would change it. The holding taxonomy has four verdicts. KEEP_COMPOUNDING needs every gate to pass and the uptrend intact. ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP needs a high-conviction name pulling back to verified support, with room in the allocation. TRIM_PROFIT_BOOKING needs an extended price or a gain to harvest. REPLACE_UNDERPERFORMER needs broken structural support, two or more failed operating measures, or a thesis that has broken.[4]

Holding audit: position health and profit and loss

The holding audit starts with the position itself. You own 890 shares with an average cost of about ₹455.75 each, so the cost basis is ₹4,05,614.41.[2] At today's close of ₹392.05 the position is worth ₹3,48,924.50, which is 7.43 percent of your ₹46,95,954.82 net worth.[2][1] The unrealised loss is ₹56,689.91, or 14 percent of cost.[2] The account also shows a realised gain of ₹12,299.71 on earlier sales of this stock, which is already in the books.[2]

How to read the position table: the market value and the cost basis tell you whether you are in profit, and the stop tells you where the plan says to leave. A position can be at a loss and still be a keep, if its trend and its business are intact. Here the trend is not intact, so the loss is not the reason for the verdict. The stop is.

The verdict and the rule that produced it

The decisive basis is the position stop. The taxonomy's replace rule names 'closed below stop' as its broken-support test, and the stock closed at ₹392.05, below the ₹415 trailing stop recorded for this holding.[3][4] That breach is the one test that decides the verdict. Three other levels are intact and are not the basis: the ₹379.55 low, the 200-session average at ₹363.42, and the rule-based stop at ₹341.76.[1][4] The second replace trigger, two or more failed operating measures, is not met, because one measure fails and one is on watch. The stage trigger is not met either, because the stock is in Stage 3, not Stage 4.[1]

The taxonomy has no neutral verdict, so the replace is partly reached by elimination. KEEP_COMPOUNDING fails because the hard gate fails and the stage is not Stage 2. ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP fails for the same gate reason. TRIM_PROFIT_BOOKING fails because there is no gain. We state the basis openly: a reader who rejects the user's ₹415 stop as a structural level would have no replace trigger met, and would be left with a choice between the hold-type verdicts, which the gates do not support.

The stop breach alone decides the verdict. A rebound to ₹415 is where the first tranche sells, and the decision is reopened only by three closes above ₹450.65 with the operating test passed. A hold would need the gates to pass after that. KEEP_COMPOUNDING would need KEEP_COMPOUNDING would need the stage back in Stage 2, a September 2026 quarter OPM of at least 42 percent, a clean FY26 audit, and an admissible target that passes the asymmetry test.

The verdict rests on one rule that is easy to check: the price closed below the stop. The gate failures and the margin explain why the thesis is weaker than the one you bought, but the verdict itself rests on the stop breach. What would change the verdict is written below as a level, so you can see it without re-reading the chart.

The action plan

Because the verdict is replace, the holding becomes a candidate for a capital swap into a challenger that passes its gates. This dossier does not choose the destination. It says only that the holding should not be added to, and that the sale should be staged so that one bad day does not decide the price.

Our plan has two tranches and one cut-off date. The first tranche is 445 shares, sold at the first close at or above ₹415, the trailing stop recorded for this holding.[3] That level is 5.9 percent above today's close, so the first tranche needs a rebound to execute. A rebound to ₹415 sells this tranche and does not by itself reopen the decision; only the ₹450.65 test in the list below does. The second tranche is the remaining 445 shares, sold on the first close below ₹379.55, the lowest level of the last 63 sessions.[1]

The cut-off date is 26 October 2026, ten sessions after the report date, assuming no exchange holiday in the window. We chose the ten-session window, and it matches the ten-session comparison the ledger already uses for the slope of the 50-session average.[1] If neither tranche's level is reached by the close on 26 October, the unsold shares are sold at the next session's open. If the first tranche's level is reached first, the second tranche keeps its own rule. If the second tranche's level is broken first, all unsold shares, including the first tranche, are sold at the next open, so no part of the holding stays behind a break of the support.

Gap risk is not covered by a rule, because a gap happens before any rule can act. An overnight gap of 8.32 percent, the size of the 1 October fall, would take the price from ₹392.05 to about ₹359.43 and cost about ₹29,032 on 890 shares. That is 0.62 percent of net worth.[2][15] Selling everything at today's close would avoid that gap. We follow the rules instead, because they set the exit at ₹415 or ₹379.55, and we show the gap cost so that you can weigh it.

We have not computed tax on the sale, because the positions file does not split the holding period by lot, and that calculation needs the lot-level record.[2]

A worked example in your own money: if the first tranche sells at ₹415, you receive about ₹1.85 lakh (445 × 415 = ₹1,84,675). If the second tranche sells at ₹379.55, you receive about ₹1.69 lakh (445 × 379.55 = ₹1,68,900). Together that is about ₹3.54 lakh, about ₹4,650 more than the present market value of ₹3,48,924.50, because the first tranche sells above today's close.

What would change the verdict

These triggers apply only while any shares are still held. Once the plan has sold every share, they inform a later decision, not this one. Each one is checked against the ledger and the filings, not against a feeling about the company.

If the stock closes above ₹450.65, the falling 50-session average, for three sessions in a row, this is the only level that reopens the decision. We would re-underwrite the holding, and we would not buy it until the stage turns back to Stage 2 and the operating test passes.

If the stock closes below ₹379.55, the 63-session low, on any session, all unsold shares are sold at the next session's open.

If the September 2026 quarter reports a consolidated OPM of 42 percent or more, the margin invalidation in your shortlist row clears, although the technical and asymmetry gates would still have to pass before any buy.

If the FY26 audit opinion is confirmed as a qualification or a material weakness, the remaining shares are sold at the next open, because that would break the thesis and not just the chart.

If the promoter holding moves away from 47.25 percent in a later filing, the ownership change is reviewed the same day, because a promoter sale would change the governance picture.

The triggers are the rule's own levels, which makes the plan easy to audit. Two point toward sale, one points toward re-underwriting, and two are about the governance question that the headlines raise. Together they make the decision readable in one glance, which is what the framework is for.

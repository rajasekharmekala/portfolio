Before you read

This dossier reviews Astra Microwave Products Limited, a defence electronics maker listed on the National Stock Exchange under the symbol ASTRAMICRO. The fund already holds 200 shares, so the question is whether to keep them, add to them, trim them, or replace them. The rules are applied in three layers, and the dossier shows which layer failed and why. Chapter 1 explains the business, and each later chapter explains one part of the decision.

1. What the company does

Astra Microwave makes the electronic parts that go inside radars, missiles, satellites and communication equipment. It was founded in 1991 in Hyderabad and listed on the exchanges in December 1994. [8] The company describes itself as a supplier of sub-systems for radio frequency and microwave systems, which is the engineering layer between a raw signal and a finished weapon or sensor. [7] Screener's company summary says it has contributed to 35 radars, 25 satellites and about 15 strategic programmes. [1]

What the products do

The most important product is the transmit and receive module, often called a T/R module. A radar needs many of these small amplifiers, each one sending and catching a signal for a single point on an antenna array. An active electronically scanned array (AESA) radar steers its beam by switching these modules electronically, with no moving parts. The company also makes electronic warfare units, which detect and jam enemy radar, and telemetry equipment, which sends test data back from a missile in flight. [7]

The product mix matters for the rest of the dossier. Defence electronics is a business where a design is qualified once and then bought for years, so a win today can produce orders for a long time. That is why the order book, rather than the quarterly sales line, is the number that management watches. The rest of this chapter shows how large that book is.

Where the revenue and orders come from

Sales in the year to March 2026 were ₹1,163 crore, up from ₹1,051 crore the year before. [1] The company said its standalone order book stood at ₹2,156 crore on 30 June 2026. Around 66 percent of that book is defence work, and 34 percent is space, meteorology and hydrology work. [21] The consolidated book, which includes subsidiaries, was ₹2,849 crore, including about ₹244 crore of service orders. [21]

The largest new contract is from Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL), for ₹2,205.23 crore. It covers 122 airborne active antenna units and 121 interface frames for the Uttam radar, which will fly on the Tejas Mk1A fighter. [26] Management said the order was booked in July 2026, and that its value is larger than the whole standalone book at the end of June. [23] India accounted for 95 percent of revenue in the first quarter, and exports for 5 percent. [23]

What this means for an owner: the company now has years of visible work, but the order is large relative to today's revenue, so it will take years to turn into sales. The order book supports the long-term story. It does not by itself say whether this quarter's profit is healthy, which is the subject of chapter 2.

Who owns the company

The promoter group holds 6.54 percent of the shares, according to the latest available shareholding data, the September 2026 quarter. [1][12] IIFL shows 6.53 percent [13] and Choice India shows 6.54 percent. [14] Foreign institutions hold 10.48 percent, domestic institutions 16.52 percent, and the public holds 66.47 percent. [1] A low promoter stake is not a warning by itself, but it means most of the shares are held by investors who can sell without a family or founder decision. Stocks Sena reports that none of the promoter holding is pledged. [12]

What this means for an owner: the business is mainly controlled by institutions and the public, not by its founders. The price is therefore set by how those investors judge the next few quarters. That is why the cash and margin tests in chapters 2 and 3 carry the weight in the decision.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

A business is strong when it earns good returns on the money in it, turns profit into cash, and does not depend on heavy borrowing. It is weak when one of those fails. Astra passes the first and the third, and it fails the second. The rest of this chapter shows the evidence, and why the cash test decides the verdict.

Returns on capital are high

ROCE (return on capital employed) is the operating profit a company earns for every rupee tied up in the business. It is the cleanest test of whether the company is a good user of money. Astra's ROCE was 20 percent in the year to March 2026. [1] Other sources agree within a point or two: StockAnalysis shows 19.80 percent for the same year, and StockFin shows 20.18 percent on a trailing basis. [3][15] The lowest figure is well above the 15 percent hard floor, so this gate passes on any of them.

What this means for an owner: the business earns more on its capital than most manufacturers do, and it does not need large new investment to grow. That is a real strength, and it is the main reason the company is not a poor business that has merely had a good year.

Leverage is light

Debt to equity compares borrowed money with the owners' capital. At the end of March 2026 the company had borrowings of ₹288 crore and reserves of ₹1,296 crore, which gives a ratio of about 0.22. [1][3] The rule is a ceiling of 0.70, so the company is far inside it. The ratio divides borrowings by total equity: ₹19 crore of share capital plus ₹1,296 crore of reserves, or ₹1,315 crore, which gives about 0.22. Lenders are not a threat to the business in the short run.

What this means for an owner: the balance sheet does not force the company to sell assets or raise equity to survive. The risk sits in the cash flow, not in the debt.

Cash conversion fails the test

The cash test asks how much of the reported profit actually came in as operating cash. Operating cash flow was negative in two of the last three years: minus ₹182 crore in the year to March 2024, minus ₹90 crore in the year to March 2025, and plus ₹387 crore in the year to March 2026. [1][4] Over the same three years, net profit was ₹121 crore, ₹154 crore and ₹193 crore. [1]

The cause is visible in the working-capital lines. The company's receivables, the money owed by customers, rose by ₹2,223 million in the year to March 2024 and by ₹2,732 million in the year to March 2025. In the year to March 2026 they fell by ₹975 million, which released cash. [4] Debtor days, which measure how long customers take to pay, were 216 in March 2026, down from 273 a year earlier but still very high. [1] Stocks Sena reports that over five years operating cash flow has been only about 35 percent of net profit. [11]

What this means for an owner: the company reports profit, but for two years most of that profit sat in customers' unpaid bills. The March 2026 year was strong, and it is the first sign of change, but one good year does not repair a three-year record. The gate is written on three years, so the single strong year does not clear it.

Growth is strong over three years, but the latest quarter fell

Sales grew about 13 percent a year over the three years to March 2026, and net profit grew about 40 percent a year over the same period. [1] Those rates are high, but the profit rate starts from a low level in the year to March 2023. The growth is real, though, because sales have risen in each of the last three years.

The June 2026 quarter does not fit that picture. Consolidated sales were ₹177 crore, down 11.5 percent from ₹200 crore a year earlier. Consolidated profit after tax was ₹12 crore, down from ₹16 crore. [1][23] Management blamed temporary delays in customer approvals and a few last technical issues. [21] That may prove right, but the number is what the scorecard sees. The quarter before, March 2026, had sales of ₹488 crore, so the quarterly line swings a great deal.

What this means for an owner: the growth is real over three years, but the most recent quarter went the wrong way, and the company has not yet shown that the delayed revenue will arrive. The next results will show whether management's explanation holds.

Margins: high, but the latest quarter fell

The operating margin, which is operating profit as a share of sales, was 29 percent in the year to March 2026, up from 21 percent two years earlier. [1] In the June 2026 quarter it fell to 19 percent. [1] Investywise reports the EBITDA margin, which is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization as a share of sales, at 18.7 percent in June 2026, against 20.5 percent a year earlier. [23] The two figures use slightly different definitions, but both show the same fall.

What this means for an owner: the margin is the number to watch next. A high margin on a large order book is good, but if it keeps falling while sales are flat, the earnings forecasts in chapter 6 are at risk.

7. What we would do and when

The decision is to replace the holding, not to add to it. This chapter sets out the rule, the levels that would change the verdict, and the dates to watch.

The verdict and its rule

The verdict is REPLACE_UNDERPERFORMER. The four holding verdicts are KEEP_COMPOUNDING, ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP, TRIM_PROFIT_BOOKING and REPLACE_UNDERPERFORMER. KEEP requires every gate to pass and the Stage 2 uptrend to be intact, and neither holds. ACCUMULATE requires the same passes plus a buyable price, and neither holds. TRIM needs an extended price, an RSI above 75, a bull target hit, or a breach of the single-name cap, and none applies: RSI is 53.56, the weight is 7.20 percent and the gain is only 0.30 percent. [27]

REPLACE applies when the thesis is impaired, and the clearest sign of that here is a failed hard financial gate on aggregator-reported cash figures that match across two sources, not yet checked against the audited annual report. Three years of cash conversion at 0.25 against a floor of 0.70 is that sign. This is a judgement, because the rule does not spell out how one failed hard gate compares with a broken trend. The alternative reading treats the Stage 3 as a warning and the cash gate as a historical record. Under that reading no trim rule fires, because the price is not extended and the gain is small. The result would be a hold with no add, but a hold needs every gate to pass, and the cash and asymmetry gates do not. So the verdict stays REPLACE, and holding the 200 shares is only the interim action until a challenger passes all five tests. The failed 3.5 floor is not overridden, because no verified anchor of ₹2,321.69 or more exists, and the cash finding is provisional until the audited annual report can be checked.

Levels that would change the verdict

The verdict moves only with the evidence. A daily close below ₹1,509.26, the formula stop, makes the exit mandatory under the stop rule.

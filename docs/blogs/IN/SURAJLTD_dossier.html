Suraj Limited makes seamless stainless steel pipes and tubes at two plants in Mehsana, Gujarat, and about 70 percent of its sales go abroad[2][15]. We say REJECTED because sales have fallen 44 percent in three years[2][5]. Much of last year's profit came from currency gains and a smaller depreciation charge, and at ₹214.70 a share the stock costs about 47 times its last twelve months of profit[2][1]. Our central value a year from now is ₹155, below today's price, so at our central value the possible loss is larger than the possible gain at every buying price we tested. We are wrong if the June 2026 margin of 16.5 percent proves lasting (at 14 percent or more) and quarterly sales climb back above ₹50 crore, which the results due on 29 October 2026 begin to test[6][17].

1. What the company does

Suraj Limited turns solid stainless steel bars into hollow pipes and tubes that carry hot, cold or corrosive liquids and gases. The company buys round bars from Indian steel makers, heats and pierces them into thick 'mother pipes', and then draws or rolls those down to the final size on cold pilger and drawing mills[15]. It also runs an electro-polishing line that gives the inside and outside of a tube a smooth, clean finish[2]. Its products include heat exchanger tubes, U-shaped tubes for heat exchangers, thin instrumentation tubes, pipes, butt-weld fittings, flanges and stub ends[15][36]. A heat exchanger is a metal vessel in which hot fluid warms or cools another fluid through tube walls, which is why the tube has to be thin, uniform and resistant to rust.

Customers are makers of process equipment and project contractors in oil and gas, refineries, petrochemicals, power, fertilisers, pharmaceuticals and water treatment[33][2]. Suraj is a government-recognised export house, and its website says it sells in more than 70 countries[14]. In the year to March 2026 the company earned foreign currency worth ₹144.93 crore, which is 70.4 percent of its ₹205.85 crore of revenue[2]. Two buyers matter most: T.L.P. S.p.A. took ₹35.72 crore of sales (17.4 percent) and Srinox BVBA took ₹22.87 crore (11.1 percent), and the same two names were above 10 percent in the year before[2]. Together they were 28.5 percent of revenue and held ₹21.53 crore of the ₹36.70 crore that customers owed at year end[2].

The company is controlled by the Shah family and related promoters. Twelve named promoters hold 75.00 percent, and no shares are shown as pledged to lenders[2][1]. The group dates from 1960, and Mr Ashok Shah is Chairman and Chief Financial Officer, Mr Kunal Shah is Managing Director and Mr Gunvant Shah is a Whole-time Director[14]. The paid-up capital is 1,83,64,100 shares of ₹10 each[2]. Only about a quarter of the shares, with a value of ₹98.57 crore, is in public hands, and the session ledger records only 2,366 shares traded on 27 October 2026, so the stock is thinly traded[16].

Money comes in lumps and is tied to metal prices. Raw material used was ₹144.84 crore, or 70.4 percent of revenue, and after the change in stock of unfinished and finished goods the net material cost was 62.6 percent[2]. Nickel is the costly ingredient of 300-series stainless steel, and London nickel moved from about US$16,365 a tonne in early July 2026 to a year-to-date low of US$15,100 on 15 October[26]. When metal falls, the company can either keep its selling price and widen its margin or pass the saving on, and the filings do not say which it did.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • relative strength index (RSI) is a meter from 0 to 100 of how fast a share price has moved lately; the full name is relative strength index, readings under 30 are called oversold and above 70 overbought.
  • ATR stands for average true range, the average size of a stock's daily price swing in rupees; it is used to measure how far the price sits from a level.
  • EBITDA stands for earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, a profit measure that ignores borrowing costs, tax and the wearing out of equipment.
  • ROCE stands for return on capital employed, the profit earned each year on the money tied up in the business.
  • EPS stands for earnings per share, which is the company's profit divided by the number of shares.
  • free cash flow is the cash left after running the business and buying equipment.
  • FCF is the short form of free cash flow, as defined in the line above.
  • operating margin is the profit left from each rupee of sales after the costs of running the business, before interest and tax.
  • debt-to-equity is total borrowings divided by shareholders' money; below 0.7 is a comfortable level.
  • net debt is borrowings minus the cash a company holds.
  • price-to-earnings (P/E) is the share price divided by earnings per share; it tells you how many years of current profit you pay for up front.
  • cash flow from operations is the cash the business itself generates, before spending on equipment or paying financiers.
  • working capital is the money tied up in stock and unpaid customer bills, less what the company owes its suppliers.
  • capital expenditure is spending on plants, machines and buildings.
  • buyback is a company buying its own shares back from the market, which shrinks the number of shares.
  • guidance is a forecast that the company itself publishes for a coming period.
  • consensus estimate is the average of the profit forecasts that brokerage analysts publish.
  • 20-day moving average is the average closing price of the last 20 trading days; the 50-day moving average and the 200-day moving average are the same idea over 50 and 200 days, and the price often pauses or turns near them.
  • Stage 2 is the Weinstein stage of a confirmed uptrend, and Stage 4 is a confirmed downtrend; Stage 1 is a flat floor after a decline.
  • A base is a flat stretch of trading, often several months long, that a price builds before its next big move; its pivot is the top of that stretch.
  • A breakout is a move above the pivot on strong trading volume, and a retest is the pullback that comes back to check the pivot from above.
  • A stop loss is the price at which we agree the thesis is wrong and we sell; a limit order is an order that fills only at your price or better.
  • A reverse DCF works backwards from today's share price to the yearly profit growth that the price needs; the discount rate is the yearly return an investor demands, which is used to shrink future cash to today's value.
  • The entry zone is the band of prices where we would be willing to buy, and asymmetry (also called reward-to-risk) is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop: (target minus price) divided by (price minus stop).

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The business has real strengths. It belongs to a group founded in 1960. It has sold to the same two large overseas buyers for at least two years. It keeps a testing laboratory in its own works[14][2][33]. It makes tubes up to 30 metres long and finishes them by pickling, annealing and electro-polishing. Drug makers and other buyers that need clean surfaces value that finish[14][2]. The company also has almost no long-term debt. The annual report says long-term borrowings were nil at 31 March 2026. Its bank loan is working capital, guaranteed by three promoter directors and a partnership firm, Suraj Impex LLP[2]. A buyer that puts a tube in a refinery wants a supplier that will still be there in ten years. A family-run exporter with a six-decade record gives some comfort on that point.

The weaknesses are scale, pricing power and protection. Annual revenue of ₹205.85 crore is less than one quarter of Venus Pipes and Tubes, which reported ₹320.5 crore for April to June 2026. It is about one twenty-second of Ratnamani Metals, which reported ₹4,494 crore for the year to March 2026[2][28][30]. India's anti-dumping duty on seamless tubes is under review until January 2027. It covers iron, alloy and non-alloy steel. It explicitly excludes stainless steel, so Suraj has no trade-remedy shield against cheap imports[22][23]. Chinese stainless steel exports to India were 65,000 tonnes in September 2026, which is 367 percent above a year earlier. In the first nine months they were 359,000 tonnes, up 172.9 percent[25]. The data cover all stainless steel, not seamless tube alone. They still show how much low-cost metal is moving toward India.

Demand is growing, but it is not reaching Suraj. The Indian Stainless Steel Development Association says national consumption was about 4.8 million tonnes in 2024-25. It expects 7 to 8 percent growth a year to about 6.8 million tonnes by 2030. India uses 3.4 kg per person, against a world average of 6 to 6.5 kg[24]. Yet Suraj's output fell 7.9 percent to 5,522 tonnes in 2025-26. Its sales fell 22.5 percent in April to June 2026[2][20]. In the same quarter Venus grew sales 16 percent, with domestic sales up 31 percent. Ratnamani's standalone sales fell about 30 percent on lower government spending[28][31]. The market for stainless tube is therefore split. Buyers of welded pipe and project pipe are being served. Buyers of the narrow, high-grade tubes that Suraj makes are not clearly placing more orders.

The company's own explanation is thin. The Chairman's letter blames raw material price swings, margin pressure, uneven steel demand, and higher energy and logistics costs[2]. Energy is a visible example. Total electricity bought rose 15 percent to 46.30 lakh kilowatt-hours while output fell 7.9 percent. Power use per tonne therefore rose about 25 percent, from 669 to about 838 kilowatt-hours. The annual report prints 989.07 for 2025-26, which does not fit its own totals, so we use the figure the totals give. The report does not explain the rise[2]. The company publishes no order book, no capacity utilisation and no sales volume other than output. An outside reader cannot tell how much of the fall is lower prices and how much is lost orders. We searched for an order book, an investor presentation and earnings call transcripts and found none.

The capacity plan and what it needs to prove

On 8 April 2026 the company told the exchanges it would raise capacity from 5,000 to 7,000 tonnes a year. The new line can make pipe up to 170 millimetres across. The cost is about ₹15 crore, paid from the company's own cash, and completion is expected by September 2026[12]. Two details need care. First, the same article says existing lines run at near-full use. Yet the annual report shows 5,522 tonnes produced in 2025-26, which is above the 5,000 tonnes the filing calls capacity. The filings we hold do not reconcile the two, and the 5,000 tonne figure may refer to a single line[12][2]. Second, the annual report dated 7 May 2026 shows no capital work in progress at 31 March 2026. A plant due within about five months had not yet been booked as under construction[2]. The plan helps only if buyers place orders for larger pipe. That test comes with the results due on 29 October and again in January 2027[17]. Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Management gives no numerical guidance, and we found no earnings call transcript or investor presentation, so there are no forecasts to compare with outcomes. What it does publish is a Chairman's letter, a Management Discussion section, one exchange filing about capacity and a record of how it spent money. We compared each with what happened next. The pattern is that stated intentions have been broad, and the results have gone the other way for three years: sales of ₹366.64 crore in 2022-23, ₹330.67 crore in 2023-24, ₹233.74 crore in 2024-25 and ₹205.85 crore in 2025-26[5][4][3][2].

Capital allocation is mixed. The company paid an interim dividend of ₹1.50 a share, which is ₹2.75 crore, in each of the last three years, including 2024-25 when cash flow from operations was negative ₹9.01 crore and borrowings rose by ₹30.42 crore[3][2]. Layer 1 underwriting: static levels, trajectory (rate of change), and archetype fit

The June 2026 quarter made 16.5 percent, and four of the five quarters in the table of Chapter 3 were below 10 percent, so the true level is somewhere between. If June's 16.5 percent lasted through 2027-28 on the same ₹210 crore of sales, earnings per share would be about ₹10.35, and 23 times that is ₹238, which is 11 percent above today's price[6][11][10][9][8]. We think a 12 percent margin is fair because that was the level of two full years, but it is an estimate. Two quarters at 14 percent or more on sales above ₹50 crore would force a rethink, and that is the single threshold we use throughout.

The multiple may be too low for a scarce, thinly traded stock

We use 23 times because it is the average of two peers that differ widely. A company with only a quarter of its shares in public hands, a family that does not sell, and very few listed rivals that make seamless stainless tube can hold a premium that has little to do with earnings[16][2]. The current 46.4 times (47.5 at the ₹214.70 close) is such a premium. If the market keeps paying 35 times, the central value is ₹236 and the reward-to-risk at the top of the entry zone is 2.3. We do not assume the premium persists because it has no earnings support at a 7 percent return on capital[1].

Our information has gaps

We found no earnings call transcript, no order book, no investor presentation and no analyst coverage, so we cannot test management's view of demand. The June 2026 filing is a scanned image, which we read by optical character recognition and cross-checked against a published summary[6][7]. The company's own debt-to-equity figure of 0.65 does not match the balance sheet, which gives 0.45, and the filings do not explain the negative depreciation in the March 2026 quarter or the halved charge since[2][8]. The half-year results due on 29 October, after our price date, will fill some gaps and may open new ones[17][18].

Control and related-party risk is a one-way risk

Related-party purchases rose from 4.5 percent to 28.2 percent of raw material in one year, the company borrowed ₹5.37 crore from an associate in which it holds ₹12.78 crore of equity, and the accounting software did not log some direct changes to the database[2]. The Company Secretary resigns on 31 October 2026[1]. None of these is proof of a problem, and the shareholders approved the related-party limits[13]. They can only lower the value we assign.

The decision is REJECTED, and the rule is the reward-to-risk floor. A turnaround idea must offer at least 2.5 times as much gain as loss. Our central value of ₹155 is below the ₹214.70 close. It is also below the ₹191.50 top of the entry zone. The ratio is the gain to the central value divided by the loss to the stop. The ratio is therefore negative at both prices. It is -1.41 at the close and -1.91 at the zone top, with a stop of ₹172.36. We do not bend the target to find a positive number. Even the bull case of ₹307 gives 2.18 at the close, below the floor.

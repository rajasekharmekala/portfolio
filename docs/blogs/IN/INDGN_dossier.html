Lead

History and operating identity

Indegene was incorporated in 1998 and operates as a digital-first life-sciences partner spanning discovery, evidence, regulatory work, commercial launch, content, engagement, and patient outcomes. The FY26 snapshot reports 27 years of healthcare expertise, relationships with all 20 top global pharmaceutical companies, more than 5,500 people, 17-plus delivery centres, and presence in more than 80 markets. These indicators support scale, but they are not a substitute for contract retention or organic growth.[2]

The acquisition programme has added medical communications, consulting, and regional delivery capability. The FY26 materials identify the Cake Kommunikations group as acquired on 30 January 2026. The diligence question is whether acquired revenue preserves the parent margin and cash profile after integration; a deal-level return bridge is N/A in the fetched extracts.[3]

Business model and unit economics

The company combines specialist people, governed processes, analytics, and purpose-built technology. Its unit economics depend on utilisation, billing rates, retention, delivery quality, and platform reuse rather than physical capacity. Top-client revenue was 9.3%, top five 31.2%, top ten 47.3%, and top twenty 68.6% in the FY26 operating snapshot. The concentration profile passes the 40% client screen, but a few procurement decisions can still affect a quarter.[4]

Sales rose from ₹2,306 crore in FY23 to ₹3,510 crore in FY26, a calculated three-year CAGR of 15.1%; PAT rose from ₹266 crore to ₹401 crore, a calculated CAGR of 14.7%. OPM was 18% in FY26 versus 20% in FY24, so the business is profitable but not yet showing a clean margin-expansion trend.[5]

Financial record and cash discipline

Consolidated FY26 sales were ₹3,510 crore, operating profit ₹619 crore, PAT ₹401 crore, and CFO ₹651 crore. FY25 CFO was ₹442 crore and FY24 CFO ₹508 crore. The three-year cumulative CFO/PAT is (651+442+508)/(401+407+337) = 1.40x, above the 0.70x hard floor. Debtor days were 102 in FY26 versus 91 in FY24, so cash generation is strong but collections remain a monitoring item.[6]

Forensic checks and governance

Governance diligence starts with the annual report, not with a market narrative. The FY26 source set identifies the registered office, exchange identifiers, consolidated subsidiaries, and statutory reporting architecture. The practical test is whether acquisition accounting, related-party balances, and cross-border cash movements reconcile to the consolidated statements. The public extract confirms the existence of subsidiary accounts but does not provide a complete sensitivity bridge for every earn-out, so those sensitivities remain N/A.[15]

Capital allocation has three competing uses: reinvestment in specialist talent and platforms, acquisitions that widen the service catalogue, and distributions to shareholders. FY26 free cash flow of ₹606 crore gives the company flexibility, yet acquisition spending can make reported investing cash flow negative without implying operating weakness. The investor should separate maintenance investment from purchase consideration and judge each acquisition against organic growth and margin retention.[16]

Valuation and scenario framing

At ₹586.15 and a reported P/E of 33.3x, the market is valuing Indegene as a growth specialist rather than a slow services vendor. That premium can be earned by durable organic growth, stable or expanding margins, and high cash conversion. It becomes vulnerable if FY26 growth is mainly acquired, if OPM falls below 16%, or if the next two quarters show debtor-led cash absorption. Forward EPS and a verified three-year median P/E were not available in the primary extracts, so a forward-multiple target is N/A.[18]

The base target in this dossier is not an analyst guess. It is the 63-day base low plus the measured pivot distance supplied by the frozen technical ledger: ₹481.95 + 5.33 × ₹19.54 = approximately ₹586.15? The ledger's pivot-distance convention places the mechanical target at approximately ₹690.36; because the target convention is not fully documented in the public source, it is treated conservatively as the available measured-move anchor rather than a forecast. This ambiguity reinforces, rather than weakens, the asymmetry rejection.[19]

A bull case requires three events together: sustained double-digit organic growth, OPM recovery toward 20%, and stable cash conversion above 1.0x. A bear case needs only one of them to fail if the multiple stays elevated. That asymmetry in business risk is why a good operating KPI score does not automatically create a buy signal.[20]

What an owner should do

An owner should not chase the stock merely because it sits above its moving averages. The price is near the support band, but the hard rule is target-to-stop asymmetry. Wait for either a lower-risk entry or a new base that increases the measured target. A close below ₹549.23 with failed recovery is the tactical invalidation level.[26]

An owner who already holds the stock should separate portfolio management from new-entry underwriting. This dedicated dossier has no holding transaction record and therefore does not issue a current-holding keep, trim, or replace instruction. For a fresh pipeline allocation, however, the failed asymmetry gate is decisive and the candidate is rejected from the autowritten Tactical Momentum book.[27]

The thesis can be reconsidered after the next results if sales remain strong, OPM is at least 18%, CFO remains at least 0.70x PAT on a rolling three-year basis, and the technical target recalculates to at least ₹690.36. If those conditions are met and the price has not already moved beyond the support ladder, the action can change to accumulate on dip. Otherwise the correct action remains wait, not a forced purchase.[28]

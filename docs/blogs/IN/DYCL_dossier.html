Terms used in this report

This chapter explains each technical and financial word once, with the plain meaning first. Each later use assumes the reader has read it here.

RSI stands for relative strength index. It is a 14-day speed reading from 0 to 100 that rises when recent gains outweigh recent losses. Readings above 70 mean the stock has run hot, and readings below 30 mean it has been sold hard.

ATR stands for average true range, which is the typical daily price swing over 14 sessions. We use it as a ruler: a stop or a support distance is counted in units of ATR so that a volatile stock and a calm stock are measured on the same scale.

ROCE stands for return on capital employed, which is the profit a company earns before tax and financing costs divided by the money it has tied up in the business. A business that earns above 15 percent on its capital can grow without constantly needing new money.

50-day moving average is the average closing price over the last 50 sessions. It is the most-watched medium-term trend line. A rising line means buyers have been in control for about two months.

200-day moving average is the average closing price over the last 200 sessions, roughly a year. A price above a rising 200-day line is the basic sign of a healthy long-term trend.

Stage 2 is the markup phase in the four-phase cycle of a stock. The price is above a rising 50-day and 200-day average. Stage 4 is the markdown phase, when the price sits below a falling long-term average and sellers are in control.

base is a sideways range that a stock builds after a fall, usually lasting several weeks. The low point of the range is the base low. A pivot is the price level just above the top of the base, where a move out of the range would begin.

breakout means a close above the pivot on heavy volume, which signals that the base has resolved upward. A measured move is an estimate of the next target, equal to the height of the base added to the breakout level.

stop loss is a price fixed in advance at which the position is exited to limit a loss. Here the stop is placed below the nearest support and then one ATR lower, so ordinary noise does not trigger it.

asymmetry is the ratio of the upside to the target divided by the downside to the stop. A ratio of 3.0 means the planned gain is three times the planned loss. The Core floor in this framework is 3.0.

price-to-earnings ratio is the share price divided by the profit per share over the last year. A ratio of 20 means investors pay twenty rupees for each rupee of annual profit.

debt-to-equity is total borrowings divided by the shareholders' equity of the company. A value near zero means the business is almost free of borrowed money.

working capital is the money tied up in receivables and inventory, net of what the company owes its suppliers. Rising working capital days mean cash is taking longer to come back from customers.

operating cash flow is the cash the business generates from its operations before buying new equipment. We compare it with reported profit over three years to check whether earnings turn into cash.

What the company does and how it earns

Dynamic Cables manufactures a broad range of power cables and conductors. Its products cover low-voltage, medium-voltage, high-voltage and extra-high-voltage power cables, control and instrumentation cables, solar cables, railway signalling cables, building wires and aluminium conductors, for both domestic and export customers[1].

Why this matters to a reader: cable makers are paid for projects. Utilities, railways and infrastructure contractors place large orders, and the timing of payment depends on how fast those projects are completed. A company can report strong profit and still wait a long time for cash, which is exactly the pattern the cash gate is designed to catch.

The Screener header reports a return on capital employed of 26.2 percent and a return on equity of 19.9 percent[1]. Its debt-to-equity ratio is 0.09, and total borrowings are about ₹43 crore (₹430.92 million)[9]. These figures pass the financial floors comfortably.

Fatal-flaw red team

The strongest case against this verdict is that cash conversion is lumpy. A cable maker that collects a large order late can show weak operating cash in one year and strong cash in the next. The annual table shows exactly that: operating cash was ₹1.26 crore in FY2024 and ₹61.59 crore in FY2026. Over five years (FY2022 to FY2026) the ratio is 0.85, above the floor. The framework uses three years, so the gate stays failed, but the verdict rests on the most recent three years, and a reader should know that a longer window would pass.

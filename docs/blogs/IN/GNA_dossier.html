Chapter 1: How the Business Got Here — Punjab Forging Roots, Global OEM Cycles & Pivots

Core Investor Question: How did a post-Partition Punjab engineering workshop evolve into a ₹1,778 Cr TTM global axle-shaft supplier, and how have its revenues and margins behaved across boom-bust freight and tractor cycles?

From Bundala's 1946 Workshop to Mehtiana's Global Axle Hub (1946–2016)

To understand GNA Axles Limited, you and I have to start in 1946 in Bundala, a town in Punjab's Jalandhar district [8]. There, Late Sardar Amar Singh Ji founded the GNA Group, originally Guru Nanak Auto Enterprises, as a small engineering and spare-parts workshop [8]. For nearly five decades, the family built deep metallurgical skill in Punjab's Doaba belt. It sat far from coastal auto clusters in Pune or Chennai, yet right inside India's tractor heartland. In 1993, the group formed GNA Axles Limited in Mehtiana (Hoshiarpur district, Punjab). The new OEM-focused unit made rear axle shafts and transmission parts to global tolerances [7][8].

Chairman Gursaran Singh led the company alongside Executive Directors Ranbir Singh Seehra (now MD & CEO), Jasvinder Singh Seehra, and Kulwin Seehra [3][8]. Through the 1990s and 2000s, they did the hard work of winning Tier-1 OEM approvals. Unlike aftermarket parts—where price rules and margins evaporate—an OEM rear axle shaft is a safety-critical component. If a 40-tonne truck's rear axle shaft shears on a highway grade, the wheel assembly detaches. Winning approval from Dana, Meritor (now Cummins-Meritor), and John Deere took years of metallurgical audits [11][16][17]. GNA also had to build horizontal upset forging and automated induction-hardening lines at Mehtiana and its second plant at Gulabgarh Jattan (Kapurthala district) [7].

After twenty-three years as a private manufacturer, GNA Axles went public in September 2016. Its ₹130.41 crore IPO priced at ₹207 per share, listing on the NSE and BSE on September 26, 2016 [9]. Notice how GNA used the IPO proceeds and internal cash. Instead of chasing unrelated ventures, it cut high-cost term debt from ₹153 Cr in FY15 to ₹117 Cr by FY17 [1]. It also funded automated Lasco extrusion presses and CNC machining lines for heavier export platforms [7].

Segment 1: Rear Axle Shafts (~74.3% of Revenue) — Concrete End-to-End Example

A rear axle shaft is a solid forged steel bar inside the rear axle housing. It has an upset-forged wheel flange at one end and precision splines at the other. When the differential splits engine torque, the shaft twists to turn the rear wheels. It also bears vertical and cornering shock loads [6].

Concrete Real-World Example (North American Class 8 Truck Axle Shaft): Picture a heavy-duty Class 8 tractor-trailer running on US Interstate 80. Freightliner or Kenworth builds the truck around a tandem drive axle from Dana or Cummins-Meritor [11][16]. That rig hauls 80,000 pounds (36 tonnes) over mountain grades in sub-zero winters and desert heat. Dana cannot risk a torsional fatigue crack at the spline root. Under multi-year contracts, GNA buys certified micro-alloy steel bars and shears them to length at Mehtiana. Workers heat the bar ends and use horizontal upsetters and Lasco extrusion presses to form the wheel flange in one continuous grain-flow [7][24]. Next, CNC lathes turn the shaft and cut or roll the splines. In-house induction lines then harden the outer skin while keeping the core ductile and shock-resistant [7][24]. After 100% magnetic-particle crack checks, GNA rust-proofs and packs the shafts in containers. Ships carry them from Mundra or Nhava Sheva to Dana's North American plants, billed in USD with alloy-steel price pass-through [11][16]. Notably, U.S.-bound medium- and heavy-duty truck parts have faced a 25% U.S. Section 232 tariff since November 1, 2025; while USTR's November 25, 2026 Section 301 notice proposes exempting Section 232-covered truck parts from additional duty stacking, USMCA/Mexican peers still benefit from non-U.S.-content carve-outs [30][31].

Segment 2: Spindles (~17.2% of Revenue) — Concrete End-to-End Example

Unlike a rotating drive shaft, a spindle (or stub axle) does not spin. It is a high-strength forged steel journal bolted or welded to the steering knuckle or trailer beam. Wheel hub bearings ride directly on its ground journals. That means the spindle carries the vehicle's full static and cornering weight [6].

Concrete Real-World Example (Global Agricultural Tractor & Off-Highway Spindle): Picture a 75-to-120 HP row-crop tractor built by John Deere or Kubota working muddy fields in Iowa, Brazil, or Punjab [11][17]. When the tractor turns hard with a loaded front bucket or raised rotavator, heavy bending shear hits the front spindle shoulder. At Gulabgarh Jattan, GNA drop-forges alloy steel billets into near-net spindle shapes. Continuous carburizing furnaces heat-treat the parts to prevent brittle fracture, and grinders finish the bearing seats to micron tolerances [7][24]. Priced per piece with steel pass-through clauses, these spindles ship straight to John Deere's global axle lines. GNA holds single-source or dual-source status on many of those part numbers [5][17]. However, off-highway agricultural and construction spindles exported to U.S. plants sit outside Section 232 on-highway vehicle exemptions and therefore face direct exposure to USTR's November 25, 2026 proposed 25% Section 301 tariff on Indian goods (17% if India concludes a reciprocal trade deal), with public comments due January 5, 2027 [30][31].

Segment 3: Drive Shafts, Splined Shafts & The New SUV/EV Expansion (~8.5% of Revenue)

The third pillar covers drive shafts, PTO (power take-off) shafts, and splined transmission shafts. It makes up ~8.5% of historical sales, but it anchors GNA's push into SUVs and electric vehicles [10][22]. Legacy Class 8 shafts are heavy (30–165 kg) and cyclical. By contrast, SUVs, pickups, and EVs need lighter, tighter-tolerance half-shafts and e-axle rotor shafts built for instant electric torque [6][22].

Concrete Real-World Example (Domestic Tractor PTO & Light-Vehicle/SUV Shafts): On a 50-HP Mahindra or TAFE tractor in Madhya Pradesh, a GNA PTO drive shaft sends engine power out the back at 540 RPM to run a thresher or rotavator [11]. Meanwhile, GNA's light-vehicle and SUV line is tracking above a ₹240 Cr FY27 annual run-rate (well ahead of an earlier ₹150 Cr target), while management has deployed ₹40 Cr of an ₹80 Cr EV and allied shaft capex tranche—with a further ₹40 Cr planned over the next 6–8 months through its wholly owned subsidiary GNA Mobility Limited (note: a June 8, 2026 exchange corrigendum confirmed GNA Mobility's investment plan is ₹80 Cr, correcting an ₹800 Cr misprint in the FY26 Annual Report) [4][22][38]. Across Mehtiana (~1.2M off-highway components/yr) and Gulabgarh Jattan (up to ~5.0M PV/CV/spindle components/yr), installed capacity has expanded to ~6.2–6.7 million parts per year [6][22].

Chapter 3: How It Is Sold & Delivered — OEM Logistics, Working Capital & Unit Economics

Core Investor Question: How does GNA Axles move forged steel from Punjab to North American and Indian assembly lines, why do debtor days sit at 151 days, and how does ₹100 of FY26 revenue cascade down to Free Cash Flow?

Direct OEM Qualification & Global Delivery Mechanics

Consumer brands sell through distributors or retail dealers. By contrast, GNA Axles runs a 100% direct B2B OEM and Tier-1 supply model [5][11]. No middlemen take a 10%–15% wholesale cut. To win a new platform, GNA's metallurgists and tooling engineers co-develop prototypes with an OEM's drivetrain team. They submit PPAP (Production Part Approval Process) samples and complete 12 to 24 months of fatigue-rig and field tests [5][7]. Once approved, GNA receives rolling quarterly schedules and monthly call-offs.

Domestically (~53% of sales), GNA trucks finished shafts straight from Mehtiana and Gulabgarh Jattan to plants across Punjab, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, and Haryana [7][10]. For exports (~47% of sales), workers rust-proof and palletize parts, then send them by rail or truck to Mundra and Nhava Sheva ports [5][10]. Ocean freight carries them to North America, South America, Europe, and Asia. Destination warehouses then handle just-in-time delivery to Dana, Cummins-Meritor, and John Deere plants [11]. GNA buys specialty alloy steel bars from qualified Indian and global mills under quarterly or semi-annual pass-through clauses. When steel prices jump, revenue rises and percentage margins dip slightly on paper, while absolute rupee profit per shaft stays protected.

The 151-Day Receivable Puzzle & Export Credit Financing

Now let us look past the clean P&L to check balance sheet friction. In FY26, GNA's Debtor Days stood at 151 days. That was up from 119–130 days pre-COVID and flat against 151 days in FY25 [1]. Combined with 117 Inventory Days and 129 Days Payable, GNA's net Cash Conversion Cycle reached 139 days (Working Capital Days: 133 days) [1]. Why does GNA wait five months to collect cash?

Two structural forces explain the 151-day DSO. First, ocean transit to the Americas and Europe plus customs and warehousing take 45–60 days. Only then does the clock start on standard 60–90 day OEM credit terms. Second, global Tier-1s like Dana and Meritor hold strong bargaining power over mid-cap suppliers. To bridge this five-month gap, GNA uses low-cost Packing Credit in Foreign Currency (PCFC) and bill-discounting export credit. That kept FY26 finance costs at just ₹13 Cr on ₹1,478 Cr of sales [1][2]. Still, 151 debtor days acts as a structural tax on ROCE, and the October 23, 2026 H1 FY27 unaudited balance sheet proves how fast working capital swells during a sales ramp: Trade Receivables jumped 19.9% in six months from ₹582.55 Cr (March 2026) to ₹698.27 Cr (September 2026) (~143 days on TTM sales), inventories climbed 42.7% YoY to ₹312.64 Cr, and Other Liabilities swelled 52% to ₹453 Cr [1][4][35]. As a result, H1 FY27 Operating Cash Flow fell to ₹61.15 Cr against ₹82.34 Cr of H1 PAT (74.3% conversion), while Q2 FY27 EBITDA margin compressed 188 bps YoY (from 17.7% to 15.8%) as Other Expenses (+74.0% YoY to ₹93.18 Cr) and Employee Costs (+55.6% YoY to ₹31.33 Cr) outpaced +49.8% revenue growth [4][35][36].

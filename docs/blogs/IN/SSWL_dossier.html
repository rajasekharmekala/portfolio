Chapter 1

From a Single Punjab Rolling Line to India's Dominant Wheel Maker

Steel Strips Wheels Limited (SSWL) began as a focused bet on the stamped steel wheel rim [2]. Incorporated in 1985, it started commercial production in 1991 at Dappar in Punjab [1][2]. Unlike aftermarket brands where marketing drives pricing, OEM wheel supply is an unforgiving engineering business. A single defective weld on a truck wheel can cause catastrophic highway failure. Automakers therefore audit metallurgy, fatigue testing, and delivery reliability for years before awarding a line [2]. That barrier helped SSWL expand into Chennai, Jamshedpur, Mehsana, Seraikela, and Bhuj [2][25].

SSWL aligned its physical footprint directly with raw-material suppliers and anchor customers [11][25]. Rather than shipping heavy steel rims across India from one hub, management built plants beside OEM lines and steel mills. Its Jamshedpur plant sits next to Tata Steel, which holds a ~6.9% equity stake, while Nippon Steel holds ~5.4% [11][27]. Those cross-holdings anchored steel coil supply and backward integration into hot-rolled profiles at Seraikela [2][25]. By the mid-2010s, SSWL operated ~20 million units of steel wheel capacity. It commanded ~54% of India's M&HCV steel wheel market and 42% of tractor wheels [11][27].

The 2018–2021 Stress Test: Debt, Promoter Pledges, and the Auto Downturn

A balanced audit must also examine how SSWL behaved when the cycle turned against it. Between FY18 and FY20, the company lived through a severe industrial stress test [18]. After investing in commercial vehicle lines and a greenfield Mehsana alloy plant, total debt hit ₹1,103 Cr in FY18 and stayed near ₹1,001 Cr in FY20 [1][18]. India's auto sector then entered a downturn driven by the NBFC liquidity squeeze, revised axle-load norms, and the BS-VI transition. Consolidated revenue fell 23% from ₹2,041 Cr in FY19 to ₹1,563 Cr in FY20, while EBITDA dropped from ₹246 Cr to ₹171 Cr [1].

The balance-sheet strain during that period was compounded by promoter share pledges. In 2019, promoters had roughly 50% to 57% of their shareholding pledged, partly to support associate group entities such as Indian Acrylics Ltd [18]. For an outside owner, high debt combined with heavy promoter pledges during an industry recession is a flashing red warning light. What separates SSWL from distressed peers that never recovered is what happened next: when COVID-19 disrupted Q1 FY21, management pivoted aggressively toward exports and higher-margin alloy wheels, staged a recovery to ₹1,749 Cr revenue in FY21, and began systematically paying down debt and releasing promoter pledges [1][18]. By 2024–2026, promoter shareholding stood steady near 61.2% with 0% pledged shares [4][6].

Three Strategic Pivots: Alloy Wheels, AMW Bhuj, and Aluminium Knuckles

Over the past five years, SSWL executed three pivots that reshaped its earnings profile [10][12][15]. First, it expanded from commodity steel rims into cast aluminium alloy wheels at Mehsana. Alloy capacity scaled toward 5 million units, lifting alloy's revenue share to ~32% by FY25 [10][27]. Second, in January 2024, SSWL acquired AMW Autocomponent in Bhuj through the IBC process for ₹138.15 Cr (₹5.00 Cr equity plus ₹133.15 Cr inter-corporate loans) [12]. Rather than absorbing legacy overheads, management shifted CV and OTR machinery to Jamshedpur and Chennai. It then repurposed Bhuj for clean-sheet alloy wheel and aluminium lines [15].

Third, SSWL entered aluminium steering knuckles—critical suspension components that reduce unsprung mass in passenger cars and electric vehicles [15][27]. Initial capacity of 0.5 million to 0.6 million units ramped rapidly toward 85%–90% utilization, prompting an expansion of another 1 million units in Gujarat [15]. In Q2 FY27 (quarter ended September 2026), those pivots converged with a domestic commercial vehicle recovery and export traction to deliver record quarterly revenue of ₹1,930 Cr (+61% YoY) and EBITDA of ₹236 Cr (12.2% margin) [1][7]. Yet immediately following that milestone, in October 2026, the Board approved a ₹1,400 Cr greenfield plant in Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar over 60 months [8][9]. You can see the recurring pattern: every time SSWL digests one capex cycle, management launches the next.

Chapter 2

Anatomy of the Wheel Portfolio: Steel vs. Alloy and New Lightweighting Components

At first glance, SSWL looks like a single-product company: it makes wheels. Look closer at the factory floor, however, and you will find two fundamentally different manufacturing businesses—stamped steel rims and cast aluminium alloy wheels—plus an emerging precision-cast suspension business in aluminium steering knuckles [2][10][15]. In FY25, steel wheels generated roughly 68% to 69% of consolidated revenue, while aluminium alloy wheels contributed roughly 32% (on ~3.33 million alloy wheels sold) [10]. By FY26 and H1 FY27, alloy's contribution climbed further toward the 35%–45% band as Mehsana and Bhuj ramped up and management deliberately walked away from low-margin legacy passenger-car steel wheel contracts [10][24]. Consolidated sales reached ₹5,183 Cr in FY26 and ₹6,235 Cr on a trailing twelve-month (TTM) basis through September 2026 [1].

Why does the split between steel and alloy matter so much to you? Because a stamped steel wheel is a high-volume, thin-margin conversion business, whereas a low-pressure die-cast (LPDC) aluminium alloy wheel carries roughly three to four times higher realization per unit and substantially higher EBITDA per wheel [10][14]. Across the overall portfolio, SSWL's EBITDA per wheel expanded from ₹242 in prior comparable periods to ₹404 in recent reporting, driven by alloy mix, commercial vehicle recovery, and exports [14]. Segment 1: Passenger Vehicle Alloy Wheels — The Premiumization Engine

Consider a concrete end-to-end example of how SSWL wins and delivers an alloy wheel program. Take an Indian SUV maker such as Mahindra & Mahindra or Tata Motors launching a mid-tier electric or internal-combustion SUV. The automaker's engineering problem is dual: buyers increasingly demand diamond-cut, multi-spoke aesthetics even on mid-level trims, while vehicle engineers must shave unsprung rotational weight to meet range and fuel-economy targets [10][24]. SSWL's design team at Mehsana co-engineers the wheel geometry using finite-element impact and radial-fatigue simulations [2]. Once validated, molten A356 aluminium alloy is injected upward into precision steel dies using Low Pressure Die Casting (LPDC), minimizing porosity [2]. Each casting undergoes X-ray inspection, heat treatment, CNC diamond-cut machining, and multi-coat powder painting before being sequenced to the OEM assembly line [2]. Pricing is structured as a conversion fee plus a trailing three-month aluminium index pass-through, shielding SSWL from raw-metal spikes over an annual cycle [16].

Segment 2: Commercial Vehicle Steel Wheels — The Heavy-Duty Backbone

Now walk across to Jamshedpur, where SSWL commands roughly 54% of India's M&HCV steel wheel market and supplies 53% to 60% of Tata Motors' wheel requirements alongside 64% to 65% of Ashok Leyland's [11]. Here, the customer is a commercial truck OEM building 35-tonne to 55-tonne tractor-trailers for highway freight. The engineering problem is not styling; it is structural load endurance under overloaded Indian road conditions without adding dead weight that eats into payload. SSWL procures high-strength low-alloy (HSLA) hot-rolled steel coils directly from neighbouring Tata Steel, profiles the rim band through roll-forming and flash-butt welding, forms the heavy centre disc on hydraulic presses, joins them via submerged-arc welding, and applies cathodic electro-deposition (CED) primer [2][25]. Pricing follows a quarterly steel pass-through formula, meaning SSWL earns a stable conversion spread per kilogram or per wheel while carrying the working-capital float between coil purchase and OEM collection [16].

Segment 3: Tractors, OTR, and 2W/3W Steel Wheels — Rural & EV Niches

In tractors and off-the-road (OTR) equipment—where SSWL holds ~42% domestic market share—a tractor manufacturer such as Mahindra Farm Equipment needs deep-dish, heavy-gauge rear wheels capable of surviving high-torque mud traction and ballasted ploughing [11]. SSWL manufactures these thick-section rims at Dappar and Bhuj, where lower competitive intensity yields healthier spreads than commodity car steel wheels [10][25]. Meanwhile, in two- and three-wheelers, the growth catalyst has shifted to electric scooters and e-rickshaws. An Indian EV two-wheeler OEM requires custom-stamped steel wheels engineered to house rear hub motors and withstand higher instant torque; SSWL's high-speed stamping lines deliver these at scale, allowing the 2W/3W segment to outpace broader domestic industry volumes [10].

Segment 4: Aluminium Steering Knuckles — Beyond the Wheel

SSWL's newest segment moves inboard from the wheel rim to the suspension upright. An automotive steering knuckle connects the wheel hub, brake caliper, and suspension strut. Traditionally made from heavy cast iron, modern EVs and SUVs are switching to cast aluminium knuckles to cut unsprung weight by 40% to 50% and improve steering responsiveness [15][27]. Previously, Indian OEMs imported these safety-critical castings. SSWL commissioned an initial 0.5 million to 0.6 million unit line—ramping to 85%–90% utilization—and is adding another 1 million units in Gujarat [15][27]. For an EV platform like Mahindra's born-electric SUVs, SSWL casts, heat-treats, and CNC-machines the knuckle to micron-level bearing tolerances, pricing it on a specialized casting-plus-machining contract that mirrors its alloy wheel playbook [15].

Segment 5: Global Exports (US & EU) — The High-Margin Counterweight

Exports generated ₹561 Cr in FY25—split 64% to the United States and 32% to the European Union—and continued scaling in FY26 and H1 FY27 [10]. Consider a North American trailer or OTR distributor seeking alternatives to Chinese steel wheels hit by U.S. Section 232 tariffs and anti-dumping duties [23]. SSWL manufactures TUV-certified steel trailer wheels and aftermarket/OEM alloy wheels in Chennai and Mehsana, packs them into ocean containers, and ships them under multi-year supply agreements [2][10]. Export wheels carry higher EBITDA per unit than domestic OEM steel wheels, though they expose SSWL to longer ocean-transit inventory cycles and U.S./EU trade-policy shifts [14][23].

NARRATOR'S REFLECTION: Why Product Mix Drives the Entire P&L

If you treat SSWL as a generic auto-ancillary maker, you miss the real story. Walking away from low-margin passenger car steel wheels while doubling down on alloy wheels, aluminium knuckles, and US/EU trailer and alloy exports is why EBITDA per wheel jumped from ₹242 to ₹404.

Every percentage point of revenue that shifts from a commodity stamped steel rim into an LPDC alloy wheel or a CNC-machined steering knuckle raises the profit earned per ton of factory floor space—provided the plants stay above 75%–80% utilization.

Go-To-Market Anatomy: Direct B2B OEM Sequencing vs. Export Distributors

Unlike consumer brands that rely on retail dealers, SSWL runs a direct business-to-business (B2B) model [2][11]. Roughly 85% to 88% of revenue goes straight to domestic and global OEMs under multi-year platform nominations. The balance moves through US/EU export distributors and selective domestic aftermarket channels [10][11]. Winning an OEM platform follows a rigid 12-to-24-month engineering cycle [2][11]. SSWL engineers join at the CAD stage, build prototype tooling, and run TUV-certified fatigue and impact tests. Plants must also pass PPAP audits before a single production wheel is billed [2].

Once SSWL wins a Share of Business (SOB) allocation—such as 53%–60% at Tata Motors, 56%–64% at Mahindra, 64%–65% at Ashok Leyland, or 32%–36% at Maruti Suzuki—its dispatch team integrates directly with the OEM's just-in-time (JIT) enterprise resource planning schedules [11]. Wheels are bulky, heavy, and expensive to haul across highways, so shipping finished rims more than 300 to 400 kilometers erodes conversion margins rapidly. That physical reality explains SSWL's multi-hub plant map across India [25].

Raw Material Pass-Through, Working Capital Cycle & DSO Mechanics

Understanding how SSWL prices its products protects you from misreading quarterly revenue swings. Both hot-rolled steel coils and A356 aluminium alloy ingots are contractually structured as pass-through costs with OEMs [16]. Steel prices reset quarterly, while aluminium prices reset on a trailing three-month average [16]. When metal prices spike, SSWL issues supplementary invoices to OEMs—lifting reported revenue without adding proportional EBITDA rupees, which optically compresses EBITDA margin percentage even though EBITDA per wheel holds steady [14][16]. Conversely, when metal prices fall, reported revenue softens while percentage margins expand optically. The cleaner gauge of operational health is EBITDA per wheel (which rose from ₹242 to ₹404 recently) rather than raw OPM percentage alone [14].

Working capital requires tight discipline in this business. In FY26, Debtor Days stood at 43, Inventory Days at 102, and Days Payable at 108 [1]. That yielded a 38-day Cash Conversion Cycle and near-zero Working Capital Days [1]. Inventory Days sit near 90–102 days because SSWL holds steel coils, aluminium ingots, and ocean-transit export containers [1][10]. Supplier credit of 95–108 days from domestic steel and aluminium mills offsets most of that inventory load [1].

The Competitive Battlefield: Oligopoly in Steel, Capacity Race in Alloys

The Indian automotive wheel industry is an engineering oligopoly split into two arenas [24]. Heavy steel wheels (M&HCV and tractors) are mature and consolidated. SSWL (~54% M&HCV share, ~42% tractor share) and TVS-backed Wheels India dominate this segment [11][24]. Heavy tooling costs and freight economics deter new steel-wheel entrants. By contrast, passenger vehicle aluminium alloy wheels are far more crowded [24]. SSWL competes directly with Uno Minda (scaling 4W alloy capacity from ~4.8M toward 9M+ units), Iochpe-Maxion, Enkei Wheels India, and Synergies Castings [19][20][24].

Automakers rarely award 100% of a wheel program to one vendor because a single furnace outage cannot halt an assembly line. OEMs dual-source or triple-source programs. They reward the lowest-defect, closest-proximity vendor with the largest Share of Business (SOB) [11]. SSWL's moat rests on four pillars: TUV/OEM fatigue qualification, plant co-location, 24–25M wheel scale, and Seraikela hot rolling [2][11][25]. Yet that moat has limits. SSWL lacks pricing power over raw steel or aluminium, faces fierce alloy additions from Uno Minda and Maxion, and must spend capex to defend EV platform SOB [16][20][24].

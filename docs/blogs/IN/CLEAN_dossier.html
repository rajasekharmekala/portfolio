Before you read

Clean Science and Technology makes specialty chemicals in Maharashtra and sells them to paint, plastics, drug, crop-science and personal-care customers in India and abroad. Its finances are clean: no borrowings, about ₹433 crore of cash and investments, and operating cash flow that has matched reported profit. The verdict is rejected for now, and the reason is the price chart, not the company: the 2026-10-09 ledger puts the stock in Stage 3, below its 50-day average and 3.14 units of average daily range above its support. The conclusion would be wrong if the stock reclaims about ₹815 on heavy volume, or if the next quarterly result shows margins holding at 36 percent or better while sales grow. Until then the numbers do not allow a buy.

What the company does

Clean Science makes chemicals that other manufacturers use as ingredients. In the quarter to June 2026, performance chemicals were 81 percent of sales, pharma and agro intermediates 12 percent, and chemicals for consumer products 7 percent.[9] Performance chemicals are additives that make plastics and coatings last longer in sunlight and heat. One group, called HALS, was 22 percent of sales and is the main new product line.[9] The older products still matter: the top four of them were about 60 percent of sales in Q1 FY27, down from about 85 percent in Q4 FY23.[9] That fall means the company is slowly becoming less dependent on a few products, which lowers the damage any one price war can do.

The customers are manufacturers, not households, and many are overseas. Rating agency CRISIL says exports are 60 to 65 percent of sales and the ten largest customers are about 30 percent.[8] A five-year supply contract with Kemin is expected to lift offtake by 20 to 40 percent and needs extra capacity.[10] The company publishes its product range and investor material on its website.[24] Management names BASF and Sabo in Europe, and Rianlon and Unitechem in China, as its competitors.[10] Raw materials were 39.7 percent of Q1 FY27 revenue, so a jump in feedstock prices reaches profit quickly unless contracts let the company pass the cost on.[9] In Q1 the company says it lost about two weeks of output when a key Indian supplier shut for lack of propylene, a raw-material gas, and it passed the cost on only in part because long-term contracts were honoured.[10]

The business is split into a parent plant and a wholly owned subsidiary, Clean Fino-Chem Ltd (CFCL). Management says all new products and new lines will sit in CFCL, and about ₹850 crore has been put into it so far, including roughly ₹100 crore in Q1 FY27.[10] The parent alone earned a 42.7 percent operating profit margin on ₹207 crore of revenue in Q1, while the group earned 36.5 percent on ₹268 crore.[9] The gap suggests the newer CFCL plants earn less per rupee of sales than the mature parent plant, which is normal while a plant is filling up but means group margin depends on how fast those plants ramp.

Three projects decide the next two years. A hydroquinone and catechol plant, launched in FY26, has stabilised, has customer approvals, and management expects revenue from August or September 2026.[10] The annual report sizes the market for these two chemicals at about ₹4,200 crore and says the 10,000 tonne plant could add ₹260 to ₹300 crore of revenue at full use.[16] A second performance-chemicals plant is due to start in Q3 FY27, with major revenue only from Q1 FY28.[10] A tie-up with Swiss firm Geneus Chem on a related product needs about ₹25 crore of extra spending and is expected to add ₹300 to ₹350 crore of revenue over three to four years.[10] Each of these is a management statement, not a result, so the next chapter treats them as promises to be tested.

Glossary of terms used in this report

  • HALS is a group of light stabilisers added to plastics and coatings so they resist damage from sunlight.
  • Hydroquinone and catechol are two chemicals used as ingredients in agro and other products; the company now makes them in-house.
  • EBITDA stands for earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization; it is profit from running the business before financing, tax and wear-and-tear charges.
  • EPS stands for earnings per share, which is profit divided by the number of shares in issue.
  • ROCE stands for return on capital employed, which is operating profit divided by the money tied up in the business; it shows how hard each rupee of capital works.
  • Operating margin is operating profit as a share of sales, which shows how many paise of each rupee of sales are left after running costs.
  • Price-to-earnings is the share price divided by earnings per share; it shows how many rupees buyers pay for one rupee of yearly profit.
  • Debt-to-equity is borrowings divided by shareholders' equity; zero means the company owes lenders nothing.
  • Net debt is borrowings minus cash and liquid investments; a negative figure means the company holds more cash than debt.
  • Working capital is the money tied up in stock and unpaid customer bills, less what the company owes suppliers.
  • Cash flow from operations is the cash the business generates from its day-to-day activity after tax paid.
  • Capital expenditure is cash spent on plants and equipment; this report also calls it capex.
  • Free cash flow (FCF) is cash flow from operations minus capital spending; it is what is left for owners.
  • Guidance is the outlook a company's managers give for future results; it is a statement of intent, not a result.
  • Consensus estimate is the average forecast of analysts who follow a stock.
  • Dilution is the fall in each owner's share of a company when new shares are issued.
  • 20-day moving average is the average closing price of the last 20 sessions; it shows the short-term trend.
  • 50-day moving average is the average closing price of the last 50 sessions; it shows the medium-term trend.
  • 200-day moving average is the average closing price of the last 200 sessions; it shows the long-term trend.
  • RSI stands for relative strength index, a 0 to 100 gauge of recent buying against selling; readings above 70 are stretched and below 30 are weak.
  • ATR stands for average true range, which is the average daily price swing over 14 sessions; this report uses it as a ruler for distances on the chart.
  • Stage 2 is the rising phase of a stock cycle, with price above rising 50-day and 200-day averages.
  • Stage 4 is the falling phase of a stock cycle, with price below a falling 200-day average.
  • Base is a flat resting range after a fall, where a stock builds a floor before any new advance.
  • Pivot is the price at the top edge of a base, where a move upward would be confirmed.
  • Breakout is a close above a pivot, ideally on higher than usual trading volume.
  • Retest is a pullback to a former resistance level, now acting as support, to see whether it holds.
  • Stop loss is a preset price below which a position is sold, to cap the loss if the idea is wrong.
  • Limit order is an order to buy at a chosen price or better; it will not fill at a higher price.
  • Entry zone is the price range in which this report would be willing to buy.
  • Asymmetry is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop; 3.0 means three rupees of possible gain for each rupee at risk.
  • Reward-to-risk is another name for asymmetry, written as a ratio such as 3 to 1.
  • Measured move is a target set by repeating the height of an earlier price range from the breakout point.

Why the business is strong or weak

The strength is financial. The company has no borrowings, and CRISIL reaffirmed AA-/Stable and A1+ ratings on 23 September 2026, citing gearing of nil, interest cover of 522 times and a current ratio of 5.[8][7] It held about ₹480 crore of unencumbered liquid investments in August 2026.[8] The March 2026 balance sheet agrees: ₹3,471.54 million of current investments, ₹577.81 million of non-current investments and ₹280.20 million of cash, which add up to ₹433.1 crore.[11] Cash is 5.1 percent of the ₹8,443 crore market value, so it is a cushion, not a reason to buy.[2] Contingent liabilities, claims not booked as debts, were only ₹15.34 million, or ₹1.5 crore, at March 2026, and contracts left to pay for plant were ₹292.17 million.[14]

The weakness is that growth and margin have both slipped. Screener's operating profit margin fell from 44 percent in FY22 to 37 percent in FY26 and 36 percent over the last twelve months.[2] CRISIL puts FY26 operating margin at 37.67 percent against 40.87 percent in FY25, and warns it could cut the rating if margin falls below 33 percent or revenue declines.[8] Sales compounded at only 1 percent a year over three years and profit fell 8 percent a year over the same period, which means the large capital spending of recent years has not yet paid back in earnings.[2] Q1 FY27 was a better quarter: record consolidated sales of ₹268 crore, up 11 percent from a year earlier, and profit of ₹73 crore, up 5 percent.[9] One quarter does not reverse three years, but it moves the direction of travel.

Ownership changed sharply. Screener shows promoters at 74.96 percent in June 2025 and 51.29 percent in June 2026, with foreign funds at 13.37 percent, domestic funds at 16.23 percent and the public at 19.11 percent.[2] Business Standard reported in August 2025 that the promoter group planned to sell up to 24 percent through a block deal at a floor of ₹1,030 a share, about ₹2,626 crore, and the company described the reason as family estate planning and liquidity.[19] A broker error briefly showed 56 percent as traded, which the company corrected.[20] The sale is not a statement about the business, but it shows the founders chose to hold less, and the stock closed the 2026-10-09 session 22.9 percent below that block floor.[1] Whether promoter shares are pledged to lenders is N/A: no shareholding-pattern table was read that gives it.

Two smaller risks need naming. The Maharashtra GST authorities searched the registered office, the Kurkumbh units and CFCL from 23 to 29 September 2026; the company states the determined liability was ₹9.9 crore under reverse charge, fully creditable as input tax credit, with a net cost of ₹2.0 crore and no operational disruption.[5][6] Separately, the auditor, Price Waterhouse Chartered Accountants LLP, gave an unmodified opinion but noted that the accounting software kept backup logs only from 7 May 2025 and did not have the database-level audit log switched on.[15] Neither changes the numbers, but both are the kind of control gap worth watching.

What could happen next

Management expects hydroquinone and catechol revenue from August or September 2026 and a second performance-chemicals plant in Q3 FY27, but major revenue from that plant only from Q1 FY28.[10] No consensus estimate was found in sources that could be checked, so the forward earnings figure in the next box is this report's own assumption, labelled as such.

The number

We estimate earnings per share of ₹24.11 for the next twelve months and a cautious target price of ₹964, 21.4 percent above the ₹794.40 close.

Why these estimates might be wrong

The weakest assumption in the forward earnings estimate is the 10 percent growth. Three-year profit growth was negative 8 percent a year, twelve-month profit growth is negative 13 percent, and one record quarter is the only support for a turn.[2] The evidence that would show it wrong is a second quarter with margin under 36 percent or sales below ₹268 crore. The next test is the Q2 FY27 result; the board-meeting date is N/A, and the trading window has been closed since 1 October 2026 until 48 hours after those results.[21]

The weakest assumption in the target is the exit multiple. The whole 3.17 to 1 asymmetry depends on the stock re-rating from 36.2 times to 40 times earnings; at 36.2 times the ratio is 1.46 to 1 and fails the 3.0 floor. This is the single claim the report hinges on. If the market keeps the multiple where it is, the stock needs earnings per share of about ₹26.63, which is 21 percent growth and not 10, to reach ₹964. Evidence would come from the price itself: a re-rating shows as a rise above the 50-day average on heavy volume before any earnings arrive.

The weakest assumption in the probabilities is that the bear case is only 30 percent. The Nifty 50 is in a downtrend with 10 distribution days in 25, and sector breadth is poor, so a fall to the ₹574 level cannot be ruled out. Cash of about ₹433 crore is 5.1 percent of market value, so it limits the fall less than the balance sheet's strength suggests. If the next quarter shows a margin under the 33 percent trigger, CRISIL's rating is at risk and the bear case should carry more than 30 percent.

How it was calculated

The Core stop rule uses the lowest of the 20-session swing low (₹766.00), the 50-day average (₹814.70) and the 200-day average (₹791.01), then subtracts 1.0 times ATR. On the strict 3-year median target of ₹1,249 it is (1,248.9 − 794.4) ÷ 53.56 = 8.49, and with no re-rating (₹872.8) it is 78.4 ÷ 53.56 = 1.46.[31]

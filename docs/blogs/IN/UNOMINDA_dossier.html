1. What the company does

Uno Minda Limited makes parts for vehicles. It was incorporated in 1958 and runs from Delhi and Manesar in Haryana. [1] [2] Its customers are vehicle makers, which the industry calls original equipment manufacturers (OEMs): an OEM is the company whose badge is on the car or scooter. In the June 2026 quarter, 94 percent of Uno Minda's sales went to OEMs and 6 percent to the replacement market. [2] About 89 percent of sales were in India and 11 percent abroad. [2] By vehicle, cars and other four-wheelers were 45 percent, two-wheelers 45 percent, commercial vehicles 4 percent, three-wheelers 3 percent and off-road vehicles 3 percent. [2] So the stock moves with Indian vehicle demand, and almost half of its sales go to makers of motorbikes and scooters.

Think of a modern car as a few thousand parts. Uno Minda supplies many of the ones the driver touches or sees. The switches on the steering column and dashboard, the headlamps and tail lamps, the alloy wheels, the seats, the horn and the sunroof all come from its divisions. [2] [3] Revenue in the June 2026 quarter was ₹5,557 crore, split into six divisions: switches 24 percent, lighting 21 percent, castings 20 percent, green mobility 10 percent, seating 7 percent and a mixed group called others at 19 percent. [2] The rating agency ICRA says it is the largest Indian maker of automotive switches and of passenger-vehicle alloy wheels, and the second largest in seating and in lighting. How the company earns money, and why margins lag commodity prices

A vehicle maker picks its parts suppliers years before a model goes on sale. Once Uno Minda wins a part on a model, it builds tools and capacity, and then supplies that part for the life of the model. The money comes from three levers: more vehicles, more parts per vehicle (the industry calls this content per vehicle), and a higher price per part as features become fancier. More than 95 percent of its product portfolio works in petrol, diesel, hybrid and electric vehicles alike, which is why management says it is not betting on one fuel type. [2]

Raw materials are the biggest cost. Of each ₹100 of June 2026 quarter sales, ₹66.7 went on raw materials, ₹12.9 on staff and ₹10.1 on other running costs. That left ₹10.3 of EBITDA, which stands for earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. It is the profit from selling parts before finance and accounting charges. [2] A year earlier materials took ₹64.2 of each ₹100 and EBITDA was ₹10.7. [2] Raw-material price changes are passed on to customers, but with a delay of a quarter or half a year, and at cost with no markup. The CFO said this squeeze alone cost about 40 basis points of margin in the June 2026 quarter. [3] A basis point is one hundredth of one percent, so 40 basis points is 0.4 points of margin. The stock's recent weakness started here, and Chapter 2B tests whether the delay is temporary.

The new businesses: where the next growth is meant to come from

Uno Minda is spending heavily to add new product lines on top of the old ones. At 30 June 2026 it listed 11 projects with a total cost of ₹3,788 crore, of which ₹1,406 crore was already spent. [2] The biggest are a second car alloy-wheel plant at Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (₹792 crore), two electric-powertrain plants for cars at Khed (₹437 crore) and Sambhajinagar (₹549 crore), a car-seating plant (₹320 crore) and an airbag joint venture plant (₹283 crore). [2] The plan for FY27 alone is ₹1,750 crore of capital expenditure, which is cash spent on long-lived plant and equipment, and the CFO said the remaining ₹2,000 crore of announced projects should be spent over 18 to 24 months. [4] [3]

Order wins give some visibility, but each is a peak-year value, not revenue today. Management cites a ₹450 crore annual-peak order for two-wheeler lighting with production from the second half of FY28, a sunroof order book above ₹500 crore with the first plant due by the end of FY27, export seating orders of about ₹390 crore a year from FY28, and an infotainment order of ₹600 crore a year from the third quarter of FY29. [3] [4] EV systems (chargers, converters and motors) had record sales of ₹186 crore in the quarter, up 130 percent. [2] These numbers are why the company can say it will grow faster than the industry. They are also why Chapter 6 asks what happens if the plants arrive late.

What the numbers do not show

Three things are not published. The company does not give profit by division, so we cannot say whether green mobility earns more or less than the group margin. The CFO said some of these businesses earn less than the group average at first and should reach target profitability only in their third full year of production. [3] It does not list its largest customers by name or share. Its annual report calls customer concentration a medium risk. [12] And the electric-powertrain joint venture with Inovance of China has Indian approval but still needs Chinese approval, after China tightened its rules on such technology partnerships. [3] Where a figure is not published we write N/A and say why. Chapter 2 weighs how much these gaps matter.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • OEM: original equipment manufacturer, the vehicle maker that buys the parts.
  • EBITDA margin: EBITDA divided by revenue. It shows how much of each rupee of sales is left after the cost of making and selling the parts.
  • Basis point: one hundredth of one percent. 100 basis points equal one percentage point.
  • Content per vehicle: the rupee value of the parts one supplier fits into a single vehicle.
  • Capital expenditure (capex): cash spent on plant, machines, tools and land.
  • Pass-through: raising selling prices to recover a higher raw-material cost, usually after a delay.
  • Joint venture (JV): a company owned with a partner. Uno Minda's share of JV profit is counted in its profit but arrives as cash only when the JV pays a dividend.
  • Annual peak value: the yearly revenue an order is expected to reach at full volume. It is not revenue today.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

A business is strong when rivals cannot easily take its customers or its profit. For a parts maker, the test is whether the vehicle makers keep re-awarding it new models, and whether it earns a fair return after paying for the plant. On the first test, Uno Minda scores well. ICRA names it the largest Indian maker of switches and of car alloy wheels and the second largest in seating and lighting, and says its business profile is helped by a spread across products, vehicle types and customers. [14] The company holds technology licences and joint ventures with partners such as Toyoda Gosei, Tokai Rika, Denso Ten and Tachi-S, which lets it add products faster than building each from scratch. [2] It has 78 plants and 37 research and engineering centres. [2]

On the second test, the return on capital employed (ROCE, which is operating profit as a share of the money tied up in the business) was 19.2 percent for FY26 on the company's measure and 19.6 percent on the Screener data. [2] [1] Screener's figure was 20 percent in FY24 and FY26 and 19 percent in FY25, so it has barely moved while the company spent about ₹4,277 crore on plant over three years. [1] [13] [12] Management says ROCE would be higher if it left out land and plants under construction that earn nothing yet. [4] That is plausible, but it is a promise about the future, not a fact we can test. A good return that stays flat through a heavy build-out is solid, not exceptional.

Profit per rupee of sales is thin and has not widened. The EBITDA margin was 11.3 percent in FY24, 11.2 percent in FY25 and 11.5 percent in FY26 (11.1 percent after removing one-off income). [2] Then it fell to 10.3 percent in June 2026. A margin around 11 percent leaves little room. Raw materials are about two-thirds of sales, so a 1 percent rise in their cost, if not passed on, takes about 0.7 points off the margin, which is roughly 6 percent of operating profit. That is why the speed of price pass-through matters more to this stock than almost any other number. The company says customers are giving some monthly price adjustments to speed it up, and in the June 2026 call it kept its full-year margin target of 11 percent, give or take 50 basis points. [3]

Where the moat is thick

A moat is whatever makes it hard for a rival to take the business. Here it has four layers. The first is qualification: a vehicle maker will not swap a safety-critical or visible part such as a switch or a lamp without years of testing, so once Uno Minda is on a platform it tends to stay. The second is scale and spread. No single division is more than a quarter of sales, and the group serves both cars and two-wheelers, so a slump in one does not sink the whole. [2] The third is technology access through partners. The fourth is financing strength: ICRA rates its debt AA+ with a stable outlook, reaffirmed in March and again in August 2026, which means lenders see a very low risk of default. [15] [14]

The evidence of the moat is in outgrowth. Over FY25 the company grew revenue 20 percent while industry volumes rose 9 percent, which is about twice the market. [11] Management's long-term goal is 1.4 to 1.5 times industry growth, measured over a full year. [6] The record is mixed since then: FY26 revenue grew about 17 percent against industry production growth of about 12 percent, and the June 2026 quarter grew 26 percent against 22 percent for the industry. [2] [4] [3] That is a lead of 1.4 times and 1.2 times. The moat is real but not widening quickly.

Where it is thin, and what the evidence adds up to

Four weak spots stand out. First, customers have the bargaining power. A few large vehicle makers buy most of the output, and the company calls customer concentration a medium risk without giving shares. [12] Second, the profit pool is thin and commodity-sensitive. Aluminium alone moved casting revenue growth by 4 to 5 points in the March 2026 quarter. [4] Third, some demand is shifting against it: car makers are selling more entry-level models, which use fewer alloy wheels, and some two-wheeler programmes switched from alloy to steel wheels. Management calls this moderation in alloy-wheel penetration near-term. [4] Fourth, part of the reported profit is not cash. The company's share of profit from joint ventures was ₹249 crore in FY26, about 19 percent of consolidated net profit of ₹1,284 crore, yet it received only ₹137 crore as dividends from them. [2] [12]

Put together, the evidence says: a leading, well-financed supplier with a durable position in switches, lighting and alloy wheels, earning a good but not outstanding return, with a thin margin that depends on quick cost pass-through. That is a compounder in the sense the portfolio rules use, but not one that earns a premium price for its quality; Chapter 3 shows that it already trades at a higher multiple than its closest product peers. What this means for you: the moat justifies owning the shares at a sensible price, but it does not protect the share price from a margin scare. Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Management's promises are the best guide to how much to trust its next promise. We read the last two years of calls and presentations and picked ten statements that can be checked. On money, management has mostly delivered. It promised in May 2025 to hold the full-year EBITDA margin at 11 percent give or take 50 basis points, and FY26 came in at 11.5 percent. [11] [2] It planned about ₹1,300 crore of capex plus up to ₹250 crore of land for FY26, a total near ₹1,550 crore, and spent ₹1,572 crore. ICRA had expected ₹1,500 to ₹1,600 crore. [11] [12] [16] Net debt (borrowings minus cash) to equity fell from 0.34 in June 2025 to 0.30 in March 2026 even though capex was heavy. [9] [4]

On timing and cost, the record is weaker. The Kharkhoda car alloy-wheel plant was due in the second quarter of FY26. In November 2025 it was “now under commissioning”, the February 2026 presentation shows phase 1 commissioned, and in the June 2026 quarter it was still ramping to full output. [9] [8] [7] [3] The Inovance powertrain plant at Khed was due in the second quarter of FY27 in August 2025, then FY27 in February 2026, then the second half of FY27 in May 2026. [9] [6] [4] And project costs have crept up. The two-wheeler lighting move to Kharkhoda was listed at ₹233 crore in August 2025 and ₹279 crore in August 2026, a rise of 20 percent. The whole project list, which also had projects added and removed, grew from ₹3,446 crore to ₹3,788 crore, up 9.9 percent. [10] [2]

Management is also open about what it cannot control. In May 2026 it warned that commodity inflation and a 35 percent rise in Haryana's minimum wage would hit margins in the coming quarters and said it was asking customers to shorten price-adjustment cycles. [4] The June quarter margin then fell 101 basis points from the March quarter, and the company said pass-through diluted the margin by about 40 basis points. [2] [3] It kept its full-year target and said it expects to be at the higher end of the band. That claim is untested until the 5 November results. [3] [1]

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate in Chapter 5 rests on assumptions, and the weakest one decides how wrong the estimate can be. This chapter names the weakest assumption behind each estimate, builds the strongest honest case against our verdict, names the single claim that carries the verdict, and says when we will know. A forecast that cannot say how it could fail is not a forecast. Read the next three subsections as the places where we think we are most likely to be wrong, and then the table that sets each risk beside its evidence and its date.

Weakest assumption behind the earnings: that the margin returns to about 11 percent

Our FY27 EPS of ₹24.3 needs an EBITDA margin near 11.0 percent for the year, which means roughly 11.2 percent in the second half after 10.3 percent in June. The evidence for it is the company's repeated guidance, ICRA's view that earnings are healthy, and customers' willingness to give faster price adjustments. [3] [14] The evidence against it is that the margin has been below 11.5 percent in each of the last three quarters (11.0, 11.3 and 10.3 percent), that gross margin (the share of sales left after direct material costs) fell for three straight quarters according to one analyst on the June call, and that the CFO said new electronics products have a lower gross margin than the old ones. [1] [3] Minimum wages in Haryana rose about 35 percent, which is a step up, not a lag. [4]

What would show we are wrong: a September-quarter EBITDA margin below 10.3 percent, or a cut to the 11 percent guidance. When we will know: 5 November 2026, when the board approves the results and management holds the call. We use one set of lines for that margin everywhere in this report. At 10.8 percent or higher the dip looks like timing, which is necessary but not enough to act on. Between 10.3 and 10.8 percent we keep waiting and place no order. Below 10.3 percent the dip is a trend, our central EPS is too high by roughly 7 percent, and we drop the plan and re-underwrite. Below 10.0 percent the verdict becomes REJECTED. The 10.8 line is the midpoint between June's 10.3 percent and the roughly 11.2 percent the second half needs for an 11.0 percent year. The company's own band floor is 10.5 percent.

Weakest assumption behind the value: that the market still pays 46 times profit

The central value multiplies EPS by 46. Nothing in the filings fixes that number. It is a judgement between today's 50.8 times and the 34 to 36 times of the nearest peers. [28] [21] [22] [24] Large holders have been trimming: mutual funds went from 15.32 percent to 10.95 percent in under three years, and foreign funds held 8.78 percent in September 2026 against 10.09 percent in March 2025. [17] [1] The market is in a downtrend, with 12 percent of stocks above their 50-day lines. What would show we are wrong: a P/E below 42 times on a positive result, or an index sell-off that takes the whole auto group lower. When we will know: continuously, but the first read is the price reaction after the 5 November results. A stock that rises on a good print is being re-rated; one that falls on a good print is being sold for other reasons, and our multiple is too high.

The bear case is that this is a good company at a rich price in a market that is going down.

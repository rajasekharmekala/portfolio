Chapter 1

Kody Technolab Limited was incorporated in May 2017 in Ahmedabad, Gujarat, founded by Manav Patel and Sanjay Kidecha as a specialized enterprise software studio and digital mobility consultancy. During its foundational phase from 2017 through 2021, the company operated primarily as a boutique IT services provider. It delivered custom web applications, cross-platform mobile apps using Flutter and React Native, enterprise resource integration. Offshore staff augmentation for mid-market clients across India, North America. Europe. This initial phase established technical hygiene, engineering recruitment networks. Delivery frameworks. However, management quickly recognized the structural ceiling of conventional software outsourcing. Specifically, rising bench costs, commoditized pricing, client churn. Lack of proprietary intellectual property. [1][2]

Between 2021 and 2023, Kody Technolab executed a high-stakes strategic pivot, reallocating accumulated engineering retained earnings and balance sheet capital toward deep-tech robotics, artificial intelligence. Autonomous mobile systems. Rather than remaining an IT services vendor billing time and materials, the company incubated an in-house hardware engineering and robotics operating software division, branded as Kody Robots. The objective was audacious. Specifically, design, prototype, assemble. Commercialize indigenous service robots capable of indoor navigation, surveillance, hospitality service. Industrial material handling. By late 2022, prototypes of Dasher (a multi-tiered autonomous serving robot) and Athena (an autonomous security and surveillance robot) were operational. This set the stage for institutional commercialization. [2][3]

The company crossed a key institutional threshold in September 2023 by listing on the NSE SME Emerge platform (Symbol. Specifically, KODYTECH). The initial public offering comprised 17.20 lakh equity shares at a fixed price of ₹160 per share. It raised ₹27.52 crore. The stated capital allocation priorities in the IPO prospectus centered on establishing an advanced robotics development and assembly facility in Gandhinagar, funding working capital for robotic component procurement. Expanding international business development. The listing transformed Kody from an unheralded regional IT contractor into one of India’s very few publicly traded pure-play robotics and autonomous systems innovators. [4][5]

The corporate operating record shows a dramatic structural inflection across three distinct revenue regimes. In the pre-IPO era (FY21–FY23), revenue expanded from under ₹10 crore to ₹38.4 crore, driven primarily by offshore digital engineering. In FY24 and FY25, consolidated sales stepped up to ₹51 crore and ₹72 crore respectively, with operating profit reaching ₹25 crore in FY25 as commercial robotics deployments commenced. By FY26, audited revenue reached ₹80 crore with operating profit of ₹24 crore. Trailing-twelve-month sales through September 2026 reached ₹111 crore with operating profit of ₹26 crore. However, this top-line surge has altered the risk profile of the business. Specifically, while software consulting carried minimal capital expenditure and modest working capital, robotics manufacturing has introduced severe working capital absorption, long hardware gestation cycles. Heavy assembly tooling capex. [1][6]

Corporate financing actions over 2024–2026 highlight aggressive balance sheet expansion. The company’s equity capital expanded from ₹13 crore in March 2025 to ₹14 crore in March 2026. Meanwhile, balance sheet reserves ballooned from ₹66 crore to ₹201 crore. Further to ₹215 crore by September 2026. This expansion was driven by preferential allotments and convertible warrant issuances to non-promoter strategic investors. These include the conversion of 13.88 lakh warrants in 2026. While these capital infusions provided cash buffers to fund heavy hardware research and development and international joint ventures, they diluted the promoter shareholding pattern. Specifically, promoter ownership contracted steadily from 73.01% in March 2025 to 69.49% in September 2026 and 68.82% by October 2026. An equity investor must evaluate whether per-share economic value creation keeps pace with this ongoing capital expansion. [6][7]

Real-World Walkthrough 2: Dasher Autonomous Food & Beverage Runner in Commercial Hospitality

Customer and Problem. Specifically, A 250-seat banquet hall and multi-cuisine restaurant in Ahmedabad faces acute server shortages, high waiter turnover. Slow food expediting during peak dinner rushes. This leads to cold food delivery and customer dissatisfaction.

A granular analysis of the robotics bill of materials (BOM) reveals substantial exposure to global electronics and semiconductor supply chains. The commercial billing cycle is where Kody’s business model experiences acute working-capital friction. A standard commercial robotics engagement follows a four-stage milestone structure. Specifically, (1) 20% to 25% advance upon contract signing and design freeze. (2) 40% upon factory acceptance testing and dispatch. (3) 25% upon on-site delivery, floor mapping. Commissioning. And (4) a 10% to 15% retention fee payable after a 90-day defect liability period. Because institutional customers (shopping malls, corporate offices, municipal agencies) frequently delay site readiness or stretch commissioning sign-offs, trade receivables remain locked on the balance sheet for extended periods.

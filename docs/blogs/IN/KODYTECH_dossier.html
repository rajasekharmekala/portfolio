1. Origin, Corporate Evolution & Past Business Cycles

Core Investor Question: How did an Ahmedabad software consultancy evolve into a capital-intensive robotics and programmatic media platform—and what business cycles has it weathered?

Kody Technolab Limited began in May 2017 in Ahmedabad, Gujarat. Manav Subhashchandra Patel, a mechanical engineer by training, founded the firm after stepping away from family businesses [3]. He partnered with Chief Executive Officer Sanjay Kidecha and promoter group members Manali Krunal Patel and Pooja Sunny Patel [3]. For its first five years, Kody ran as a boutique IT services shop. The team built custom web apps, mobile tools, and machine-learning software for Indian and overseas clients [3].

The Pivot to Physical AI and the 2023 NSE Emerge Listing

Management did not stay in pure IT services. They used early software cash flows to open R&D labs, including a branch in Gujarat's GIFT City (Gandhinagar) [3]. Engineers paired computer vision, LiDAR navigation, and AI software with physical robot frames [3], [5]. That shift created the Kody Robots product line. In September 2023, Kody Technolab listed on the NSE Emerge SME exchange [3]. The IPO opened on September 15, 2023, at ₹160 per share to fund robotics R&D and plant setup [3].

Even as a young firm, Kody has already crossed three distinct operating cycles. Cycle 1 (2017–2022) was an asset-light software phase. Margins tracked engineer billing rates, and working capital stayed low. Cycle 2 (2023–FY25) was the proof-of-concept robotics cycle. Demos at the Vibrant Gujarat Summit and Gujarat Police Expo raised domestic visibility [5]. FY25 consolidated sales hit ₹72 Cr with a 35% operating margin, though debtor days stretched to 177 days [1].

Cycle 3 (FY26–H1 FY27) is the industrial scaling cycle. Kody raised fresh equity via preferential shares and convertible warrants (25% upfront, 75% on conversion) and issued a 1:1 bonus [4]. Management formed Kody Media Limited for programmatic Digital Out-of-Home (pDOOH) ads [2]. They also launched Kody Middle East Holding LLC and the Falcon Tech Robotics LLC joint venture in the UAE [6]. At home, Kody invested in World EMS Private Limited for bulk robot assembly [7].

Cycle 3 reshaped the balance sheet fast. Between March 2025 and September 2026, total assets nearly tripled from ₹112 Cr to ₹298 Cr [1]. Sales grew +12% in FY26 to ₹80 Cr. Then H1 FY27 sales surged +97% YoY to ₹62 Cr, lifting TTM sales to ₹111 Cr [1]. Yet operating margins fell 1,600 basis points from 35% in FY25 to 19% in H1 FY27 [1]. Over the same span, working capital days jumped from 62 days to 197 days [1].

3. Unit Economics, Distribution Mechanics & the ₹100 Waterfall

Core Investor Question: How does Kody Technolab distribute its robots and software, and what happens to every ₹100 of revenue before it reaches Free Cash Flow?

Kody's balance sheet nearly tripled while its sales surged. To see why, we must trace its distribution channels and cash conversion. Kody sells through three paths. Why RaaS & Backward Integration Change Unit Economics

In software or upfront hardware sales, the buyer pays on Day 1. Under RaaS and pDOOH, Kody removes the client's upfront cost [4]. Instead, Kody finances the robot parts, books the unit on its own fixed-asset ledger, and waits for monthly fees or ad sales to pay it back [4], [7]. Kody also invested in World EMS Private Limited to handle bulk PCB and robot assembly [7]. As a result, Fixed Assets plus CWIP jumped from ₹15 Cr (Mar 2025) to ₹68 Cr (Mar 2026) and ₹86 Cr (Sep 2026) [1]. By H1 FY27, depreciation quadrupled YoY from ₹1 Cr to ₹4 Cr, while OPM fell to 19% [1].

Working Capital Friction: The 192-Day Receivable Bottleneck

Working capital absorption is Kody's sharpest unit-level red flag. In FY25, Debtor Days sat at an already high 177 days [1]. Working Capital Days stood at 62 days [1]. By March 2026, Debtor Days stretched to 192 days [1]. More than six months of sales sat uncollected on the balance sheet. Working Capital Days tripled to 197 days [1]. As a result, FY26 Operating Cash Flow was just ₹12 Cr against ₹24 Cr of EBITDA and ₹17 Cr of PAT [1]. Unless collections speed up, growth will keep requiring outside debt or equity.

Note also the role of Other Income in FY26. Non-operating income added ₹7 Cr, or ₹8.75 per ₹100 of sales [1]. That cash yield came largely from unspent preferential funds sitting in deposits. Core operations alone generated ₹19 Cr of PBT before Other Income [1]. By H1 FY27, Other Income added ₹3 Cr more on ₹10 Cr of PBT, showing continued reliance on treasury returns [1].

Unit-Economics Litmus Test for RaaS & pDOOH

Each Odigo or Athena unit under RaaS must earn enough monthly cash margin to pay back its build cost within 18 to 24 months. Receivables must also drop below 90 days. Until CFO/EBITDA recovers above 75% and FCF turns positive without equity raises, unit economics remain unproven at scale.

In India's public sector, domestic sourcing rules give Athena and Medigo an edge over foreign rivals [5], [7]. Police and civic buyers prefer local data handling and Indian assembly. Even so, long tender cycles and 192-day receivables reduce the cash appeal of government deals [1]. Commercial retail and healthcare exports must therefore carry most of Kody's long-term margin weight [2], [6].

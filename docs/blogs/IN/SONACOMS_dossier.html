1. What the company does

In plain words: Sona Comstar makes the gears, motors and brakes that sit inside vehicles and trains. It sells to carmakers in India, North America and Europe and is moving toward electric drivetrains, railway parts and robot parts.

Sona BLW Precision Forgings Limited, trading as Sona Comstar, describes itself as a mobility technology company that supplies systems and components for electrified and non-electrified powertrains. [8] [5] Its biggest lines are differential gears and assemblies (the parts that let the two wheels on one axle turn at different speeds), starter motors, and traction motors and controllers (the motor and electronics that turn an electric vehicle's wheels). It also makes suspension motors, radar sensors through its 54 percent subsidiary NOVELIC of Serbia, and, since 1 June 2025, brakes, couplers and suspension for Indian Railways. [15] [2] The railway business had revenue of ₹912.8 crore in FY25, before the purchase, and in July 2026 the company announced a robotics vertical with an order book of ₹800 crore. [14] [8] The company reports 12 plants, 29 products and a global share of 8.7 percent in differential gears and 4.2 percent in starter motors for 2025. [6]

The company earns money on the gap between what it charges and what steel, copper, magnets, labour and energy cost. Management says a large part of commodity inflation is passed through to customers, with a lag, and part is not. [7] In FY26 revenue from operations was ₹4,449.46 crore, up 25.5 percent. The gross margin, the share of sales left after materials, fell to 52.9 percent from 56.4 percent in FY25 on our arithmetic from the audited statements. Staff cost rose to 9.8 percent of revenue from 8.9 percent. [1] The company's own EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization), which is operating profit before depreciation and includes foreign-exchange gains, was ₹1,106.9 crore, a margin of 24.7 percent against 27.4 percent a year earlier. [5] [6]

The customers are carmakers, tractor and truck makers and electric two-wheeler makers. One customer was above 10 percent of revenue in FY26, with ₹789.4 crore, or 17.7 percent of revenue from operations (Chapter 3 shows the other bases); two customers together were 28.6 percent in FY25. [1] Chapter 3 puts every share on one table with its own denominator, so the percentages are not mixed.

In FY26 the company paid ₹491.84 crore for plant, intangibles and capital advances, and had ₹145.97 crore of further capital commitments at 31 March 2026, up from ₹70.01 crore a year earlier. Free cash flow is the cash left after capital spending, and the company puts it at ₹290 crore for FY26. [1] [2]

2. Why the business is strong or weak

In plain words: This is a strong engineering business with a net cash balance sheet and a large order book. Margins are slipping, cash conversion was weak in FY26, one customer is large, and the promoter family is in a dispute.

Sona Comstar is a good business that the market prices as a very good one. The strengths are a leading position in differential gears, a big electric order book and net cash. The weaknesses are falling margins, a return on capital that fell after the share sale and the railway purchase, heavy capital spending, and a dispute over who controls the 28 percent promoter stake. Chapter 2B scores the operating measures, and Chapter 3 puts numbers on each point. [8] [1]

Where the strength is

The company holds an estimated 8.7 percent of the world's differential-gear market and 4.2 percent of starter motors, and supplies 7 of the top 10 passenger-car makers, 7 of the top 10 tractor makers and 3 of the top 10 truck makers. [8]

The net order book was ₹24,000 crore at 30 June 2026, which the company says is 5.4 times FY26 revenue. Of this, 64 percent (₹15,400 crore, 46 programmes, 30 customers) is electric, 28 percent non-electric, 5 percent railway and 3 percent robotics. [8] Revenue grew 54 percent in Q1 FY27 (52 percent before foreign-exchange gains). About 18 points of that came from the railway business counting for a full quarter against one month a year earlier, on our arithmetic from the FY25 railway revenue of ₹912.8 crore. [8] [14]

The balance sheet is the third strength. At 31 March 2026 the group held ₹1,268.9 crore of cash, deposits and mutual funds against ₹242.7 crore of borrowings; the company calls the difference a net cash position of ₹1,026 crore. [1] [2] Management expects FY27 to add to net cash. [2]

Where the weakness is

Margins have fallen. The company's EBITDA margin was 27.4 percent in FY25, 24.7 percent in FY26 and 23.1 percent in Q1 FY27, while the gross margin fell 3.6 points in FY26 on our arithmetic. [6] [8] [1] The CEO blamed commodity cost lags, wage rises in Haryana and a product mix that leans toward traction motors, the lowest-margin line. [7] [9] The company now says a margin band of 23 to 25 percent applies after the railway purchase, down from 24 to 26 percent before. [7]

Receivables rose 63.2 percent to ₹1,150.7 crore at 31 March 2026 while revenue rose 25.5 percent, and inventory rose 76.5 percent to ₹615.6 crore. [1] Part of that is the Q4 sales rate, which was 47 percent above Q4 FY25, and part is the acquired railway business. [6] Q1 FY27 reversed some of it: the company says debtor days fell sharply and calls Q1 its best quarter for operating cash. [9]

North American revenue fell 18.0 percent to ₹1,096.2 crore in FY26 and Europe was flat at ₹651.3 crore, while India rose 84.2 percent, helped by the railway purchase. [1] The CEO said that in the prior year the company lost ₹300 crore of sales from one customer alone when a model was discontinued. [7]

Governance: the promoter dispute

The promoter is Aureus Investment Private Limited, with 174,208,904 shares, or 28.01 percent, unchanged in the audited note and the June 2026 pattern. [1] [16] An August 2025 report says the RK Family Trust owns 65 percent of Aureus, and that after the chairman Sunjay Kapur died on 12 June 2025 his widow Priya Sachdev Kapur and his mother Rani Kapur disputed control of that stake. [20] [3] The CEO said then that there was 'far more sensationalism around this than there is impact'. [20] The report is 14 months old, and we found no primary filing on how the dispute stands today.

The board is not controlled by the promoter. It has nine directors, six of them independent, and an independent chairman, Jeffrey Mark Overly, since 23 June 2025; Priya Sachdev Kapur joined as a non-executive, non-independent director. [3] [4] [6] The risk is therefore about the 28 percent holder, not the running of the business: an unsettled owner could sell, pledge or contest the stake. No pledge was observed in the readable evidence: the audited note lists the promoter shares with no encumbrance statement, and the secondary source shows 0.0 percent pledged in every quarter to June 2026 (Chapter 3). [1] [16]

Acquisitions, debt and overseas units

The Escorts Kubota deal was the purchase of a business, not a joint venture. Sona Comstar bought the Railway Equipment Division by slump sale from 1 June 2025 and paid ₹1,642.63 crore in cash in FY26, plus about ₹110 crore for land. [1] [12] [7] Goodwill rose to ₹556.41 crore, of which ₹176.02 crore is NOVELIC, and other intangibles rose by ₹868.4 crore. Auditors flagged the purchase-price allocation as a key audit matter because it rests on management's estimates, and the goodwill test found no impairment. [1]

Debt did not fund the purchase. Borrowings were ₹242.7 crore at 31 March 2026: ₹219.0 crore of rupee loans repayable on demand (₹74 crore working-capital loan and ₹145 crore export credit, at 6.01 to 6.33 percent linked to treasury bills), ₹15.3 crore of foreign-currency loans, and an ₹8.4 crore term loan at a Chinese subsidiary secured by a corporate guarantee. [1] The CFO says the September 2024 share sale left capital not yet deployed, which is why return on capital fell. [7] The cash came from that sale.

The new commitments are the DENSO joint ventures announced on 22 July 2026. In the first, for high-voltage traction systems in four-wheelers and larger vehicles, DENSO would hold 51 percent and Sona Comstar 49 percent. In the second, for two- and three-wheelers, Sona Comstar would move its existing traction motor business into a subsidiary and sell 49 percent to DENSO at an enterprise value of ₹1,750 crore. Both need regulatory approvals. [10] [8] We do not know what Sona Comstar will pay into the first venture or when the second closes, so neither is in our estimates. Overseas, the group has plants in the United States, Mexico, Serbia and China. [8]

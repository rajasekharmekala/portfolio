What the company does

Sona BLW makes the parts that move power inside a car. Its main products are differential assemblies, which let the two driving wheels turn at different speeds, precision bevel gears, starter motors, and motors that drive electric vehicles directly [3]. It sells to global carmakers, which the industry calls OEMs, short for original equipment manufacturers. Its shares trade on both the NSE and the BSE [3].

The company earns money by winning a place in a carmaker's new model and then supplying parts for the life of that model. A signed order is not yet a sale. It turns into sales when the carmaker builds the car. That is why the company reports its order book, the value of signed business still to be delivered. At the end of FY26 the order book stood at ₹237 billion (₹23,700 crore), about 5.3 times the year's revenue, and nearly 70 per cent of it was for electric-vehicle programs [5]. Read the order book as a promise of future sales, not as sales already earned.

The mix is changing fast. In February 2025 the company agreed to buy the railway equipment division of Escorts Kubota, effective 1 June 2025 [6]. On 22 July 2026 it signed two joint ventures with DENSO of Japan for electric and hybrid powertrain systems, each split 51:49. As part of the same deal, it will sell its existing EV motors and controllers business at an enterprise value of ₹17,500 million (₹1,750 crore) [7]. It has also entered robotics, where it holds ₹8 billion (₹800 crore) of orders [7].

Terms used in this report. An order book is the value of signed contracts not yet delivered. An OEM is a carmaker or a company that builds for one. An EV is an electric vehicle. A BEV is a battery electric vehicle, a car that runs only on a battery. A joint venture is a company that two firms own together.

Why the business is strong or weak

Scale and spread are the main strengths. At the end of FY26 the company had 67 EV programs with 35 customers, and 37 of those programs were already in production [8]. India became its largest market, rising to 51 per cent of revenue from 29 per cent in FY25 [8]. A supplier that sells across customers, products and countries is less exposed when one carmaker or one region slows.

The balance sheet is conservative. Borrowings were ₹442 crore at March 2026, against equity of about ₹5,983 crore (computed as equity capital of ₹622 crore plus reserves of ₹5,361 crore, both from [3]). The debt-to-equity ratio is borrowings divided by shareholders' equity, and the published figure is 0.07 [9]. Cash from operations was ₹659 crore in FY26, close to reported profit of ₹629 crore [3]. Cash that keeps pace with profit is a good sign that earnings are real. Promoters held 28.01 per cent of the shares at June 2026 [2] [10] [11].

The weak spots are the margin and the US market. The operating profit margin fell to 24 per cent in FY26 from 28 per cent in FY24 [3]. In the June 2026 quarter, EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) margin was 23.1 per cent, down 70 basis points on a year earlier. Management blamed input cost inflation and a lag in passing costs on to customers [4]. The company also says US demand for battery electric vehicles is weak, although its BEV revenue still grew 107 per cent [4].

Guidance is the company's own forecast of future results. Management said margins should improve progressively from the second quarter onward, as price increases catch up with costs [4] [12]. The company's projection, as summarised by India Ratings, is revenue growth of 20 to 25 per cent in FY27 [13]. The industry view is more modest: ICRA expects the auto component sector to grow 7 to 9 per cent in FY27 [14]. The market is growing too. Passenger vehicle sales reached 1.27 million units in the June 2026 quarter, up 25.9 per cent [15], and electric vehicle sales rose 25 per cent to about 2.55 million units in FY26 [16].

Taken together, the business is strong on scale and cash, and weaker on margin and returns. The returns are the subject of the next two chapters, because they decide whether the business passes the first hard floor.

1. What the company does

Centum Electronics is a Bengaluru company, founded in 1993 by A. V. Mallavarapu, that designs and builds electronic systems for places where a failure is costly. [2] Its products sit inside radars, electronic-warfare payloads, satellites, air-traffic systems, battery systems for electric vehicles, medical X-ray equipment and the machines that make semiconductor chips. [2] It employs about 1,650 people and runs two plants in Bengaluru, one at Yelahanka for defence and space work and one at the KIADB Aerospace Park in Devanahalli for volume manufacturing. [2] Think of it as a specialist workshop: customers who cannot afford a field failure pay it to design, qualify and build the boards and boxes that do the hard job.

The company has two businesses. Electronic manufacturing services (EMS) builds electronics to a customer's own design. It made 72 percent of standalone revenue in Q1 FY27, or ₹148.2 crore of ₹204.8 crore. [2][25] The customers are global equipment makers and Indian industrial firms, and pricing is cost plus a margin. Management says the EBITDA margin benchmark is 10 to 11 percent. [4] EBITDA stands for earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. It is operating profit before the accounting charges for wear on assets and for financing, and the margin is that profit as a share of sales. Build-to-specification (BTS) is the second business. Centum designs and builds a system to a government or prime contractor's specification, such as a radar for a helicopter or payload electronics for an ISRO satellite. BTS is 28 percent of revenue, runs two to two and a half years per project, and targets an EBITDA margin of 18 to 20 percent or more. [2] It also ties up cash. The company puts working capital, which is the money locked in stock and unpaid bills, at 225 to 350 days for BTS against 85 to 110 days for EMS. [2]

By end market, Q1 FY27 revenue was 45 percent defence, space and aerospace, 17 percent semiconductor equipment, 14 percent healthcare, 12 percent transport and automotive, and 12 percent industry and energy. [2] By geography, India was 46 percent, Europe and the UK 23 percent, and North America and the rest of the world 31 percent. The company says exports were 54 percent of revenue. [2] The public customers are the space agency ISRO, the defence research body DRDO, the aircraft maker HAL, the electronics maker BEL and the shipyard GRSE, and Crisil records a 20 to 25 year association with DRDO and ISRO. [2][8] The largest private growth story is one global maker of semiconductor machines, which management calls a key anchor customer. Its revenue went from almost nothing in FY25 to more than ₹100 crore in FY26. [5][4] That is about a tenth of the whole business, and it depends on one buyer's decisions.

Orders arrive before revenue, which is why the order book matters. The order book on 30 June 2026 was ₹1,797 crore, up 31 percent in a year, split ₹925 crore in BTS and ₹872 crore in EMS. [7][2] That is about 1.8 times the last twelve months' sales of ₹993 crore. [10][40] EMS orders turn into sales in 9 to 12 months and BTS orders over 25 to 30 months, so the BTS half is a multi-year runway. [3] The next table sets the two engines side by side.

The ideas this report uses

Profit is not the same as cash, and a project business can show one without the other. Cash flow from operations is the cash the business produces from running itself, before it buys equipment or repays lenders. Capital expenditure is money spent on plants, machines and software that last for years. Free cash flow (FCF) is cash flow from operations less capital expenditure, which is the cash an owner could take out. The gap matters at Centum because it pays for parts long before a customer pays for a finished system, so chapter 3 tests cash conversion directly.

Profit per share and price are the next group. EPS stands for earnings per share, which is profit divided by the number of shares; Centum has about 1.47 crore shares (14.74 million). [2] The price-to-earnings ratio is the share price divided by EPS, so at 60 times you pay ₹60 for each ₹1 of yearly profit. ROCE is return on capital employed, the profit earned on all the money the business uses. Debt-to-equity is borrowings divided by shareholders' money. The operating margin is profit from running the business as a share of sales.

Forecasts come from two places. Guidance is the company's own forecast of its coming results, and a consensus estimate is the average of the forecasts of analysts who follow the stock; only three analysts feed Centum's average. [1] The book-to-bill ratio is new orders divided by sales in the same period, and above 1 means the order book is growing. Dilution is the fall in each existing owner's slice when a company issues new shares, as Centum did in its March 2025 share placement. [14]

Chart and trading terms come last. A base is a sideways stretch in price that a stock builds before its next move, and the pivot is the top of that stretch. A breakout is a close above the pivot on higher trading volume. A stop loss is the price at which we say the idea is wrong and sell. A limit order is an order to buy only at a named price or lower, and the entry zone is the band of prices where we plan to buy. Asymmetry, also called reward-to-risk, is the gain from the entry to the target divided by the loss from the entry to the stop. The portfolio rule asks for at least 2.0 for a core holding. [39]

The group after June 2026: the overseas businesses are gone

Until this year Centum was a larger and weaker group. In 2016 it bought Adetel, a stressed French engineering firm, through its UK subsidiary and renamed it Centum T&S Group, and that group also held Canadian operations. [7] The units lost money in each of the five fiscal years to March 2026, mainly because fixed-price build-to-specification projects met customer change requests and left staff and parts idle. [8] The board decided in the December 2025 quarter to wind them down. [17] The French court approved the sale of the operating businesses to MBDA France and SII on 4 June 2026, Centum deconsolidated the entities that day, and a liquidator was named from 2 July 2026. [3][13] Management says it expects no further liabilities from them. [4] The filings we read do not name a company called Centum Rakon; the consolidated balance sheet shows ₹8.4 crore of investments in joint ventures and associates at March 2024 and none at March 2025 or March 2026. [2]

This matters for how you read the data. Screener's consolidated table still blends five years of the old group, with three-year sales growth of 1 percent and a net loss of ₹52 crore in FY26. [11] The stock-screen figures that seeded this study, a trailing price-to-earnings ratio of 368 and revenue down 25 percent, come from the same mixed base. [1] The 25 percent fall compares the new, smaller group with the old one: HV Investing shows ₹204 crore against ₹273 crore, while continuing consolidated revenue actually grew 14.3 percent in Q1 FY27. [27][3] CARE has already switched its analysis from consolidated to standalone, because all operating subsidiaries are in liquidation. 2. Why the business is strong or weak

A business is strong when customers cannot easily switch, when it earns more than its cost of capital, and when it turns profit into cash. Centum clears the first test in its defence and space work, has cleared the second only in the latest year, and has not yet cleared the third. The case for owning it rests on how much weight you give the first test, so this chapter sets out the evidence on each side before the numbers in chapter 3.

What protects the business

Qualification is the main barrier. A customer who buys a satellite payload or a radar subsystem needs proof that the maker has delivered before, so the supplier list is short and changes rarely. Centum holds the highest SAMAR maturity level for DRDO tenders, works to military and space-grade manufacturing standards, and holds 17 patents. [2] CARE says it has delivered mission-critical electronics on almost all ISRO satellite programmes, including the Chandrayaan and Mangalyaan missions. [7] Of its FY26 revenue, over 30 percent came from customers of more than 20 years and over 40 percent from customers of 10 to 20 years. [7] The company says 70 to 80 percent of the products it makes are single-sourced, which means the customer has no second supplier for them. [2]

Recent wins show the position is real. For the HAL helicopter radar (the UHM programme), management says more than 10 firms showed interest, three were shortlisted and Centum was the lowest bidder. [4] The programme is worth more than ₹570 crore over its life, of which Phase 1 is ₹66 crore of design work and Phase 2 could be ₹500 crore of production. [6][2] Centum is also delivering a three-satellite surveillance constellation worth about ₹350 crore and a ₹29 crore naval navigation order from GRSE. [2] In September 2026 it won a USD 3.22 million (about ₹30.5 crore) export order from a global defence OEM, to be delivered in seven months. [15]

Credit agencies read the same picture. CARE upgraded the company to BBB+ with a stable outlook on 17 September 2026 and expects revenue growth of 20 to 25 percent a year and operating margins up 50 to 100 basis points. [7] Crisil rates it BBB with a positive outlook. [8][32] Both ratings sit at the low end of investment grade, which says the company is sound but not a fortress.

What weakens the business

Most of the revenue is the lower-margin, easier-to-copy business. EMS was 72 percent of FY26 revenue, and CARE describes it as having relatively low entry barriers and many competitors, which can press prices and margins. [7] Management calls its margin a cost-plus 10 to 11 percent. [4] The BTS business is the moat, but it was only 28 percent of FY26 sales. [2] Blended margin therefore moves slowly: FY26 EBITDA margin was 12.42 percent, against 12.12 percent in FY25 and 12.44 percent in FY24, and the guidance for 13 to 15 percent has not been met. [2][5]

Customer concentration is high. The annual report says two customer groups each provided more than 10 percent of FY26 revenue, together ₹424.4 crore or 43.6 percent of ₹973.1 crore, against one group at 39.5 percent a year earlier. The top five groups gave more than 65 percent, up from 59 percent. [9][40] Names and individual shares are not published. The filings do not say whether the anchor semiconductor customer is one of them. The loss or a capex pause at one buyer would move the whole income statement. Crisil adds that a significant share of revenue comes from public-sector and defence bodies, which can change sourcing policy. [8]

The cash cycle is long. Standalone inventory rose 40 percent in FY26 to ₹457 crore while sales rose 25 percent, and inventory days were 254. [2][10] CARE says working capital needs may rise further, and that the West Asia crisis has raised input costs and delayed logistics. [7] Bank-limit use is 67 percent, down from 77 percent a year earlier, which gives room but also shows the business leans on lenders. [7]

Governance carries two flags. First, the founder-promoter sold 4.53 percent of the company, 6.65 lakh shares, on 8 August 2025 at an average ₹2,300.93 for ₹153.19 crore. [19] That sale came two days after the 6 August 2025 call at which management was still evaluating how to stop losses at the Canadian subsidiary. [18] Second, the overseas units destroyed capital: the standalone company wrote off ₹153.8 crore of investment and ₹39.6 crore of receivables from them. [9] Promoters now hold 46.81 percent with nothing pledged, which is a clean position. [12]

How it compares with listed peers

Four listed electronics-manufacturing names give a yardstick. Kaynes Technology trades at 61.0 times earnings with a return on capital employed of 12.7 percent, Syrma SGS at 81.2 times and 16.8 percent, Astra Microwave at 78.7 times and 20.3 percent, and Cyient DLM at 72.1 times and 9.9 percent. [28][29][30][31] Centum at 67.5 times and a Screener-reported 25.5 percent return sits in the middle of the valuation range and at the top on returns. [11] The whole group is priced for strong growth, and the price risk of that is visible: Kaynes has fallen 54 percent over twelve months at a 61 times multiple. [28]

On growth, Centum's standalone three-year sales growth of 25 percent trails Kaynes at 48 percent and Syrma at 33 percent but beats Astra at 13 percent and Cyient DLM at 15 percent. [10][28][29][30][31] Its edge over the pure assemblers is the BTS design business, which earns higher margins and needs more qualification. Its disadvantage is scale: with a ₹5,898 crore market value it is a fifth of Syrma. [11][29]

Our reading is that the business is moderately strong in BTS and ordinary in EMS. That is a good business to own at a fair price, and the next chapter tests whether the numbers support the quality, before chapters 4 to 7 test the price.

Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Management has two kinds of record to judge. On the standalone India business it delivers growth, explains its numbers on the call, and has hit its revenue targets. On capital allocation, particularly the overseas subsidiaries, the record is poor, because it took five years to exit units that never earned a profit. [8] The table sets what was promised against what happened.

The sharpest contrast is in August 2025. On the 6 August call management said it was evaluating strategic actions to reposition the Canadian subsidiary and that the company was not pumping money into subsidiaries. [18] Four months later it wrote off the whole ₹153.8 crore investment in the overseas group and a further ₹39.6 crore of receivables from it, and the consolidated accounts took a ₹152.5 crore loss from discontinued operations for FY26. [9][6] The loss arrived after the promoter's 8 August sale of 4.53 percent at ₹2,300.93, which we record as a fact and not as proof of intent. [19]

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every number in chapter 5 rests on a few assumptions that could be wrong, and this chapter names the weakest one under each, says what evidence would show it wrong, and says when we will see that evidence. It then gives the strongest honest case against our own conclusion, so that the decision has faced its best objection before chapter 7. The first date that matters is 4 November 2026, when the board approves the September-quarter results and the call follows on 5 November. [22]

FY27 and FY28 earnings: the margin and the tax rate

Our EPS forecast needs an EBITDA margin of 12.5 percent and then 13 percent, and a 26 percent tax charge. The weakest part is the margin. Q1 FY27 was 11.28 percent, which management blames on a thin BTS contribution, and it expects 13 percent or more for the year. [3][4] Our base case already reaches 13.0 percent in FY28. If BTS revenue arrives early and in size, the blended margin runs at 13.5 percent or more in FY27 and our FY27 number is too low. If EMS, which is cost-plus at 10 to 11 percent, grows faster than BTS, the margin stays near 12 percent and our number is too high. The Q2 FY27 margin and the tax rate in that quarter are the first test. [4]

The tax assumption is the one most likely to look wrong in the short term. The 12 percent rate in the FY26 continuing profit is unusually low, and the filings we read do not explain it, so we treat it as one-off. The Q1 FY27 rate was 26.2 percent, so our assumption has two data points behind it. [2][3] If the company's Q2 rate stays below 20 percent, we should revisit it.

The target multiple of 50 times FY28 earnings

The 50 times is a judgment and the weakest input in the target. We could not use a 3-year median price-to-earnings ratio because consolidated earnings were near zero or negative, and we set 50 times below the peer median of about 75 times trailing earnings. [11][28][29][30][31] Defence and electronics stocks move on sentiment as much as on results, and a sector rally could keep Centum at 60 times or more FY28 earnings, which would lift our base value to ₹4,400 or more. The opposite also happens: Kaynes is down 54 percent in a year with a similar multiple. [28]

The downside matters more than the upside here. Holding FY28 EPS at ₹73.8, a multiple of 45 times gives a base value of ₹3,321 and a weighted value of ₹3,453, so the return at ₹3,240 falls from 12.3 to 6.6 percent. At 40 times the base value is ₹2,952 (a loss of 8.9 percent from ₹3,240 and 26.2 percent from ₹4,000) and the weighted value is ₹3,268.5, a return of only 0.9 percent. The highest entry that still earns 12 percent falls to ₹3,083 at 45 times and ₹2,918 at 40 times, the second being below our stop. [40] So the zone holds up under a higher multiple and breaks under a lower one, which is why chapter 7 makes it provisional.

We will know in two ways. The first is the peers' multiples, which are visible every day. The second is whether the next two quarterly results, due on 4 November 2026 and in about mid-February 2027, show growth that earns a premium. The Q3 FY26 presentation was dated 15 February 2026, so a similar date is likely but not announced. [17]

Customer concentration and the semiconductor anchor customer

The weakest assumption behind both the base and bull cases is that the large customers keep ordering. Two customer groups gave 43.6 percent of FY26 revenue, the top five over 65 percent, and the semiconductor equipment business, now 17 percent of Q1 sales, depends on one anchor customer whose capex cycle Centum does not control. [9][2][5] Management says demand is visible for two to three years and that it is talking to other buyers, but it has no named second customer yet. [4] A single pause would take the 20 percent growth in our base case down toward the 10 percent of the bear case.

The annual report for FY27, due around mid-2027, will repeat the customer note, and the quarterly mix by end market will show earlier whether semiconductor equipment is still growing. [9][2]

The strongest honest bear case

Start with price. At ₹4,000 the shares cost 79.5 times normalised FY26 profit and 69 times our FY27 forecast, for a company whose EBITDA margin has sat between 10.7 and 12.4 percent for four years. [40][2] A business that has never earned the 13 to 15 percent it has promised is being valued as though it will. Management itself said the margin target was missed in FY26 because of the product mix in EMS, the faster-growing and lower-margin business, and that logic does not go away. [5]

Then orders. The BTS order book is real, but part of it is development work. For the HAL helicopter radar, prototypes are expected next year and serial production comes after HAL decides, so revenue lags orders by years. [4] In Q1 FY27 BTS revenue was ₹56.6 crore, below the FY26 quarterly average of ₹69.3 crore, while BTS orders rose 40 percent. [2][40] The company needs Q2 to Q4 revenue to grow about 28 percent to hit 25 percent for the year. Its quarters in FY26 grew between 23 and 39 percent, so the target needs the top of last year's pace on a larger base. [3][10]

Then cash and trust. Three-year operating cash was only 55 paise per rupee of profit even before the free cash flow deficit, inventory grew 40 percent, the credit rating is only BBB+, and the founder sold stock two days after a call in which management said it was assessing options for the Canadian loss-maker. [10][2][7][18][19] Management has already shown that guidance can be wrong in a costly way: the overseas group was meant to be turned round and was liquidated. [7] If the margin and cash do not improve by FY28, EPS is about ₹56 and a market multiple of 40 times gives ₹2,240.

What we may be too harsh about

The counter-case is strong enough that we assign it a one-in-four probability. Order cover is 1.81 times, BTS orders are up 40 percent, mutual funds added six schemes in a quarter, and a national push for defence indigenisation and chip-equipment supply chains supports the story. [7][4][12][2] Company-linked news is mostly positive: a ₹106 crore ECMS approval, a USD 3.22 million export order and a ₹500 crore Karnataka facility announced by the state, though Centum's own capex there is guided at ₹50 to 70 crore from FY28 because the land belongs to another group company. [16][15][20][4] If management hits 25 percent growth and a margin of 13.5 percent in FY27 and 14.0 percent in FY28, FY28 EPS is about ₹90 and the stock is worth ₹4,930. We have priced that in the bull case and are not willing to pay for it in advance.

7. What we would do, and when

The decision is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT, and the buy zone set out here is provisional. The rule is the routing rule in the scoring file. A company that passes the hard red lines and the KPI floor but sits in a pullback above a rising 200-day average is kept, not rejected, and is bought only in an entry zone. [39] The gate status is PASSED_CONDITIONAL. At ₹4,000 the reward-to-risk is -0.32 against a floor of 2.0. The expected 12-month return is -9.1 percent against a hurdle of 12 percent. The support distance is about 1.5 times the average daily swing, which is the narrowest buying band and allows only a starter-only position, meaning a small first purchase and not a full-size holding. Any one of those stops a purchase at market. [1][40]

The model entry zone is ₹3,150 to ₹3,240. It sits below the 63-day base low of ₹3,255.10 and below the 200-day average of ₹3,276.79, so a fill there would be a purchase after both structural supports have failed, not a pullback that held. [1] The 1.51 support distance in the frozen snapshot is measured at ₹4,000 from the ₹3,670.40 swing low. At ₹3,240 that swing low is already broken, and the price would be about 2.0 times the average daily swing below it. The old distance therefore says nothing about the zone, and the gate must be run again at the entry date. At the top of the zone the reward-to-risk is 2.21 and the probability-weighted return is 12.3 percent, which clears the hurdle by only 0.3 points. That cushion exists only at a 50-times multiple: at 45 times the return is 6.6 percent and at 40 times it is 0.9 percent (chapter 5). [40][1]

The stop is ₹3,036. That is the 63-day base low of ₹3,255.10 less one average daily swing of ₹218.63, the rule for a core name in a base. [39][1] The stop is 6.3 percent below the top of the zone. The base target is ₹3,690, which is 50 times FY28 EPS of ₹73.8. The bull target is ₹4,930 and the bear value is ₹2,240, with probabilities of 25 and 25 percent. A buyer at the top of the zone risks ₹204 a share to gain ₹450 in the base case. Stops here are judged on closing prices, so a gap down or a fast intraday fall can fill an exit well below ₹3,036, and the real loss can exceed the ₹204 quoted.

Because the zone clears the hurdle only under one judgmental multiple, we cut the conviction tier from TIER_3 to TIER_4. That tier carries a risk budget of 35 basis points of the portfolio. Divided by the 6.3 percent stop distance, it would allow a 5.6 percent position, but the tier cap is 2.0 percent, so the starting position is 2.0 percent of the portfolio. We would build it in two pieces, the first only after the checks below pass. [39][40] Once held, the let-winners-run rule applies. Do not sell only because the RSI passes 70. Trail the stop to the rising 21-day average or 50-day average. Let the position grow with the price, up to the 22 percent ceiling. [39]

We changed the strategy bucket from the seed's Tactical Momentum to Core Alpha. The momentum tests fail today, so a tactical case cannot be made. The quality tests pass, and the return depends on 20 percent or better profit growth for several years. This is a weak fit for the Core book. Customer concentration fails the KPI test and cash conversion is only a watch. So the position is small and the zone is demanding. The name belongs in the pipeline, not yet in the portfolio.

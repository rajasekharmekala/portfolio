1. What the company does

In plain words: Tata Consumer sells everyday food and drink under Tata brands. This chapter covers what it sells, how it is built, what the partly owned businesses are, and the terms we use.

Tata Consumer Products Limited is the Tata group's packaged food and drink company. Its brands include Tata Tea, Tetley, Tata Coffee Grand, Eight O'Clock Coffee, Tata Salt, Tata Sampann, Ching's Secret, Organic India and Himalayan water. It reaches about 290 million households in India[14]. In FY26 (the year to 31 March 2026) revenue was Rs 20,290.43 crore and profit for the year Rs 1,546.80 crore[2]. At the 23 October close of Rs 963.90 on 989,645,268 shares, the market value is about Rs 95,392 crore[19][24].

The company earns money in four places. India beverages (tea, coffee, water, ready-to-drink) and India foods (salt, Sampann pulses and spices, Soulfull, Capital Foods) each bring in about a third of sales. International (Tetley tea in the UK and Canada, Eight O'Clock coffee in the US) brings about a quarter. A non-branded arm (tea and coffee plantations, and instant coffee made to order for other brands) brings the rest and is mostly a pass-through of coffee prices[16][15]. Management calls Sampann, ready-to-drink, Soulfull, vending, Capital Foods and Organic India its 'growth businesses'; they were 31 percent of the India business in FY26 and 36 percent in the June 2026 quarter[14].

Two purchases shape the accounts. Goodwill is the price paid above the fair value of what was bought, and intangibles are brands and similar rights bought in a deal; together we call them the purchase-price base. Organic India was bought on 16 April 2024 for Rs 1,952.09 crore; the filing shows goodwill of Rs 863.75 crore and brands of Rs 1,286.00 crore from that deal[11]. Capital Foods is held 75 percent; the other 25 percent is carried at Rs 1,287.81 crore, and the company has promised to buy it, a commitment of Rs 1,330.90 crore that sits outside borrowings[5]. Capital Foods' own goodwill is not shown in the reports we read. Total goodwill is Rs 11,883.90 crore and other intangibles (mostly brands) Rs 7,111.76 crore, which is 55.1 percent of all assets[1][4]. Most of that base predates these two deals: the India unit alone carried Rs 6,086.17 crore of goodwill before FY25, and the UK, US and Canada units carry Rs 5,054 crore[4]. Chapter 3 shows returns with and without it.

Three names often grouped with the partly owned businesses need a plain correction. Tata Coffee Limited is a wholly owned subsidiary (100.00 percent), consolidated line by line, not an associate[5]. NourishCo does not appear in the list of subsidiaries, joint ventures or associates; the annual report names it only as a company that uses contract plants[5]. Tata Starbucks Private Limited is the one real joint venture (50.00 percent), a company run with a partner. An associate is a company the group owns 20 to 50 percent of and does not control. The associates are two tea plantation companies, Amalgamated Plantations (41.03 percent) and Kanan Devan Hills (28.52 percent)[5].

Size and trading come first for any order. The 23 October session traded 1,474,671 shares, 1.12 times the prior 20-day average, and the 20-day average traded value is about Rs 130.5 crore a day[24][23]. The portfolio's net worth is Rs 41,95,770.32 and its account cash is Rs 3,677.62, so any purchase needs a separate sale[27]. Tata Consumer is not a holding. This is a screen, not a fill estimate.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

In plain words: the business is real, cash-generating and well branded, but the accounts carry a big purchase-price base, a loss-making cafe venture and a price that already expects fast growth.

The case for the business is brand trust, reach and cash. Management says Tata Salt is the largest salt brand in India with a share near 39 percent, and Tata Tea the second largest tea brand[14][15]. India working capital is slightly negative, so growth does not need extra cash, and operating cash was Rs 2,421.85 crore, or 86 percent of EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization)[3][7][16]. Operating profit of the India segment rose from Rs 1,020.98 crore to Rs 1,503.74 crore in FY26, and from 9.1 to 11.8 percent of segment revenue[6].

The case against it is in four places. First, return on the book capital is low: the large purchase-price base earns only 8.4 percent on our reading and 9.24 percent on Screener's[21][1]. Second, International and Non-branded margins fell in FY26: segment profit over revenue went from 14.7 to 11.9 percent for International and from 21.3 to 11.7 percent for Non-branded[6]. Third, tea share is not clearly rising: Nielsen showed a 50 basis point fall in value share to March 2026, and management then said it would stop reporting that measure because it misses quick commerce and part of modern trade[16][17][15].

Governance is simple to read. The promoter group holds 334,813,834 shares, 33.83 percent, of which Tata Sons holds 28.68 percent. The 30 June 2026 filing answers 'No' to whether promoter shares are pledged[19]. ICRA rates the group's bank lines AAA (Stable)[7].

Segment mix and margin trend: where the profit comes from

Audited segment profit over segment revenue shows where profit comes from[6]. India Business earned 9.1 percent in FY25 and 11.8 percent in FY26 (Rs 1,020.98 crore to Rs 1,503.74 crore). International fell from 14.7 to 11.9 percent (Rs 666.61 to Rs 626.13 crore) and Non-branded from 21.3 to 11.7 percent (Rs 407.11 to Rs 280.47 crore), which management ties to coffee cost for International and to last year's elevated pricing for Non-branded[17][18]. In the June 2026 quarter management's figures were India 11.1 percent against 9.3, International 13.0 against 13.5 and Non-branded 9.9 against 12.0, with the consolidated EBITDA margin at 13.6 percent against 12.9[14]. The CFO said India margins were hit by cost inflation, higher advertising and currency losses on hedges[15]. What this means for you: India is now the profit engine, and tea cost decides how much of it reaches the bottom line.

Tea and coffee cost: what moves the margin

Tea is bought at auction and in the field, so margin follows leaf cost with a lag. North India auction prices ran at about Rs 250 a kilogram in the second quarter of FY25 and Rs 204 in the second quarter of FY26, and Rs 162 in Q4 FY26 against Rs 152 a year earlier (our reading of the presentation's chart values)[16]. India tea revenue was up 6 percent in FY26 on 3 percent more volume, with price cuts passed on as cost fell[16].

On 8 May 2026 the CEO said tea cost was 'largely benign' and about level with the year before[17]. On 24 July he said the company was 'seeing about 7% to 10% I would say inflation' with the peak season still ahead, and added that he had 'stopped trying to forecast tea completely'[15]. For coffee, Arabica was at $2.99 a pound on 8 May, and the price of the cheaper coffee bean was down 26 percent in the June quarter[17][14]. Falling coffee helps the US branded business and hurts the non-branded arm's sales, but the arm is largely a pass-through, so the net effect is positive in management's words[15]. A tea cost rise is the risk the model in Chapter 5 tests.

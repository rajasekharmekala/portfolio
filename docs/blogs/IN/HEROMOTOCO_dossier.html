1. What the company does

Hero MotoCorp is the world's largest maker of motorcycles and scooters by volume, a title the company says it has held for 25 consecutive years.[1] It sold 64.69 lakh vehicles in FY26, up 10% on FY25, and booked standalone revenue from operations of ₹46,830 crore.[10] One lakh is 100,000. The group, meaning the parent plus its subsidiaries and equity-accounted associates, reported sales of ₹53,562 crore over the twelve months to September 2026.[30] Eight plants make the vehicles: six in India and one each in Colombia and Bangladesh.[1] The annual report counts more than 14,000 customer touchpoints and 130 million customers, and the CEO said in May that 93% of Indian talukas, the sub-district administrative units, have a Hero point of sale.[13][11]

The range runs from entry commuters to premium bikes. Splendor, HF Deluxe and Passion sit at the low end, where Hero held 84.4% of the 100cc motorcycle segment in Q2 FY27 and 91% of the Deluxe 100cc category in Q3 FY26, the highest since FY18.[1][12] Entry-level bikes were around 88% of domestic motorcycle volume in the nine months to December 2025, and that demand is rural and sensitive to price.[18] Scooters and the VIDA electric range are the growth lines. In Q2 FY27 the scooter portfolio rose 47% to 2.40 lakh units and made up 13% of the volume mix, against 10% a year earlier.[1][2]

Revenue comes from three places. Vehicles are the bulk. Parts, accessories and merchandise brought in ₹1,941 crore in Q2 FY27, up 27%, and about ₹6,200 crore in FY26.[1][11] Exports, which Hero calls its global business, reached 4.02 lakh units and about ₹3,500 crore in FY26, and the company now sells in 54 markets.[11][1] The third source is the equity stake Hero holds in other companies. It owned 29.88% of the listed electric scooter maker Ather Energy on 25 August 2026 and agreed that month to buy more shares for up to ₹1,758 crore, which would take the stake to up to about 32.8% (completion is not confirmed in the sources we hold). It also holds close to 37% of Euler Motors and a stake in the lender Hero FinCorp.[21][11][18]

The money engine is simple. Hero buys steel, aluminium, rubber, plastics and electronics, and raw materials were 78% of its expenses in Q2 FY27.[4] It collects from dealers in about 18 days and pays suppliers after about 88 days, so the cash conversion cycle (days to turn spending back into cash) is minus 40 days: suppliers finance the business.[30] That structure explains why a company with a 14% operating margin earns a 35.2% return on capital and can pay out about 70% of profit as dividends while holding almost no debt.[30][13]

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The first strength is the entry-level franchise. Hero held 84.4% of the 100cc motorcycle segment in Q2 FY27, and Splendor Plus was India's best-selling two-wheeler in September 2026 with 248,592 registrations.[1][16] A buyer can find a Hero dealer and a Hero service point almost anywhere in the country, which is a moat built over more than four decades and hard to copy in a price-led segment.[11][13] CRISIL, which rates the company, calls its position in the domestic two-wheeler market leading and its brand and dealer network strong.[18]

The second strength is the balance sheet. CRISIL reaffirmed AAA/Stable in February 2026 and ICRA shows AAA (Stable) today, with negligible debt and a cash reserve of ₹13,286 crore at December 2025.[18][19] Standalone interest cover was 316.91 times in FY26 and the debt-to-equity ratio was 0.01.[13] Standalone cash flow from operations was ₹9,395 crore in FY26, up 80%, helped by lower working capital.[11] Return on capital employed (ROCE) has risen from 18% in FY22 to 35% in FY26 as operating leverage and price increases lifted profit faster than capital grew.[30]

The weakness is relative performance. Hero's share of retail two-wheeler sales was 24.26% in September 2026 against 24.57% a year earlier, while Honda held 26.19% and TVS Motor rose from 19.03% to 20.37%.[15] CRISIL put Hero's share at about 24% for the nine months to December 2025, down from about 25%.[18] In Q2 FY27 Hero's vehicle volume grew 10% while the industry's two-wheeler wholesale volume grew 15.2%.[1][17] Management argues that share is rising in each growth segment and that the overall figure will turn once the mix catches up.[11] The data so far do not show the turn.

The second weakness is cost. Raw materials rose 26% year on year in Q2 FY27 to ₹100.62 billion, and the standalone EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) margin fell to 13.4% from 15.0% a year earlier.[4][3] In May the CEO said the 2% price increase of April covered only part of a bill-of-materials increase in the high single digits.[11] Peers did better: TVS Motor lifted its margin to 13.0% from 12.7%, and Bajaj Auto lifted its margin by 40 basis points to 20.9% on revenue of ₹20,003 crore and profit of ₹3,354 crore.[6][26][24][25] Hero was the only one of the three whose margin fell on the year.[6]

How good was the latest quarter?

The headline says consolidated profit after tax rose 41% to ₹1,861 crore.[1] The same release says the quarter includes a ₹397 crore gain from the equity consolidation of an associate, which Informist identifies as Ather Energy after its share issue.[1][4] Strip the gain out and consolidated profit is about ₹1,464 crore, roughly level with the ₹1,456 crore standalone figure, which grew 5%.[1] That is our own arithmetic, and it assumes the gain carries no tax. Informist called it the lowest profit growth in five quarters, and standalone profit missed the analysts' average of ₹1,527 crore by about ₹71 crore.[5][4]

Consolidated other income is also large: ₹619 crore in the September quarter against ₹240 crore a year earlier, and ₹939 crore in the June 2025 quarter.[30] Those lines are real cash or real accounting gains, but they do not come from selling bikes. For underwriting we therefore track standalone profit as the core earnings line and treat the associate gains as a separate, lumpy item.

What the Ather stake does to the picture

Hero owned 29.88% of Ather on 25 August 2026 and agreed to buy more shares from an existing holder for up to ₹1,758 crore, which would take its fully diluted stake to up to about 32.8%.[21] The source says the deal was expected to complete by 3 September 2026, but we hold no confirmation, so we show both cases. Ather's market value was ₹66,054 crore when the Screener page was fetched, with the share price at ₹1,663 against a 52-week range of ₹582 to ₹1,744.[22] Multiply and Hero's stake is worth about ₹19,700 crore at 29.88% (about ₹990 per Hero share) or about ₹21,700 crore at 32.8% (about ₹1,080 per share). That is 19% to 21% of Hero's own market value. It is our arithmetic, and it moves with Ather's share price.[22]

Ather reported its first quarterly profit in Q2 FY27, a net profit of ₹1.26 crore against a loss of ₹154.07 crore a year earlier.[23] The stake is therefore an asset the market can see and price, but it is also a source of noise in Hero's reported profit. 3. The numbers, in plain words

Hero's consolidated sales grew from ₹29,551 crore in FY22 to ₹47,411 crore in FY26 and reached ₹53,562 crore over the twelve months to September 2026.[30] The three-year sales growth rate is 12% a year, the five-year rate is 9% and the ten-year rate is 5%, so recent growth is well above the long-run record.[30] The jump came after the GST rate cuts of 2025 lifted affordability, and Q2 FY27 sales of ₹14,970 crore were 22.5% above the year before.[30][9]

Operating margin rose from 11% in FY22 to 15% in FY26 and sits at 14% on a twelve-month view.[30] Net profit rose from ₹2,329 crore to ₹5,776 crore over the same span, and earnings per share (EPS) rose from ₹115.95 to ₹286.96. FY26 is flattered by a one-off. The company said Q1 FY26 profit of ₹1,706 crore included a gain of about ₹700 crore before tax and that the normalised figure was about ₹1,100 crore.[30][9] Take the one-off out and three-year profit growth is about 23% a year rather than the 28% that Screener prints.[30]

Cash flow is the best part of the record. Consolidated cash from operations was ₹8,315 crore in FY26 against ₹4,297 crore in FY25, and free cash flow (FCF), the cash left after capital spending, was ₹7,227 crore.[30] Part of that is a working-capital release (the cash conversion cycle went from minus 20 to minus 40 days), which will not repeat.[30] Over three years, cash from operations of ₹17,535 crore is 1.26 times profit of ₹13,894 crore, comfortably above the 0.70 floor.[30]

The balance sheet carries ₹21,004 crore of investments against ₹1,419 crore of borrowings at September 2026.[30] Borrowings rose from ₹779 crore in March, which we flag as a trajectory item, but at 0.06 times equity it is not a solvency question.[30] The company paid ₹185 per share for FY26 and the shares yield 3.62% at today's price.[29][30] At ₹5,105 the shares trade on a price-to-earnings ratio of 16.8, which is the share price divided by earnings per share.[30]

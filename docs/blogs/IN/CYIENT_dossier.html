Before you read

Cyient is an Indian engineering services group. It designs and supports aircraft parts, railways, telecom networks and utilities for large overseas clients, and it also owns an electronics maker and a chip designer[2][28]. We rate it WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT: the business is improving, with record order intake and its best margin in ten quarters, but the share price of ₹1,182.30 already pays for much of that recovery, and our central value of ₹1,172 leaves no reward at today's price[3][31]. We would buy only on a pullback to ₹1,044 to ₹1,060, where each rupee at risk would offer at least ₹2.50 of reward. The case is wrong if organic growth stays near zero, if the chip unit's break-even slips again, or if the stock closes below ₹1,016[4][30].

1. What the company does

Cyient was founded in Hyderabad in 1991 as Infotech Enterprises.[2] Its main job is engineering services: it supplies engineers who design, test and maintain products and networks that other companies sell. The customers are aircraft makers, railway equipment makers, telecom operators, utilities and energy firms. The company says it serves more than 300 customers in over 30 countries.[20] In the year to March 2026 the group earned about $820.8 million. Of that, DET earned $657.6 million and the chip unit $25.7 million.[6] DLM and smaller lines make up the rest, about $137 million after any eliminations. One secondary source puts DLM alone at $142 million, which does not fit, so we have not reconciled it. A Choice research note shows $65 million for the chip unit, which conflicts with the company's own $25.7 million, and we use the company figure.[28][11]

The engineering services unit is called DET, short for Digital, Engineering and Technology. It is the part of the business that analysts judge first, and management's own calls focus on it.[4] DET revenue in the September 2026 quarter was $174.2 million, or ₹1,664 crore.[3] By service line, Transportation & Mobility (aerospace, rail and automotive work) was 43.7% of DET revenue, Network & Infrastructure (telecom and utilities) was 30.4%, and the Strategic Units (energy, health and mining) were 20.3%.[8] By region, the Americas gave 53.9%, Asia Pacific 23.5% and Europe 22.7%, and Europe fell 15.3% from a year earlier.[8]

DET sells engineers' time and finished projects. A client signs a contract, Cyient staffs it, and revenue arrives as the work is done. About 55% to 60% of contracts renew every quarter, and project-based work is 25% to 30% of revenue by one brokerage estimate and about 40% by another report of management's remarks.[12][10] This is why growth is uneven: a few clients that delay a project can move a quarter. In April 2026 management said three clients had delayed program starts, and that this explained the revenue dip.[6] Management wants a 70:30 mix of long-term, sticky revenue and project revenue, and says it is 20 to 30 percentage points short today.[4]

The second unit is Cyient DLM, a listed subsidiary that builds electronics for aerospace, defence, medical and industrial clients. Its revenue was ₹393.1 crore in the September quarter, and its operating profit before depreciation, known as EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization), was 8.7% of revenue. Its order book hit a record ₹2,813.7 crore, about 1.8 times its annualised sales. DLM's own plan is to lift margins from about 9% to 11% today toward 13% to 18% after FY30.[13][31][14] Cyient owns about 52% of it, so the parent reports all of DLM's sales but keeps only about half of its profit.[11][29] The third unit is Cyient Semiconductors. It designs custom chips for clients (called ASICs) and is building its own power chips (called ASSPs) that it can sell to many buyers.[4] It sold $19.3 million in the September quarter and still makes an operating loss, because it spends heavily on product development.[21][4]

Here is how the money works. In a services firm, people are the main cost. So profit depends on the price Cyient gets for an engineer-hour compared with the salary it pays, and on how fully its engineers are billed. A weaker rupee has been a tailwind: DET revenue grew 15.7% in rupees in September but only 6.3% in constant currency.[3][4] Management hedges about half of its net dollar exposure for the next 12 months, at an average of ₹95.6 per dollar on about $70 million.[4] Wage rises arrive in two steps in the December and March quarters, and management expects cost savings to offset them.[4] The group had about 19,500 people in September 2026, including about 3,000 who joined with the TAO Digital purchase.[4]

Customers, concentration and the business cycle

Aerospace carries most of the growth. Management said that the bulk of Transportation & Mobility growth comes from aerospace aftermarket and lifecycle work, helped by rising flight hours, and that higher oil prices could hurt commercial aviation.[4] That makes the group more exposed to one industry than the three-service-line split suggests. The other large exposure is telecom and fibre, which was flat in the quarter, and energy, which is in decline.[4][8]

Client concentration is moderate but moving. The top five and top 10 clients both declined in the September quarter, while clients outside the top 10 grew strongly. Management called this a one-quarter aberration.[8][4] The number of clients also fell in every size band, which management said was partly a planned pruning of a long tail of small accounts.[4] Fewer, larger accounts raise concentration risk even as they raise stickiness.

The business has been cyclical in the last four years. Group sales rose from ₹4,534 crore in FY22 to ₹7,147 crore in FY24, then barely moved to ₹7,268 crore in FY26, while net profit fell from ₹703 crore in FY24 to ₹463 crore in FY26.[2] Management describes FY26 as a year of stabilisation and expects FY27 and FY28 to mark a recovery.[10]

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • DET, DLM: Cyient's engineering services unit and its listed electronics-manufacturing unit (Design-Led Manufacturing).
  • Organic constant-currency growth: growth excluding purchased companies and excluding currency swings. It is the cleanest read of underlying demand.
  • EBIT margin: operating profit after depreciation, as a share of revenue.
  • Order intake and book-to-bill: the value of new contracts won in a period, and the ratio of that value to revenue billed. Above 1 means the order book is growing.
  • ASIC and ASSP: a custom chip built for one client, and a standard chip sold to many buyers.
  • Sum of the parts: valuing each unit of a group separately and adding the results.
  • Support: a price level, such as a moving average or a recent low, where buyers have stepped in before.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

A good engineering services business wins on three things: deep knowledge of its clients' products, a cost structure that is cheaper than the client's own, and long relationships that turn into repeat work. Cyient has the first and the third. It has worked in aerospace, rail and telecom for more than three decades, and it now says it supports lifecycle work, such as maintenance, in 58% of its accounts.[4] Its latest numbers back this up in one area: Transportation & Mobility has grown for six quarters in a row, by 16.5% over a year in constant currency in September.[4][3]

The company is also trying to change what it sells. Management says that traditional engineering outsourcing is a market of $80 billion to $100 billion, and that selling services across a product's whole life, including aftermarket, opens a market about 20 times larger.[5] That claim comes from the company and is not independently measured. What can be checked is the order flow: DET order intake was $158.4 million in the September quarter, up about 21% from a year earlier and the highest for a second quarter, and management says new business was in the mid-30s per cent of it.[4][3]

The weak points are growth, returns and focus. Organic DET revenue grew just 0.4% over a year in constant currency in September, after a 0.7% fall in FY26.[4][6] The operating margin is operating profit as a share of sales. Over the last twelve months it was 12.7%, below the 18% earned in FY24, and the return on capital employed is 12.3%, against 26.7%, 26.3%, 20.9% and 60.0% at four listed peers.[2][23][25][26][31] The leadership has changed: a new chief financial officer took over in April 2026 and a new chief growth officer joined in February. A large deal called Project Astro was paused after the company spent ₹71 crore on due diligence.[6]

Competition comes from two sides. Larger listed engineering and IT firms, such as the four in the peer table in Chapter 3, bid for the same work. At the same time, clients are testing how much of the work artificial intelligence can do. Management itself cited the speed of AI change as one reason to pause Project Astro, the other being geopolitical uncertainty. It also argues that domain knowledge keeps its work hard to replace.[6][7] We cannot test that claim from filings. It is the main long-run uncertainty, and it is the reason we do not give the business credit for a high multiple today.

Where the business is strong

Four facts support the case. DET's operating margin reached 14.2% in September, up 107 basis points over the June quarter and 206 over the year before, its highest in ten quarters.[4] DET free cash flow, which is the cash left after running the business and buying assets, was ₹214 crore, or 139% of its profit. A three-day cut in the days clients take to pay helped.[3][4] The promoters pledge none of their shares.[16] And CRISIL reaffirmed its bank facilities at AA with a stable outlook, the second-highest long-term grade, in September 2025. Screener lists a later CRISIL update dated 9 June, which we could not open.[22][2]

Where the business is weak

Reported growth is not organic growth. DET's reported revenue rose 6.3% in constant currency, but the TAO Digital purchase supplied one month of that, worth $9.6 million. Without it, growth was 1.6% over the June quarter and 0.4% over the year.[4] The chip unit lost money, and its break-even date has moved from FY27 to FY28 in nine months.[7][4] Net cash fell from a surplus of ₹1,644 crore in June to a deficit of ₹114 crore in September, after the buyback and the TAO purchase. A buyback is a purchase by a company of its own shares.[18]

How the three units fit together

The parent is a holding of three different businesses, and the market price blends them. Cyient owns about 52% of DLM, whose market value is ₹6,092 crore, so its stake is worth about ₹3,175 crore, or 26% of Cyient's value of about ₹12,380 crore (104.7 million shares at the ₹1,182.30 close).[29][11][31][1] Subtract that, and about ₹9,204 crore is left for DET and the chip unit. That is about 12.7 times our FY28 estimate of DET profit (₹723 crore), close to the 10 to 12 times that Choice and Motilal Oswal use for DET alone. DLM's ₹6,092 crore is Screener's 28 October figure (share price ₹767), while Cyient's value uses the 27 October close. If both are taken at 28 October prices, with Cyient at about ₹1,157, the multiple is 12.4 times.[31][11][8] So the market is not paying a large premium or discount for the engineering core. It is paying for a recovery that has begun and is not complete.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate has a weakest link. This chapter names it for each number in Chapter 5, says what evidence would show it wrong, and says when we will know. It then prices each warning sign with one value, the FY28 earnings per share that results, times 18.6. Last, it sets out the strongest honest case against our own conclusion, and names the one claim on which the whole view hangs.

One rule runs through the chapter. The entry zone survives only if the central value stays at or above about ₹1,114, which is FY28 earnings per share of about ₹59.9 at 18.6 times. Below that, the highest price that still gives 2.5 to 1 against the ₹1,016 stop falls under ₹1,044, the swing low, and no price meets both the reward floor and the stop.[31][30]

FY27 earnings per share of ₹47: the weakest assumption is that the margin holds through the wage rises

We assume a 14% DET margin in the second half. Management says cost savings will cover the wage rises, which come in two equal steps in the December and March quarters.[4] If the margin is 13.5% for the second half, FY27 earnings per share is about ₹45.8, because each half-point of second-half margin is about ₹1.3. The evidence arrives with Q3 FY27 results, expected in late January 2027 (last year's call was on 22 January), though the date is not confirmed.[7][31]

FY28 earnings per share of ₹63: the weakest assumption is that three things go right together

The ₹63 needs about 5% organic growth, a 14.5% margin and a smaller chip loss together. The 5% is arithmetic: FY27 organic DET revenue is about $661 million, 5% growth makes about $694 million, and TAO's $105 million brings the total to the $800 million we assume.[4][31] The chip unit has said it will lose money while it spends on high-voltage chips. It also says foundry supply is tight and wafer prices are rising, which management expects to weigh on margin in the immediate term.[4] Evidence will come from the first customer samples, due at the end of 2026 or early 2027, and from the chip unit's quarterly loss in the March 2027 quarter.[4]

The central value of ₹1,172: the weakest assumption is the 18.6 times multiple

The multiple is a judgement: 60% of the peer median trailing ratio. Peers have fallen hard (KPIT shares are down 54% over a year and Tata Elxsi shares 33%), so peer multiples may fall further.[25][24] Suppose the peer median falls to 24 times with no cut to earnings. Our multiple becomes 14.4, the central value ₹907, and the reward-to-risk at ₹1,060 is minus 3.47 to 1. Keeping the bull and bear values and probabilities unchanged, the probability-weighted value falls to ₹1,044, which is 1.5% below ₹1,060 and 11.7% below today's price.[31] The evidence is the peers' own results over the next two quarters.

The Reverse DCF comparison: the weakest assumption is the starting free cash flow figure

The two bases give 6.9% and 14.4% a year. The half-year cash flow statement published with the September results would show which is closer to the truth. We did not have it in our sources. The group's own cash flow statement may include acquisition costs. If trailing cash flow is the right base, the price already assumes a recovery larger than any in the last five years.[2][1]

The ₹70 upgrade case is an assumption, and it is not needed for the decision

Chapter 5 shows the full bridge from ₹70 of earnings per share to a ₹1,302 target to a ₹1,098 entry ceiling. It needs about 8% organic growth (not 5%) and a 15% margin (not 14.5%), with a smaller chip loss. It is not a forecast. Organic growth alone cannot set group earnings, because margin, interest, tax and the chip loss all matter, and each $20 million of FY28 revenue is worth only about ₹1.9 per share. We therefore do not raise the entry zone on growth figures. We raise it only after the valuation is rerun on reported numbers.[31]

7. What we would do, and when

The decision is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT, which means do not buy at the market today. The rule behind it is the reward test. A turnaround or tactical idea needs at least 2.5 rupees of reward for each rupee at risk. At ₹1,182.30 the ratio is minus 0.06 to 1, because the central value of ₹1,172 is below the price. All hard lines pass and the Layer 2 score is 4.0 out of 5. So the stock stays on the shortlist.[30][31]

The entry zone is ₹1,044 to ₹1,060, to be bought with a limit order, which is an order that fills only at or below a price you set. ₹1,044 is the 20-day swing low, the lowest price of the last 20 sessions, and ₹1,060 is where the reward test is passed: (₹1,172 minus ₹1,060) over (₹1,060 minus ₹1,016) is 2.55 to 1.[1][31] The zone is 10.3% to 11.7% below today's price, so the order may not fill. We accept that, because paying more removes the reward.

A price inside the zone is necessary but not enough. Before the verdict changes to ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP we rerun the valuation with the latest reported numbers: FY28 earnings per share, the peer median multiple, the central value and the reward-to-risk at the planned fill price. The result must still be at least 2.5 to 1. The stress test shows why. At a 24-times peer median, the central value is ₹907 and the ratio at ₹1,060 is minus 3.47 to 1. The probability-weighted value is ₹1,044, a return of minus 1.5% from ₹1,060. A falling price alone would not qualify.[31]

The stop is ₹1,016. It is the lower of the 20-day swing low (₹1,044) and the 50-day average (₹1,082.12), less half an average true range of ₹56.09. In the current capitulation-watch regime, a first purchase should be at most half of that, about 1.75%.[30][1]

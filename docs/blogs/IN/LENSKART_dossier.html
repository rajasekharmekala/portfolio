From Dorm-Room Classifieds to Automated Eyewear Factories

Every durable consumer franchise begins when a founder treats the first business plan as tuition. Peyush Bansal, Amit Chaudhary, Sumeet Kapahi, and Neha Bansal incorporated Valyoo Technologies in 2008 [1][15]. Bansal returned from Microsoft to launch SearchMyCampus.com, a student portal, before pivoting to niche e-commerce. In late 2010, the team launched Lenskart online with contact lenses to avoid frame-fitting friction [15]. Between 2011 and 2012, they also added WatchKart, BagKart, and JewelsKart [15].

By 2014, running four unrelated portals broke down on unit economics. Watches, bags, and jewelry faced brutal marketplace competition and high acquisition costs. Eyewear offered a rare anomaly: gross margins above 65% without middlemen, steady repeat demand, and a fragmented local optician base [3][10]. Management shut down WatchKart, BagKart, and JewelsKart to focus capital on Lenskart. They then made a bold bet for an internet startup: opening physical stores and building in-house manufacturing [3][15].

Lenskart's 18-year arc is defined by three deliberate pivots: pruning non-core e-commerce in 2014, backwards-integrating into robotics and physical stores in 2016–2021, and using cross-border M&A (Owndays, Meller) plus public equity capital (2025 IPO) to build a pan-Asian and European eyewear platform.

The second inflection arrived between 2016 and 2021. Buying finished frames and lenses from third parties capped both quality and delivery speed. Traditional Indian optical orders passed through importers, distributors, local opticians, and external labs. That chain took five to seven days and piled up 3x to 5x markups. Lenskart bypassed it by building an automated lens plant in Bhiwadi, Rajasthan, using Addverb robotics for surfacing, coating, and assembly [8]. This spine enabled the 'Home Eye Test' program and rapid store rollouts across Tier-1, Tier-2, and Tier-3 cities.

From FY2020 to FY2022, Lenskart operated in venture-backed expansion mode. Revenue rose from ₹900 crore in FY20 to ₹1,503 crore in FY22 [1]. Operating profit stayed negative (-₹32 crore in FY20, -₹47 crore in FY21, and -₹115 crore in FY22) as equity funded store rollouts [1]. Equity reserves swelled from ₹2,333 crore in March 2020 to ₹5,441 crore by March 2023. SoftBank Vision Fund, Kedaara Capital, Alpha Wave Global, Temasek, and ADIA led those private rounds [1][11].

In mid-2022, Lenskart acquired a controlling stake in Japanese eyewear chain Owndays for roughly $400 million in cash and stock [6]. Overnight, Lenskart gained a retail footprint across 13 Asian markets, including Japan, Singapore, Thailand, and Taiwan. Consolidated revenue jumped 152% in FY23 to ₹3,788 crore and 43% in FY24 to ₹5,428 crore [1][6]. The deal also reshaped the balance sheet. Fixed assets leaped from ₹649 crore in FY22 to ₹5,067 crore in FY23, while lease borrowings rose from ₹34 crore to ₹934 crore and ₹2,343 crore in FY24 [1].

The latest chapter unfolded in 2025 and 2026. In July 2025, Lenskart bought an 80% stake in Barcelona-based D2C brand Meller for roughly ₹407 crore [9]. In November 2025, Lenskart listed on the NSE and BSE via a ₹7,278.02 crore IPO (₹2,150 crore fresh issue; ₹5,128.02 crore OFS) [4]. FY26 revenue grew 32% to ₹8,814 crore, with ₹501 crore net profit. TTM revenue through September 2026 reached ₹10,383 crore (+41%), with ₹797 crore PAT [1].

Concrete Real-World Example 1: Prescription Eyeglasses (Tier-2 India Omnichannel Order)

A software engineer in Indore notices eye strain and downloads the Lenskart app. She books a ₹99 'Home Eye Test' or visits a nearby Lenskart store for a 12-step robotic eye exam. She picks two Vincent Chase frames using Lenskart Gold (Buy-One-Get-One) for roughly ₹3,500–₹4,500.

The store tablet sends her prescription straight to Bhiwadi's automated lens lab. Robotics grind, coat, and edge the lenses, shipping finished glasses within 24 to 72 hours [8]. Her prescription stays in the cloud, turning an 18-to-24-month replacement into a frictionless reorder.

Concrete Real-World Example 2: Sunglasses & Fashion Adjacency (Meller & John Jacobs)

A college student in Bengaluru sees an Instagram ad for Meller—the Barcelona sunglasses brand Lenskart acquired in July 2025 [9]. She buys a ₹2,800 polarized pair online or at a mall kiosk. Private-label sourcing yields ~65%–70% gross margins while expanding Lenskart's fashion reach.

Concrete Real-World Example 3: Contact Lenses & Recurring Replenishment (Aqualens)

A Mumbai consultant wears monthly disposable lenses for the gym and weekends. He subscribes to Lenskart's private-label Aqualens packs at ₹1,200–₹1,800 per box instead of costlier imported brands. Monthly and quarterly reorders keep customers active on the Lenskart app year-round.

Concrete Real-World Example 4: International Operations (Owndays Japan & SE Asia)

In Tokyo or Singapore, a commuter walks into an Owndays store inside a rail mall [6]. Owndays offers a flat all-inclusive price (frame plus standard index lenses) with 20-minute on-site lens cutting. Lenskart is migrating Owndays' backend sourcing to India to lift international margins toward India levels.

Across all four segments, Gold membership drives 35%–37% repeat orders at minimal acquisition cost [10]. Over 80% of revenue comes from recurring prescription eyewear, insulating the business from pure fashion cycles.

Why does this product mix matter for revenue durability? Prescription eyewear (>80% of sales) behaves like a non-discretionary medical staple rather than pure fashion [2]. Customers replace glasses every 18 to 24 months as eye power shifts or frames wear out. Sunglasses (10%–15%) add higher-margin fashion upside through Vincent Chase, John Jacobs, and Meller [2][9]. Aqualens contact lenses (5%–10%) turn an infrequent purchase into monthly or quarterly app reorders. Geographically, India (~59% of sales) delivers higher store margins, while Owndays (~41% of sales) provides scale across 13 Asian markets [6][12].

Store economics also benefit from a wide price ladder across segments [2][11]. Entry-level Vincent Chase prescription glasses start near ₹1,500–₹2,500, drawing first-time buyers out of unorganized shops. Mid-tier John Jacobs and Lenskart Air frames lift average selling prices to ₹3,500–₹5,500 in metros. Meanwhile, Owndays and Meller capture higher-ticket international and fashion demand [6][9]. That multi-brand ladder lets Lenskart serve both Tier-3 towns and Tokyo commuter malls without diluting its core brand.

Eyewear has long operated as a barbell. European luxury giants like EssilorLuxottica charge 4x–6x markups at the top, while unorganized local opticians control the bottom with uneven quality [3][11]. Lenskart Solutions Ltd. attacked the underserved middle—offering standardized eye exams, stylish private-label frames, and coated lenses at transparent prices [1][10].

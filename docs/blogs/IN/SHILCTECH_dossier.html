Terms used in this report

This chapter explains each technical and financial word once, with the plain meaning first. Later uses assume the reader has read it here.

RSI stands for relative strength index. It is a 14-day speed reading from 0 to 100 that rises when recent gains outweigh recent losses. Readings above 70 mean the stock has run hot, and readings below 30 mean it has been sold hard.

ATR stands for average true range, the typical daily price swing over 14 sessions. We use it as a ruler. A stop or a support distance is counted in units of ATR so that a volatile stock and a calm stock are measured on the same scale.

ROCE stands for return on capital employed. It is the profit a company earns before tax and financing costs, divided by the money tied up in the business. A business that earns above 15 percent on its capital can grow without constantly needing new money.

50-day moving average is the average closing price over the last 50 sessions. It is the most-watched medium-term trend line. A falling line means sellers have had the upper hand for about two months.

200-day moving average is the average closing price over the last 200 sessions, roughly one year. A price below a falling 200-day line is the basic sign of a damaged long-term trend.

Stage 2 is the markup phase in the four-phase cycle of a stock. The price sits above a rising 50-day and 200-day average. Stage 1 is the basing phase that comes before a markup. Stage 4 is the markdown phase, when the price sits below a falling long-term average and sellers are in control.

base is a sideways range that a stock builds after a fall. The pivot is the price just above the top of the base, where a move out of the range would begin. A breakout is a close above the pivot, ideally on heavy volume.

stop loss is a price fixed in advance at which a position is exited to limit a loss. Under the Core rule the stop sits below the nearest swing low or moving average, and then one ATR lower, so ordinary noise does not trigger it.

asymmetry is the planned gain divided by the planned loss. A ratio of 3.0 means the target is three times as far above the price as the stop is below it. The Core floor in this framework is 3.0.

price-to-earnings ratio, or P/E, is the share price divided by the profit per share over the last year. A ratio of 20 means investors pay twenty rupees for each rupee of annual profit.

debt-to-equity is total borrowings divided by shareholders' equity. A value near zero means the business is almost free of borrowed money.

operating cash flow is the cash the business generates from its operations. We compare three years of it with reported profit to check whether earnings turn into cash.

EBITDA stands for earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. It is a profit measure taken before financing costs and accounting charges, so it shows the operating result more directly.

EPS stands for earnings per share. It is the profit attributable to shareholders divided by the number of shares, and it is the base for any earnings-driven target.

operating margin is the share of revenue left after the day-to-day costs of making and selling the product. A falling operating margin means each rupee of sales keeps less profit.

guidance is management's own forecast of revenue or margin for a coming period. Guidance that is cut or held under pressure tells a reader how management sees the next few quarters.

retest is a return of the price to a level it has recently broken through. A retest that holds is a sign that the level has turned into support.

measured move is an estimate of the next target, equal to the height of a base added to the breakout level. It is one of the two accepted ways to anchor a target.

momentum gate is a code test in this framework. Test A asks whether the stock is in a healthy uptrend. Test B asks whether a stock is entering one from a base. A candidate that fails both tests gets no allocation, whatever its business quality.

What the company does and how it earns

Shilchar Technologies makes transformers. Its profile on Screener describes the business as manufacturing electronics and telecom transformers, and power and distribution transformers, and says it has recently started making ferrite transformers [3]. Its range includes power transformers up to 66 KV and distribution, inverter-duty and generator transformers up to 33 KV, serving solar, wind, hydro, oil and gas, cement, steel and sugar customers [2].

The company's focus is transformers up to 50 MVA (megavolt-amperes, the unit in which transformer capacity is rated) in the 132 KV class, and it is extending into the 220 KV class [2]. According to the Tijori summary of the management call, the company is adding 6,500 MVA of capacity to its 7,500 MVA base for higher-voltage export orders, with the flagship Vadodara expansion due in April 2027 [8].

Why this matters to a reader: transformers are heavy, custom-built equipment sold to utilities, industrial plants and renewable-energy projects. Revenue depends on order flow and on the timing of dispatches. Margins depend on how the company prices its orders against the cost of making them, and the latest quarter shows how far margins can fall when that balance moves against it [6].

The business metrics that matter for this framework are its return on capital, its borrowing and the conversion of profit into cash. Screener shows a return on capital employed of 50.7 percent and a return on equity of 37.8 percent for the latest reported year [2]. The company reports no borrowings at the March 2026 year end [2] [9]. It also reports that over 80 percent of its business comes from repeat customers [8].

The stock trades at a P/E of 32.3 on the Screener header [2]. Its trailing earnings per share are ₹120.22 [10] [4]. A P/E in the thirties means investors are paying for growth that the recent quarter has not yet shown. That is one reason the red team below treats valuation as a live risk even though the business itself is sound.

Ownership is concentrated. Promoters held 62.12 percent in the September 2026 quarter [2] [11] [12]. The promoter share has fallen by 3.73 percentage points over three years [2], and the promoter holding shows no pledge of shares as of September 2026 [11].

How the framework works for this company

The framework is a set of hurdles that a company must pass in a fixed order. Layer 1 is the pass-or-fail stage. It asks whether the business earns enough on its capital, whether its borrowing is modest, whether its profits turn into cash, whether the price is in a healthy trend and whether the price offers a reward that justifies the risk. If any Layer 1 test fails, the candidate is rejected and the later layers are not run.

The order matters. A company can have excellent business numbers and still be the wrong buy at the wrong price. The framework therefore checks the trend before it scores operating detail. A stock in a markdown can stay there for months while its business quality does not change.

This run uses the Core Alpha rules, because the prior row sat in that bucket. Asymmetry and target anchoring

The asymmetry test compares the upside to a target with the downside to a stop. The target must be anchored to something verifiable. The two accepted anchors are forward earnings multiplied by a three-year median P/E, or a technical measured move. An arbitrary target is not allowed.

The stop comes first, because it is set by rule. Under the Core rule the stop sits below the lower of the 20-day swing low and the 50- and 200-day averages, then one ATR lower. The 20-day swing low is ₹3,837.10 and the ATR is ₹121.31 (ledger). The rule gives a stop of ₹3,837.10 minus ₹121.31, or ₹3,715.79. Fatal-flaw red team

The red team asks what would make the verdict wrong, or what would make a good business a bad buy. Three fatal-flaw scenarios are set out below.

Scenario 1: the margin does not recover. Q1 operating margin was 16.4 percent, against about 29 percent in the historical record [6], and Screener shows an FY2026 operating margin of 29 percent [2]. Profit fell 49.7 percent on revenue down 15.2 percent [6]. If the next quarter shows the same squeeze, the earnings base for any target falls, and the forward multiple becomes harder to defend.

Scenario 2: the growth guidance is out of reach. The FY2027 guidance of about ₹800 crore needs far more revenue than the first-quarter run rate of about ₹540 crore [6]. The company holds an order book of about ₹500 crore [6], and the record does not break that book down by margin. Capacity use of 60 to 65 percent [6] means the plant has room to add volume, but that only helps if orders convert at acceptable margins. If the guidance is cut, the price could fall further from its current level.

Scenario 3: the trend keeps falling. The 50-day average has fallen for about four weeks (21 sessions), and relative strength against the Nifty 50 is negative over 63 sessions (ledger). The stock is 27.82 percent below its 52-week high [13]. If the price closes below the 52-week low of ₹2,852.50 (ledger), the markdown is confirmed and the stop framework no longer applies.

The bull case is strong too.

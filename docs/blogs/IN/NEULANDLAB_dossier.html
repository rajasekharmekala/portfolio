1. What the company does

Neuland Laboratories Limited, listed as NEULANDLAB on the NSE and 524558 on the BSE, was established in 1984 and is based in Hyderabad.[4][1] It makes active pharmaceutical ingredients, or APIs, the chemicals that do the medical work in a tablet or an injection. It also makes them to order for drug developers, and that second job is now the larger one.[1] The group has two lines of business. Generic drug substances are sold to makers of generic medicines. Custom manufacturing solutions, called CMS, are developed and supplied exclusively for one innovator's new drug.[1]

Three plants do the work: Unit I at Bonthapally (256 kilolitres of reactor volume), Unit II at Pashamylaram (389) and Unit III at Gaddapotharam (581), a total of 1,226 kilolitres.[4][1] In FY26 consolidated revenue from operations was ₹2,022.99 crore, up from ₹1,476.84 crore, and consolidated profit after tax was ₹363.998 crore.[1]

The mix changed fast. Crisil, a rating agency, says CMS was 61 percent of FY26 sales against 43 percent a year earlier, that CMS grew 94 percent, and that sales of the large-volume Prime APIs fell 5 percent and sales of the low-volume Specialty APIs fell 16 percent.[11] In effect, a buyer of this share is now mostly buying a contract manufacturer that depends on a few drugs owned by other companies. By line, CMS was 61 percent of FY26 sales and 67 percent of the June 2026 quarter, Prime APIs about 23 percent and 18 percent, Specialty APIs about 11 percent and 8 percent, and other income lines about 6 percent in both.[1][4] Prime and Specialty together were about a third of FY26 sales, so the generic business still matters to the total, but it no longer sets the growth rate.

Customers, regions and how concentrated the sales are

The audited annual report says three customer groups each contributed more than 10 percent of revenue, together ₹1,087.83 crore, against ₹504.27 crore the year before.[1] That is 56.3 percent of FY26 revenue against 35.2 percent in FY25 (our division of the filed figures, excluding other operating revenue).[1] Crisil gives a wider view: the top five CMS customers supplied 93 percent of CMS revenue, the top five CMS products 94 percent, and across the whole group the top ten customers about 73 percent and the top five products about 65 percent of FY26 revenue.[11] The company itself says CMS is 'highly concentrated in terms of customers' because it serves innovators on an exclusive basis.[4]

Management added in August 2026 that commercial CMS growth is driven by 'maybe about 3' molecules, which it described as active every quarter with a healthy outlook over the next five to six years, though orders may not arrive every quarter.[5] In May 2026 an analyst named a customer and a drug; the company answered that CMS molecules sit under confidentiality clauses and would not confirm them.[6] We treat the naming as unconfirmed and use it in no figure. One customer decision or one problem on a single drug can move the group's profit.

The generic business: large-volume API pricing and the Levetiracetam question

The prior seed for this name described a Levetiracetam concentration in the large-volume range. We tested it. Screener's company description, a secondary source, still names Levetiracetam and Mirtazapine as the key molecules.[23] The FY26 annual report names Ezetimibe and Mirtazapine as the key Prime products and does not mention Levetiracetam at all.[1] Management last named Levetiracetam in May 2025, with Mirtazapine and Escitalopram, as a strong Prime product.[9] So the seed's concentration is not visible in the current record; the concentration that matters now sits in CMS.

Neuland does not publish price or volume for its Prime APIs, so we cannot split price from volume: N/A. What the record shows is the result. Prime sales fell 5 percent in FY26, which Crisil ties to shipment delays, volatile order flow and a higher share of lower-margin products, and it lists intense competition and pricing pressure as a standing weakness of the bulk-drug industry.[11] Prime fell from 40 percent of quarterly revenue in the March 2025 quarter to 18 percent in the June 2026 quarter, mostly because CMS grew, but in rupees the June 2026 quarter was about ₹117 crore against about ₹96 crore a year earlier (our product of the chart share and total income).[10][4] We treat Prime as low-growth, not as a source of pricing power.

Plants, capital spending and ownership

In May 2026 the finance chief said utilisation was 'close to between 85% to 90%' with 'the last unit' around 65 percent; the wording does not say which units are meant, and we do not read it more precisely.[6] Capital spending is rising. 2. Why the business is strong or weak

The case for the business is a 26 percent return on capital, a net cash balance, a Crisil rating of A+ with a positive outlook, and a contract business that grew 94 percent in a year.[1][11] The case against is that profit arrives in lumps, a handful of drugs and customers carry it, the generic business shrank in FY26, and half of last year's operating cash came from customer advances.

Profit arrives in lumps

Total income was ₹300.6 crore, ₹516.1 crore, ₹447.8 crore and ₹788.7 crore in the four quarters of FY26, and ₹650.1 crore in June 2026. Profit after tax went from ₹13.7 crore to ₹96.5 crore, ₹40.4 crore, ₹212.5 crore and ₹147.4 crore (standalone figures from the company's presentation).[4] Management called the March 2026 quarter 'exceptionally inflated' because it was 'more of a Q3 plus Q4' shipment pattern, and said the business is lumpy and should be judged over 10 to 12 quarters.[5][6] On 15 May 2025 management called FY25 a degrowth of about 4.5 to 5 percent on FY24, and revenue from operations fell 5.2 percent.[9][1][2] What this means for you: EPS (earnings per share, profit divided by the number of shares) over the last four quarters, ₹387.97, includes two quarters that management itself calls unusual, so the multiple built on it is a weak guide. The price-to-earnings multiple is the share price divided by earnings per share.[23]

Margins are high, mix-driven and helped by the rupee

The EBITDA margin (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization as a share of sales) was 29.4 percent in FY26 against 22.9 percent in FY25, on the company's total-income basis, and 40.5 and 35.5 percent in the last two quarters.[1][4] The gross margin, sales less the direct cost of making the product as a share of sales, was 61.2 percent in June 2026 against 55.3 percent a year earlier, which management ties to mix.[5] The chief executive said FY26 'benefited from favorable exchange rate movements', that the company plans on 25 percent plus, and that anything above that is a bonus.[6][5] A margin that has been 12 to 40 percent by quarter cannot be treated as a level.

Working capital, cash and debt

Working capital is the cash tied up in stock and customer bills, less supplier credit. The company reports a cash conversion cycle of 137 days for FY26. Crisil reports debtors of 102 days (80 a year earlier) and stock of 130 days. The company says working-capital days fell to 84 in June 2026, mainly because receivables fell.[1][11][4] The sharper point is in the cash flow. Advances from customers, a liability, rose from ₹72.64 crore to ₹244.50 crore in FY26, a rise of ₹171.86 crore, while consolidated cash from operations was ₹346.68 crore.[1] Chapter 3 works through what that does to the cash ratio.

At 31 March 2026 borrowings were ₹196.61 crore and cash and bank balances ₹354.96 crore. That left net cash of ₹158.35 crore. The company reports net cash of ₹308.5 crore at 30 June 2026.[1][4] Crisil expects borrowing to rise to fund the new projects but projects peak gearing, debt over equity, below 0.45 times over three years.[11]

The regulatory record, limited to what the filings say

Crisil says none of Neuland's USFDA inspections has been classed as official action indicated, which is the regulator's worst outcome, and that the company has never received a warning letter or import alert.[11] The company's presentation lists an audit of Unit I in 2024 with zero Form 483 observations (a Form 483 is the regulator's list of things its inspector saw) and says the group has cleared 18 USFDA inspections.[4] The annual report says all three units have completed USFDA inspections and that Unit II, at Pashamylaram, completed its most recent one in 2025, counting one inspection as 'successfully conducted' in FY26; it gives no observation count or closure for it, and we found no filing that does.[1] Unit III was inspected from 10 to 14 August 2026, received a Form 483 with one observation about water and environmental monitoring procedures, and the regulator issued an EIR (an establishment inspection report, the document that closes an inspection) on 14 October 2026 stating the inspection is closed.[12][13] Unit I at Bonthapally has no inspection after 2024 in the record we read.

Layer 1 underwriting: static levels, trajectory (rate of change), and archetype fit

Layer 1 asks whether the company breaks a hard red line and which archetype it fits. A base is a flat period when a share trades sideways and builds a floor. Stage 2 is the rising phase of a trend and Stage 4 is the falling phase. The debt-to-equity ratio is borrowings divided by shareholders' equity. The four India red lines in the scoring file cover debt-to-equity of 1.20 or more (or interest cover under 2.0), "Promoter pledge >= 20% of promoter holding or rising pledge into a falling stock", "3-Year Cumulative CFO / PAT < 0.25 (or < 0.40 for mature non-order-book businesses) without working-capital/order-book turnaround evidence", and "Stage 4 breakdown (Close < falling SMA50 < falling SMA200) with no base formation (Test C not passing)". A contract manufacturer with long-term customer programmes is not clearly a mature non-order-book business, so we apply the stricter 0.40 limb and use no relief clause. ROCE (return on capital employed) is operating profit divided by the money invested in the business. An entry zone is the price range where a buy would be allowed, a limit order is an order to buy only at a set price or lower, and a stop loss is the exit price if the idea is wrong. Asymmetry, also called reward-to-risk, is the possible gain divided by the possible loss. The scoring file measures it as "(target_base - entry_ref) / (entry_ref - stop_loss)", where entry_ref is the close for an immediate entry and the top of the entry zone for a limit order. A breakout is a close above the top of a trading range. The COMPOUNDER standard is "ROCE (or adjusted ROCE ex-goodwill / ROE for financials) >= 15% (prefers >18%), D/E < 0.70, 3Y CFO/PAT >= 0.70", and its asymmetry floor is 2.0. Neuland's return on capital is 26.1 percent on average capital (23.1 percent on closing capital, our recompute).[1]

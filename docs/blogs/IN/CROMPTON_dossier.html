Crompton Greaves Consumer Electricals sells fans, pumps, lights and home appliances in India and owns the Butterfly kitchen brand.[12] We rate it REJECTED for now because of the chart: the shares closed at ₹196.25 on 27 October 2026, below falling 50-day and 200-day averages, a Stage 4 breakdown that our rules do not let us buy.[33] The company is sound, with no borrowings, sales up 18.2% and pre-tax profit before one-offs up 36.2% in September, but half-year cash from operations was minus ₹237 crore.[14][15][16] The verdict changes only if the chart passes the recovery test (Chapter 7 gives the conditions), and cash returning would then raise our conviction and position size.[33] In the ₹193 to ₹201 zone a buyer would risk 4% to 8% to gain 18% to 23%, and we would pay no more than about ₹202.

1. What the company does

Crompton makes the everyday electrical goods that Indian households and farms use: ceiling fans, water pumps, lamps and panels, water heaters, air coolers and kitchen appliances. Its customers are ordinary families, farmers, shop owners, builders and state governments. They do not buy from Crompton directly. They buy from one of about 2.75 lakh retailers, and the company says its products are in one of every three Indian homes.[15] In the year to March 2026 the group earned ₹8,096 crore of revenue, so the business is a mid-sized consumer-goods company, not a niche supplier.[5]

The money comes from three engines.[12] Electrical consumer durables, which the company calls ECD, made ₹6,095.90 crore, or about 75% of revenue, from fans, pumps and appliances, and it earned ₹808.96 crore of segment operating profit.[36] Lighting made ₹1,084.60 crore, and Butterfly, the kitchen-appliance company Crompton bought and runs as a subsidiary, made ₹915.02 crore.[36] Each segment is a different business. Fans and coolers sell in summer and depend on the weather. Pumps follow the farm cycle and government schemes. Lighting is a price-cutting trade. Kitchen appliances follow festivals and weddings.

Crompton does not make everything it sells. It runs seven manufacturing units, and a large part of the range, such as pumps and many appliances, is bought from outside makers and sold under the Crompton name.[15][17] That choice keeps the company light on machinery, which is why a business with ₹8,096 crore of sales has only ₹439.90 crore of plant and equipment.[5][7] The strength is not the factory. It is the brand, the service network of more than 1,300 centres and the dealer reach, because a retailer will stock a fan that customers already trust.[15] General trade, meaning small independent shops, is about 80% of the business, according to management.[17]

Nobody owns Crompton in the old sense. There is no promoter, which is the founding family or parent that usually holds a controlling stake. Promoter holding fell to zero by March 2023, and Screener shows it at 0.00% in September 2026.[4] Domestic institutions hold 65.80% and foreign funds hold 19.21%, so the shares are owned by institutions.[4] The investor day deck describes a move from private-equity ownership to management-led control.[24] The consequence for an investor is simple: there is no promoter pledge to worry about, but there is also no single owner whose interests are tied to the shares.

The plan has a name, Crompton 2.0. It rests on four ideas: sell more premium products such as brushless fans, reach more shops, spend more on the brand, and launch new products.[23] It also adds three new categories: solar pumps, solar rooftop systems and house wires. House wires launched in March 2026 under the Crompton Armor brand in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and Kerala and have since been extended to Punjab, Delhi NCR and Gujarat.[19][17] Management says new businesses were about 10% of revenue in the September quarter and should reach about 14% to 15% within three years.[15] Chapter 2 asks whether that plan rests on a strong business.

  • BLDC fan — a brushless direct-current fan. It uses about half the electricity of an ordinary fan and costs more to buy, so it is the premium end of the market.
  • BEE star rating — the energy label set by the Bureau of Energy Efficiency. Ceiling fans moved to a tighter table (BEE 2.0) and Crompton completed the change without disruption.
  • ECD — electrical consumer durables: fans, pumps, appliances, solar pumps, rooftop solar and wires.
  • General trade — small independent shops. It is the largest channel for Crompton and pays better than online sales.
  • Material margin — sales minus the cost of the goods sold, as a share of sales. It shows how much is left to pay for staff, adverts and profit.
  • Pass-through — raising selling prices to recover higher input costs. Management says about 80% of cost increases have been passed on.
  • Solar rooftop — complete solar systems fitted on homes and buildings. Crompton sells and installs them, partly under a state government order.
  • Unnati — the company's cost-saving programme, which management credits for part of the margin gain.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

Crompton is a strong business in the one way that matters most for fans and pumps: customers know the name. It is the number 1 brand in ceiling fans and residential pumps and it is a top-three name in lighting, water heaters and small kitchen appliances.[12][15] A shopper choosing a fan that must run for ten years reads the brand as a promise of repair, and the company backs that with 1,300 service centres and a stated call-handling time under 180 seconds.[15] That is hard for a new entrant to copy quickly.

The second strength is the balance sheet. The company repaid its last ₹300 crore of bonds on 22 July 2025 and has no bank borrowings, only lease liabilities of ₹202.97 crore at September 2026.[11][16] Credit raters keep the long-term rating at AA+ with a stable outlook, which is one notch below the top.[11][32] It also holds cash, bank balances and investments of ₹659.75 crore after paying a ₹193.17 crore dividend in the half year.[16]

The third strength is momentum in the product mix. Brushless fans grew about 45% in the June quarter and 40% in general trade in the September quarter.[17][18] Premium fans made up 25.4% of fan sales a year ago and management wants 30% by FY31.[23][24] Lighting has also turned: revenue grew 14.3% in the September quarter and its FY26 operating margin of 13.1% was above the 11.8% earned a year earlier.[14][19] Operating margin is operating profit as a share of revenue. Group revenue growth has risen every quarter since June 2025, from minus 6.5% to 18.2% in September 2026.[15]

What holds the business back

The first weakness is growth and profit over a longer span. Screener shows three-year sales growth of 6% a year and a three-year profit decline of 10% a year to March 2026.[1] The investor day deck itself says revenue grew 6% a year in the FY17 to FY22 phase and 11% in the FY23 to FY26 phase.[24] That is the record of a steady business, not of a compounder, and it is why we test the stock against the compounder standard with caution.

The second weakness is the profit earned on each rupee of sales. The EBITDA margin was 11.5% in FY25 and 10.2% in FY26, and the material margin fell from 32.9% to 31.9%.[19] In the September 2026 quarter the material margin was 29.8% against 31.6% a year earlier.[15] Segment profit shows the same drift: ECD operating margin fell from 15.4% to 13.3% in FY26, and the quarterly figure was 11.6% in September 2026.[36][14] Price rises of 11% to 15% have recovered about 80% of the cost increase, but a percentage margin is diluted when a company raises prices just to hold its rupee profit.[17]

The third weakness is that part of the recent profit gain came from spending less on advertising. Consolidated advertising and promotion fell from ₹175 crore to ₹144 crore in the half year, a saving of ₹31 crore, while profit before tax and one-off items rose by ₹69 crore, from ₹289 crore to ₹358 crore.[15] So about 45% of the half-year profit gain came from lower advertising. Management says the cut is mostly in online promotion and that brand-launch costs sit elsewhere, and it says the full-year spend should stay close to last year, so the benefit should fade in the second half.[17]

What the ₹716 crore write-down tells us

In the March 2026 quarter Crompton wrote down its investment in Butterfly and the related trademarks by ₹716.04 crore: ₹683.13 crore on the investment and ₹32.91 crore on trademarks.[8] The auditors made it a key audit matter, and management based it on an independent valuation using discounted cash flows. The charge is non-cash, so it did not touch the bank balance. Management says it does not reflect adversely on Butterfly's present or future cash flows, and the plain reading is that the old carrying value was higher than the value of the cash flows now expected.[8][19]

The write-down left goodwill of ₹779.41 crore and other intangible assets of ₹1,155.81 crore on the balance sheet at March 2026, together ₹1,935.22 crore against shareholders' equity of ₹2,966.30 crore.[7] That is about 65% of the equity held in assets that cannot be sold at a known price. Another write-down is possible if Butterfly misses its plan, and it would cut book value without costing any cash. The next test is the annual impairment review at March 2027.[8]

Competition and pricing power

Management says it led the industry on price rises, raising ECD prices twice in the year after the war began and about 17% to 18% on fans in nine months, and that general-trade and rural volumes stayed positive.[20][17] In the June quarter, it said many rivals had not followed the price rises, perhaps because they held cheaper stock.[18] If that is true, the price gap is a short-term cost for Crompton and will close as rivals' stocks run out.

Online sales are the weak channel: e-commerce was subdued because the company is resetting that channel to earn better margins.[17] Lighting is the most exposed to price cuts: the September quarter margin was 12.9% against 15.5% a year earlier, because business customers had orders at pre-agreed prices.[14] An analyst note after the investor day also refers to the entry of new players in fans, and the company's target is to add about 100 basis points of fan market share by FY31.[25][24] A basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point. We read what management promised in call transcripts and presentations between May 2025 and August 2026 and checked what happened. We also read its guidance, which is the company's own published forecast for a coming period. The record is mixed. Management delivered on entry into new categories and on the fan label change, and it kept cost and supply promises. It was late on the factory and the large-kitchen turnaround, and it missed one growth target for Butterfly.[21][22][23][24]

The most important open promise is solar rooftop. The weakest assumption is an EBITDA margin of 10.0% in FY27 and 10.3% in FY28. The margin was 11.5% in FY25 and 10.2% in FY26, and 9.0% in the September 2026 quarter.[19][15] The assumption fails if commodity costs keep rising after the 80% pass-through, if rivals do not follow price rises, or if advertising rises more than planned. On the August call an analyst described copper as at an all-time high, and management linked the supply shock to the war, which it cannot control.[18][20] We will know from the third quarter, expected in early February 2027, because last year's board meeting for the same quarter was on 6 February.[34]

If the margin falls half a point in each year, FY27 EPS would be about ₹8.5 and FY28 about ₹9.7. At 23 times that is ₹223, which is 5.9% under our central value. The bear case in Chapter 5 needs a larger fall, to a margin of 9.5%.

Estimate 2 (₹237 and 23 times): the multiple holds

The weakest assumption is that the stock keeps a multiple near today's 23 times profit while it grows 17% a year. The three-year Screener median is 41.0 times and the low is 25.8, so the stock is already at the bottom of its own range.[26] The multiple could still fall if growth fades or if cash does not return, because the market would then see a company that sells more and earns less cash. We will know from the share price reaction to the third-quarter and fourth-quarter results, in February and May 2027.

The multiple has two sides. The stock now trades at 22.9 times our trailing profit and 18.9 times FY28 consensus profit, so it is cheap on forward earnings. But a falling stock can stay cheap. Each turn of multiple is about ₹10.3 a share, so a fall to 21 times would cost about ₹21 against our central value. That is almost twice the ₹11.75 between the close and the stop.

Estimate 3 (cash from operations of ₹413 crore): stock turns into cash

The weakest assumption is that the first-half inventory rise reverses. Inventories were ₹1,069.63 crore at September against ₹744.18 crore at March, and the stock was bought while input prices were high.[16][18] If prices of copper and aluminium fall, rivals will cut prices and Crompton may need to discount high-cost stock. Receivables also rose, from ₹702.43 crore to ₹1,188.37 crore in 18 months, and receivables from state-government orders, such as the Andhra Pradesh rooftop contract, are a risk if payment is slow.[7][16] We will know when the FY27 results are published; the FY26 results came on 13 May 2026.[35]

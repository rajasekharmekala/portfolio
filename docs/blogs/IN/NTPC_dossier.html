Tokenx Alpha: Observation 24 alpha: Entry 24: Think of NTPC as a very large wholesaler of electricity. It builds and runs power stations, then sells the output in bulk to state electricity distribution companies. In the year to March 2026 the group generated 432.2 billion units of electricity, about 23% of India’s total from its 17% share of installed capacity. [3] [14] It owns 53 power stations, and 94% of its segment revenue is generation. [27]

Tokeny Alpha: Observation 25 alpha: Entry 25: Most of its customers are tied to it by a PPA , a power purchase agreement, which is a long-term contract that fixes how the buyer pays for a plant. CARE Ratings notes that NTPC’s coal plants were available 89.53% of the time in the nine months to December 2025, above the 85% normative level, and that this meant full recovery of fixed charges. [4] In plain words, NTPC is paid for keeping its plants ready, even when the grid does not call on them every hour. That is why its profits are steady.

Tokenz Alpha: Observation 26 alpha: Entry 26: The group is also widening beyond coal. In FY26 it added a record 9,619 MW of capacity, of which 5,488 MW was renewable (solar, wind and pumped storage). [14] Its coal mines produced 48.65 million tonnes, and it traded 46.52 billion units of power for others. [14] The listed subsidiary NTPC Green Energy generated 14.6 billion units in FY26, and in Q1 FY27 it reported revenue of ₹1,107 crore and profit of ₹305 crore. [11] [19]

Tokenaa Alpha: Observation 27 alpha: Entry 27: Management’s plan, given at the 27 August 2026 annual meeting, is 149 GW by 2032 and 244 GW by 2037, with ₹16.86 lakh crore of capital expenditure , which is money spent on building plants and equipment rather than running them. [11] [13] The 2032 mix is 83.6 GW thermal, 4.7 GW hydro, 60 GW renewables and 0.7 GW nuclear, plus 1 GW of pumped storage and 22 GWh of battery storage (a BESS , or battery energy storage system, stores power for later use). [8] A nuclear joint venture, ASHVINI, in which NTPC holds 49% and NPCIL 51%, is building the 2.8 GW Mahi Banswara plant. [10] [26] In July 2026 it floated a tender of more than ₹28,000 crore for the nuclear island, the reactor core package. [18]

What this means for you. NTPC is a steady, state-backed utility whose growth means spending very large sums on new plants. The question for an investor is not whether the business works. It is whether the return on all that spending is high enough to justify the price.

Why the business is strong or weak

Tokenab Alpha: Observation 28 alpha: Entry 28: Where it is strong. NTPC runs its plants harder than the rest of the country. Its coal plants ran at a plant load factor (the share of possible output actually produced) of 72.04% in FY26 against 63.20% for the rest of India, and at 76.71% against 70.32% in Q1 FY27. [1] [2] In FY25 the figure was 77.44% against a national 69.96%. [17] High use means fixed costs are spread over more units. Three agencies give it their top rating: CARE AAA (Stable, April 2026), India Ratings IND AAA (Stable, February 2026) and ICRA AAA (Stable, the current listing). [4] [6] [7] The construction pipeline is about 35.7 GW, which gives growth that can be seen years ahead. [11]

Tokenac Alpha: Observation 29 alpha: Entry 29: Where it is weak. The weak point is what the business earns on the money it ties up. ROCE , or return on capital employed, is operating profit after depreciation as a share of the money invested in the business, whether borrowed or owned. Screener’s figures put NTPC’s ROCE between 7% and 11% in every one of the last twelve years, and 8.92% now. [27] A utility with regulated returns will always earn modestly. But a modest return on a growing pile of capital means shareholders’ money is put to work slowly.

Tokenad Alpha: Observation 30 alpha: Entry 30: Two other points deserve a flag. Customers are paying more slowly: debtor days (the days of sales still unpaid) rose from 62 in FY23 to 71 in FY26. [27] And fuel supply is tight across India. The CEA counted 59 coal plants with critically low stock on 9 September 2026, up from 31 at the end of July, with total plant stock down to 25.84 million tonnes. [21] NTPC’s own plant-level stock is N/A in this report, because the plant-wise CEA data could not be retrieved. Its captive mines, at 48.65 million tonnes in FY26, are a partial cushion. [14]

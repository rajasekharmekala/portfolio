Before you read

VA Tech Wabag designs, builds and operates water treatment plants, from sea-water desalination to city sewage works, and it holds a ₹19,394 crore order book against ₹4,097 crore of trailing sales[2][1]. The business passes our financial floor: debt is small, returns on capital are above 20 percent and the company holds net cash[1]. The stock fails the price test, because it is in a topping stage and trades below a flat-to-falling 50-day average[29]. The reward-to-risk ratio of 3.15 to 1 also misses the 3.5 to 1 floor. The conclusion is therefore no purchase now, and the idea becomes wrong if profit quality keeps deteriorating or the price loses ₹1,856.

What the company does

Wabag is an engineering contractor for water. A city, a state water board or an industrial plant pays it to design and build a plant that cleans drinking water, treats sewage, recycles effluent or turns seawater into fresh water. It then often charges again to run the plant for years, which it calls operations and maintenance (O&M). Think of it as a builder who also stays on as the caretaker. The company describes itself as a top-three private water operator and desalination supplier by a 2024 industry survey, with presence in more than 25 countries[2].

Who pays, and for what

In Q1 FY27 (April to June 2026) revenue was ₹887 crore: 82 percent from building projects (EPC) and 18 percent from running them (O&M); 75 percent from municipal clients and 25 percent from industry; 48 percent from India and 52 percent from overseas[2]. Municipal clients are governments, so payment depends on public budgets and multilateral funding. That matters later, because slow-paying public clients are why the receivables chapter is the weak spot in this business. Next, the order book shows how long this work will last.

The order book

The closing order book on 30 June 2026 was ₹19,394 crore, split 66 percent EPC and 34 percent O&M, and 51 percent India and 49 percent overseas[2]. It was ₹17,235 crore on 31 March 2026[5]. During Q1 the company won ₹3,431 crore of orders, 77 percent from overseas, including a 272 million litres per day sea-water plant in Kuwait and a sewage plant in Ajman[2]. Management also said it removed about ₹600 crore of framework orders still awaiting effectiveness, so the ₹19,394 crore figure holds only orders that are effective or ready to start[4][24].

Glossary for this report

The order book is signed work the company has not yet billed. EPC means engineering, procurement and construction, the build phase of a project. O&M means operations and maintenance, the years of running a plant after it is built. HAM means hybrid annuity model, where the government pays part during construction and the rest in instalments. ROCE (return on capital employed) is profit earned on each rupee of capital the business uses. EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) is operating profit before accounting and finance adjustments. EPS (earnings per share) is profit divided across all shares. CFO/PAT means cash from operations divided by profit after tax, a check that profit turns into cash. ATR (average true range) is the average daily price swing in rupees. RSI (relative strength index) is a 0 to 100 meter of how fast the price has been moving. A 20/50/200-day moving average is the average closing price over that many trading days. Stage 3 means the Weinstein topping or pullback stage, where an uptrend has stalled. Forex gain means profit from exchange-rate moves on invoices and costs.

What this means for an owner: you own a project pipeline of about 4.7 years of sales, but its profit depends on cost control and collecting from public clients. The next chapter tests how strong that position is.

Why the business is strong or weak

Strength here has three parts: demand visibility, balance-sheet safety and profit quality. We score five measures that govern a project-based water contractor and give each PASS, WATCH or FAIL. The score is 3 PASS, 2 WATCH and 0 FAIL, which is below the 4 of 5 we want for a clean pass.

Layer 1: the financial floor

The floor requires ROCE of at least 15 percent, debt-to-equity below 0.70 and three-year cash conversion of at least 0.70. Wabag reports ROCE of 21.3 percent[1]. Borrowings were ₹228 crore against equity of ₹2,568 crore, which is 0.089[1]. Cash from operations over FY24 to FY26 was ₹696 crore against profit of ₹915 crore, a ratio of 0.76[1]. All three pass, but the FY26 cash ratio alone was 0.56 (₹207 crore over ₹370 crore), so the pass is narrower than the three-year figure suggests.

Profit quality: the forex line

Q1 FY27 reported EBITDA of ₹116.3 crore includes a forex gain of ₹36.8 crore. Revenue minus cost of sales minus other operating costs gives ₹79.5 crore, or 9.0 percent of sales, against ₹109.2 crore, or 14.9 percent, in Q1 FY26, which carried a ₹13.6 crore forex loss (our arithmetic on the company's P&L)[2]. Screener's own operating profit of ₹80 crore and 9 percent margin agree[1]. Management says forex gains and losses are transactional and belong in operating results, and that translation effects stay in the balance sheet[4]. That is a fair accounting view. The consequence for an investor is that a 13 to 15 percent margin target is being met partly by currency moves that can reverse, and FY26 showed the same pattern: reported 13.3 percent, 12.1 percent without forex[5].

Receivables and judgement risk

The auditor named two key audit matters in the FY26 standalone report: revenue recognised by percentage of completion, which depends on cost estimates, and the recoverability of unbilled dues and trade receivables[6]. Standalone gross trade receivables were ₹2,387 crore at 31 March 2026. Of these, ₹412 crore (17.3 percent) were more than one year past due and 32.2 percent were more than six months past due. The expected credit loss allowance rose from ₹51.1 crore to ₹94.6 crore[7]. Indirect-tax disputes rose to ₹227.9 crore from ₹101.1 crore[8]. An analyst also raised about ₹140 crore owed by a state generator, in arbitration; management said a Supreme Court ruling favours the company but gave no timeline[4].

What this means for an owner: the business is not fragile, since it holds net cash and an AA- rating[11][2], but the profit you see is softer than the headline. The numbers chapter shows the five-year record.

What could happen next

We build three outcomes for the next 12 months from one rule: next year's profit per share times the market's usual multiple. Management's outlook is revenue growth of 15 to 20 percent a year, EBITDA of 13 to 15 percent, and an FY27 range it called realistically 13 to 14 percent[2][4]. The number

Base case (FX-inclusive): FY27 profit per share of about ₹69.9 at a multiple of 30.2 gives a ₹2,110 target, 8.9 percent above today's ₹1,936.80.

How it was calculated

FY26 revenue of ₹3,944.2 crore grows 17.5 percent (middle of 15 to 20) to ₹4,634 crore. At FY26's 9.4 percent profit margin that is ₹435.6 crore, divided by 6.2355 crore shares gives ₹69.9. Multiplying by the three-year median multiple of 30.2 gives ₹2,110.

The inputs

FY26 revenue and margin[5]; growth outlook[2]; share count 62,354,776[2]; median multiple[14].

The range

Bear ₹1,350: revenue growth 15 percent, margin 8.0 percent (EPS ₹58.2) at 23.2 times, the midpoint of the 10-year median of 16.2 and the three-year median of 30.2. Bull ₹2,338: growth 20 percent and Q1's 10.2 percent margin (EPS ₹77.4) at 30.2 times.

Reward against risk

The stop follows our tactical rule: the lowest of the 20-day swing low (₹1,921.30), the 20-day average and the 50-day average, minus half an ATR. That is ₹1,921.30 − ₹39.45 = ₹1,881.85, a risk of ₹54.95 or 2.84 percent. Reward to risk is (2,110 − 1,936.8) ÷ 54.95 = 3.15, below the 3.5 floor. The bull case reaches 7.3 but carries a 20 percent probability. A stop this tight sits just under the price, so ordinary noise of one ATR (₹78.90) can trigger it.

What this means for an owner: the expected return is about zero and the asymmetry floor fails, so waiting costs little. The next chapter lists what could make these estimates wrong.

Why these estimates might be wrong

Each estimate rests on one weak assumption, and each has a date by which we will know more.

Base case (FX-inclusive): the profit margin holds at 9.4 percent

The weakest assumption is that forex gains recur. FY25's forex gain was ₹7.9 crore and FY26's was ₹46.7 crore[5], so the gain is volatile. Evidence against us: Q2 FY27 EBITDA without forex below 10 percent for a second quarter. The results date is N/A; the quarter ends 30 September 2026 and the trading window closed on 28 September[21].

Growth: execution of an order book that is half overseas

An analyst asked on the 13 August call about Middle East execution risk, since about half the backlog is outside India[4]. Management's reply was not captured in our extract (N/A). Project delays shift revenue into later years, which lowers FY27 EPS. We will see this in quarterly revenue against the 15 to 20 percent path.

Cash: receivables and tax disputes

If overdue receivables keep growing, profit stays on paper. The allowance already rose by ₹43.5 crore in FY26[7], and indirect-tax disputes more than doubled[8]. Evidence against us: FY27 cash from operations below 0.7 times profit, visible in the May 2027 annual results.

Valuation: the 30.2 multiple

The three-year median covers a period when profit was rising fast, and the 10-year median is 16.2 times[14]. If the market pays a lower multiple, the base target falls below today's price. Late-breaking positives we cannot rule out: a faster order conversion, a TSGENCO recovery[4], or the refreshed five-year strategy that management plans to present in May 2027[4].

What this means for an owner: the largest risk is margin quality, not demand. The final chapter turns this into a decision.

What we would do, and when

The verdict is REJECTED for new allocation in this run. The rule is the Layer 1 technical floor, which requires Stage 2 above a rising 50-day and 200-day average; the stock is Stage 3 below a flat-to-falling 50-day, and the asymmetry floor also fails at 3.15 against 3.5. The business itself passes the financial floor, so this is a timing and price rejection, not a quality rejection.

Entry posture (Layer 3)

The ladder measures distance below support in ATR. The 50-day average is above the price, so that support is broken. The nearest support below is the 20-day swing low at ₹1,921.30 (0.20 ATR below), then the 63-day base low at ₹1,856 (1.02 ATR), then the 200-day average at ₹1,606.87 (4.18 ATR)[29]. Posture: wait; do not buy until support is reclaimed.

Trigger to revisit: two closes above ₹2,019.70, with 63-day relative strength at or above zero. At that point re-run the full underwriting with Q2 FY27 numbers.

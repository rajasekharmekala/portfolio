1. What the company does

In plain words: Cohance makes molecules and medicines for other drug companies, and the merger and renaming change how every older number reads.

Cohance Lifesciences Limited is a Hyderabad-based contract development and manufacturing organisation (CDMO). Drug companies hire it to design a chemical route to a molecule, make the starting materials and the active ingredient, and sometimes the finished tablet. It reports three businesses: Pharma CDMO (39 percent of FY26 revenue of ₹2,268.55 crore), which makes custom molecules for large innovators, including antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) warheads and nucleic-acid building blocks; API+ (48 percent), which sells active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) and formulations; and Specialty Chemicals (13 percent), which serves agrochemical and performance-material customers. In the June 2026 quarter the mix was 30, 62 and 8 percent. [4] [1]

The listed company was Suven Pharmaceuticals. Cohance Lifesciences, a sister company under the same private equity owner, Advent International, merged into it with effect from 1 May 2025, and the survivor took the name Cohance Lifesciences from 7 May 2025. [17] [18] On NSE the symbol changed from SUVENPHAR to COHANCE on 19 May 2025, so COHANCE is the live symbol. [18] Our records still carry the old key SUVENPHAR, and the ledger prices come from the COHANCE.NS series. [33] The promoter group is two Advent holding companies, Berhyanda Limited (33.34 percent) and Jusmiral Holdings Limited (24.15 percent), 57.48 percent together. [12]

The merger joined two companies under common control, so it was booked by the pooling-of-interests method: earlier periods were restated as if the two had always been one company, and no new goodwill arose from the merger itself. The company issued 12,80,02,184 new shares at 11 for every 295 held, which lifted the share count from 25.46 crore to 38.26 crore. [4] Restated FY25 revenue is ₹2,608.50 crore, against ₹1,197.58 crore as first reported before the merger. [4] Chapter 3 says what this does to any price, earnings or return series that crosses 1 May 2025.

Revenue comes from fees and product sales. Small-molecule CDMO is lumpy: a few commercial molecules carry most of the revenue, and customers restock in steps, so a destocking quarter cuts revenue sharply; management blamed destocking of two large commercial products for much of the FY26 fall. [4] [5] API+ is a products business in which the top 15 products give about half of API revenue. [5] The company bought a stake in Sapala Organics (nucleic-acid building blocks) in July 2024 and 56 percent of NJ Bio (ADC conjugation services, New Jersey) in December 2024. [1] [22] In September 2026 it agreed to raise its NJ Bio stake to 67.3 percent and to take 65 percent of a related start-up, Aruka Bio. [30] It has also set a path to full ownership of Sapala by FY30. [3]

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • CDMO (contract development and manufacturing organisation): a company that develops and makes molecules for drug companies under contract.
  • ADC (antibody-drug conjugate): a medicine in which a toxic warhead is attached to an antibody by a linker; NJ Bio does the joining, called bioconjugation.
  • Pooling of interests: an accounting method for mergers of companies under common control, in which earlier years are restated as if the two had always been combined.
  • Goodwill: the price paid for a business above the fair value of its assets; it is tested for impairment each year and written down if the business no longer supports it.
  • Non-controlling interest (NCI): the part of a subsidiary owned by outside holders, such as the founders of NJ Bio and Sapala; the forward liability is the company's obligation to buy them out at a later date.
  • Pledge: shares handed to lenders as security; if the borrower defaults or a value test fails, the lenders may sell them. A promoter is the controlling owner; here it is the Advent holding companies.
  • Form 483, warning letter and OAI: the US Food and Drug Administration (USFDA) issues a Form 483 listing inspection findings; a plant classed Official Action Indicated (OAI) may get a warning letter, the most serious step short of a ban on shipments.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

In plain words: A platform with real customers and cash has been hit by a bad year, quality findings and an ownership problem.

The business has real assets and a bad year on top of them. The assets are a platform that works with the top 20 global innovators, net cash, ten molecules in Phase III trials, backward integration in APIs and a gross margin near 71 percent. The bad year shows in every profit line. Revenue fell 13.0 percent in FY26 and 23.1 percent in the June 2026 quarter. Adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) margin (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, after the company's own add-backs) went from 33.7 percent in FY25 to 21.0 percent in FY26 and 2.2 percent in June 2026. The company made a consolidated loss in that quarter. [4] [1] [2]

Where the strength is

The standalone company, without the two newer subsidiaries, still earns real profit: adjusted EBITDA margin was 24.6 percent in FY26 and 9.2 percent in the June 2026 quarter, and Sapala grew revenue 2.5 times to ₹27.4 crore with a strong margin. [4] [1] [3] Management said delivery was close to 100 percent on time and customer audits at several sites found nothing critical. [3] The Phase III pipeline stands at ten programmes, two molecules have entered commercial supply and a restocking order was secured for a molecule that had destocked. No order-book value is published, so order cover cannot be scored in numbers and is not a Layer 2 row; management describes the API order book as healthy and says six intermediates ship in the second and third quarters. [4] [3] Net cash was ₹251.2 crore at 30 June 2026 against gross borrowings of ₹207.7 crore, and CRISIL measured adjusted debt over adjusted net worth at 0.04 times for FY26. [1] [22] India Ratings rated ₹685 crore of bank lines IND AA/Stable in December 2025. [21]

[31] [30] Goodwill of ₹466.20 crore sits against NJ Bio inside a total of ₹1,283.65 crore, and the auditor lists the goodwill impairment test as a key audit matter. [11]

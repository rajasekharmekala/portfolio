1. What the company does

Eicher Motors Limited is the listed holding company of two businesses. The first is Royal Enfield, which describes itself as the oldest motorcycle brand in continuous production, with motorcycles made since 1901. It sells four engine families: the 350cc range (Bullet, Classic, Goan Classic, Hunter and Meteor), the 440cc Scram, the 450cc Guerrilla and Himalayan, and the 650cc twins (Classic, Continental GT, Interceptor, Bear, Shotgun and Super Meteor). [13] The second business is a 54.4% stake in VE Commercial Vehicles (VECV), a joint venture with the Volvo Group of Sweden that makes Eicher trucks and buses and sells Volvo trucks in India. [13] Royal Enfield's accounts are added line by line into the group figures. VECV is not: its profit enters as one line called share of profit of joint venture, and its sales are left out of group revenue. [4][5]

Three groups of customers pay the company. Indian riders paid ₹16,768 crore in FY26, which is 72% of group revenue of ₹23,408 crore. Riders in more than 80 other countries paid ₹3,288 crore, or 14%. The remaining ₹3,351 crore, also 14%, came from the allied business: spare parts, servicing, accessories and riding apparel. [13] Volumes tell the same story. Royal Enfield sold 1,227,977 motorcycles in FY26, of which 1,107,343 were in India and 120,634 were exports. [13] In the six months to September 2026 it sold 709,096, up 20%, with India up 23% and exports down 5%. [10] The export fall is worth keeping in mind, because the next chapters show it is one of the weaker spots in an otherwise strong year.

Each motorcycle brings in about ₹1.99 lakh of revenue and about ₹48,000 of operating profit. We got those two numbers by dividing the June 2026 quarter's revenue of ₹6,632 crore and operating profit of ₹1,591 crore by the 332,940 motorcycles sold in the quarter. [3][6] The operating profit figure is EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization), which means profit before borrowing costs, tax and the wear on machines is deducted. It was 24.0% of revenue in the quarter. [4] Because these are group averages that include exports, accessories and service, they say nothing about any single model.

The main costs are materials and parts. In the June 2026 quarter they took 58.5% of revenue, after allowing for the change in stock, against 55.8% a year earlier. [4] The chief financial officer said steel, aluminium, crude oil, copper and precious metals had all risen, and that the net hit from input costs was about 4% to 4.5% of revenue. Price rises of 1.75% on most 350cc models in April 2026 recovered about 1.2 points, and value engineering recovered about 0.4 points. [6] A second source of profit is interest on cash. Other income was ₹466 crore in the quarter, about a quarter of profit before tax. [4] An owner should therefore read the income statement in two halves: a motorcycle business that earns a 24% operating profit margin, and a treasury whose income was between about 18% and 28% of pre-tax profit in the three quarters shown in chapter 3.

Royal Enfield sells through more than 2,074 stores in India and 1,212 abroad, and buyers financed 59.3% of FY26 sales. The service network handled 10.3 million job cards, which are individual service visits, during FY26. [13] The company also runs assembly plants for imported kits in Argentina, Brazil, Colombia, Thailand, Nepal and Bangladesh, which the annual report says gives cheaper and faster entry to those markets. [13] In the June 2026 quarter international revenue crossed ₹1,000 crore for the first time and was about 15% of revenue, and the allied business was also about 15%. [6]

VECV runs on a different rhythm because trucks are bought by businesses, and orders follow the economy. It sold 103,404 trucks and buses in FY26, led India's light and medium-duty truck market with a 34.9% share, and held 9.7% of heavy-duty trucks. [13] From April to September 2026 it sold 52,886 vehicles, up 21.5%, and September alone rose 49.6% to 11,396. [11] Its profit margin is thin. EBITDA was 8.4% of revenue in the June quarter against 9.2% a year earlier. [6] Eicher's share of VECV's profit was ₹797.8 crore in FY26, about 14% of group profit, and ₹168 crore in the June quarter. [5] In May 2026 the group also agreed to invest up to ₹750 crore for half of a vehicle-finance joint venture with Volvo Financial Services India, a business that held ₹1,825 crore of loans on 31 March 2026; closing depends on regulatory approval. [15]

The newest business is electric. Flying Flea is a separate brand for city electric motorcycles. The first model, the FF.C6, was launched in Bengaluru in the first week of April 2026, and about 100 had been delivered within two months. [7][6] Deliveries in five cities in Europe and the UK start by October 2026. [10] At this size the electric business matters for the brand story and for future options, not yet for profit.

Where the company stands in its market

A share of 9.1% of all Indian motorcycles in April to June 2026 may look small, but it hides how Royal Enfield is positioned. The company puts its share of the mid-size segment, the 250cc to 750cc band, at about 85% for the June 2026 quarter. [5] ICRA, the rating agency, puts it at 87% to 88% for motorcycles above 250cc and says the lead has lasted more than a decade. [18] In FY26 Royal Enfield held 8.4% of all domestic motorcycles and 31.3% of those above 125cc. [13]

The segment is a niche inside a very large market. ICRA notes that the 250cc to 750cc band is only 8% to 10% of all motorcycles in India. [18] That is why a share of 9.1% of all motorcycles means a lot of choice for riders and a lot of room to grow for the company, but it also means that Royal Enfield's fortunes are tied to a single, premium-leaning type of buyer. Chapter 2 tests how well that position holds up against new rivals, and chapter 2B tests the operating numbers that show whether it is slipping.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • RSI (relative strength index) is a 0 to 100 gauge of how fast a price has moved lately; below 30 is called oversold and above 70 overbought.
  • ROCE (return on capital employed) is yearly operating profit divided by the money put to work in the business; it shows what each rupee invested earns.
  • EPS (earnings per share) is profit divided by the number of shares, so it is the profit that belongs to one share.
  • Free cash flow (FCF) is the cash from running the business minus the money spent on plants and machines, which makes it the cash left for owners.
  • Cash flow from operations is the cash collected from customers less the cash paid for costs and tax; it is the check on whether reported profit is real.
  • Gross margin is revenue minus the cost of materials, shown as a share of revenue.
  • Operating margin is operating profit as a share of revenue; for this company it is almost the same as the EBITDA margin.
  • Net margin is profit after tax as a share of revenue.
  • Debt-to-equity is borrowings divided by shareholders' money; a number near zero means the company lives on its own cash.
  • Net debt is borrowings minus cash; a negative number means the company holds more cash than debt.
  • Price-to-earnings (P/E) is the share price divided by profit per share, so it says how many years of current profit the price equals.
  • Working capital is the money tied up in stock and unpaid customer bills, less what the company still owes its suppliers.
  • Capital expenditure is money spent on plants, machines and new products that last longer than a year.
  • Guidance is what management says it expects to deliver, such as a capacity date or a margin level.
  • Consensus estimate is the average forecast of the analysts who cover the stock.
  • The 20-day moving average, 50-day moving average and 200-day moving average are the average closing price over the last 20, 50 and 200 trading days; they are the lines chartists use to see the trend.
  • Stage 2 is the rising phase of a stock's life, with the price above a rising 50-day and 200-day average, and Stage 4 is the falling phase, with the price below falling averages.
  • A base is a stretch of sideways trading after a fall, in which the price builds a floor before it tries to rise again.
  • A pivot is the price just above the top of that base, the level a rise has to clear to count as real.
  • A breakout is a close above the pivot, and a retest is a pull-back to the broken level that holds instead of failing.
  • A stop loss is the price at which we sell to limit the loss if the idea is wrong.
  • A limit order is an instruction to buy only at or below a price you set.
  • The entry zone is the price range in which we are willing to buy.
  • Reward-to-risk, also called asymmetry, is the gain from the entry price to our target divided by the loss from the entry price to the stop; 2.0 means two rupees of gain for each rupee at risk.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

Royal Enfield wins on brand, not on price or on technology. That is the starting point for judging the business. The company's own research says that 56% of buyers in the 200cc-plus category name Royal Enfield first when asked which brand comes to mind, against 17% for the nearest rival, and that 83% would consider buying one, against 54%. [5] Those are company survey figures, so treat them as a claim, but the sales data point the same way. In September 2026, Royal Enfield models took 84.14% of the 139,262 motorcycles in the tracked 350cc to 500cc segment. [25] Dealers also rate the company: it won the FADA dealer satisfaction survey for the second year running, with 878 index points. [10]

The brand turns into high returns because the business needs little capital. ROCE was 30.5% on the Screener calculation and 24.5% as the company reports it; the two formulas differ, and we treat the lower company figure as the cautious one. [19][13] ROCE has climbed from 18% in FY22 to 31% in FY26. [19] Return on equity was 24.0%. [19] Part of the reason is the cash cycle. Dealers pay quickly and suppliers are paid slowly, so cash conversion was minus 16 days in FY26: stock of 55 days and customer bills of 6 days are more than covered by 76 days of supplier credit. [19] The business therefore grows without borrowing.

The balance sheet is the strongest part of the story. Cash and investments, other than stakes in subsidiaries and the joint venture, were ₹19,091 crore on 31 March 2026, and borrowings were only ₹514 crore on the Screener balance sheet. [13][19] That cash equals about 9.6% of the company's market value of ₹198,971 crore, or about ₹695 for each of the 274.5 million shares. [19][12] In FY26 the company produced ₹4,805 crore of cash from operations, spent ₹1,275 crore on plants and equipment, and paid ₹1,920 crore of dividends. [5] ICRA rates the bank facilities [ICRA]AAA with a stable outlook and expects expansion to be paid from cash already held. [18]

The weak points are narrowness and competition. ICRA names two: the product range sits mainly in the 250cc to 750cc band, which is only 8% to 10% of the motorcycle market, and the Classic range carries a large share of volume. [18] Rivals are now arriving in the 350cc class. In September 2026 the Honda CB350 rose 55.85% to 9,552 units, while Royal Enfield's Meteor 350 fell 25.38% to 10,772 and the Himalayan fell 23.10% to 3,029. [25] Bajaj, KTM, Husqvarna and Triumph together rose 23.56% to 9,761 units, about 7% of the segment, while Triumph's 400 fell to 98 units from 4,030 as the brand moved to a 350cc model that sold 1,756. [25] The pattern is that Royal Enfield still dominates, but its individual models are no longer immune.

Capacity, not demand, is the near-term limit. On 29 July 2026 the company said its 15 lakh a year of capacity at Oragadam and Vallam Vadagal was approaching full use. [14] Dealer stock was only 10 to 12 days, and daily output had risen to about 5,000 motorcycles after a new production module began in the last week of July. [6] That helps explain why September dispatches rose only 8%, while the whole Indian two-wheeler market registered 32.93% more vehicles than a year earlier. The two figures are not strictly comparable, because one counts factory dispatches and the other counts retail registrations, but the gap is wide. [10][26] A second module was due by the first week of October, the Cheyyar brownfield expansion should take capacity to about 20 lakh by FY 2027-28, and a new plant in Andhra Pradesh adds 4.5 lakh by FY 2029-30, paid from internal accruals. [6][14]

What this means for an owner: the business is one of the best in Indian two-wheelers on returns, cash and brand, and its main risks are not solvency but growth quality. Those risks are mix, exports and margin, which are the three things that chapter 2B scores. The two subsections that follow turn to GST and exports, the two outside forces that moved the numbers most in the past year.

GST changed which Royal Enfield riders pay more

Since 22 September 2025, motorcycles up to 350cc pay 18% GST, down from 28%, and motorcycles above 350cc pay 40%, up from 28% plus a 3% cess. [24] The cut made the whole 350cc family, which is most of Royal Enfield's volume, cheaper. The rise made the 450cc and 650cc motorcycles, which carry the highest prices and the most engineering, dearer. In the six months to September 2026 the up-to-350cc group, which includes electric models, sold 634,312 units, up 24%, while the above-350cc group sold 74,784, down 9%. In September alone, above-350cc sales fell 26% to 12,463. [10] ICRA says the above-350cc group historically made up only 10% to 12% of domestic volumes. [18]

Management chose not to cut the engine size of its 450cc and 650cc motorcycles to reach the lower tax slab. The chief executive said, 'We held on to our 450 cc and 650 cc,' and that Guerrilla 450 sales had recovered to about 2,500 a month and the 650cc twins to about 4,000 to 4,200 a month, which he called pre-GST levels. [6] That claim sits awkwardly with the September data, where the whole above-350cc group is 26% below last year, and the Himalayan is one of the models falling. [10][25] We do not treat the claim as false, because the Himalayan 440 launched in September was booked out for six months, but we do not count it as proven until above-350cc dispatches turn positive. [10]

Exports are growing in revenue but not in units

International business is about 14% of revenue and grew from ₹2,546 crore in FY25 to ₹3,288 crore in FY26. [13] Management says international revenue crossed ₹1,000 crore in a single quarter for the first time in April to June 2026. [6] Yet export units fell 5% in the six months to September 2026, to 67,075 from 70,421, even though September itself rose 17%. [10] The gap is explained partly by currency and partly by weak markets: the chief executive described Europe as a market adjustment phase, said Indonesia caps imports at about 10,000 motorcycles a year, and said the United States showed 'green shoots' after a trade deal cut tariffs on motorcycles. [6] Brazil is now the largest market outside India, and Royal Enfield is second in the middleweight segment there. [6]

The company's share of the middleweight segment is 7.6% in Europe, the Middle East and Africa, 9.7% in Asia-Pacific and 6.6% in the Americas. [5] Those shares are small next to the 85% held in India, which is why exports could add a lot of growth, and also why growth there is slower to arrive than in India.

Guidance is management's forecast of what it will deliver, and walking the talk means doing what it said, by the date it said. Eicher's management gives few numbers for profit. 3. The numbers, in plain words

The numbers answer three questions: how fast the business has grown, whether profit turns into cash, and how much cushion the balance sheet gives. Between FY22 and FY26, revenue rose from ₹10,298 crore to ₹23,408 crore and profit after tax from ₹1,677 crore to ₹5,515 crore. [5] Motorcycles sold rose from 595,474 to 1,227,977. [5] Operating margin, which means operating profit as a share of revenue, rose from 21.1% to a peak of 26.2% in FY24 and has since eased to 24.7%. [13]

Profit turned into cash. Over FY24 to FY26 the company generated ₹12,508 crore of cash from operations against ₹14,251 crore of reported profit. [5] Because ₹1,945 crore of that profit is the VECV share, which is booked as profit but is not operating cash unless VECV pays it out, the comparison is better stated as 1.02 times the profit that the business itself earned. [5] Working capital is also helpful: stock days were 55, customer bill days 6 and supplier days 76 in FY26. [19]

The balance sheet gives a large cushion. Cash and investments of ₹19,091 crore compare with borrowings of ₹514 crore, so net debt is negative by about ₹18,600 crore. [13][19] Net worth was ₹25,100 crore. The cash was built while paying out ₹1,920 crore of dividends in FY26 and spending ₹1,275 crore on plants and equipment. [5] Three things follow. First, the company can fund its announced plant spending without borrowing. Second, other income was between about 18% and 28% of pre-tax profit in the three quarters shown below, so treasury returns matter to EPS. How it was calculated

A reverse DCF (discounted cash flow) works backwards from today's price. We grow FY26 profit for ten years at a trial rate, add the value of each year's profit discounted at 12%, add a terminal value of 20 times year-ten profit discounted the same way, and adjust the trial rate until the total equals the company's market value. The scoring rules set the 12% rate, the 20 times multiple and the ten-year horizon for India.

The inputs

FY26 profit after tax ₹5,515.2 crore; market value ₹198,971 crore; free cash flow ₹3,529.5 crore, which is cash from operations of ₹4,804.8 crore less spending on plants and machines of ₹1,275.3 crore. That sensitivity is why we do not call the stock cheap and why we want the entry at ₹7,038 or lower.

The number

Weighting the three cases 25%, 50% and 25%, the expected value is ₹7,998 a share, which is 10.5% above the close, 13.6% above the zone top of ₹7,038 and 15.9% above the zone floor of ₹6,900. We treat these as one-year moves, and the horizon is explained under the range.

Each case is FY28 EPS times a multiple, and the values are weighted by our probabilities: 0.25 times ₹9,870, plus 0.50 times ₹8,260, plus 0.25 times ₹5,600. The probabilities rest on what we have seen: management has kept its dated promises, the Street is near our FY27 figures, and the risks are in mix, exports and margin.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate has a weakest assumption, and the honest question is which one, if wrong, hurts most. We take the three estimates in turn, then give the strongest case against our own verdict, then name the single claim the verdict depends on and the date we will learn whether it holds.

Weakest assumption of the EPS estimate: the 24.0% margin holds

The estimate holds the operating margin at 24.0% for FY27 and FY28. The reading for the June quarter was 24.0%, but gross margin fell from 44.2% to 41.5% and other expenses fell from 12.9% to 10.7% of revenue. [4] Of the 2.2-point fall in other expenses, the chief financial officer pointed to marketing that did not repeat and tighter spending, and said commodity costs remain volatile and she could not give guidance on them. [6] If other expenses return to 12.0% and gross margin stays at 41.5%, the operating margin would be about 22.7% and FY27 EPS about ₹8 lower than our estimate.

What would show the assumption is wrong: Q2 FY27 other expenses above 11.5% of revenue, or an EBITDA margin below 23.0%, which on our Q2 revenue estimate of ₹7,448 crore is EBITDA below about ₹1,713 crore. We will know when the Q2 FY27 results are filed, due by 14 November 2026. [30]

Weakest assumption of the target: the market keeps paying 32.8 times profit

The base value assumes the multiple stays near its three-year median of 32.8 while profit growth slows from 14.6% in FY27 to 9.3% in FY28. [20] A multiple of about 33 on growth of about 9% is a rich price for growth, and the multiple was 26.7 at its low in the past three years. At 27 times, FY28 EPS of ₹251.9 is worth ₹6,801, which is 6.0% below the close and only 2.9% above the stop. Foreign investors have cut their stake from 30.27% in December 2023 to 24.03% in September 2026, and domestic institutions have raised theirs from 9.73% to 17.61%, so the buyer base has shifted but not shrunk. [19]

Weakest assumption of the probabilities: that the base case is the likeliest

We give the base case 50%. The company has kept its capacity and launch dates, and the Street's FY27 figures match ours. [22][6][14] The weakness is that the comparison base changes after September. Royal Enfield sold over 2.49 lakh motorcycles in September and October 2025, which management called its best ever festive performance. The GST cut that lifted that period is now in the base. [9] Management says bookings, walk-ins and enquiries are running ahead of volumes. That supports growth on a higher base. But those are management statements, and they cannot be checked against filings. [6]

The red-team bear case against our verdict

The strongest case against waiting for a pullback, and for avoiding the stock, runs as follows. The shares are at 34.2 times profit, above the 32.8 median, for a business whose profit growth is slowing from 14.6% to about 9%. [20] Premium mix is falling and exports are falling in units. Margin was protected by a one-quarter drop in other expenses. The house DCF values the shares at about ₹5,981. Three of the five Layer 2 KPIs are WATCH. [10][4] The strongest evidence for the bear is the table above: any two WATCH scores turning to FAIL would take Layer 2 below the 3.0 floor.

Our reply is that the bear case is a case against paying today's price, which we also do not recommend. Our plan, once the Q2 results have been read and the orders are active, buys half at ₹7,038 and half at ₹6,900, where the base value gives 2.85 and 4.68 times the risk, and the stop at ₹6,609.11 caps the planned loss at about 0.21% of net worth for a full 3.5% position. The bear case also helps explain why we do not rate the stock a REJECTED name: nothing in the solvency, governance or cash tests is broken, and the 22.6% fall to ₹5,600 is a drawdown, not a loss of the business.

Ador Welding is a 75-year-old Indian maker of welding rods, wires and machines, listed as ADOR on the NSE and 517041 on the BSE. It sells about ₹1,140 crore a year, mostly to fabricators through 437 dealers[6][1]; sales rose 22.8% in the first half of FY27, but in the September quarter profit grew only 7.7% on sales up 22.6%[1]. Our verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT: the business passes every red line, but the price is 1.64 average-true-range steps above support and the reward is only 0.37 times the risk[1][30]. The 2.0 floor is met only in a ₹1,240 to ₹1,262 buying zone. 1. What the company does

Welding joins two pieces of metal by melting a third metal between them. Ador sells the metal that gets melted (rods and wires, called consumables), the machines that supply the electric current, and the cutting and robotic systems around them. Every steel bridge, ship hull, boiler and railway wagon needs welds, so the company's sales follow how much steel is being fabricated in India. The company says it makes 90,000 tonnes of consumables and 30,000 welding machines a year at five plants[1].

Before the numbers, a short word list. Stage 2 is a confirmed uptrend and Stage 4 is a confirmed downtrend on the Weinstein scale. A base is a sideways stretch that a price builds before it moves. The pivot is the top of that base. A breakout is a close above the pivot on strong volume. A limit order is an order that fills only at your price or better. An entry zone is the band of prices where you plan to buy. Reward-to-risk is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop.

Five more terms. RSI (relative strength index) is a 0 to 100 meter of how fast the price has moved. ATR (average true range) is the typical daily price swing in rupees. EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) is a quick measure of operating profit. ROCE (return on capital employed) is profit earned on each rupee the business uses. EPS (earnings per share) is the company's profit divided across all shares.

Free cash flow is the cash left after running the business and buying plant. Tables show it as FCF (free cash flow). Gross margin is sales minus raw-material cost, as a share of sales. Operating margin is sales minus all running costs, as a share of sales. Debt-to-equity is debt divided by shareholders' money.

Ador was founded in 1951 as India's first welding manufacturer, in a joint venture with a Swiss group, and is now the flagship of the Ador Group, promoted by J. B. Advani & Company, which holds 45.23% of the shares[1][6]. In September 2024 it absorbed its sister company Ador Fontech, which made wear-resistant products and repaired heavy equipment. The deal was backdated to 1 April 2022 and gave Fontech holders five new Ador shares for every 46 they held[10]. That is why five-year history in this book uses the restated figures. Press releases still print the old name: one prints NSE: ADORWELD[32].

The ticker needs a note. The watchlist and some news sites still call the stock ADORWELD. The company's own exchange filings use the symbol ADOR on the NSE and scrip code 517041 on the BSE[1]. The ledger maps the old name to the Yahoo ticker ADOR.NS[30]. We use ADOR for the exchange and keep ADORWELD for the filename and the watchlist.

Customers are fabricators, power plants, refineries, railways, shipyards, cement and steel makers, and defence and nuclear projects. They rarely buy from Ador directly. About 67.8% of sales go through 437 dealers and distributors, and the ten largest take 37.3% of that channel[6]. Exports are 15% to 20% of revenue, and 80% to 85% of those exports go to the Middle East through a Dubai office[3][29]. The company has said it wants to sell more directly to the top 50 to 100 steel-consuming customers[3].

How a rupee of sales turns into profit

A welding rod is mostly steel wire plus chemical coating, so raw material is the biggest cost. In FY26 the company kept 37.8% of each rupee of sales after raw material, which is the gross margin, and 11.6% after all running costs, which is the EBITDA margin[1]. EBITDA stands for earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, a quick measure of operating profit.

The price of steel is the swing factor. The company said steel rose 7% to 8% between the June and September 2026 quarters, and that it passed the increase on to customers a little late[3]. Hot-rolled coil, a common flat steel, reached about ₹62,000 a tonne in early September 2026, a four-year high[25]. A lag of one quarter is enough to take a point or two off the margin, which is what the Q2 numbers show.

What this means for an owner: the sales line is tied to steel volumes and steel prices, and the profit line is tied to how fast Ador can raise its own prices. The next chapter asks whether the company has any pricing power to rely on.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

Ador's strengths are old and real. It describes itself as the only top-tier welding company in India that is not part of a global group[4], it has approvals from the Nuclear Power Corporation, it serves oil and gas and shipbuilding customers, and it runs a government-recognised research centre with 38 researchers[1]. CARE Ratings rates it A+ with a stable outlook and calls its brand and dealer network strong[8]. CRISIL also rated it A+ stable before the company asked it to withdraw the rating in May 2024[9]. The balance sheet is almost free of debt: borrowings were ₹27.7 crore against equity of ₹569.6 crore in September 2026[1].

The weaknesses are about money earned per rupee of sales. Ador's EBITDA margin was 11.6% in FY26 and 10.9% in the September 2026 quarter[1]. ESAB India, the closest listed rival, made an operating profit of about 17.0% of revenue in FY26 (₹256.74 crore on ₹1,514.18 crore)[19]. A gap of five to six points is large. Possible reasons are smaller scale and a different product mix, but filings do not split margin by product, so we cannot say which.

Competition is wide. The company says welding consumables carry a couple of thousand product codes and customers in three tiers of quality approval, and that unorganised producers do not compete for its engineered customers[3]. We cannot test that claim from filings. What we can see is that the market is growing slowly: the annual report cites industry growth of over 6% a year to 2028[6]. India made 164.9 million tonnes of crude steel in 2025, up 10.4%[27]. IMARC puts the 2025 Indian consumables market at US$1,322.88 million, growing 5.76% a year[26]. Ador grew 22.8% in the latest half. That is well above the industry, but a good part is price, so the volume gain is the number to watch.

What the moat is and what it is not

Ador's moat is its approvals and its reach. A rod approved for a nuclear plant or a ship classification society cannot be swapped for a cheaper one without months of testing. It serves 31,235 pin codes through 437 dealers[1][6]. That reach is a real barrier for small rivals.

It is not a cost moat. Steel and other inputs are bought at market prices, and in the September 2026 quarter raw-material consumption rose about 44.7% year on year while revenue rose 22.6%[22]. Only a company with strong pricing power can absorb that. Ador passed the cost through, but late, and its incremental EBITDA margin on the extra ₹63.65 crore of quarterly sales was only 4.7%[22].

What this means for an owner: the business is durable but not high-margin. Value comes from steady volume and from lifting mix, not from a wide price lead. The scorecard in the next chapter tests this with five measures.

Who owns it and what the group takes out

The promoter group holds 53.65%, down from 56.90% before the Fontech merger, and none of it is pledged to lenders in any of the last 12 quarters[12][13]. Mutual funds hold 4.12%[13][14]. Related-party dealings are small. Purchases from related parties were 4.23% of all purchases and sales to them 0.50% of sales; the main counterparty is the holding company, from which Ador bought ₹30.95 crore of goods and services in FY26 and to which it paid ₹15.74 crore in dividends[6].

The Executive Chairman and the Managing Director each earned about 30 times the median employee's pay in FY26[6]. The filings show no licence or royalty fee paid to the group, only the purchases and dividends described above.

The number

We calculate three-year cash conversion at 1.36 times: for each ₹1 of profit from FY24 to FY26, the company produced ₹1.36 of operating cash flow.

How it was calculated

We added operating cash flow for FY24 (₹58 crore), FY25 (₹137.1 crore) and FY26 (₹115.9 crore). That gives ₹311.0 crore. We added profit after tax for the same years (₹86 crore, ₹60.0 crore and ₹82.0 crore). That gives ₹228.0 crore. The ratio is ₹311.0 crore divided by ₹228.0 crore.

The inputs

FY24 operating cash flow and profit come from Screener[12]; FY25 and FY26 come from the company's investor presentation[1]. The FY24 figure is as shown by Screener for the merged entity.

The range

Take out the two working-capital releases (₹48.9 crore in FY25 and ₹22.1 crore in FY26). Operating cash flow then falls to about ₹240 crore, and the ratio to 1.05. In a bear case the ₹73.0 crore first-half build is not reversed. FY27 operating cash flow would be near zero. The three-year ratio to March 2027 would then be about 1.0[1].

If the number is off

The red line is 0.25 and the compounder floor is 0.70, so a miss of this size would not change Layer 1. It would move the cash-conversion KPI from WATCH to FAIL, which would drop the Layer 2 score to 3.0 out of 5.0, the minimum for qualification.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every number in the last chapter rests on a short list of assumptions. This chapter takes the weakest one in each estimate, says what would show it wrong, and says when we will know. It also states the strongest honest bear case. The thesis was handed to us as a momentum idea built on a revenue-doubling goal. We tested that idea and it does not stand on those terms.

Weak point 1: the margin recovery that every value depends on

Our base value needs the EBITDA margin to rise from 10.9% to about 12.0% by FY28. The company calls 12% to 13% its target and has said so for several quarters[3][5]. Yet the margin was 12.4% a year ago, 11.5% in the June 2026 quarter and 10.9% in September, so the trend is the wrong way[1][22]. Steel rose 7% to 8% in the quarter, hot-rolled coil hit a four-year high, and the CEO said he does not see steel softening soon[3][25]. A pass-through that works would show up as a gross margin back at 36% or better; a pass-through that fails would leave it near 34%.

We will know from the third-quarter FY27 results. The third-quarter FY26 results were announced on 16 January 2026, so mid-January 2027 is the likely date, though the company has not announced one[16].

Weak point 2: growth that uses cash

First-half sales rose 22.8%, but operating cash flow was −₹8.5 crore after a ₹73.0 crore working-capital build[1]. Stock rose 16.6% in six months while payables fell 24.9%, and payable days were 100 in FY26, which is long and may not repeat[1][6]. If suppliers ask for faster payment, working capital would absorb more cash. The cash-conversion record over three years is strong (1.36), but two of those three years benefited from releases of working capital[1].

The test is operating cash flow for the second half of FY27 and debtor days at March 2027, against the rating agency's own trigger of an operating cycle under 60 days[8].

Weak point 3: one region, one pipe for exports

Exports are 15% to 20% of revenue and 80% to 85% of exports go to the Middle East through a single Dubai hub[3]. That is about 12% to 17% of company sales in one region. The company itself says it is monitoring developments in the region closely[1]. The firm is also still testing new markets in Europe, the US and Africa, with lower margins at first[3].

A sustained disruption in Gulf shipping or demand would hit the strongest part of the growth story first. We cannot size this from filings, and we have not tried to guess.

Weak point 4: a ₹36 crore claim with no provision

In 2023 the Bureau of Indian Standards allowed the company to settle an alleged offence over imported goods on payment of ₹36.43 crore. The company calls the amount unfair, went to the Bombay High Court, and the matter was sent back to the BIS appellate authority for fresh review. No provision has been made[6]. The detained goods themselves are worth only ₹3.36 crore, so the claim is about ten times the inventory[6].

The claim equals 1.5% of the ₹2,385 crore market value, 44% of FY26 profit and about ₹21 a share. Total contingent liabilities in the notes are ₹72.8 crore, including ₹15.76 crore of disputed income tax and ₹9.36 crore of excise[6]. The secretarial auditor notes that the BIS matter is still pending before the Magistrate Court at Bhiwandi[6]. The date of a decision is not published.

Some labels need a plain meaning. The pipeline measures how far a price sits above its nearest support in average-true-range steps. Within 0.6 steps a full-size order is allowed (FULL_SIZE). From 0.6 to 1.2 steps the order is standard size (STANDARD). From 1.2 to 1.6 steps only a smaller limit order is allowed (LIMIT_ORDER_REDUCE). Beyond 1.6 steps no order is placed at the market price (NO_BUY). Passed-conditional (PASSED_CONDITIONAL) means the business passes its tests and only the timing test is still open. A position tier sets the usual size, and TIER_4 is the smallest, at 1% to 2% of the portfolio[30].

The plan is a limit order inside ₹1,240 to ₹1,262. The top of the zone is 0.19 steps above the 20-day swing low of ₹1,248.20, which is the FULL_SIZE band (0.6 steps or less), and the bottom sits just above the rising 200-day average at ₹1,235.74.

This dossier answers one question about AGI
Chart • Screener (AGI Greenpac Limited, listed on the National Stock Exchange of India). Should a buyer put new money into the shares now, and if not, what would have to change first? The answer comes from a fixed process, not from a feeling about the company.

The process has three layers. Layer 1 is a set of hard gates. A gate is a yes-or-no test with a number attached. If any gate fails, the stock is rejected and the later layers are not run. That rule keeps two questions apart: whether a company is a good business, and whether its shares are a good purchase today.

Layer 2 is a scorecard of three to five operating measures for the type of business. Layer 3 is the entry posture. It measures how far the price sits from its nearest support level, in units of ATR, which is the average true range: the typical daily price swing, averaged over the last fourteen sessions.

Three technical terms recur. A 50-day moving average is the average closing price over the last 50 sessions, and a 200-day moving average is the same measure over 200 sessions. A base is a sideways stretch in which a stock stops falling and builds a floor. A breakout is a move above the level that has capped the price.

The market ledger gives each stock a Weinstein stage. Stage 2 is the markup phase. The ledger's rule for it is that the close sits above the 50-day average, the 50-day average sits above the 200-day average, and the 50-day average is rising. Stage 4 is the reverse, a markdown below falling averages. The tactical rule adds one more condition, that the 200-day average is also rising.

The momentum gate is a separate test in the scoring rules. It requires either an established uptrend with relative strength, or a confirmed breakout from a base. Relative strength is how far a stock's return has beaten its benchmark over a set window. Here the benchmark is the Nifty 50, the main index of large listed Indian companies. A strong business that fails this test is not bought.

2 What the company does

AGI makes glass and plastic packaging. Screener (as of 31 Mar 2026) says the company was incorporated in 1960 and manufactures and sells container glass bottles, PET bottles, and security caps and closures under its Packaging Products segment. The same source puts the packaging segment at 99 percent of the business. The plain version is that AGI sells glass bottles and the caps that seal them. A bottle maker's sales follow the volume of the goods it packs, so the company's results follow demand for those goods.

The business in three parts

Container glass is the core product. It is made by melting raw materials in a furnace. Because a furnace is costly to switch off, the plant earns its keep mainly when it is full. That is why utilisation matters. The company's January 2026 investor call, summarised by InvestyWise (as of 29 Jan 2026), said capacity utilisation was approximately 95 percent. That is high, so the existing lines are close to full and any growth needs new capacity.

Security caps and closures and PET bottles complete the range, according to Screener (as of 31 Mar 2026). The sources used do not give a revenue figure for these lines, so the report does not put one on them.

The momentum gate

The momentum gate is the most restrictive test in the scoring rules, and it is the one that decides allocation. It exists because strong numbers can still be a poor buy if the share price is not yet confirming them. Test A asks for a clean uptrend, and it needs all four conditions. Test B asks for a breakout or a return to momentum, and it needs all five. A base pivot is the price level above which a base counts as broken out. The ledger does not record that price. It measures distance from the 63-day low instead, and the report uses the ledger's measure. A retest is a return to a broken level that then holds.

Test A has four conditions. The structure condition passes: the close is above the 50-day average, which is above the 200-day average. The 200-day condition fails, because that average is falling. The relative-strength condition passes, at 14.9 percentage points over 63 sessions. The distance condition fails: the price is 3.92 ATR above the 63-day low, against a limit of 1.2 ATR. Test A therefore fails.

Test B has five conditions. The 50-day condition passes, because the average may fall by no more than 0.5 percent over 21 sessions, and it has risen 5.3 percent. The relative-strength condition passes, because it must be rising over 28 sessions, and it is 6.3 points. The pivot-distance condition fails, at 3.92 ATR against a limit of 1.0. The volume dry-up condition passes, with a ratio of 0.18 against a maximum of 0.9. The volume expansion condition fails, with a 20-session ratio of 0.33 against a minimum of 1.0. Test B fails on two conditions, pivot distance and volume expansion.

The volume ratios come from the market ledger. The report has no second volume series to check them against. Test A fails without them, on the 200-day slope and the distance condition. The gate's own note says an allocation is made only on a confirmed breakout over the base pivot, or on a retest that holds. Neither has happened.

6 Layer 3 entry posture

Layer 3 is the execution gate. It asks how far the price sits from its nearest verified support, measured in ATR, and maps that distance onto a ladder of entry bands. A support level is a price where buyers have stepped in before, and moving averages serve that role here. The ladder reads as follows. Within 0.5 ATR of support is the full-size band. Within 1.0 ATR is the standard band, and within 1.2 ATR is a reduced band that accepts only a limit order, which is an order that fills only at a set price or better. Beyond that is the no-buy band, where the rule says to wait for a pullback, a retest, or a new base.

Layer 3 is not evaluated for AGI, because Layer 1 failed. The reference reading that follows is shown so the reader can see what the posture would be if the stock passed. The nearest verified support is the 50-day average of 752.97. The close sits 17.48 rupees above it, which is 0.61 ATR. On the ladder that is the standard band. The rule would then point to accumulating on a dip. That is a reference only, because the momentum gate forbids any allocation.

8 Fatal flaws and red team

A red-team review asks what would make the decision wrong, and it puts the strongest honest case against the report's own conclusions. The four points below are the risks a careful reader should weigh. Each has a number attached, so it can be checked.

Fatal flaw one: the long trend is not yet turning up

The 200-day average is the slowest measure of trend. The close of 770.45 is above it, at 656.57. The problem is the slope. The average has fallen from 678.82 over sixty-three sessions. A falling average means older, higher prices are dropping out of the window, and newer prices have not yet replaced them. Until the 200-day average turns up, the tactical test cannot pass. The core test does not require that. The report cannot say when the average will turn, only what would show that it has.

Fatal flaw two: the target cannot be verified

The asymmetry test rests on a target the report cannot verify. The ledger holds no 63-day base high, so no measured move can be computed. No consensus forward earnings estimate exists. A bull case that relies on a price of 1,000 rupees or more would be a story, and the rules do not allow stories. The honest statement is that the price may rise, but the rules cannot say by how much, so the reward cannot be sized against the risk. This is the flaw that another data set could resolve.

Fatal flaw three: the capital programme is larger than the cash flow

Operating cash flow was 571 crore in FY26, and capital spending was 397 crore in the same year. The Hathras can plant is 1,000 crore on its own. The sources used do not say how it will be financed. If it is financed mainly by borrowing, debt-to-equity could move from 0.10 toward the 0.70 limit. On the Screener FY26 equity of 2,407 crore, the limit would be reached at about 1,685 crore of borrowings, against 241 crore today. Either a share issue or new debt would change the financial picture that currently passes.

Fatal flaw four: pricing and the margin

Revenue has grown modestly. The three-year record shows revenue rising from 2,281.5 crore in FY23 to 2,669.0 crore in FY26, a compound growth rate of about 5.4 percent a year. Profit grew about 10.4 percent a year over the same period. Growth of that size is not rapid. Sales realisation fell by about 450 rupees per tonne against Q2 FY26, according to the January 2026 call. EBITDA rose 23.1 percent in the June 2026 quarter against a year earlier, while profit fell from the March 2026 quarter. The two facts sit side by side, and the record does not explain the fall. The report does not claim a cause.

The red team's view of the rejection

The strongest honest case against the rejection is this. The business passes every financial test, and the tactical failure rests on one average. On that view the momentum gate is applied too rigidly. The reply is that the rules ask for a confirmed breakout or a retest that holds, not a forecast of one. A close above the 20-day average of 785.10 on volume would trigger a re-run of the gate. It would not, on its own, pass the gate. Test A would still need the 200-day average to stop falling and the price to sit within 1.2 ATR of its base. Test B would still need a confirmed breakout over the base pivot or a retest that holds. Until those are met, the rule stands.

The hinge claim

The claim on which the verdict most depends is the momentum gate reading on the ledger. The 200-day average is falling, the price is 3.92 ATR above its 63-day low against a limit of 1.2, and the 20-session volume ratio is 0.33 against a minimum of 1.0. The falsifier is a re-run in which the 200-day average stops falling, the price is within 1.2 ATR of the base, and a confirmed breakout or a holding retest occurs on volume of at least 1.0 times the 20-day average. A second, independent claim is that no verified target reaches the 3.0 to 1 floor of 1,198.49 rupees. Meeting that target would clear the asymmetry test, but it would not on its own clear the momentum gate.

9 Verdict, action and triggers

The verdict is REJECTED. Three Layer 1 tests fail. The momentum test fails and is the recorded gate status, FAIL_MOMENTUM. The asymmetry test fails as FAIL_ASYMMETRY. The tactical technical test fails because the 200-day average is falling. Under the rules, a Layer 1 failure rejects the stock, and Layers 2 and 3 are not evaluated. The financial tests pass, so the rejection concerns price, trend and target, not business quality. The stock is not assigned to a strategy bucket, because neither bucket is allowed while Layer 1 fails.

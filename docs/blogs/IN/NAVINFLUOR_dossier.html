Terms used in this report

A 20-day moving average is the average closing price over the last 20 trading sessions. The 50-day moving average and the 200-day moving average work the same way over 50 and 200 sessions. Traders use them to see whether a stock is trending up or down. In this report, "the 50-day average" means the 50-day moving average.

Stage 2 is the rising phase of a stock's cycle, where the price sits above a rising 50-day average and a rising 200-day average. Stage 4 is the falling phase, where the price has broken down below those averages.

A base is a sideways pause in the price after a rise, usually lasting several weeks or months. The pivot is the price level at the top of that base, where a rise is expected to start. A breakout is a close above the pivot on heavy trading, and a retest is a return to the broken pivot that holds above it. The technical gate needs Stage 2 for a Tactical buy, and a base breakout or Stage 2 for a Core buy.

The average true range (ATR) measures how far a stock typically moves in a day. This report uses the 14-day ATR, which is ₹222.92 for NAVINFLUOR. Distances in this report are counted in ATR, so a distance of 1.0 means one typical day's move. The relative strength index (RSI) is a 0 to 100 gauge of recent buying pressure. A reading above 70 is often read as overheated.

A stop loss is the price at which a position is sold to cap a loss. The Core rule places it below the lower of the recent swing low and the 50-day or 200-day average, less one ATR. The return on capital employed (ROCE) shows how much profit the business earns on the money tied up in it. Debt-to-equity compares borrowed money with the owners' money. A ratio under 0.70 is the floor in this framework.

EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, a measure of operating profit before financing and accounting charges. EPS means earnings per share, the profit attributed to each share. The price-to-earnings ratio (P/E) is the share price divided by earnings per share. Operating margin is operating profit as a share of sales. The asymmetry ratio compares the upside to a target with the downside to the stop loss. The framework requires at least 3.0 for Core names.

A consensus estimate is the average forecast of the analysts who cover a company. It is a useful check, but it is not a target this framework accepts on its own.

What the company does

Navin Fluorine is a specialty chemical maker. The company's Screener profile says it is primarily engaged in producing refrigeration gases, inorganic fluorides, specialty organofluorines and contract research and manufacturing services.[2] Its profile also gives two counts for its product range, 60+ fluorinated compounds in one place and 50+ in another, so the exact number is not settled by the sources.[2]

The company reports three business verticals. In the first quarter of FY27 (April to June 2026), the High Performance Products and refrigerant vertical, which covers R32 and HFO gases, reported revenue of ₹540 crore, up 33% year on year.[5] The company reports its revenue in these verticals and does not publish an order book in the sources reviewed, so the order book is not part of this analysis.

Scale has grown fast. Consolidated sales were ₹2,077 crore in FY2023 and ₹3,314 crore in FY2026.[1] Quarterly sales reached ₹1,045 crore in the June 2026 quarter, against ₹725 crore a year earlier.[1] The promoter group held 27.08% of the company as of June 2026.[13][14]

The share is a mid-cap on the data providers' labels.[15] Its listed name is NAVINFLUOR on the NSE and 532504 on the BSE.[1] That mix matters for the outlook: the refrigerant and fluorine chemistry sets the company's margins and its capacity plans, and those plans are what the later sections test against the framework.

How the framework decides

The framework has three layers and runs them in order. Layer 1 is a set of hard floors: return on capital, debt, cash backing for profit, technical posture, and an asymmetry test on the target. If any floor fails, the verdict is REJECTED and the later layers are not run. Layer 2 scores three to five company-specific measures. Layer 3 maps the distance from support, measured in ATR, into an entry posture.

Any candidate must also pass the momentum gate, which the code runs from the frozen price file. The gate has two routes, Test A for a stock already moving up and Test B for a stock entering a move from a base. A stock that fails both tests gets no allocation, however good the business. This run's ledger shows Stage 2, but the momentum gate reports the stock as extended, so the stock fails the technical floor.

The fatal-flaw test

The strongest case against this verdict is that the momentum rule is too strict for a business compounding this fast. Sales rose from ₹2,065 crore in FY2024 to ₹3,314 crore in FY2026, and operating profit margin rose to 33%.[1] A buyer who waited for the rule could have missed the move. That is a fair point, and the rule is a timing rule, not a verdict on the business.

The fatal flaw in the bull case is the price. The stock is extended 4.93 ATR above its base, its P/E is 54.6 against peers in the low 30s, and the sales path is uneven: FY2024 sales were flat on FY2023.[1][19] Growth from here depends on the new Dahej capacity filling up. The R32 expansion of 15,000 tonnes is due in Q3 FY27.[5] If that capacity slips, the price has the least room to absorb a miss. Two more risks sit beside it. The September-quarter result, due at the October 30, 2026 board meeting, will refresh every financial input in this report.[1] Demand visibility is not evidenced in this run, because no order book was found in the sources reviewed.

1. What the company does

In plain words: Repro prints books for publishers and now mainly sells them on online stores. This chapter says who pays, for what, and how the money is made.

Repro India Limited prints books for publishers and now mostly sells them. It is based in Mumbai, trades on the NSE as REPRO and on the BSE as 532687, and was incorporated in 1993[8][18]. The group reports one segment, value-added print solutions[11]. Management describes four layers: long-run printing of school books for large publishers, digital print, a platform business that sells publishers' titles on Amazon, Flipkart and other channels, and a consumer site called Bookscape[12][13].

Money comes in two ways. In long-run printing a publisher pays for a large batch made to its order. In the platform business Repro lists a publisher's title on a marketplace, prints a copy when a reader orders it and keeps the gap between the selling price and its cost to print and deliver. The company's own figures put digital revenue at about ₹394 crore in FY26 and the platform part at about 71 percent of digital revenue in the June 2026 quarter[13][14]. A crore is ten million rupees and a lakh is one hundred thousand; this book converts lakh to crore.

The older printing business has shrunk. In 2025 management said the national textbook body NCERT had decided to print and distribute school books itself, which cut long-run revenue by more than 65 percent from its steady state, and later by more than 75 percent[16][17]. The long-run vertical earned about ₹104 crore in FY26[14]. What is left of growth is a thin-margin business of selling other publishers' books through other companies' websites[12].

On 22 May 2026 the company assigned its leasehold rights in the shut Mahape factory plot in Navi Mumbai to STT Global Data Centres India Private Limited for ₹282 crore, all received on signing[7]. A labour dispute at that plant was settled on 13 October 2025 at a one-time cost of ₹18.46 crore[4]. The sale produced a net exceptional gain of ₹167.29 crore in the June 2026 quarter, and by August the company called itself debt free with a cash surplus of about ₹70 crore[11][13]. The promoter group, led by Repro Enterprises Private Limited, held 47.5 percent in September 2026[19].

2. Why the business is strong or weak

In plain words: the business has scale and a clean balance sheet but does not yet earn money on what it sells. This chapter gives the evidence on both sides.

Repro has real scale for its niche, a clean balance sheet since May 2026 and a sales model that fits marketplaces. It does not yet earn money on what it sells. Chapter 2B scores the five measures that matter; this chapter gives the evidence on both sides.

Where the strength is

Position and growth. Management says it is the second-largest bookseller on Amazon and the largest on Flipkart, with about 7 percent and 8 percent of those marketplaces' book sales[15]. It had 851 publishers onboarded, up 17 percent, and 43,636 digital books a day, up 3 percent, in the June 2026 quarter[13]. Revenue from operations in the June 2026 quarter was ₹139.91 crore, up 20.1 percent on ₹116.47 crore a year earlier and level with the March quarter[11][18].

Balance sheet. The latest balance sheet filed before the session is the audited one at 31 March 2026: borrowings ₹165.97 crore, lease liabilities ₹19.56 crore, equity ₹349.62 crore and debtor days 55[1][9]. The company says that after the Mahape sale it was debt free with a cash surplus of about ₹70 crore in August; no balance sheet was filed for June, so that is a company statement[13]. The September balance sheet is in the overlay table in Chapter 3.

Ownership. The promoter group's holding rose from 46.71 to 47.52 percent in the September quarter[19]. No pledge is shown: the annual report's shareholding table has a dash against pledged shares at 31 March 2026 and Trendlyne shows 0.0 percent for every quarter to September 2026[5][19].

Where the weakness is

Operating profit. Operating profit before other income was ₹3.82 crore on ₹139.91 crore in the June quarter, a 2.7 percent margin against 5.9 percent a year earlier[18]. Management now expenses all technology spending instead of capitalising it, which depresses reported margin[13]. After depreciation of ₹8.80 crore the quarter showed a loss of ₹7.26 crore before exceptional items[11][18].

Cash. In FY26 cash from operations was ₹3.26 crore against net capital spending of ₹70.60 crore; over FY24 to FY26 cash after capital spending, interest and leases was negative by ₹162.97 crore, funded by loans and a ₹77.50 crore share issue in FY24[1][9][10]. By the company's account the loans were cleared mainly from the ₹282 crore sale proceeds, not from operations; no cash flow has been filed since March, and the caveats on the Mahape cash are set out in Chapter 3[13]. Of ₹24.56 crore of technology cost in FY26, ₹20.77 crore was capitalised, a key audit matter[3].

Legal and structural. Contingent liabilities not provided for are ₹62.22 crore, mainly a ₹58.31 crore customs duty demand on software imported by the erstwhile Microsoft business in 2006 to 2009, under appeal[4]. Most platform sales run through two marketplaces, but the filings give no customer split. The NCERT change cut the print base[14][17].

Governance, auditor and related parties

The statutory auditor, M S K A & Associates LLP, gave an unmodified opinion on the FY26 statements and an unmodified conclusion on the September 2026 limited review (overlay); neither carries a going-concern paragraph[3][11]. The CARO report, the auditor's statutory checklist, is adverse on one clause: returns filed with banks differ from the books by ₹53.43 to ₹55.07 crore a quarter because related-party receivables were left out (₹63.00 crore was owed by the subsidiary Repro Books Limited at March 2026), and the auditor reports no material uncertainty on repaying liabilities[3][6]. ICRA rated the loans [ICRA]BBB+ and [ICRA]A2 at 31 March 2026; its outlook was not readable[5][32].

Related-party sales to the subsidiary were ₹44.42 crore (removed on consolidation), purchases of packing material and paper from promoter-group company Repro Enterprises were ₹2.19 crore, and rent and maintenance was ₹1.40 crore to Repro Enterprises and ₹2.55 crore to two enterprises influenced by key management; the board reports no materially significant related-party transaction[5][6]. The Dubai subsidiary is in liquidation with ₹0.50 crore provided[4]. We found no fraud, going-concern or wilful-default statement in the filings read.

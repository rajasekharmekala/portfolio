Reading the framework

Read this start section first, because every later number is judged against the rules explained here.

ATR stands for average true range, which is the typical daily price swing over the last 14 sessions, so it works as a ruler. A stop placed one ATR away leaves room for an ordinary day's movement.

EPS stands for earnings per share, which is a company's profit divided by the number of its shares. The framework multiplies a forward EPS figure by a median P/E to build an earnings anchor, when one can be verified.

Retest is a return of the price to a level it has already broken out through, to check whether that level now holds as support.

Asymmetry is the ratio of expected gain to possible loss, measured against the stop. A ratio of 3.0 means three rupees of expected gain for each rupee at risk.

Measured move is a target made by adding the height of a base to the level it breaks out from. The framework accepts it only when the base is recorded in the data.

Gross margin is the share of sales left after the direct cost of the product itself.

Price-to-earnings, or P/E, is the share price divided by profit per share.

Guidance is the company's own forecast of what it expects to achieve in the coming period.

Working capital is the cash tied up in stock, receivables and payables needed to run daily operations.

The framework has three layers, and a company must clear them in order. Layer 1 is a set of hard floors, each one pass or fail. It has a financial part, which asks whether the business earns a good return on the capital it uses, borrows modestly and turns its profit into cash. It has a technical part, which asks whether the share price chart shows that the market agrees with the business. It also has an upside-to-downside floor, which compares the expected gain with the loss to a stop, and that gain must be tied to a verified anchor rather than an estimate. If any Layer 1 gate fails, the verdict is REJECTED and Layers 2 and 3 are not evaluated. Layer 2 would then score three to five operating measures for the company, and Layer 3 would decide the timing of an entry.

The momentum gate is a test that the code runs on the same frozen price file as this dossier. It has two routes, and a stock must pass one of them. Test A asks whether the stock is already in momentum. That means the price is above its 50-day average, the 50-day average is above the 200-day average, the stock has beaten its benchmark over 63 sessions, and the price is within 1.2 average true ranges of support. Test B asks whether the stock is entering momentum. That means the 50-day average has not fallen by more than half a percent over 21 sessions, relative strength has risen over 28 sessions, the price sits near the pivot of a base, volume has dried up during the base, and volume then expands on the move. Failing both routes means no allocation, however good the business. The thresholds are defaults that the configuration file flags as needing calibration against live data. They are applied as written, and they are not presented as fitted values.

Support is measured in average true range, or ATR. ATR is the typical daily price swing over the last 14 sessions, so it works as a ruler. A stop placed one ATR away leaves room for an ordinary day's movement, and a level one ATR away from the price is therefore a sensible yardstick for how far a price sits from a place where buyers have stepped in before. The support ladder then sorts that distance into bands. Within half an ATR of support is the FULL_SIZE band. Within one ATR is the STANDARD band. Within 1.2 ATR is a limit-order-only band at reduced size. Beyond that is NO_BUY, which means wait for a pullback, a retest, or a new base.

The verdict labels follow from those rules. REJECTED means a Layer 1 gate failed, and the rule that fired is named in the verdict box. IMMEDIATE_BUY, ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP and WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT apply only to a candidate that passes all three layers, and they depend on which support band the price sits in. BROKEN STRUCTURE applies when the price has closed below its invalidation stop. Every verdict in this dossier names its rule and the number that triggered it, and it states the level that would change the answer. Asymmetry and target anchoring

Asymmetry is the ratio that compares what a stock might earn with what it could lose. The framework computes it as the target minus the current price, divided by the current price minus the stop. A ratio of 3.0 means the expected gain is three times the risk to the stop. The floor is 3.0 for the Core bucket and 3.5 for the Tactical bucket, and the formula is written in the scoring configuration file, so it does not change from one run to the next. The framework requires that the target be anchored to a method that can be checked, either forward earnings multiplied by a three-year median P/E, or a measured move from a recorded base. An arbitrary target fails the gate, and a target that cannot be verified is recorded as FAIL_ASYMMETRY (unverified).

The stop comes first, because it fixes the size of the risk. The Core stop rule places the stop below the lower of the swing low and the 50-day and 200-day averages, minus one ATR. On the ledger, the swing low is ₹1,745.00 and it is the lower of the three reference levels, so the stop is ₹1,745.00 minus ₹32.04, which is ₹1,712.96. The distance from the close of ₹1,770.70 to that stop is ₹57.74 per share, or 3.26 percent of the price. That is the risk the target has to justify.

The target is where the record runs out. A measured move needs a base top, and the ledger records the base low of ₹1,745.00 but not the top of the base. The stop-loss estimate

The stop is an estimate of where the trend would count as broken. It is not a prediction that the price will fall to it. The Core stop is computed in code from the ledger, and it moves when the swing low or the averages move. Because the stop sits one ATR below the reference level, an ordinary day's swing should not trigger it, which is the purpose of the rule. If the close falls below ₹1,745.00, the reference level itself breaks, and the Core stop of ₹1,712.96 becomes the next line the framework watches.

The number

The Core stop is ₹1,712.96, measured on the 13 October 2026 ledger.

How it was calculated

The Core rule places the stop below the lowest of the swing low and the 50-day and 200-day averages, minus one ATR. On the ledger the lowest of those is the swing low of ₹1,745.00, and one ATR is ₹32.04, so the stop is ₹1,745.00 minus ₹32.04.

The inputs

The swing low of ₹1,745.00, the 50-day average of ₹1,882.67, the 200-day average of ₹1,807.84 and the ATR of ₹32.04 are all ledger values for 13 October 2026. None of them is a company-reported figure.

The range

The stop moves only when one of its inputs moves. A change in the swing low moves the stop by the same amount in the same direction, while a larger ATR lowers the stop, because the buffer below the reference level grows.

If the number is off

If the swing low or the ATR in the ledger were wrong, the stop would be wrong by the same amount, and the risk of ₹57.74 per share in this chapter would change with it. A wrong stop would change the asymmetry ratio but not the technical verdict, which rests on the momentum gate, so the check is to recompute the stop from the next ledger.

Why the broker average is not used

Analysts publish target prices, and those averages are easy to find. The framework does not use them, because an average of analysts' views cannot be traced to a formula, and two brokers with the same earnings can publish very different targets. The framework asks instead for a number that a reader can rebuild from the record. For this company, that means either the forward earnings and the three-year median multiple, or a measured move from a recorded base. Both are absent from the record, so the target is recorded as unverified, and the search results on broker targets are listed in the references as leads only.

Fatal-flaw red team

A red team looks for the reason the verdict could be wrong or the reason the business could be a poor holding even when the rules pass. The three flaws below are the strongest case against the company on the evidence in the record. Each one names the evidence and says whether it changes the verdict. None of them is needed to reach the REJECTED verdict, because the technical floor already fails, but each matters to a reader who wants to know what could go wrong next.

Flaw one: the trend has already turned

The first flaw is that the business is good and the market has simply moved on. The 50-day average has fallen 2.48 percent over 21 sessions, the price is 5.95 percent below it, and relative strength is negative over both 63 and 28 sessions. The company is lagging its benchmark by 1.69 percentage points over 63 sessions and by 5.15 percentage points over 28 sessions, which is the kind of gap that can persist when a stock is out of favour. The evidence here comes from the ledger, not from the company. It is consistent with the momentum gate's NO_MOMENTUM result. It does not change the verdict, because it is the verdict's own cause. It does tell a reader that the wait may be long, and that a buyer who acts on the financial strength alone is betting against the trend.

Flaw two: US generics and the Halol plant

The second flaw is the company's exposure to the US, where the generics business is shrinking and a key plant has been under regulatory restriction. US formulation sales fell 9.7 percent to $427 million in the June 2026 quarter, and the decline was attributed to lenalidomide erosion and competition in certain products [4]. The US business is 26.6 percent of consolidated sales, so a continued decline is a material drag on the group [4]. The Halol plant was classified Official Action Indicated in September 2025, which means it could not ship products into the US except for medicines in shortage [6]. The record does not show whether that restriction still applies in 2026. That gap is recorded as not found after three distinct queries, so the reader should treat the plant as an open risk. This flaw could change the verdict if a new regulatory action or a clearance were confirmed in the ledger's future data, because the business case depends on US access.

Flaw three: capital raising, board changes and ownership signals

The third flaw is the set of signals from the balance sheet, the board and the shareholding. Sun Pharma is acquiring Organon; Organon's shareholders have approved the deal and it is on track to close in early 2027 [4]. In October 2026 the board approved non-convertible debentures of up to ₹10,000 crore, and a credit rating agency assigned AAA to a proposed ₹15,000 crore issue [1]. An independent director resigned effective 12 October 2026 [1]. The pledge on promoter holdings rose from 1.42 percent in March 2026 to 1.65 percent in June 2026 [2]. In September 2026 shareholders approved reclassifying the promoter group as part of the public category from the next filing, which changes how ownership is presented but does not, by itself, change who owns the shares [5]. The debt-to-equity of 0.06 is the 31 March 2026 figure. The proposed debentures and the Organon closing are not yet in that ratio, and the record does not show how much new borrowing they would add, so their effect on borrowings cannot be measured here. The AAA rating is a comfort signal from the lender's side. The size of new debt against the current base is an open item to watch, and these are governance and disclosure signals as well. The pledge figure moving above its June level would be a reason to re-underwrite, as the invalidation chapter states.

An invalidation level is a price or event that ends a view. For a rejected stock, the relevant levels are the ones that would reopen the case and the ones that would confirm the rejection. The levels are taken from the ledger and the framework's own formulas, and none of them is a forecast.

A close below ₹1,745.00, the base low and the 20-day swing low, would break the base. The Core stop of ₹1,712.96 would then be the next line to watch, and a close below it would end the Core structure and move the verdict to BROKEN STRUCTURE.

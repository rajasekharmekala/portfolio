Company, products and segment economics

This chapter explains how Alldigi earns its money before any gate is applied, because a verdict on a stock is only as good as the reader's picture of the business behind it.

Two shops under one roof

Think of Alldigi as two shops under one roof. The first shop, BPM, answers customer calls, chats and back-office queues for banks, insurers and healthcare firms, mostly in the US and the Philippines. It earned ₹442 Cr in FY26, about 73% of group revenue in Q1 FY27, and it runs on thin margins: 11.9% segment margin in Q1 FY27 against 13.8% a year earlier.[2][5] The second shop, T&D, runs managed payroll for companies: it holds each employee's pay data and processes it every month. Clients rarely switch because payroll errors are costly and migrations are slow. That stickiness is why T&D earns a 42.9% segment margin on about 27% of revenue.[4]

The mix is shifting abroad. International revenue was 68.8% of Q1 FY27 sales against 65.2% a year earlier, and management says it is deliberately exiting low-margin domestic BPM work, about a tenth of the BPM book, through FY27.[4][5] A weaker rupee helps because overseas revenue converts into more rupees while most costs are rupee costs. The price of that choice is visible: domestic BPM revenue fell 14.1% year on year to ₹20.9 Cr in Q1 FY27, and group revenue grew only 4.4%.[2]

International mix. International revenue was 68.8% of Q1 FY27 sales, up from 65.2% a year earlier, a gain of 3.6 points. The story is that the company chose to shed domestic BPM work and lean on US and Philippines clients. The rule is that the KPI passes if the share keeps rising year on year. It would fail if the share fell back below 65%, and the concern would grow if currency moves reversed. Because the share is rising, this KPI is PASS, and it matters here because it raises both margin and exposure to US policy.

Tech & Digital. T&D revenue was ₹40.6 Cr in Q1 FY27, up 11.9% on a year earlier, at a segment margin of 42.9% against 43.5%. The story is a sticky payroll franchise with 11 new logos in the quarter. The rule asks for a margin of at least 40% with growth. It would fail if the margin dropped below 40% or growth turned negative, and the Aeonox SaaS launch, still unconfirmed, would be the next test. Result: PASS, since both numbers clear the bar.

Return on capital employed asks how much operating profit the company earns on the money tied up in it. The slide from 31% in FY25 to 28% in FY26 reflects a larger asset base after fixed assets doubled from ₹102 Cr to ₹208 Cr, so the next two years need to show that the new capacity earns its keep. Result: PASS.

ROCE gate. The number is 27.9% on Screener and 24.9% on a year-end check, against a floor of 15%. The story is high returns from a light-asset services model, now diluted by a bigger asset base. Glossary

BPM
Business process management: outsourced customer-service and back-office work.
T&D
Tech & Digital: managed payroll and HR technology.
ROCE
Return on capital employed: operating profit divided by the capital tied up in the business.
CFO/PAT
Cash from operations divided by profit after tax, a test of whether profit is real cash.
ATR
Average true range: the typical daily price movement, used to scale stops and entries.
RSI
Relative strength index: a 0-to-100 speed reading of recent price moves.
Stage 4
A declining trend, with price below a falling long-term average.
Pivot
The price that would end a base pattern and start a breakout.
Measured move
A target equal to the pivot plus the depth of the base.
Asymmetry
Reward divided by risk for a trade, here with a floor of 3.5 for tactical entries.
Free float
The share of the company that is freely tradable, excluding the promoter's holding.
ACV
Annual contract value of new business won.
DSO
Days sales outstanding: how long customers take to pay.
Distribution day
A session where the price falls on heavier volume, a sign of selling by large holders.

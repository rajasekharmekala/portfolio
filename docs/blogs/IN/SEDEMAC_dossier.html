Chapter 1

SEDEMAC Mechatronics Ltd was born in 2007 inside the Society for Innovation and Entrepreneurship (SINE) at the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Bombay [2], [12]. Co-founded by Prof. Shashikanth Suryanarayanan of the Department of Mechanical Engineering alongside his students Pushkaraj Joshi, Amit Dixit, and Manish Sharma, the company took its name from a precise control-systems thesis: Separating Decision Making from Actuation [2], [12]. In conventional small-engine and motor architectures, mechanical linkages, physical position sensors, and discrete relays performed the logic of timing, cranking, and load regulation. SEDEMAC's founding premise was that embedded software algorithms running on compact electronic control units (ECUs) could strip out physical sensors and mechanical sub-assemblies while improving reliability and cost [2], [7].

The company's first decade was a study in patient product-market discovery rather than instant hypergrowth. In its early years, SEDEMAC operated as an R&D-heavy engineering lab. It was backed by Nexus Venture Partners, the India Innovation Fund, and the NRJN Family Trust [2], [6]. Instead of chasing passenger-car Tier-1 contracts dominated by global incumbents, management targeted an overlooked niche around 2012–2013: Automatic Mains Failure (AMF) and governing controllers for diesel and gas generator sets [8]. By delivering rugged, software-driven controllers tailored to Indian grid instability, SEDEMAC built a cash-generating beachhead. Over time, it captured roughly 75% of the domestic Indian genset controller market [8].

The decisive inflection arrived in 2018. SEDEMAC commercialized its proprietary Sensorless Commutation (SLC) Integrated Starter Generator (ISG) technology at scale with anchor customer TVS Motor Company [7], [13]. Traditional two-wheeler engines relied on a separate starter motor, Bendix gear, and mechanical one-way clutch to crank. Brushless conversions also required Hall-effect position sensors. SEDEMAC's patented algorithms eliminated both the starter motor and the physical rotor-position sensors. They used the existing wet magneto as both starter and alternator [7], [10], [11]. India's shift from carburetors to BS-VI Electronic Fuel Injection (EFI) in 2020 and OBD Stage II-B diagnostics accelerated adoption. As a result, SEDEMAC's revenue compounded from ₹240 Cr in FY21 to ₹1,058 Cr by FY26 [1], [4], [15].

Concrete Example 1 — Mobility ICE: Wet-Magneto Sensorless ISG + EFI on a Mass-Market 2W

Customer & Problem: TVS Motor needed BS-VI compliance, silent starting, and idle start-stop on high-volume platforms like Jupiter, Ntorq, and XL100 [7], [15]. Conventional starter motors added weight, gear noise, and mechanical wear. Placing Hall-effect sensors inside hot, oil-immersed magnetos also posed reliability risks [7].

SEDEMAC Product & Integration: SEDEMAC supplies a combined ISG + EFI ECU powered by patented Sensorless Commutation (SLC) software (US12015315B2 / US12119769B2) [7], [10], [11]. At low RPM, the ECU injects current pulses and reads intra-PWM ripples in the stator windings [11]. This infers exact rotor position without physical sensors. It cranks the engine silently, then switches to battery-charging alternator mode and manages fuel injection [7], [15]. Sold under multi-year OEM contracts, this single-box design lowers system BOM and assembly complexity [7].

Concrete Example 2 — Mobility EV: ISAAC Motor Control Unit & Logan Traction Systems

Customer & Problem: Indian electric two-wheeler and three-wheeler OEMs suffer field failures when water, dust, or heat degrades Hall-effect sensors inside traction motors [9]. They also face supply-chain exposure to Chinese rare-earth magnets [9].

SEDEMAC Product & Integration: SEDEMAC supplies its ISAAC sensorless Motor Control Units (MCUs) and Logan rare-earth-free motor platforms [9]. Running sensorless control in firmware eliminates the fragile motor-side sensor harness and cuts connector failures [9]. EV MCUs scaled from 0.3% of mobility sales in FY24 to meaningful commercial volumes in FY26–H1 FY27, hedging long-term ICE-to-EV substitution [1], [5].

Concrete Example 3 — Industrial: GC500/GC800 AMF & CPCB IV+ EFI for Backup Gensets

Customer & Problem: Hospitals, offices, and telecom sites running diesel or gas generators (from Kirloskar, Mahindra Powerol, or Generac) need fast grid-failure detection and sub-10-second cranking [8], [13]. They also require true-RMS phase protection and strict CPCB IV+ emission compliance up to 8 cylinders [8].

SEDEMAC Product & Integration: SEDEMAC supplies panel-mounted GC500/GC800 AMF controllers alongside engine-mounted, IP67-rated EFI ECUs and electronic governors [8]. The AMF unit monitors utility voltage, commands transfer switching, and logs fault telemetry. Meanwhile, the EFI ECU meters air-fuel ratios in real time to meet CPCB IV+ limits [8]. In FY26, this industrial vertical grew 55% YoY from ₹94 Cr to ₹146 Cr [4], [8].

A clear strategic thread links all three examples. SEDEMAC is extending its sensorless control stack into cordless power tools and smart FET voltage regulators [5], [9]. It is also preparing wet-magneto ISG kits and export 3-wheeler ISG ECUs for H2 FY27 and FY28 rollouts [5]. Still, investors must weigh the current revenue reality. ISG-led ICE two-wheeler electronics generate most cash flow today, while EV MCUs and new OEM programs are still ramping [1], [13].

Chapter 3

SEDEMAC's go-to-market model is overwhelmingly direct B2B OEM co-development rather than retail distribution [7], [13]. In Mobility, engineers embed directly with OEM powertrain teams 18 to 36 months before a vehicle launch to calibrate ISG, EFI, or EV MCU firmware to the specific engine or motor [7]. Once homologated under BS-VI, OBD Stage II-B, or EV safety norms, SEDEMAC ships directly to OEM assembly plants on just-in-time schedules [15]. In Industrial gensets, SEDEMAC combines direct OEM sales (to Kirloskar, Mahindra Powerol, and North American engine/genset makers like Generac) with an aftermarket and panel-builder distributor network for GC-series AMF controllers [8], [13].

On the manufacturing side, SEDEMAC is executing a major capacity expansion centered in Pune and expanding into Tamil Nadu [5]. Its primary 40,000 sq. ft. plant in Chakan, Pune (MF1) has been running above 90% utilization, prompting the build-out of MF3—a 120,000 sq. ft. facility in Chakan (~3x MF1's footprint) that commenced pilot production in Q2 FY27 and is expected to operate around 65–70% utilization post-ramp [5]. A smaller 9,000 sq. ft. unit (MF4) in Chakan is dedicated to electric machines with commercial shipments slated for Q3 FY27, while SEDEMAC also acquired ~13 acres at Shoolagiri (SIPCOT, Tamil Nadu) for MF5 to sit closer to South Indian OEMs [5].

The supply chain's Achilles' heel sits upstream in silicon and printed circuit boards (PCBs) [14]. Over 70% of SEDEMAC's raw material bill is tied to semiconductors and imported electronic components, including PCBs sourced from China and global fabs [14]. During H1 FY27, tighter global semiconductor lead times (exacerbated by AI data-center demand) and PCB inflation squeezed gross margins and forced management to shorten accounts payable days—paying chip vendors faster to lock in allocations—while building inventory buffers [5]. This directly explains why working capital expanded from ~₹135 Cr in March 2026 to ~₹258 Cr by September 2026 [5].

Unit Economics Reality Check: IP Margins vs. Working-Capital & Capex Intensity

At the gross and EBITDA line, SEDEMAC earns software-like incremental economics on standardized hardware: ₹20.51 EBITDA per ₹100 of revenue in FY26 despite 61% material costs [1], [4].

Below the EBITDA line, however, SEDEMAC behaves like a capital-intensive electronics manufacturer. Between FY25 and H1 FY27, Debtor Days stretched from 24 days (29.7 days baseline) to 46.1 days, Inventory Days hovered at 106–120 days, and FY26 capex of ₹175 Cr pushed Free Cash Flow to -₹47 Cr [1].

Chapter 4

SEDEMAC competes in two structurally distinct arenas—two- and three-wheeler powertrain electronics, and industrial generator controls—against both global Tier-1 conglomerates and specialized niche incumbents [7], [8]. In Mobility, its rivals include multinational giants such as Bosch, Denso, Continental, and Mahle, alongside domestic electrical/electronics players [13]. In Industrial gensets, its primary global benchmarks are UK-based Deep Sea Electronics (DSE) and Czech-based ComAp, which dominate advanced synchronizing and paralleling controllers globally [8], [16].

SEDEMAC's moat rests on three reinforcing pillars. First is Patented Sensorless IP. Granted US and Indian patents such as US12015315B2 and US12119769B2 protect intra-PWM current-ripple estimation and sensorless BLDC/ISG control [10], [11]. Second is Sustained R&D Intensity, with roughly 6% to 7% of annual revenue (₹44–79 Cr) invested in control-theory engineering [13]. Third is Deep OEM Switching Costs. Once a combined ISG+EFI ECU is homologated under BS-VI and OBD Stage II-B on a high-volume scooter, swapping suppliers mid-lifecycle requires costly re-validation and emissions re-certification [7], [15].

However, an unbiased moat audit must weigh two severe vulnerabilities against those strengths [6], [14]. First is extreme customer concentration. Anchor client TVS Motor accounts for roughly 75% to 81% of revenue, the top 3 customers exceed 90%, and the top 10 represent ~98% [6], [14]. That asymmetry gives TVS strong pricing leverage at contract renewals and exposes SEDEMAC to dual-sourcing risk [14]. Second is technological transition risk. Roughly 60–64%+ of revenue still relies on ICE ISG controllers [4], [13]. If 2W electrification outpaces SEDEMAC's non-TVS EV wins, or if global Tier-1s localize sensorless algorithms, moat durability will be tested [14].

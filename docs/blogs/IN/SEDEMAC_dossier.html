Before you read

This dossier reviews Sedemac Mechatronics Limited, a company listed on the National Stock Exchange under the symbol SEDEMAC, which the fund already holds. It asks two questions: is the business still sound enough to keep owning, and is the price reasonable enough to add to? The answer to the first is mostly yes, while the answer to the second is that the data needed to decide is not yet available. Chapter 1 explains the rules and terms used to reach these answers.

1. What the company does

Sedemac designs and sells small electronic control units that tell an engine, motor or generator how to start, run and stop. Think of it as the brain that sits between the battery and the motor. The company was incorporated in 2007 in Pune, and its products are built into original equipment manufacturer (OEM) platforms, so once a design is accepted by a motorcycle or scooter maker it is difficult for that maker to switch suppliers. [28]

How the three layers work

Every candidate passes three layers in order. Layer 1 is a set of hard gates: pass or fail, with no discretion. Layer 2 is a scorecard of three to five operating numbers that matter for this specific business, each marked PASS, WATCH or FAIL. Layer 3 is timing: how far the price sits above a verified support level, measured in ATR. ATR, or average true range, is the typical daily price swing over the last 14 sessions. Dividing a distance by ATR tells you how many normal days of movement separate the price from its support.

The verdict words

For a holding, the verdict is one of four words. KEEP_COMPOUNDING means the position stays at full size. ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP means add on a pullback to support. TRIM_PROFIT_BOOKING means sell 30 to 50 percent to lock in gains because the stock is extended. REPLACE_UNDERPERFORMER means the thesis or the trend has broken and the capital should move elsewhere. Each word has a rule that triggers it, and this dossier names that rule for each decision. Provisional means the verdict stands on the evidence available today and is re-read as soon as a gate that cannot yet be tested becomes testable.

Three rules that shape the numbers

The stop is set by a formula: the lower of the 20-day swing low and the 20- or 50-day average, minus half an ATR, for a Tactical name. The asymmetry ratio compares the distance to a target with the distance to the stop, and a Tactical name needs at least 3.5 to one. The target itself must be anchored, meaning built from forward earnings multiplied by a three-year median price-to-earnings ratio, or from a measured move on a documented base. A price-to-earnings ratio is the share price divided by the earnings per share, so a ratio of 20 means investors pay 20 rupees for each rupee of annual profit. A measured move is a target found by adding the height of a base to the level at which the price breaks out of it. A brokerage target that is not built this way does not count. Finally, the 60-day rule says every number should be no older than 60 days. Annual and quarterly figures often are older than that, so this dossier discloses each one in the run notes rather than hiding it.

What the stage words mean

A stock in Stage 2 is in a sustained uptrend: its price sits above its rising 50-day and 200-day averages. Stage 4 means the stock has broken down, usually below a falling long-term average. Under the rules in this dossier, a Stage 4 breakdown on heavy volume starts a replacement review, so the stage words carry real weight in the verdicts that follow.

What the company makes

The core technology is sensorless commutation. A conventional motor needs physical position sensors to know where its rotor is, and those sensors add cost, wear out and need calibration. Sedemac's software infers the rotor position from the electrical signals alone. That is why the company's integrated starter generator (ISG) units, which let a motorcycle engine shut off at traffic lights and restart cleanly, have become its main business. The company's own summary says it has sold more than 13 million control-intensive units cumulatively. [20]

The second product family is the motor control unit (MCU) used in electric two- and three-wheelers, power tools and light commercial vehicles, plus electronic fuel injection (EFI) units for generators. HSBC, which initiated coverage in July 2026, names these as the growth drivers beyond ISGs. [13]

Who pays for it

Revenue comes mainly from the mobility segment, meaning two- and three-wheeler OEMs. In the year to March 2026, mobility revenue was about ₹911 crore (9,106 million rupees) of a total of about ₹1,058 crore, and the industrial business made up the rest. [2] The rating agency ICRA notes that 77 percent of revenue comes from the single largest client. [11] That single fact drives much of the risk in this dossier, and it is discussed in chapter 2.

What this means for an owner: the business is a small number of very sticky product relationships, not a broad base of customers. Each design win can last for years, which is good for predictability, but a single OEM's volume decision can move the whole company's results.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

A business is strong when it earns high returns on the money invested in it, turns those earnings into cash, and does not need heavy borrowing to grow. It is weak when one of those three is missing. Sedemac passes the first two tests comfortably and is weak on the third, customer concentration. The rest of this chapter explains why both facts matter.

The strengths: high returns and cash that follows profit

Return on capital employed (ROCE) measures how much operating profit the company earns for every rupee tied up in the business. Sedemac's ROCE was 30.70 percent in the year to March 2026. [1] That is high for a manufacturer, and it means the company does not need much capital to grow. Other sources show a range: ValueResearch calculates 37.22 percent on its own definition, and StockFin shows 29.7 percent. [5][6] The lowest of the three still clears the 15 percent hard floor by a wide margin.

Cash conversion asks whether reported profit turns into actual cash. Over the three years to March 2026, operating cash flow totalled about ₹2,783 million against net profit of about ₹1,785 million, a ratio of 1.56. [3] A ratio above one means the company collected more cash than it reported as profit. An independent cross-check from etmoney gives about 1.79 on its own numbers, so the conclusion does not depend on which source is used. [24]

Leverage is light. Debt to equity was 0.16 at the last full year, which means the company has about 16 rupees of borrowing for every 100 rupees of shareholder capital. [1] ICRA describes gearing at 0.2 times and interest coverage of 26.5 times for FY2026. [11] Because borrowing is small relative to equity, a downturn is less likely to force the company into asset sales to repay lenders.

What this means for an owner: the business earns well and converts its earnings into cash. That is the reason to keep the holding, not the reason to add to it at today's valuation, which is the subject of chapter 3.

The weaknesses: one customer and one supply chain

Customer concentration is the single weakest number in the scorecard. ICRA states that 77 percent of revenue comes from the largest client. [11] The same filing notes that three quarters of raw materials are imported. A supplier with that profile can grow quickly while the anchor customer grows, but it cannot easily diversify away from that customer in the short term. The company's own Q1 FY27 commentary, as summarised from the investor presentation, flags semiconductor supply tightening and commodity inflation as cost pressures. [18]

The company is also expanding its capacity heavily. It started limited commercial production at a new Pune plant, MF3, on 25 September 2026. [28] Capacity expansion helps only if demand keeps arriving. If an anchor OEM slows, new plants add fixed cost without matching volume.

The working-capital line is moving the wrong way. Receivable days rose from about 14 in March 2024 to about 31 in March 2026, which means the company is waiting longer to be paid by its customers. [5] That is not a red flag on its own, because the company is growing fast, but it means cash is being tied up in receivables faster than before.

What this means for an owner: the franchise is strong, but it is concentrated. The main risk to the holding is not a balance-sheet problem. It is a single customer or a single product cycle, and the stop and KPI rules in chapter 7 are designed to catch that risk early.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

An estimate is only as good as the assumptions under it. This chapter sets out the places where the numbers in this dossier could be wrong, and what would show that.

The forecast rests on three analysts

The consensus forward earnings of ₹41.67 per share for FY2027 is an average of three analysts polled by S&P Global. [4] The average target is ₹3,558, with a low of ₹3,075 and a high of ₹4,000. [4] A consensus built on three analysts is thin, and it can move sharply when one of them revises. HSBC initiated coverage with a Buy rating and a target of ₹3,700 at the end of July, when the stock closed near ₹2,645. [13][16][17] Since then the price has risen by roughly a fifth, so the remaining upside to HSBC's target is about 15 percent, not the 37 to 40 percent that was headlined at the time. [14][16]

HSBC's case rests on capacity tripling in the second half of calendar 2026, on utilisation above 90 percent, and on research and development spending of about 9 percent of revenue. [15] Those are plausible, but they are the analyst's assumptions, not verified outcomes. Since the target is not built from the approved anchor, it cannot be used to pass the asymmetry test.

The margin story is not settled

Two sources disagree on the June-quarter growth rates. Quartr reports EBITDA up 31 percent and profit up 95 percent. [20] Arthneeti reports EBITDA up 95 percent and profit up 31 percent. [18] The two sources have swapped the two numbers, and the company's own figures settle which split is right. Revenue rose 43 percent while the EBITDA margin fell from 21.2 to 19.4 percent, so EBITDA growth is about 1.43 × 19.4 ÷ 21.2, or roughly 31 percent. That matches Quartr's split. The EBITDA margin itself, 19.4 percent against 21.2 percent a year earlier, is the figure used in the scorecard. A margin that falls for two or more consecutive quarters would be the first real evidence that the company is buying volume with price.

The single customer is the biggest unknown

The 77 percent concentration figure is the most important number in this dossier, and it comes from a rating summary rather than from the company's own segment disclosure. If the largest client's volumes fall, the company's results would fall sharply. Nothing in the cached sources suggests that is happening now, but the holding has no protection against it except the stop. The annual report, which could not be read in this run, is the primary filing that would confirm or reverse the 77 percent figure.

If the September-quarter results arrive before the 200-session test, the stop is the only protection until the review at the results.

When the gates can be tested

The 200-day average becomes computable after about 200 sessions of price history. At the current pace of trading that is around late December 2026, which the ledger can confirm. The dated trigger is that day: once the 200-day average can be computed, a close below it would fail the Tactical technical test and move the verdict to a review, while a close below ₹2,798.59 at any time moves it to REPLACE_UNDERPERFORMER. The three-year median P/E cannot exist until about 2029, so the asymmetry test cannot be passed by the method the rule names until then. A base measured move is the only alternative. The ledger does not yet record a base high, so the pivot (the price level a stock must clear before it starts a new move) and the base would have to be defined before that route can be used. Until then the asymmetry gate is FAIL.

What this means for an owner

Keep the position and do not add. Confirm the live stop, which should be the formula stop unless a tighter level was deliberately set. Review the holding at the September-quarter results, when the margin, the concentration and the trend should be read again together. If the stop is hit, the holding exits and the capital is reviewed against the shortlist challengers. If the stop is not hit and the next results show margin pressure, the review becomes a replacement decision.

How it was calculated

The formula is (target minus close) divided by (close minus stop). The close is ₹3,223.20 and the formula stop is ₹2,798.59, so the risk is ₹424.61, which is 2.67 ATR. The target is the missing piece. The approved method multiplies forward earnings per share of ₹41.67 by a three-year median price-to-earnings ratio, and that median does not exist yet for a company listed in March 2026.

  • The close, ₹3,223.20, from the ledger for 12 October 2026.
  • The swing low, ₹2,878.10, and the ATR of ₹159.03, both from the ledger.
  • The formula stop, ₹2,798.59, derived from those two ledger values.
  • Forward earnings of ₹41.67 per share for FY2027, the S&P consensus of three analysts.
  • The three-year median P/E, which is missing, and so the target base is N/A.

The range

Read the reference ratios as a range rather than a point. On the S&P average target the reward to the stop is 0.79 times the risk, and on HSBC's target it is 1.12 times. Both sit far below the 3.5 floor. In cash terms, those targets imply a reward of about ₹335 (S&P average) and ₹477 (HSBC) a share, or 10 to 15 percent above the close, against a risk of ₹424.61. The target that would be needed to reach the floor, about ₹4,709, is roughly 46 percent above the close.

If the number is off

If a verified anchor appeared, the ratio would replace N/A and the gate would be run again. If the stop were set at the prior trailing level of ₹2,950.00, the risk would shrink and the ratio would rise, so the stop must be fixed before any ratio is quoted.

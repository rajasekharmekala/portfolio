1. What the company does

SEDEMAC Mechatronics Limited is a Pune company that began in 2007 inside a research lab at the Indian Institute of Technology Bombay.[1] It makes electronic control units (ECUs). An ECU is a small computer in a box that reads signals from a motor or engine and decides, many times a second, how much current or fuel to give it. The company says the units it sells are critical to the application: if the box fails, the vehicle or the generator loses a core function.[4] It sold more than 3.9 million of these units in FY2026, which was 60% more than in FY2025, and more than 13 million since it started.[2][5]

Its best-known product is the integrated starter generator controller, or ISG ECU. In a normal scooter or motorcycle there is a separate starter motor and a separate alternator. An ISG replaces both with one electric machine, and the ECU tells that machine when to crank the engine and when to charge the battery. SEDEMAC's version uses sensorless commutation, a control method that finds the position of the spinning rotor from electrical signals instead of from a physical sensor. That matters because many motorcycles use a wet magneto, a design where the standard sensor-based ISG cannot be used, so only a sensorless design fits.[4] The company reports that about 8.4 million two- and three-wheelers built in India in FY2026 carried an ISG, up from about 7.1 million a year earlier, and that it supplied more than 80% of the extra volume.[2]

The same control know-how goes into other boxes. ISG+EFI ECUs combine the starter-generator controller with the electronic fuel injection controller in one unit, and the company crossed one million cumulative sales of that product in September 2026, 23 months after it began.[17] Motor control units (MCUs) run the motors of electric scooters and three-wheelers. Genset controllers start and govern backup generators, and SEDEMAC is the largest Indian supplier of them with an estimated 75-77% share of Indian volume.[1] A controller for the exhaust after-treatment systems of commercial vehicles, a motor controller for power tools, and electric machines made in a new plant are in development and are not yet in sales.[3][4]

Who pays, and how it earns. SEDEMAC sells straight to vehicle and equipment makers, which the industry calls original equipment manufacturers (OEMs). It is a Tier-I supplier, meaning it sits one step below the vehicle maker with no distributor in between.[1] The price is per unit and, in the company's words, hardens over years of talks with each customer.[4] The customers are not bound to buy a minimum quantity.[1] Management also says the usual car-industry pattern applies: the maker gives a broad idea for the year, a firmer idea for the quarter and firm orders for about a month, so there is no published order book to read.[4]

Unit economics: what it costs to add a rupee of sales

Management gave a rule of thumb on the May 2026 call. To add 100 rupees of yearly revenue it expects to spend about 20 to 22 rupees on plant and equipment, plus about 12 to 15 rupees on working capital, which is the money tied up in stock and unpaid bills.[4][31] It also reports a cumulative product-development spend of about 256 crore rupees, which it treats as an investment in designs that later become products.[3] The practical meaning is that growth needs cash. FY2026 revenue rose by about 400 crore rupees, and cash from operations of 128 crore rupees did not cover capital spending of about 175 crore rupees, which is ₹138.6 crore on plant plus ₹36.9 crore on product development.[2][11] The next chapters test whether that gap is a one-year effect of a fast build-out or a standing feature of the model.

Who competes with it

In ISG controllers the prospectus names Shindengen and Denso as the main other suppliers, and notes that most of their designs use position sensors while SEDEMAC's does not.[1] HSBC notes that Honda is the exception among the large makers, because it uses its own sensor-based ISG.[16] In genset controllers the named rivals are DeepSea, SmartGen, ComAp, Datakom and Fortrust.[1] The managing director told investors that nobody keeps a monopoly forever and that competitors have tried to copy the design for seven or eight years.[4]

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • ECU: electronic control unit, a small computer in a box that controls one machine function.
  • ISG: integrated starter generator, one electric machine that replaces the starter motor and the alternator.
  • EFI: electronic fuel injection, the control of fuel spray by computer instead of a carburettor.
  • MCU: motor control unit, the controller that drives an electric vehicle motor.
  • Genset: generator set, an engine plus an electricity generator, usually for backup power.
  • SOP: start of production, the date a new part first ships to a customer.
  • TTM: trailing twelve months, the latest four quarters added together.
  • TReDS: an online platform where a seller can get a buyer's bill paid early by a financier.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The business is strong where it is hard to copy and weak where it is concentrated. The strength is technical: a control method that works without a rotor-position sensor, protected by 12 granted patents, and proven in more than 10 million engine-powered two- and three-wheelers.[1][2] ICRA, the credit rating agency, writes that the company faces limited competition for its main product because the design is patented, and that this supports its margins.[6] Three of the four largest Indian two-wheeler makers now use the sensorless ISG, and about 40% of the ISG-equipped two- and three-wheelers built in FY2026 used SEDEMAC's version.[2]

The weakness is the other side of the same coin. One customer, a leading two- and three-wheeler maker named in the prospectus as TVS Motor, supplied 79.05%, 83.46%, 80.46% and 75.48% of revenue in FY2023, FY2024, FY2025 and the nine months to December 2025.[1] ICRA puts the largest customer at about 77% of FY2026 revenue and one product, the ISG family, at 67-68% of revenue in FY2025 and FY2026.[6] Management's own concentration measure, the biggest customer's share minus the next four customers' combined share, fell from 70% in FY2024 to 66% in FY2025 and 49% in FY2026.[3] The two figures use different definitions and we could not reconcile them to the last point, but both say the same thing: the mix is improving from a very concentrated start.

The evidence for the moat

Evidence one is adoption. In FY2026 the number of Indian two- and three-wheelers with an ISG rose by about 1.3 million, and the company says more than 80% of that rise came from its own product.[2] Evidence two is the ramp-up of new products with new customers. The ISG ECU for engine-powered three-wheelers became widespread after an emissions rule change on 1 April 2025, and the same product started shipping for exports in the March 2026 quarter.[4][5] Evidence three is margin. Operating profit margin rose from 19.4% in FY2025 to 21.5% in FY2026 on ICRA's calculation, and ICRA expects 18% to 20% for FY2027 and 19% to 22% over the near to medium term.[6] A supplier with no real alternative can hold its price; a supplier that is easy to replace has to give price back each year. The margin trend suggests SEDEMAC is in the first group today.

The evidence for the weaknesses

Imports are the first weakness. About 75% of raw material by value is imported, mainly semiconductors and passive electronic parts, and the prospectus lists China as a source of critical chips and circuit boards.[6][1] The managing director said on the call that nobody makes these electronics in India, so every maker faces the same exposure.[4] Margins already felt it: the cost of materials rose to 62.6% of sales in the June 2026 quarter from 59.6% in March, and the company said it expects margins to hold or improve from that level.[7][5]

Cyclicality is the second. More than 80% of revenue comes from Indian two- and three-wheelers, so a weak monsoon or a fall in two-wheeler demand reaches the company quickly.[6] The industrial segment is tied to generators, and the company says weak US hurricane seasons reduce demand for home standby generators. In the June 2026 quarter that segment fell 16%.[5][23]

Controls are the third. In the audit reports for FY2024 and FY2025, and again for FY2026, the auditors noted that the accounting software did not log every direct change to the books (an audit trail, or edit log) for parts of the ledger covering purchases, payables, revenue and receivables.[1][2] The auditors added that where the log was on, they found no case of tampering. This is a controls weakness, not a finding of misstatement, but it is the kind of gap a listed company is expected to close, and it is still open in the latest annual report.[2]

What this means for an owner

For an owner the competitive position is that of a small, technology-led supplier with a real edge in one product family and a very dependent relationship with one buyer. The edge explains why return on capital is high. The dependence explains why the stock should not be valued like a diversified auto-parts company. The next chapter turns these qualities into five measurable indicators and checks whether management did what it said it would do.

5. What could happen next

A reverse DCF (discounted cash flow) works backwards from the price. It asks what yearly profit growth, sustained for ten years and then sold at a normal multiple, would make today's market value a fair price at a stated required return. In plain terms, profit would have to grow about 14.9 times, from ₹117.8 crore to roughly ₹1,750 crore, and the price today assumes that happens.

Our variant perception

A variant perception is a view that differs from what the market price already assumes. It matters because only a difference from the price can justify holding more or less than the market does. This subsection lists where we agree and where we differ. For this holding the net result is that our below-consensus profit estimates and a thin expected return argue for holding less, not more.

Where we agree with the price. The volume growth is real and the next leg is visible: the company is adding two plants, three motorcycle launches are scheduled, an electric-scooter controller is ramping and the Q2 FY27 volume update (+37.5%) shows no break in trend.[17][5] A business with a 35% return on capital that is adding capacity is worth a premium to the auto-parts group.

Where we differ. First, near-term profit. What would make us wrong in the other direction. If ISG adoption keeps compounding in the top models (the ISG-equipped share of Indian two- and three-wheelers is only about one in three) and the new markets deliver, ₹36 and ₹50 will prove too low.[2] The honest summary is that the business is better than the Street fears on quality and slightly worse than the Street hopes on near-term profit.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate in chapter 5 rests on one assumption that is weaker than the rest. This chapter names that assumption, says what evidence would show it wrong and when that evidence arrives, then gives the strongest honest case against the idea, called the red-team bear case, and the one claim on which the whole view hinges.

Weakest assumption in the FY27 profit estimate: the margin holds near 19.8%

Management expects the margin to hold or improve from the June level, ICRA expects 18-20% for FY27 specifically (19-22% over the near to medium term), and the stock's 77.9 times FY27 consensus multiple leaves no room for a margin below that.[5][6] What would show it wrong is a September 2026 quarter with an EBITDA margin more than three points under the year-earlier quarter, or a material cost line above the 62.6% of sales seen in June. The test uses the company-reported margin in the September presentation against the company-reported figure for the September 2025 quarter, which we have not yet seen. On ICICI's calculation the year-earlier margin was 21.9%, so the line is about 19%.[7] We will know when the September quarter results are filed. The listing rules give 45 days from quarter end, so by 14 November 2026 at the latest; an aggregator shows 28 October but the company has not confirmed the board date.[29]

Weakest assumption in the FY28 profit estimate: sales grow another 30% on a base that is already large

This needs the new plants to fill and the third motorcycle launch (planned for the March 2027 quarter) to convert into volumes.[5] The company's own rule of thumb is ₹20-22 of plant spending and ₹12-15 of working capital for each extra ₹100 of revenue, so 30% growth means roughly ₹444 crore of extra revenue and ₹142-164 crore of extra investment (our arithmetic) against ICRA's operating cash estimate of ₹120-150 crore a year.[4][6] The evidence arrives with the volume update for the December quarter (likely early January 2027) and the FY27 annual results (around May 2027).

Weakest assumption in the twelve-month value: a 25% chance of a breakout to ₹4,740

The chart target is a geometric rule, not a forecast of earnings. It would be wrong if the stock fails at ₹3,650 for a second time, which would turn the 63-day shelf into a topping pattern.[34] The stock tested this level on 5 October and now sits 11.0% below it (down 5.4% over the last two weeks); a close above ₹3,650 on rising volume is the evidence we are waiting for, and a failure to get there by the December 2026 quarter results in February 2027 would cut the base probability.[24]

Red-team bear case

The strongest case against the position starts with the customer. About 77% of sales come from one vehicle maker, which is under no obligation to buy a minimum quantity and could split its orders with a second supplier or build its own control unit.[6][1] The managing director has said that nobody keeps a monopoly forever. If that customer's wallet share fell by a quarter, revenue would fall by about 19 points (our arithmetic: 77% × 25%), and with higher depreciation from the new plants the profit fall would be steeper.[4] The second line of attack is the price. At 122 times trailing earnings and 57 times FY28 consensus earnings, a growth slowdown to 20% would justify 40 to 45 times, and ₹38.7 of FY28 profit at 45 times is ₹1,740, 46% below today's close.

Third, earnings are less cash-rich than they look. ₹36.9 crore of product-development spending was capitalised in FY26, about 17% of the year's ₹222 crore EBITDA, and the auditors treat the capitalisation of development cost as a key audit matter.[11][3][2] Free cash flow was negative ₹47 crore in FY26 on Screener's definition, and operating cash flow was helped by ₹140.6 crore more owed to suppliers while ₹89.8 crore more was owed by customers.[8][2] Fourth, employee options add a small dilution, which is a rise in the share count that shrinks each share's slice. The exchanges approved listing of up to 9,75,000 more shares, about 2.2% of the share count, and options granted on 13 October carry a ₹2,700 exercise price.[8][20] Fifth, supply: about 75% of raw materials are imported and the company has stocked more because of the Iran conflict, which will show up in FY27 working capital.[6][2] Sixth, a share overhang: pre-IPO investors sold 48.8 lakh shares in blocks on 24 September, A91 still holds 7.29% and Xponentia and other early holders still hold stakes, while promoters hold 26.2% with 76.4% of those shares locked in.[19][20][9] The bear case is credible but not certain: ICRA upgraded the company on 15 September on the strength of the same numbers, so the quality of the business is not what is in doubt. The verdict for the 200-share holding is TRIM_PROFIT_BOOKING. It means sell 100 shares, half the position, and keep the other 100 with a stop. The rule needs care. The rulebook's risk-unit formula sizes new money, and we use it here only as a yardstick.[35][36]

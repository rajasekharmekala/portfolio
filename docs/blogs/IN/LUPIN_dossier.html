Lupin is a large Indian drug maker. It earns 42 percent of its sales by selling copies of brand-name medicines in the United States and 30 percent from branded medicines in India.[12] Profit has more than doubled in two years, but US launches such as Tolvaptan and Mirabegron drove much of it, and both now face new rivals.[3][1] We reject the stock for now because the chart is in a downtrend that fails the recovery test, and because our value of ₹1,989 a share, plus the ₹18 dividend, gives a 12-month return of only 1.7 percent against the 12 percent we require.[38] The view would change if the price closes back above its 20-day average and the 3 November results show US sales holding above USD 280 million a quarter[3][17].

1. What the company does

Lupin Limited is a drug maker based in Mumbai. It sells medicines in more than 100 countries. It runs 15 factories and 7 research centres, and employs more than 26,000 people.[2] Think of it as four businesses under one roof. The first sells copies of brand-name medicines in the United States. The second sells branded medicines to doctors in India. The third sells in Europe, Canada, Australia and emerging countries such as Brazil and South Africa. The fourth makes the raw chemicals that go into medicines, mostly for Lupin's own use.[5]

The United States is the largest market. In the year to March 2026 it produced ₹116,783 million of sales, or USD 1,318 million. That is 42 percent of the total and 46 percent more than a year earlier.[5] A generic drug is a copy of a brand-name drug that is sold after the brand's patent ends. Lupin is the third largest generics company in the US by prescriptions filled. It sells 149 generic products there and leads the market in 56 of them.[2] The money in generics depends on timing. The first company to file a copy application and win approval can get 180 days with few or no rivals, and that keeps prices high.[27] When more copies arrive, prices fall, often quickly.

Two first-mover products were major drivers of the recent jump in US sales. Tolvaptan is a copy of Otsuka's Jynarque kidney medicine, which had US brand sales of USD 1,467 million in 2024. Lupin launched it in May 2025 with 180 days of exclusivity.[27] Mirabegron is a copy of Astellas's bladder medicine Myrbetriq. It launched in 2024. In February 2026 Lupin settled its patent dispute with Astellas by paying USD 90.0 million, of which USD 15.0 million was expensed.[11] Both products now face more competition. Management said Apotex and Teva enter Tolvaptan from the September quarter, with a possible fourth seller. Teva's own catalog lists a Tolvaptan launch date of 27 August 2026.[3][28]

India is the second pillar. Sales were ₹81,140 million in FY26, or 30 percent of the total, and Lupin ranks eighth in the Indian drug market.[5][12] About two thirds of its prescription sales come from long-term illnesses such as diabetes and heart disease. About 11,300 field staff call on doctors.[3] Other developed markets (Europe, Canada, Australia) made up 14 percent of Q1 FY27 sales. That includes the VISUfarma eye-care business, added from the June quarter. Emerging markets made up 12 percent.[2][3] Smaller side businesses still lose money. They include Lupin Diagnostics, digital health, consumer health and contract manufacturing. The finance chief put the drag at 1 to 1.5 points of profit margin.[3]

Here is how a rupee of sales turns into profit. In Q1 FY27, the April to June 2026 quarter, the company kept 74.6 percent of every sales rupee after paying for materials.[2] That share is the gross margin, which is sales less the cost of materials, as a share of sales. Staff cost took 16.8 percent of sales. Manufacturing and other costs took 28.5 percent.[2] EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) is operating profit before those four items. Excluding forex gains and other income, it was 30.0 percent of sales.[4] The three lines above leave 29.3 percent. The other 0.7 points are other operating income of ₹597 million, which the company reports separately.[2] The operating margin is operating profit as a share of sales. Research spending was 7.4 percent of sales and sits inside the cost lines above.[2] Customers are US drug wholesalers and pharmacy chains, Indian patients who buy on a doctor's advice, and government buyers who place tenders. Lupin has many small payers, not one large one.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Generic drug - a copy of a brand-name medicine, sold after the brand's patent ends, usually at a much lower price.
  • First-to-file (FTF) - the first copy-maker to apply for approval. It can win 180 days of exclusive sales, which is where most generic profit is made.
  • ANDA - abbreviated new drug application, the form a generics company files with the US drug regulator (FDA) to sell a copy. Lupin has filed 429 and won 350.[2]
  • Form 483 - a list of problems FDA inspectors say they saw at a plant. It is a warning, not yet a penalty.
  • VAI and OAI - FDA inspection results. Voluntary action indicated (VAI) means minor issues. Official action indicated (OAI) means serious issues that can hold back new approvals from that plant.
  • Warning letter - a formal FDA letter that can block new product approvals from a plant until the problems are fixed.
  • IPM - the Indian pharmaceutical market. Growth of 1.1 times IPM means Lupin grew 1.1 times as fast as the market.
  • 505(b)(2) - a US approval route for a modified version of an existing drug. It can earn a period without direct generic rivals.
  • Reverse DCF - a calculation that works backwards from today's price to find the growth rate the price assumes over ten years.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

Lupin's strength is that it is a large, low-debt business with real depth in a few hard-to-copy product types. Simple tablets attract many copy-makers, and prices collapse. Inhalers and injectable drugs need special devices and factories that few rivals have. Lupin says respiratory products are a little over 20 percent of its existing US business. They include generic Spiriva (tiotropium), Albuterol and Xopenex.[3] It holds 50 first-to-file filings, 21 of them exclusive. It has filed 429 ANDAs in total.[2] Brokers put its market share at 35 percent in generic Spiriva, 19 percent in Mirabegron and 16 percent in Albuterol.[20]

The second strength is the balance sheet. ICRA, the rating agency, recorded debt of 0.8 times operating profit for FY26. It recorded gearing of 0.3 times and interest cover of 20.3 times. Cash and liquid investments were ₹9,230 crore at 31 March 2026.[12] By 30 June 2026 the company held net cash of ₹2,831 crore, after paying for VISUfarma.[3] The third strength is spread. Outside the US, organic sales grew more than 20 percent in Q1 FY27. Double-digit growth is guided for Europe and other markets.[3]

US price erosion and the products that matter

US generic prices fall as copies multiply. In FY26 Lupin's existing US products lost a low single-digit percentage to price. Higher volume more than covered it.[6] The bigger issue is concentration. Management expects US sales of USD 1.1 billion to USD 1.2 billion in FY27, against USD 1,318 million in FY26.[3][5] It also expects USD 250 million to USD 280 million a quarter from the September quarter, against USD 366 million in Q1.[3] That is a fall of 24 to 32 percent from the June quarter level. Management named two reasons. New sellers are entering Tolvaptan, and Mirabegron will have a full quarter of extra competition.[3] Other US generics makers show the same pressure. Dr Reddy's North America revenue fell 23 percent in its September quarter, and its profit fell 35.6 percent.[29][30] A sector preview expected US sales to drop 15 percent for the companies it covers.[31]

We checked the other products that readers often ask about. Albuterol share has settled at 16 percent. An analyst on the call said it had been 19 to 20 percent, and management called the market stable.[3] Generic Spiriva share is about 38 percent by management's count, and it is not expected to move.[3] A Revlimid-type lenalidomide product and a Brovana (arformoterol) generic do not appear in the filings we cached. We do not size them, and we treat their contribution as unverified. Lupin Diagnostics is a loss-making side business. Management expects it to reach break-even next year.[3]

FDA plant compliance

The US drug regulator inspects every plant that supplies the US. A poor result can stop new approvals. In FY26 the regulator made nine inspections of Lupin plants. They produced eight Form 483 lists with 29 observations in total. There were no new warning letters, but one plant, Pithampur Unit II, was classified OAI.[11] ICRA adds that two older warning letters, at Tarapur and Mandideep, remain unresolved. It notes that Lupin has no material US pipeline from these plants.[12] In the June 2026 quarter Ankleshwar and Somerset received establishment inspection reports (EIR) with the minor VAI result. Management said its Pithampur Unit II response was submitted and remediation was under way.[3][4] On 24 October 2026 Lupin disclosed that the Goa plant closed an inspection on 23 October with one Form 483 observation. The inspection ran from 19 to 23 October. That is down from seven observations in November 2025.[13][16][14] The company gave no detail on the observation, and the final FDA classification comes later.[15]

Legal exposures and what weakens the case

Lupin is part of the industry-wide US antitrust cases over generic drug pricing. In FY26 it booked a provision of ₹5,816.9 million. In April 2026 it settled one matter for ₹2,654.1 million. The rest of the provision is held for similar cases.[11] A US Department of Justice investigation that began with subpoenas in January 2017 is still open.[11] ICRA calls these event risks. A new settlement could land at any time.[12]

The weakest point in the business case is that the FY26 profit level was set by products with a limited life. Motilal Oswal expects earnings to stay flat from FY26 to FY28. Citi expects the existing US business to shrink to about USD 975 million by FY29. Dolat Capital cut its rating to Reduce.[19][21][20] So a company with a 30 percent return on capital and net cash can still fail our test. The quality is real. The question is what the earnings will be next year and the year after.

Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Walk-the-talk means checking past guidance, which is a company's own published forecast, against what happened. Management has a mixed record, and the pattern is useful. On profit margin it under-promises. In August 2025 it guided FY26 EBITDA margin to 24 to 25 percent.[8] At the May 2026 call the CFO said the year came in at 29.7 percent. He cited a 27 to 28 percent guide, itself above the August figure.[6] On tax it has been too low twice. FY26 guidance of about 19 percent became an actual 22.1 percent. FY27 guidance of 25 to 26 percent in May became 27 to 28 percent in August.[8][6][3] On regulatory repair, it has said Pithampur Unit II remediation is on track on two calls in a row. The site still carries an OAI label.[6][3][12]

The central point for our case is the FY27 guide of high single-digit sales growth and about 25 percent margin. It is not new. On the August 2025 call an analyst cited an FY27 margin guide of 24 to 25 percent, and the CFO answered that sales would grow at least in single digits while the company played for continuous margin expansion. That was before anyone could see how large Tolvaptan and Mirabegron would be.[8] The FY27 guide was repeated in May and August 2026.[6][3] Q1 FY27 came in at 30.0 percent margin. So a 25 percent year implies about 23 percent for the remaining three quarters, by our arithmetic. That is either cautious guidance that will be beaten, as in FY26, or a fair reading of a US business whose quarterly sales are about to drop by a quarter or more. Our estimates take management at its word. We treat a beat as the bull case.

How it was calculated

We took management's guidance of high single-digit sales growth and an EBITDA margin of about 25 percent. We applied it to the full year.

We then worked down to profit using depreciation, interest, other income and a 27.5 percent tax rate. The result is ₹91.5 on 45.73 crore shares.[3][24]

The inputs

Sales are ₹30,500 crore, which is 9 percent above FY26 revenue of ₹27,958 crore.[1] That equals Q1 sales of ₹8,277 crore plus about ₹7,400 crore for each of the next three quarters.

It fits US sales of USD 250 to 280 million a quarter.[6][3] EBITDA is 25.0 percent, or ₹7,625 crore.

Q1 delivered ₹2,464 crore, so the other quarters average about 23 percent.[3] Depreciation is about ₹1,850 crore, against ₹453 crore in Q1.

Interest is ₹440 crore, against ₹110 crore in Q1. Other income is ₹450 crore.[1]

Tax is 27.5 percent, the middle of the 27 to 28 percent guide.[3] The arithmetic is ours and the inputs are cited.

The range

In the bull case management beats its margin guide again, as in FY26. EPS then reaches about ₹103, which is the Motilal Oswal estimate.

That estimate assumes US sales above the company's own range.[19][6] In the bear case, Tolvaptan and Mirabegron prices fall faster.

Margin ends the year near 22 percent, the low end of ICRA's range. EPS is then about ₹77.[12]

If the number is off

Each point of EBITDA margin on ₹30,500 crore of sales is worth about ₹305 crore before tax. That is roughly ₹4.8 of EPS after tax.

If the September quarter shows a margin above 26 percent, our FY27 figure rises toward ₹100. At 22 percent it falls toward ₹77, and the case weakens further.

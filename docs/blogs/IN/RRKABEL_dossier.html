1. What the company does

R R Kabel Limited was set up in 1995, is run from Mumbai, and trades on the NSE and the BSE.[1] It has two businesses. Wires and cables made 91% of sales in the June 2026 quarter, and fast-moving electrical goods, called FMEG, made the other 9%.[5] Wires and cables covers house wires, factory wires, power cables and special cables such as fire-safe and solar cables.[1] FMEG covers ceiling fans, lights, switches and small appliances under the same brand.[11]

Think of it as a metal converter with a well-known name. The company buys copper and aluminium and draws them into wire. It makes its own insulation in its plants, then sells through about 7,200 distributors and dealers to more than 2,15,000 retailers.[11] A loyalty programme with more than 6,65,000 electricians matters because the electrician at the counter often picks the brand.[11] In India a wire is a branded consumer product, so a good name earns a higher price. Abroad it is closer to a commodity. On the July 2026 call an analyst recalled margins of 11% to 12% for domestic wire and 5% for export wire, and management agreed that domestic wire earns more.[4]

Because the main input is metal, sales move with the copper price. Management says it passes metal price changes to customers within about 15 days.[25] Export orders are covered by matching purchases, so the company does not carry the price risk on them.[11] On the July call management said London metal prices would be about 30% above last year in the September quarter, or about 40% after the dollar's move. That would lift its own selling prices by about 25%.[4] This price effect is why June-quarter sales grew 54% while volume grew only 17%.[4] Copper stood at 6.61 US dollars a pound on 27 October 2026, up 28.5% in a year and just under the 6.85 record of September.[26]

Two things follow. First, a percentage margin shrinks when metal rises, because the same rupee profit is divided by a bigger sales figure. So rupees of profit per unit and volume are the better tests. Second, the company holds metal in stock, so a copper fall can hurt through the stock it owns and through customers who pause buying. Management admits volume dipped in late June 2026 when metal prices softened.[4]

Where the sales come from: places and products

About 71% of June-quarter sales were in India and 29% were exports.[5] Over the past year the export share has moved between 24% and 29%.[7][5] Europe and the Middle East are the biggest export markets. The United States is a small hope that depends on tariff rules management calls unclear.[4] North and West India give about 65% of domestic sales. The South is growing faster than plan, and the East is still thin.[4] The company holds 42 international approvals and calls itself India's largest exporter of wires and cables, a claim that comes from the company.[11]

The product mix leans to wires, at roughly 70% wires and 30% cables.[25] Management wants cables to grow faster, because they need precise machines and approvals that rivals find hard to copy.[4] Domestic cables earn only 6% to 7% today, and management expects 10% to 11% once volume and supply improve.[4] This shift is the heart of Project RRise, the three-year plan of May 2025. It aims for 18% yearly growth in wires and cables, 25% in FMEG and 2.5 times the EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, a measure of operating profit) in three years.[10]

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The business is strong in three ways and exposed in three ways. The first strength is reach. About 7,200 distributors and dealers, over 2,15,000 retailers and 6,65,000 enrolled electricians give it a route to market that a newcomer needs years to copy.[11] The second is speed against the field. In the June 2026 quarter wires and cables grew 57%, against 38% at Polycab and 27% at Havells, according to a BusinessLine report.[20] Management puts industry growth at 10% to 12%, so its 17% volume growth is a real gain in share.[4] The third is a clean balance sheet. Borrowings were ₹337 crore against equity and reserves of ₹2,575 crore at March 2026, so growth does not depend on banks.[1] The shares trade at a price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 40.6, which means investors pay ₹40.6 for each ₹1 of yearly profit.[1]

The evidence is not all one way. Polycab grew September-quarter sales 52.8% and profit 43%, so the whole industry is surging. R R Kabel's edge is the gap, not the level.[27] A credit agency agrees that the business is stronger. On 16 September 2026 India Ratings raised R R Kabel's ₹1,304 crore of long-term bank limits to IND AA/Stable from IND AA-/Stable. It gave the same grade to ₹3,000 crore of new limits.[13] The auditors, B S R & Co. LLP, gave an unmodified review of the June 2026 results. The chairman says the FY26 audit had no qualification or adverse remark.[6][12]

The weak points are scale, margin and a rival with deep pockets. R R Kabel is worth ₹24,842 crore, while Polycab is worth ₹1,16,300 crore.[1][27] Its FY26 operating margin (operating profit as a share of sales) of 8% compares with 14% at Polycab and 10% to 11% at Finolex and KEI.[1][27][28][31] Wires are a larger share of its sales than at Polycab, and wires are where a new entrant can arrive fastest. Exports are 29% of sales and depend on shipping routes. Middle East shipments were disrupted from March 2026 and normalised only in May and June.[5][8][4] FMEG is also too small. It lost ₹33.0 crore in FY26 on ₹958.6 crore of sales, and sales grew 3.1% against a 25% yearly goal.[7][10]

Where the company is strong, and how we know

Brand and channel protect the wire business. Management says 2% to 3% of the market moves each year from unbranded makers to branded ones.[4] That drift favours a firm with 2,15,000 retailers and an electrician loyalty programme. The company makes its own insulation compounds and aluminium rods, so it controls quality at the recipe stage.[11] It holds 42 international approvals that open doors in Europe and the Middle East.[11] Approvals matter more for cables than for wires. Cable volume grew more than 25% in June, against about 12% for wires.[4]

Execution shows in the numbers. The wires-and-cables segment margin rose from 7.4% in FY25 to 8.9% in FY26 and to 9.9% in June 2026.[7][5] That is a gain of 232 basis points on a year earlier. A basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point, so 232 basis points is 2.32 points. Management says no inventory gain sits inside the June figure.[4] It targets a 10.5% segment margin by FY28, which needs only about 0.6 points more.[4]

Where the company is weak or exposed

The sharpest new risk is competition. In early September 2026 the Aditya Birla group launched Ultravolt, a wires and cables business inside UltraTech Cement. It has ₹1,800 crore of committed spending. It aims to be the second-largest wire maker by capacity at launch and a top-two player within five years.[17] Citi estimated that the spending could support about ₹12,500 crore of sales and a 7% to 8% market share. It named R R Kabel and Havells as more exposed than Polycab, because they sell more wires.[18]

JM Financial cut R R Kabel to Reduce with a ₹2,750 target. It estimated that UltraTech and Diamond Power together could take 11% to 12% of the market by FY29. After allowing for new entrants, it sees the domestic market growing 10.5% a year over FY26 to FY29, against 15% without them.[19] Others disagree. CLSA rates the stock Outperform with a ₹2,850 target and expects 18% yearly revenue growth and 24% yearly profit growth to FY30.[20] Management says it saw nothing special in the June quarter, though it adds that its dealer count may fall as it deepens the network.[4]

Two smaller issues need watching. The first is governance. Promoter holding fell from 61.38% to 59.33% in the quarter to September 2026, and we found no filing or news that explains it.[1][3] We treat the reason as not published. A ratings website cut its view to Hold on 19 October 2026, citing the valuation and the fall in promoter holding.[24] Pledged promoter shares were 0.0% in each of the last twelve quarters, so the sale is not a forced one.[3] The second is tax. Officials appealed a ruling that had dropped their demand to recover ₹36.9 crore of GST refunds claimed from 2018 to 2021. The company expects no material impact.[16]

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate in chapter 5 rests on one weakest assumption. This chapter names each one, says what evidence would show it is wrong, and says when we will know. The weakest assumptions are an 8.5% margin for the rest of FY27, a 9.2% margin in FY28, a multiple of 37 times and a 30% weight on the bear case. We then build the strongest honest bear case against our own call. Finally we name the one claim the call depends on most.

Weakest assumption 1: the 9.9% margin is real and lasting, not a copper effect

Management says June's margin holds no inventory gain, and an analyst on the call asked exactly that question.[4] We can check part of the claim. Gross profit was 18.5% of sales in June 2026, against 18.2% a year earlier and 18.6% in March. So the metal spread did not widen, and the margin gain came from spreading costs over more sales.[5] That supports management. The weak point is what comes next. JM Financial expects weaker revenue growth if copper stays high from the end of the December quarter.[19] Management itself says volume dipped in late June when metal prices softened.[4]

The evidence that would prove us wrong is a September-quarter segment margin below 9.0% or a group EBITDA margin below 8.5%, the level in our FY27 estimate. Either would mean June's level did not repeat. These are the same lines that block the order in chapter 7. We learn that on 28 October 2026, when the results are filed. Management explains them on the 29 October call.[15]

Weakest assumption 2: Ultravolt and other entrants will not change prices or share within two years

Our base case lets volume grow 16% to 18% and lets margin ease only 0.4 point below plan. That assumes the new rival takes little in two years. Against that, UltraTech has committed ₹1,800 crore and aims to be a top-two wire maker. It will use an existing network of building-material dealers.[17] Citi sees R R Kabel as more exposed than Polycab because of its wire weighting.[18] Management says dealer numbers may shrink as it focuses on depth.[4]

The evidence that would show we are wrong is a quarter in which volume growth falls below the 10% to 12% industry rate that management cites. Price cuts or higher dealer incentives would also count. We will know from the December-quarter results, expected around early February 2027. The date is not announced.

Weakest assumption 3: profit will turn back into cash

FY26 cash from operations was ₹295 crore on ₹492 crore of profit. Free cash flow was ₹7 crore and inventory rose 75%.[1][5] Management blames goods in transit and higher metal prices. It also says letters of credit lifted payable days to 63.[8] If metal stays high, more cash is tied up in stock. If metal falls, the stock loses value. A second year of 0.6 conversion would turn the Layer 1 cash score from a pass with a caveat into a concern.

The first evidence is the half-year cash flow statement filed with the 28 October results. We want positive cash from operations and inventory days at or below 81.[33]

Weakest assumption 4: the promoter selling is harmless

Promoter holding fell from 61.38% to 59.33% in one quarter. Foreign investors raised their stake from 10.68% to 12.51%, and mutual funds cut theirs from 11.30% to 9.70%.[1][3] Pledge is zero, so there is no sign of financial stress. But we found no filing or news that explains the sale.[3] A transfer between family holdings is harmless. A sale at a discount by an owner who knows the next two quarters is not. Our rule is simple. If the December-quarter holding falls again, or a block-deal filing shows a sale below market price, we withdraw the second tranche of the order.

Weakest assumption 5: a stop 1.3 ATR away can protect us

The stop at ₹2,052.97 is 6.45% below the close. But the stock moves 4.9% on a typical day and fell 19.4% in two weeks. A results gap can carry the price through the stop, so the loss could be 10% to 18% and not 6.45%.[32] We cannot remove that risk. We can only size for it. At the ₹1,798 bear value, which is 18.06% below the close, a 3.5% position would lose about 63 basis points of net worth. A 2.0% position loses about 36 and the 1.0% first tranche about 18. Layer 3 is the last of our three tests. Layer 1 asks whether the business is safe, Layer 2 whether the operations are healthy, and Layer 3 when to buy and how much. Size comes from a risk budget. TIER_4 allows a 35-basis-point budget and a weight of 1% to 2%. The budget is stop-based. It is the money lost if the price falls to the stop.

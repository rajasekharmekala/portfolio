Chapter 1

To understand India Glycols Limited (INDIAGLYCO) today, you must look past the surface shock of its latest quarterly income statement. In Q1 FY27 (ended June 2026) and Q2 FY27 (ended September 2026), consolidated sales printed ₹357 Cr and ₹362 Cr. That looked like a 66% and 67% year-on-year collapse from ₹1,040 Cr in Q1 FY26 and ₹1,092 Cr in Q2 FY26 [1][21]. A superficial screen would flag a business in freefall. In reality, those numbers mark the birth of a completely different listed perimeter. Effective September 1, 2026 (with a retroactive Appointed Date of April 1, 2026), India Glycols executed an NCLT-sanctioned three-way demerger. It spun out its Potable Spirits & Bio-Fuels arm (IGL Spirits Limited) and its Nutraceuticals & Bio-Polymers arm (Ennature Bio Pharma Limited) [2][3][8].

The company's roots stretch back to November 19, 1983, when the Bhartia family incorporated U.P. Glycols Limited in Kashipur, Uttarakhand (then Uttar Pradesh) [18]. Operating via Vam Organic Chemicals Ltd, founder-promoter Late M.L. Bhartia and U.S. Bhartia sited the plant in the sugarcane belt. Renamed India Glycols Limited on September 4, 1986, and publicly capitalized in July 1988, the company began commercial production at Kashipur in April 1989 [18]. Its founding technological bet was audacious. Instead of cracking naphtha or natural gas to make Ethylene Oxide (EO) and Monoethylene Glycol (MEG), IGL fermented sugarcane molasses into ethanol. It then dehydrated that ethanol into ethylene and oxidized it into 100% bio-based MEG and EO [10][18].

That green-chemistry moat came with a structural curse that defined IGL's first three decades. Its input costs were tied to Indian agricultural cycles, specifically sugarcane molasses and later grain. By contrast, its selling prices for commodity bio-MEG followed global petrochemical MEG benchmarks driven by crude oil and Middle Eastern or Chinese naphtha crackers [17]. When crude oil collapsed in FY15–FY16, global MEG prices plunged. Margins collapsed just as debt from multi-year expansions peaked at ₹2,264 Cr in March 2015 [1][17]. IGL posted consecutive net losses of -₹89 Cr in FY15 and -₹57 Cr in FY16, while promoter share encumbrances rose past 80% during the stress cycle [1][17].

Rather than staying trapped in commodity glycols, management (led by CMD U.S. Bhartia and CEO Rupark Sarswat) engineered a multi-stage pivot across 2017–2026 [1][11][12]. First, IGL scaled its high-margin UP and Uttarakhand Potable Spirits franchise and built grain-based distilleries for India's Ethanol Blending Programme (EBP) [13]. Second, on June 30, 2021, IGL carved out its Bio-EO specialty chemicals unit at Kashipur via a ₹652.22 Cr slump sale into a 51:49 joint venture with Switzerland's Clariant International (Clariant IGL Specialty Chemicals Pvt Ltd). That transaction booked a ₹239.88 Cr exceptional gain in FY22, pushing FY22 Other Income to ₹260 Cr and Net Profit to ₹340 Cr, and prepaid high-cost NBFC term loans [1][11]. Third, in November 2025, IGL completed a ₹466.99 Cr preferential share allotment (5.10 million shares, backed by promoter entity Kashipur Holdings Ltd and non-promoter institutions) to cut debt ahead of the demerger [15].

Chapter 2

Under the NCLT-approved Composite Scheme of Arrangement (Appointed Date April 1, 2026; Effective Date September 1, 2026), two engines moved into standalone companies [2][3][8]. In Q1 FY27 and Q2 FY27, continuing consolidated revenue stood at ₹357.08 Cr and ₹362.42 Cr. Those quarters produced ₹39 Cr of quarterly operating profit (11% OPM) and ₹33 Cr / ₹24 Cr in quarterly net profit [1][4][21].

Roughly 67% of FY26 revenue (Potable Spirits, Bio-Fuels, and Ennature) exited the listed perimeter on April 1, 2026. What remains is a ~₹1,440 Cr/year continuing-operations chemical and gases platform.

Segment 1 — Bio-based Specialties & Performance Chemicals (BSPC, ~96% of continuing revenue): IGL converts renewable ethanol from molasses and grain at Kashipur and Gorakhpur into bio-ethylene and bio-ethylene oxide (Bio-EO). Downstream, it produces Bio-MEG, Bio-DEG, Bio-TEG, Glycol Ethers, and Glycol Ether Acetates [10][19]. Concrete End-to-End Customer Example: Consider a European automotive coatings formulator or global beverage packaging brand seeking to cut Scope 3 carbon emissions. Rather than buying fossil-route butyl glycol acetate made from Middle Eastern naphtha, it contracts IGL Kashipur for bio-based glycol ether acetates certified for 100% renewable carbon content. IGL dehydrates bio-ethanol into ethylene, reacts it into Bio-EO, synthesizes the glycol ether, and esterifies it into acetate. It then ships ISO-tank containers via Mundra or Nhava Sheva to Rotterdam. That route captures a green specialty spread while avoiding the low-margin traded chemicals business management exited in FY25–FY26 [6][10][19].

Segment 2 — Clariant IGL Specialty Chemicals JV (49% Equity Associate): Clariant formed this JV in June 2021 by investing ₹587.74 Cr for 51% of IGL's Bio-EO derivatives unit inside Kashipur [11]. Concrete End-to-End Customer Example: When a multinational personal-care or home-care FMCG company formulates a 'bio-based' shampoo or detergent in India or Southeast Asia, it needs renewable ethoxylates and surfactants. IGL pipes Bio-EO directly across the fence at Kashipur under a long-term supply contract. The Clariant IGL JV ethoxylates that feedstock using Clariant's global formulations and sells to the FMCG buyer [10][11]. IGL earns a steady feedstock margin on Bio-EO sales, plus 49% of the JV's net profit and substantial cash dividends [10][11].

Segment 3 — Industrial Gases & IGL-SteriGas (~3%–4% of continuing revenue, ~₹46–47 Cr/yr): IGL operates an Air Separation Unit (ASU) and liquid CO2 recovery plants at Kashipur. The unit sells 67,000+ MT annually of liquid oxygen (including medical grade), liquid nitrogen, argon, food-grade LCO2, and IGL-SteriGas sterilization mixtures [12]. Concrete End-to-End Customer Example: A surgical-disposables and catheter manufacturer in NCR or Baddi cannot autoclave heat-sensitive plastic syringes. It buys cylinders of IGL-SteriGas, which IGL blends in-house from captive EO and LCO2, to sterilize medical kits at low temperature. That gives IGL a sticky, regulatory-cleared industrial niche with >20% EBITDA margins [12].

Context on Demerged Verticals (What Exited in 2026): Through March 2026, historical financials in Chapter 6 also included three exited businesses. First, Potable Spirits held market-leading country liquor brands like Bunty Bubbly in UP and Uttarakhand, IMFL brands like Macintosh, and long-term bottling contracts for Bacardi and Amrut Distilleries at Kashipur [13]. Second, Bio-Fuels supplied grain and molasses ethanol to oil marketing companies under India's E20 mandate [9]. Third, Ennature Biopharma produced plant-based APIs like Thiocolchicoside and nicotine derivatives, lutein nutraceuticals, and guar gum biopolymers [14]. None of these three verticals remain inside listed INDIAGLYCO's P&L after April 1, 2026 [2][3][8].

Chapter 3

India Glycols' physical supply chain rests on two integrated complexes: a 300-acre flagship site in Kashipur (Uttarakhand) and a complementary facility in Gorakhpur (Uttar Pradesh) [10][12][19]. Locating in the Terai sugarcane and Gangetic grain belt solves the first-mile logistics problem of bio-chemistry. Rather than railing bulky molasses or grain across India, IGL ferments regional agricultural feedstocks into ethanol on-site. It then dehydrates that ethanol into bio-ethylene and oxidizes it in air-separation-backed reactors into Bio-Ethylene Oxide (Bio-EO) [10][12].

From that central Bio-EO node, distribution splits into three distinct channels. First is pipeline-integrated JV offtake, where IGL pipes Bio-EO across the fence at Kashipur to Clariant IGL Specialty Chemicals Pvt Ltd under long-term contracts [10][11]. Second is domestic and export ISO-tank and drum logistics for Bio-MEG, Bio-DEG, Bio-TEG, and higher-margin Glycol Ethers & Acetates. Those shipments serve European, Asian, and Indian coatings, brake-fluid, textile, and packaging buyers [6][10][19]. Third is regional cryogenic tanker and cylinder distribution for Industrial Gases and IGL-SteriGas across North Indian hospitals and medical-device clusters [12].

Chapter 4

Fossil fuels drive most of the global Monoethylene Glycol (MEG) and Ethylene Oxide (EO) market. Giant complexes—Reliance in India, SABIC in the Middle East, and coal or naphtha crackers in China and the US—control supply [17][19]. India Glycols cannot beat these giants on pure scale. Instead, its moat rests on three clear pillars. First is bio-route process skill, turning molasses and grain ethanol into bio-MEG and bio-EO since 1989 [18]. Second is the Clariant IGL 51:49 JV, which pairs IGL's green Bio-EO with Clariant's formulas and global FMCG buyers [10][11]. Third is downstream focus on bio-based glycol ethers, acetates, and captive IGL-SteriGas medical blends [10][12].

IGL's green thesis faced a big test when Western drink makers moved from bio-PET (PlantBottle) to recycled PET (r-PET). That shift cut IGL's reliance on a single buyer, Coca-Cola [6][19][22]. Management adapted by adding niche buyers in Europe and Asia across coatings, personal care, and specialty polymers. Those clients pay a green premium to cut Scope 3 emissions, while IGL exited low-margin traded goods [6][10][19].

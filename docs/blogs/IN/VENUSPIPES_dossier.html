1. What the company does

Venus Pipes & Tubes Limited makes stainless steel pipes and tubes at one site at Dhaneti, on the Bhuj–Bhachau highway in Kutch, Gujarat. It started manufacturing in 2015, and the site covers 2,66,282 square metres[1]. Kandla port is 55 km away and Mundra port is 75 km away, which lowers the cost of bringing steel in and sending pipe out[1]. The shares trade on the NSE as VENUSPIPES[25]. The company has no subsidiaries or associates, so its audited accounts are the only set, and there is no separate consolidated basis to argue about[1].

There are two product families. Seamless pipe starts as a solid billet that is pierced into a hollow shell and drawn out, so it has no weld seam and takes higher pressure. Welded pipe is made by rolling flat strip and joining the edge. Installed capacity is 20,400 tonnes a year of seamless and 27,600 tonnes of welded, about 48,000 tonnes in all, and the company says its hollow-pipe plant now covers all of its seamless output[6]. In the June 2026 quarter seamless made 55 percent of sales, welded 39 percent and other items 6 percent[12]. Seamless ran at about 85 to 90 percent of capacity and welded at about 60 percent[5].

The buyers are industrial. Management names power, oil and gas, chemical, pharmaceutical and engineering customers, plus new demand from data centres for cooling pipework[5]. No customer gave more than 10 percent of revenue in FY26[1]. Domestic customers paid ₹766.80 crore in FY26 and customers abroad paid ₹400.05 crore, which is 34.3 percent of revenue[1]. In August 2025 management said 15 to 20 percent of its export orders came from the United States[9]. In August 2026 it said more than 40 percent of its ₹600 crore order book was for export[5].

The company earns money by turning stainless steel into pipe and charging for the conversion. In FY26 the cost of materials was ₹778.36 crore against revenue of ₹1,166.85 crore, and operating profit before depreciation and interest was ₹190.6 crore[1][6]. Metal prices move with nickel and steel markets, so rupee sales rise and fall with metal even when tonnes do not. To cope, the company holds ₹386.89 crore of inventory, equal to 121 days of sales[1][12]. That stock is one reason cash lags profit, a point chapters 3 and 6 return to.

Three additions matter for the next two years. A fittings line, a 4,200-tonne seamless extension and a 6,000-tonne hollow-pipe plant began commercial production on 26 May 2026[25]. A pipe-spooling plant, which prefabricates pipe, fittings and flanges into ready-to-fit pieces, began part production on 20 October 2026, ahead of the December plan[24]. A data-centre customer has given a ₹185 crore letter of intent for cooling spools, but the company said on 20 October that conversion into a firm order was still to come[23].

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The business has four real strengths. The first is integration: the company pierces its own steel into hollow shells, and its 6,000-tonne hollow-pipe plant began on 26 May 2026[25]. The second is breadth: many sizes, grades and approved products across seamless and welded pipe, and fittings since May 2026[6]. The third is location near two ports[1]. The fourth is a spread of buyers, with no customer above 10 percent of revenue and exports at 34.3 percent of FY26 sales[1].

Lenders have noticed. Crisil raised its outlook to positive in June 2026 and enlarged the rated limit to ₹600 crore[28]. Domestic sales grew 24 percent in FY26 and 31 percent in the June 2026 quarter[1][12]. A sector peer is not doing as well: Ratnamani Metals & Tubes reported standalone sales of ₹740.78 crore for the June 2026 quarter against ₹1,062.40 crore a year earlier, with a standalone margin of 14.2 percent against 21.3 percent (a secondary source, an AI-assisted summary)[41].

The weaknesses are as real. Profit has not kept up with sales. FY26 sales grew 21.7 percent but profit after tax grew 9.8 percent, to ₹101.96 crore[1][2]. Profit has sat between ₹18.0 crore and ₹27.5 crore a quarter for nine quarters[12][14][15][16]. Interest of ₹40.84 crore in FY26 was 1.7 times depreciation of ₹23.58 crore, a sign that growth was paid for with borrowed money[1]. The operating margin slipped from 18.2 percent in FY24 to 17.5 percent in FY25 and 16.3 percent in FY26, below the 'high teens' promised for the new lines in February 2024[17][26][6].

The third weakness is how the pipe is paid for. Of ₹297.33 crore of trade payables at March 2026, ₹180.62 crore was financed by banks, through letters of credit (₹60.32 crore) and purchase-invoice discounting (₹120.30 crore), due in 90 to 120 days[1]. These lines sit in trade payables, not borrowings, so the balance sheet looks lighter than the credit actually used. Chapter 3 shows the effect on each ratio.

The fourth is trade policy. Management said the United States raised its steel tariff on the company's goods from 25 to 50 percent in June 2025[9]. The EU replaced its steel safeguard on 1 July 2026 with quotas about 47 percent lower and a 50 percent duty above quota. Management said on the August call that the EU quota was cut 25 percent; the regulation text gives about 47 percent, and we use the regulation[36][37]. A US Commerce investigation into welded stainless pipe from India began on 4 August 2026, with a first subsidy ruling now due on 14 December 2026; the notice does not name Venus, and whether it will be a named respondent is not published[39][40]. Exports fell 22.0 percent in the March 2026 quarter and 9.1 percent in the June 2026 quarter, which management blamed on Middle East conflict and freight[6][5][12].

India's own duties protect the home market: the annual report calls the continued anti-dumping duties on stainless steel pipes 'a crucial safeguard'[1], and the trade remedies body issued final findings on the duty on Chinese seamless stainless tubes on 24 June 2026[38]. Management said in August 2026 that competition is 'not as such like undercutting'[5]. On balance the position is good in India, uneven abroad and held back by thin cash generation.

1. What the company does

Trent is a Tata Group company that sells clothes, shoes, beauty products and home goods through its own stores, and food and groceries through a part-owned chain. Tata Sons Private Limited holds 32.45 percent of the shares and Tata Investment Corporation Limited another 4.28 percent[15]. The three names that matter are Westside, Zudio and Star[1]. Westside stores are large, mostly 20,000 to 30,000 square feet, and stock clothing, footwear, accessories, cosmetics and home items. Zudio is a value-fashion format with stores of about 10,000 to 12,000 square feet[1]. Star sells food, groceries and daily needs, mainly through Trent Hypermarket Private Limited, a 50:50 joint venture with Tesco PLC of the UK[13].

The customer pays at the till, and Trent keeps the difference between what it pays for goods and the shelf price. The company prefers to run its own stores rather than franchise them, owns the stock at all times and sells mostly its own brands[2]. That choice means Trent keeps the whole retail gap, which is called the gross margin: the share of each sale left after paying for the goods. In the year to March 2026 it was 44.5 percent[13]. Beauty, innerwear and footwear now bring in over 21 percent of revenue, and Westside's online sales bring in over 6 percent of Westside's revenue[1].

At 30 June 2026 the group ran 301 Westside stores, 982 Zudio stores (seven of them in the UAE) and 29 stores under other lifestyle names. That is 1,312 stores in 330 cities, with over 18 million square feet of floor space[1]. By 30 September the count was 1,342, and Zudio had passed 1,000 stores[4]. Zudio now holds about 60 percent of the selling space, at 10.80 of 18.04 million square feet[2]. Standalone revenue was ₹5,666 crore in the June quarter (up 19 percent) and ₹5,788 crore in the September quarter (up 23 percent)[3][4].

Growth comes from adding stores, and each one needs capital. Management says it has launched about 250 new stores a year[6], and in FY26 it opened 272 and closed or merged 22[5]. A new Zudio store needs ₹3 to 4 crore, counting fit-out, deposits and stock[13]. Over 80 percent of the new Zudio stores in the June quarter opened in smaller cities (Tier II and III) or in new growth areas on the edge of larger cities[2]. Those stores sell less per square foot: FY26 yearly sales per square foot were ₹15,318 in metro and Tier I cities, ₹13,390 in Tier II and ₹12,504 elsewhere[13]. That gap is the first clue to the main question of this report: whether each new store earns as much as the last.

Two accounting points explain later tables. First, reported figures follow Ind AS 116, the lease standard, which turns store rent into depreciation and interest and shows the rent promise as a debt. Management's operating profit figures remove that effect[2], while the cash flow statement and Screener's borrowings line include it[13][16]. Second, consolidated revenue leaves out Star, because the joint venture is counted by the equity method and only Trent's share of its profit appears[5]. Trent Hypermarket's own total income was ₹2,773.55 crore in FY26 against ₹2,699.33 crore, a rise of about 3 percent[13]. Each table names its basis.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • RSI is the relative strength index, a 0-to-100 gauge of how fast a price has moved; below 30 is called oversold and above 70 overbought, and neither is a forecast. ATR is the average true range, the typical daily price swing over 14 sessions; this report measures distances in ATRs.
  • EPS is earnings per share, profit divided by share count. ROCE is return on capital employed, the yearly operating profit earned on the money tied up in the business. EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, a profit measure before financing and write-offs.
  • FCF stands for free cash flow, the cash left after paying for the stores, equipment and other spending a business needs to keep running and growing. Cash flow from operations is the cash a business earns from its trading before spending on stores and equipment, and capital expenditure is that spending.
  • Operating margin is operating profit as a share of sales. Working capital is the money tied up in stock and bills owed to the company, less the bills the company owes. Debt-to-equity is borrowings divided by shareholders' money, and net debt is borrowings less cash and easily sold investments.
  • Price-to-earnings is the share price divided by earnings per share, a count of years of current profit that the price pays for. Guidance is the forecast a company gives of its own results, and a consensus estimate is the average forecast of the analysts who follow the stock.
  • A base is a flat stretch of trading after a fall, where a stock builds a floor; a pivot is the price at the top of that flat stretch, and a breakout is a close above it on rising volume. A retest is a fall back to a price that held before, to see whether it holds again.
  • Stage 2 is a rising trend, with the price above a rising 50-day average and 200-day average, while Stage 4 is a falling trend, with the price below falling averages.
  • A limit order is an order to buy at a stated price or lower and never higher, and a stop loss is a price at which we would sell to cut a loss; it is an exit instruction and not a cap on the loss. The entry zone is the price range in which a limit order may sit.
  • Asymmetry and reward-to-risk mean the same thing here: the gain to the central target divided by the loss to the stop. The Core Alpha floor is 2.0 to 1. 2. Why the business is strong or weak

Trent's strength is a store model that earns a lot on little money, run at a scale few Indian fashion chains match. Consolidated revenue rose from ₹4,498 crore in FY22 to ₹20,074 crore in FY26, a compound growth of 45 percent a year[13]. On standalone figures, the operating margin (the company's own measure, which leaves out the lease standard's effect) climbed from 7.8 percent in FY22 to 11.9 percent in FY26, and the gross margin settled near 44.5 percent after falling from 51.0 percent in FY22[13]. The group says its brands are mostly its own, its stores are company-run and its sourcing is mainly in India[2][6]. Debt is small: ICRA and CARE rate its listed debentures AA+ with a stable outlook[13].

The weakness is that growth is now bought with floor space. Floor space rose 31 percent in FY26, from 13.5 to 17.7 million square feet, while standalone revenue rose 18 percent[13][5]. Like-for-like growth, which compares only stores open for over 18 months[2], was in the double digits in FY25, in the low single digits in the second quarter of FY26, marginally negative in the third quarter (management blamed a shifted festive period), and in the low single digits for FY26 and for the June 2026 quarter[14][8][7][6][2]. Management does not publish a number, so the exact rate is not known. What the words allow is a rate well below the 17 to 23 percent revenue growth, so most of the growth is new stores.

Management said it saw no material shift in competitive intensity[2], but a secondary report of a Citi note says revenue per square foot fell 12.2 percent in the June quarter and names competition and cannibalisation as concerns[26]. Management also flagged rising input costs and supplier labour shortages in some regions, tied to the Middle East conflict[6][2].

Evidence for the moat: own brands, company-run stores and low capital per store

A moat is something that protects profit from competitors, as a moat protects a castle. Trent's case rests on three facts. Its brands are its own, so it controls design, price and the full retail gap[2]. Its stores are company-operated, so it controls the customer experience and keeps the stock on its own books[2]. And its stores are cheap to open: ₹3 to 4 crore per new Zudio store[13]. At the FY26 sales rates of ₹12,504 to ₹15,318 per square foot, an 11,000 square foot store would sell about ₹14 to ₹17 crore a year (our illustration; the company does not publish per-store sales)[13][29]. That is roughly four to five times the capital put in, using the ₹3.5 crore midpoint.

The moat is real but narrow in one respect. Zudio's edge is price, and price edges are easy to copy, so the proof has to be in the numbers. They are mixed. The gross margin has held, which suggests no price war[13], but sales per square foot are falling. Either new stores mature slowly, as management says[2], or they take sales from old ones. Public data cannot tell the two apart, and that is the largest unknown in this report.

The weak spots: Star, the Inditex partnership and the pace of change

Star, the grocery arm, is a drag. The joint venture lost ₹127.92 crore before exceptional items in FY26, against ₹91.96 crore in FY25, while its total income grew about 3 percent[13]. The company itself said Star's store expansion has been slower than it expected[5]. Star had 86 stores in 12 cities at June 2026, up from 84 at March[1][5]. Star's June-quarter revenue, including Zudio counters inside its stores, was ₹885 crore, up from ₹814 crore a year before, or about 9 percent[2]. Because the venture is counted by the equity method, its loss reaches Trent's profit only as a share, which is small, but it absorbs management time and capital.

The annual report also says that Trent's associations with Inditex depend on permissions from the majority partner, and that Trent views them mainly as a financial investment[13]. Trent's share of profit from associates and joint ventures fell from ₹86.50 crore in FY25 to ₹3.90 crore in FY26[13]. That is small against ₹1,721 crore of profit. The core fashion engine is strong and the side ventures are subscale, so the price turns on whether sales density stabilises. Chapter 2B next scores the operating measures that settle that question.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate in chapter 5 rests on a few assumptions. This chapter names the weakest of each, the evidence that would show it wrong, and when we will know. It then gives the strongest honest bear case and the single claim the whole case hangs on. The ratios quoted are at the ₹2,667 zone top, which the scoring rules use for a limit-order setup, and at the ₹2,727.50 close, which the key-figures strip prints.

Weakest assumption of the central value: that sales per square foot stops falling

The central case needs revenue to grow about 20 percent a year on new stores, with margins holding. The evidence for it is real. Management says newer markets take two to three years to mature[2]. September-quarter revenue rose 23 percent, and the operating margin rose to 12.9 percent in June[4][1]. Over 80 percent of the new Zudio stores are in smaller or peripheral markets, where maturity takes time[2]. The evidence against is also real. Like-for-like growth has been in the low single digits or worse for four quarters running[8][7][6][2]. Revenue per square foot fell about 10 percent[29]. A secondary report of a Citi note names cannibalisation of nearby stores as a worry[26].

What would show the assumption wrong? Like-for-like growth in the September-quarter results described as flat or negative would do it. So would revenue per average square foot down more than 5 percent again in the second half, or an operating margin for the half under 11.5 percent. We will know at the September-quarter results. They are due on 29 October 2026 according to a Screener summary of the board notice, which we have not confirmed on the company's own site[16]. The next check is the December-quarter update, whose date is not announced.

Weakest assumption of the earnings: that margins hold

The street's FY28 EPS of ₹50.40 assumes margins stay near today's level while revenue grows about 20 percent a year. Management has flagged rising costs for some inputs[2]. Take a one-point fall in the margin on the street's FY28 revenue of ₹29,080 crore[27]. That removes about ₹291 crore before tax. At an assumed 25 percent tax rate (our assumption), it is ₹4.09 a share after tax. EPS falls to about ₹46.31, down 8.1 percent. At 62 times that is ₹2,871, and the ratio at the zone top is 1.03. So the margin alone could take the setup under the floor.

What would show the assumption wrong? An operating EBIT margin below the 11.5 percent of the year-earlier quarter would do it, as would a full-year FY27 margin below 11.9 percent. The September-quarter results will show the first. The March 2027 results will show the second.

Weakest assumption of the value: that the multiple stops at 62 times

The central value uses 62 times trailing standalone profit, just under the one-year low of 62.9 times. The multiple has already fallen from a 29-month median of 102.4 to 70.0 now[29]. What would show the assumption wrong? Trent's trailing standalone multiple falling below 60 times without a fall in earnings would do it. We will know daily, since the price is public. A refreshed ratio and a written multiple bridge are requirements of triggers 1 and 2.

Weakest assumption of the cash view: that growth can keep being funded from within

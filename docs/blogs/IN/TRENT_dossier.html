Before you read

Trent Limited is the Tata group retailer behind Westside, Zudio and the Star supermarkets. It is not a holding in this portfolio. This report concludes REJECTED for now. The business is excellent, but the price already assumes ten years of 26 percent yearly profit growth, only three of five operating tests pass, and the chart offers about 1.1 rupees of reward for each rupee risked against a minimum of 3. We would run the tests again if the 29 October 2026 results show same-store growth of 5 percent or more or sales per square foot flat or rising, or if the price falls to about 2,730 rupees.

1. What the company does

Trent sells clothes, shoes, beauty products and household items under its own brands, almost all of it through stores it runs itself. Two chains carry the group. Westside has 301 stores with 7.15 million square feet of selling space, roughly 24,000 square feet a store. Zudio has 982 stores with 10.80 million square feet, roughly 11,000 square feet a store (our calculation)[2]. On 30 June 2026 the fashion network was 1,312 stores in 330 cities and 18.04 million square feet[1][2]. By 30 September 2026 it was 1,342 stores, and Zudio had passed 1,000 stores[3]. Star, the supermarket chain, had 86 stores at June 2026. Most of them are run by a 50:50 joint venture with Tesco, and Trent shows only its share of that venture's profit, so the venture's sales are not in group revenue[1][4].

The model is simple to state. Trent designs the goods, has them made by outside factories, owns the stock and sells it in its own stores. Management says it prefers company-operated stores and ownership of the stock at all times[2]. The rating agency CARE notes that Trent buys stock outright instead of on a sale-or-return basis, which puts the risk of unsold fashion on Trent[9]. Nearly all Westside and Zudio sales are of Trent's own brands[10]. Newer lines such as beauty, innerwear and footwear were over 21 percent of revenue in the June 2026 quarter, and Westside's online sales were over 6 percent of Westside revenue[2].

Here is where a rupee of sales goes. In FY26, standalone revenue was Rs 19,701.41 crore. Goods cost 56.12 percent of net sales[6]. Staff cost Rs 1,222.04 crore, about 6.2 percent of revenue. Store occupancy, including rent, cost Rs 1,652.69 crore, about 8.4 percent. Other running costs were Rs 2,242.10 crore, about 11.4 percent, and depreciation was Rs 1,315.69 crore, about 6.7 percent[8]. The percentages of revenue are our calculation from the cited lines. What is left is a pre-tax profit of Rs 2,537.33 crore, about 12.9 percent of revenue[6]. Rent in FY25 was about Rs 179 per square foot per month, down 2 percent on the year[17]. Management says new stores in smaller towns take two to three years to mature[2].

The customers are households paying at the till, so there is no customer concentration. Trade receivables were only Rs 51.14 crore against Rs 20,074 crore of sales[6]. Trent says it has served over 120 million customers and works with over 440 supplier partners[6]. Demand is tied to the economy. Retail sales in India grew 10 percent in August 2026, and apparel also grew 10 percent[22]. Retail inflation was 5.54 percent in September 2026 and clothing and footwear prices rose 3.93 percent[23]. Since September 2025, clothing priced up to Rs 2,500 a piece carries 5 percent GST, which helps price-led chains such as Zudio[24]. Festival timing moves sales between quarters, so management says the full year is a fairer guide than any one quarter[1]. The weak spots are fashion misses, input-cost spikes and a consumer who stops spending. The rating agency named the 2026 West Asia conflict as a source of near-term demand pressure[9].

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • relative strength index (RSI) — a meter from 0 to 100 of how fast the price has been rising. Above 70 is called overheated and below 30 is called oversold.
  • average true range (ATR) — the average daily price swing in rupees over 14 days. It is used to measure how far the price sits from support.
  • earnings per share (EPS) — the company's profit divided by the number of shares.
  • return on capital employed (ROCE) — operating profit divided by the money tied up in the business. It shows how well each rupee of capital earns.
  • earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA) — profit before financing and accounting charges. Trent also reports an "operating" version that removes the effect of lease accounting.
  • free cash flow (FCF) — cash from operations minus spending on stores and equipment. For Trent we also deduct lease payments, because stores are largely rented.
  • same-store sales growth (SSSG) — how much sales grew in stores open long enough to compare. Trent calls it like-for-like growth and counts stores open over 18 months.
  • 20-day moving average — the average closing price over the last 20 trading days. The 50-day moving average and 200-day moving average are the same idea over longer spans, and the price tends to find support near them.
  • Stage 2 — the Weinstein name for a confirmed uptrend. Stage 4 is a confirmed downtrend. Stage 3 is a topping or pullback phase.
  • base — a sideways stretch of trading that a stock builds before a larger move. The pivot is the top of that stretch, and a breakout is a close above it on strong volume. A retest is a pullback to that level.
  • stop loss — the price at which the idea is wrong and the position is closed. The entry zone is the range of prices where buying is planned, and a limit order is an order that fills only at your price or better.
  • asymmetry — the size of the possible gain compared with the possible loss. reward-to-risk is the same ratio: (target minus price) divided by (price minus stop).
  • gross margin is the share of sales left after paying for the goods sold. operating margin is the share left after all running costs. net margin is the share left after every cost, tax included.
  • debt-to-equity — total borrowings divided by shareholders' money. net debt is borrowings minus cash. For Trent we show both with and without lease liabilities.
  • price-to-earnings — the price paid for each rupee of yearly profit. consensus estimate is the average of analysts' forecasts for a number.
  • cash flow from operations — the cash the business itself generates, before spending on stores and before financing. capital expenditure is money spent on stores, warehouses and equipment. working capital is the money tied up in stock and bills, net of what is owed to suppliers.
  • guidance — the company's own forecast for a coming period. dilution is the fall in each existing share's slice of the company when new shares are issued. A measured move is a target found by adding the height of a base to its pivot.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

Trent's edge is a tight loop between design, sourcing, pricing and stores. The cost of goods was 56.12 percent of net sales in FY26 against 56.42 percent in FY25, and management says it has held prices steady and does not use them as a short-term demand lever[5][6]. Staff cost was Rs 1,222.04 crore on a standalone basis against Rs 1,200.90 crore the year before, while revenue grew 18 percent, which management credits to automation such as radio-tag stock tracking[6]. The result shows up in the operating EBIT margin, which excludes lease accounting: 12.9 percent in the June 2026 quarter against 11.5 percent a year earlier[1]. In the June quarter of each year it was 7.8 percent in 2023, then 10.8, 11.5 and 12.9 percent in the three years since[2]. Management also says the current cycle of manpower-cost savings has largely been realised, so this source of margin gain is close to used up[5].

Scale and backing add to the edge. CARE rates Trent AA+ with a stable outlook, citing Tata Sons parentage, a leading position in fashion retail and gearing that fell to 0.36 times[9]. Trent repaid its Rs 500 crore bond on 29 May 2026, so its remaining debt is mostly lease liabilities[8]. In the September 2026 quarter, sales grew 23 percent, while the Retailers Association of India reported apparel retail growth of 10 percent for August. That gap suggests Trent is taking share, although the periods do not match, a quarter against one month[3][22].

Where the edge is thin

Same-store growth has faded. Like-for-like growth for the fashion portfolio was in the low single digits in FY26 and in the June 2026 quarter, and it was marginally negative in the December 2025 quarter[2][5][19]. Motilal Oswal wrote that it had already slowed to a mid single-digit rate by the March 2025 quarter, from double digits in FY25[17]. Growth now comes mainly from adding stores. Selling space grew 32.6 percent in a year, from 13.6 to 18.04 million square feet, while revenue grew 18.5 percent[1][2][21]. Our calculation in chapter 3 puts sales per square foot down about 10.7 percent. Management says newer small-town stores are not comparable with older ones[2], which may be true, but it is also the claim a bear would expect to hear.

The Star supermarket business is a drag. The Chairman wrote in April 2026 that the Star expansion programme had been slower than expected[4]. CARE lists continued losses in Booker India and the Star joint venture as a weakness[9]. The auditor treats the value of Trent's investments in Trent Hypermarket (Rs 841.29 crore) and Booker India (Rs 348.52 crore) as a key audit matter, which means a write-down is possible if those units do not improve[6]. Competition is also steady. CARE names Shoppers Stop, Lifestyle International, Aditya Birla Fashion and online sellers such as Amazon, Flipkart and Myntra, and says entry barriers in branded garments are low[9].

What the evidence says about durability

The edge looks real but not yet proven at today's scale. The strong signs are stable margins, falling stock days (inventory days were 74 in FY26 against 77 in FY25 on Screener's calculation) and a cash conversion that improved each year[16]. The weak signs are the fading same-store trend, the lower sales per square foot, a rights issue of about Rs 2,500 crore proposed for store upgrades, new brands, supply-chain expansion and Star[5][9][31], and a Chairman who reportedly told shareholders his June 2026 meeting would be his last as chairman[18]. Auditor rotation is also due: Deloitte retires after the 2027 annual meeting and B S R and Co. is proposed in its place[8]. That follows from Deloitte completing its second five-year term, so it is routine, but a new auditor of a Rs 20,000 crore company deserves a look at the first set of notes.

Taken together, we rate the moat as strong in design speed, price discipline and reach, and unproven in same-store productivity. The Tata group connection keeps funding cheap, and the stock has 14.57 percent mutual fund ownership and 6.47 percent insurer ownership[12]. Chapter 2B turns this view into five measured tests and checks what management promised against what it delivered.

2B. Company specifics, operating KPIs, and management walk-the-talk

A retailer lives or dies by a few numbers. We picked five that govern Trent: same-store growth, selling-space growth, sales per square foot, operating margin and cash conversion. The standard asks for 4 of 5 to pass when five tests are used. Each test has its rule written before the score. The same-store and per-square-foot tests are the two that separate a growing retailer from a merely expanding one, and they are the two that do not pass today.

Three tests pass (2, 4 and 5), one is a watch and one fails, so the score is 3 of 5, below the 4 of 5 needed. The failing test is the one that matters most to the current price. The September 2026 estimate for sales per square foot (about down 2.4 percent) is our calculation and not a published figure, because Trent has not yet reported its September selling space. If that figure comes in near our estimate, test 3 moves from FAIL to WATCH, and the score is still 3 of 5. Layer 2 reaches 4 of 5 if same-store growth rises to 5 percent or more, which lifts test 1 to PASS while tests 2, 4 and 5 keep passing. It also reaches 4 of 5 if revenue per square foot becomes flat or rising, which lifts test 3 to PASS. Either one is enough, and both together would make 5 of 5. A fall of 0 to 5 percent in revenue per square foot is only a WATCH and adds no pass.

Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Management's record against its own words is mostly good, and it is candid about the miss. We checked six promises made one to two years ago. Four were delivered, one was missed and one is mixed. The miss is Star. The FY25 annual report said the Star subsidiary 'envisages a phased expansion of Star stores in select regions' with 78 stores at March 2025[7]. The Chairman wrote in April 2026 that the expansion programme 'has been slower' than management expected. Star ended FY26 with 84 stores after 12 openings and 6 closures, and 86 stores in June 2026[1][4].

The mixed item is funding. In August 2026 management still wrote that it 'substantially' invests 'internal accruals'[2]. Yet in April 2026 the board approved in principle raising about Rs 2,500 crore, through a rights issue or other equity routes[5]. Growth is still mostly self-funded, because the bond was repaid in May 2026[8], but a request for new equity is a change in tone that holders should note.

"A promise earns its price only when the plant turns it into cash."

You and I should begin not with the share chart but with a place: a single industrial site at SIPCOT, Cuddalore, where TANFAC Industries has made fluorochemicals since commercial production began in 1985. Its chain starts with imported fluorspar and domestic sulphur, turns sulphur into acid and oleum, then uses that chemistry to make hydrofluoric acid and downstream products. This is an integrated operating platform, not a collection of unrelated businesses. The integration can support cost and supply control; it also concentrates execution, weather, equipment and logistics risk in one site.

CHAPTER II

One Fluorine Platform, Several Ways to Earn

"A product mix is a map of choices; a profit mix is the map investors still have to earn."
— Institutional Research Desk maxim

To understand TANFAC, begin with a distinction that prevents analytical confusion. In its FY26 audited accounts, the company reports one statutory segment, spelled “Fluro-Chemicals in India.” It does not publish separate segment profits or profit by product. The Q2 FY27 presentation gives a management view of H1 revenue streams: 75% Hydrofluoric Acid (HF) and 25% Sulphuric Acid & Other Value Added Products. This is not a second statutory segment or a margin table. You can say HF-linked streams supplied three quarters of reported H1 revenue; you cannot say they supplied three quarters of profit or assign a share to any downstream product. Keep the labels honest and conclusions proportionate.

The portfolio makes more sense as a chain than a list. TANFAC’s Cuddalore site brings together fluorspar and sulphur, makes sulphuric acid and oleum in-house, then produces HF and downstream grades. This is a shared industrial foundation, not proof that every tonne of input becomes a particular product. Captive power and waste-heat recovery meet a large share of site energy needs; fluorspar comes from suppliers including South Africa, Vietnam, Thailand and Kenya, while sulphur is sourced domestically. Chennai port supports imported inputs and exports. These facts explain the operating context, not product-level cost advantages or margins. Fluorspar supplies the fluorine-bearing feedstock, while sulphur sits upstream of acid production; that sequence helps explain why acid and HF families belong in one operating story without collapsing them into one product.

Start with sulphuric acid and oleum, the platform’s upstream side. TANFAC lists 100,000 tonnes per annum (TPA) of combined capacity at FY26, with 101% utilisation. The company offers 98% sulphuric acid; oleum is a more concentrated, fuming form of sulphuric acid used where processes require a stronger sulphonating or dehydrating agent. Imagine a customer buying a bulk acid input to specification. The acid and oleum operation is also part of the wider fluorspar-to-HF chain. For this family, therefore, you have both external-product economics and an enabling role in the site's chemistry, but the public reporting does not separate those uses. TANFAC does not publish the acid-versus-oleum revenue split, profit contribution or buyer-to-product map.

Anhydrous hydrofluoric acid (AHF) is both a key intermediate and a product. “Anhydrous” means water is absent or tightly controlled; it differs from diluted HF. TANFAC expanded AHF capacity from 14,850 to 30,000 TPA in October 2024 for about ₹100 crore. At FY26, HF capacity was 30,000 TPA with 95% utilisation. AHF can be shipped in bulk ISO containers and used as an input in refrigerants and other fluorinated chemicals. An illustrative chemical producer buys a controlled bulk input for downstream manufacturing. That buyer must receive the specified grade and manage industrial handling, then convert the input into its own product; the steps after delivery are the customer's, and TANFAC does not describe them. The disclosed information does not identify an AHF buyer or map a client logo to this product.

AHF also shows why a high selling price is not necessarily a high margin. Management cited Q1 FY27 realization of ₹275–₹280 per kilogram; contracts are typically monthly or quarterly, and raw-material pass-through generally lags 30–45 days. This is a dated realization, not a current price guarantee, product margin or forecast. Do not apply it to diluted HF or solar DHF, for which equivalent product-level pricing is not published. In H1 FY27, management said AHF gross profit per tonne was broadly stable while gross-margin percentage was lower amid higher realizations, sulphur, new-product ramp-up and power, fuel and freight.

Diluted hydrofluoric acid (DHF) adds water to HF for a required concentration and application. TANFAC lists it separately from solar-grade DHF: ordinary diluted HF is not automatically a solar-grade material. Applications can include glass etching and industrial surface treatment, but no product-specific customer list, price or revenue share is disclosed. Imagine an illustrative glass processor sourcing a grade to specification for an etching or cleaning step. The buyer's needs determine concentration and handling arrangements, while the supplier's job is to deliver a consistent specified grade. This explains the commercial logic; it is not a claim about a TANFAC customer. Q2 client logos are not assigned product by product.

Solar-grade DHF is the clearest high-purity downstream product. TANFAC specifies trace elements below 10 parts per billion and use in solar-wafer texturing, cleaning and etching. In an illustrative customer journey, a solar-materials maker specifies low contaminant levels, qualifies a supply and uses the solution in wafer preparation. Qualification and repeatability matter as much as a catalogue specification in a high-purity chemical relationship, although TANFAC has not published the customers' internal criteria. Do not add undisclosed customer-side process steps. Public orders evidence demand: ₹732 crore, excluding GST, for Kredence Electronics Materials India through FY2028–29, and ₹336 crore, excluding GST, for Krishna Organics through FY2028–29. Together these announcements represent ₹1,068 crore of order visibility, not revenue already recognized; the releases say no additional capex was required for the Kredence order.

Capacity chronology matters. At FY26 year-end, solar DHF capacity was 20,000 TPA, commissioned in June and October 2025. By the 2 November 2026 company presentation, ₹47 crore of debottlenecking from internal accruals had raised current capacity to 25,000 TPA. Use 25,000 TPA for today’s operating picture and 20,000 only for FY26. Updated contract coverage against 25,000 TPA, order-specific price formula and customer process are undisclosed. The investor question is how quickly capacity becomes qualified, shipped and collected sales, and what returns it earns. Public data gives demand and capacity markers, not the full bridge.

Specialty inorganic fluorides extend the downstream menu. At FY26, TANFAC reported 5,000 TPA capacity across aluminium fluoride (AlF3), potassium fluoride (KF), potassium bifluoride (KHF2), boron trifluoride (BF3) and sodium silicofluoride, at 41% utilisation. “Specialty” does not establish high margins; product-level profitability is unpublished. AlF3 offers an illustrative walkthrough: in aluminium smelting it is a make-up ingredient in molten reduction-cell electrolyte, helping maintain bath chemistry. TANFAC lists 50 kg, 1,000 kg and 1,500 kg packs, formats that illustrate how industrial logistics can serve different shipment sizes without revealing a particular customer's buying pattern. A hypothetical smelter replenishing a process input is an archetype, not a disclosed buyer.

KF gives you a different downstream picture. Potassium fluoride can act as a fluorinating agent in organic chemistry; an illustrative specialty-chemical maker may use it to introduce fluorine into an organic molecule. This locates the product in a value chain, but identifies neither TANFAC’s customers nor their molecules, process conditions or order economics. Potassium bifluoride and sodium silicofluoride broaden the range, while BF3 is included in the specialty-fluoride capacity grouping. Product pages establish a catalogue, not every end-use, buyer, price or profit contribution.

HFC-32 is the major announced refrigerant-related expansion, but remains separate from established AHF and DHF until commissioning and operating performance are demonstrated. The 2 November 2026 deck states 20,000 TPA capacity at Cuddalore and expected commissioning in Q3 FY27; at 30 September, committed spend was ₹360 crore and actual spend ₹231 crore. Earlier materials differ: FY26/Q4 referred to an approximately ₹495 crore integrated package, while the Q1 call gave ₹390 crore / ₹315 crore committed. These are dated or scoped estimates, not amounts to add. Public materials describe a Japanese customer, an MNC MoU and an air-conditioning/commercial-refrigeration customer; do not infer that the broader Japanese fluorinated-chemical agreement is all HFC-32. R-32 is a lower-GWP alternative to R-410A, not a zero-impact or permanent refrigerant: it remains an HFC exposed to phasedown and transition rules. That makes demand growth a market opportunity with a policy horizon, rather than a simple one-way substitution story.

The HFC-32 commercial story has promise and a boundary. The Q2 deck says agreements and MoUs cover 65% of planned capacity and gives aggregate order value of about ₹3,673 crore. Q1 management described about 65% long-term coverage and a formula-based contracted price near US$5.5/kg at that time; Q4 materials showed 67.5% of proposed capacity. Preserve these dated differences. Management described the relevant contracted tranche as having a fixed EBITDA margin, but realized economics and commissioning remain to be observed. Capacity is not output: production, qualification, delivery, working capital and cash conversion still matter. Contract coverage reduces one uncertainty; it does not remove execution risk.

The revenue split gives you a compass, not a product map. H1 FY27’s 75% HF and 25% Sulphuric Acid & Other VAP classification does not mean 75% AHF: it does not disclose individual shares for AHF, ordinary DHF, solar DHF, specialty fluorides or other downstream products. The 25% also combines sulphuric acid with other value-added products. Nor is this the geographic sales mix: FY26 sales were about 95% domestic and 5% export, while Q1 management described roughly 90%/10%. Geography and product streams answer different questions. Since product and stream profits are unpublished, you cannot identify the highest contribution margin or cash return. Label any finer mix estimate as an analyst assumption, not a reported fact.

Build an operating picture by separating capacity, production, sales, revenue, profit and cash. FY26 figures are historical: sulphuric acid and oleum 100,000 TPA at 101% utilisation; HF 30,000 TPA at 95%; specialty inorganic fluorides 5,000 TPA at 41%; and solar DHF 20,000 TPA. The later 25,000 TPA solar figure follows debottlenecking. Do not sum these capacities into a total plant figure, because HF is also an intermediate in downstream output. A contract value is not delivered volume, and a technical application does not prove profitability.

The strategic wager is to move from an established acid-and-HF base into differentiated downstream grades such as solar DHF and HFC-32. Downstreaming can deepen customer specification relationships and potentially add value per tonne, but it also requires qualification, capex, ramp-up, operating discipline and working capital. New DHF orders, 25,000 TPA current solar capacity and HFC-32 commitments show the transition; a product-level margin bridge is still absent. H1 EBITDA margin was below the prior-year period, and management cited input costs and ramp-up. Growth and a better mix do not guarantee immediate margin expansion. Track what each step proves and what remains unresolved.

Keep the portfolio layered in your mind. Upstream are sulphuric acid and oleum, both external products and part of the site’s chemistry platform. The HF layer includes AHF for unnamed industrial and refrigerant-related buyers and diluted HF for uses including glass etching. Downstream sit high-purity solar DHF, specialty inorganic fluorides such as AlF3 and KF, and the new HFC-32 project. Shared infrastructure does not make the products interchangeable, and a logo is not a product attribution. In future results, look for evidence linking the layers to tonnes, revenue, margin, cash and repeat orders. Precision means drawing the boundary, not filling it with guesses.

In FY26, sulphuric acid and oleum capacity of 100,000 tonnes per year ran at 101%, while 30,000 tonnes of HF capacity ran at 95%. Those utilization rates suggest an established operating base, not a greenfield promise. Captive power and waste-heat recovery, domestic sulphur sourcing, multiple fluorspar origins and proximity to Chennai port add practical supports, though none eliminates input or logistics risk.

Solar-grade diluted hydrofluoric acid is the Bull's differentiated leg. TANFAC reports trace elements below 10 parts per billion and use in solar-wafer texturing, cleaning and etching, and calls the platform India's first or only. Capacity rose from 20,000 tonnes at FY26 close to 25,000 tonnes by November 2026 after a ₹47 crore internally funded debottleneck. If customers value purity, qualification and dependable local supply, the addition could serve a growing manufacturing chain. But first-mover status alone proves neither a moat nor durable pricing power.

The Bull can point to named demand rather than rely only on sector forecasts. TANFAC disclosed a ₹732 crore, ex-GST order for Kredence Electronics Materials India and a separate ₹336 crore, ex-GST contract with Krishna Organics, both for solar-grade DHF through FY2028-29. Together, ₹1,068 crore is meaningful order visibility against the current capacity, and the releases say no additional capex is needed for this order execution. Still, the public material does not reveal customer-side process details, price formulas, product margins or updated coverage of the expanded 25,000-tonne capacity. You can regard the contracts as evidence of demand, not as guaranteed profit or proof that the same economics will repeat.

The next leg is larger and more consequential: a 20,000-tonne HFC-32 facility expected, as of 2 November 2026, to commission in Q3 FY27. The company has described long-duration supply arrangements and an aggregate commitment covering about 65% of HFC-32 capacity. A January release names an unnamed Japanese customer for 7,500 tonnes annually of fluorinated chemicals, with estimated annual value of ₹337.5 crore for seven years from 1 January 2027; it says this represents 37.5% of planned downstream capacity. Do not assume that all of this unnamed product is HFC-32. Other disclosed supply arrangements include an MNC MoU and an air-conditioning/commercial-refrigeration customer agreement. Together they make the utilization case plausible, but contract labels, scope and timing must be kept distinct.

Timely commissioning, specification-compliant output, customer offtake and contracted margins could change TANFAC's scale. At Q1, management described a roughly $5.5/kg formula-based price and fixed EBITDA margin for one tranche; that is not a guarantee for every tonne or cost. The Q2 deck listed ₹360 crore committed and ₹231 crore spent at 30 September; Q1 had cited ₹390 crore and ₹315 crore committed, while earlier materials described an approximately ₹495 crore package. These dated or differently scoped estimates must not be combined. Your test is saleable output and cash contribution, not nameplate capacity.

The Bear's strongest case is not that growth cannot happen; it is that investors may already be paying for much of it before cash economics are visible. FY26 revenue reached a record ₹711.1 crore, but EBITDA fell to ₹112.1 crore from ₹128.9 crore and PAT to ₹70.1 crore from ₹88.1 crore. Operating EBITDA margin was 15.8%. In H1 FY27, revenue rose 21.2% to ₹417.7 crore, but EBITDA rose only 5.6% to ₹60.6 crore, margin contracted to 14.4% from 16.6%, and PAT slipped 1.6% to ₹35.9 crore. Q2 improved sequentially in reported growth, but its 13.3% EBITDA margin remained below the prior-year quarter's 16.2%. Higher realizations, sulphur, new-product ramp and power, fuel and freight were cited as pressures. The bear asks whether volume growth can become profitable growth.

FY26 operating cash flow was ₹43.4 crore after working capital and tax; ₹82.4 crore of PPE/CWIP cash purchases left FCF negative ₹39.0 crore. CFO equaled 61.9% of PAT. H1 borrowings and liquid assets are separately reported, so the company's “net debt negative” label depends on what counts as available cash. The ₹250 crore QIP and roughly ₹100 crore preferential issue add financing but are new equity, not internally generated FCF. The Bear reasonably demands proof that growth can fund working capital and capex and still produce cash.

Execution risk has several connected forms. TANFAC operates one reportable Fluoro-Chemicals segment at a single Cuddalore site; there is no product-level profit disclosure. A disruption, commissioning problem, utility constraint or environmental incident could therefore have a concentrated effect. At the same time, product and customer disclosure is too broad to independently reconstruct margins, concentration or contract protections. Fluorspar cost ₹313.4 crore and sulphur ₹104.6 crore in FY26, within ₹453.5 crore of raw and packing materials. Management described a 30–45-day lag in raw-material pass-through for AHF, so a price shock or timing mismatch can temporarily squeeze economics even where eventual pass-through is possible. The specialty inorganic fluorides business ran at 41% utilization in FY26; it is not yet proof that every downstream investment earns attractive returns.

R-32 has lower GWP than R-410A, but at roughly 675–677 it remains an HFC—not zero-emission or regulation-proof. U.S. phasedown rules and EU Regulation 2024/573 make transition risk relevant. Arkema markets Forane 32 for air conditioning and heat pumps; Arkema and Honeywell also describe a commercial arrangement for lower-GWP HFO blends. These show portfolio adaptation, not TANFAC-equivalent economics or an immediate end to R-32 demand. The Bear questions duration and substitution; the Bull answers that contracted near-term demand can still have value.

MENTAL MODEL — From capacity to cash

"capacity → qualified output → profitable shipment → collected cash."

Use a four-link conversion chain: capacity → qualified output → profitable shipment → collected cash. A plant announcement proves only the first link. Customer qualification and commissioning bridge to saleable volume; realized price minus feedstock, utilities, freight and ramp costs determines contribution; working capital and capex determine what reaches free cash flow. TANFAC earns a higher valuation only as evidence travels down this chain. Track each link independently so that a strong order book does not conceal weak margins, and a good quarter does not conceal cash absorbed by inventory or construction.

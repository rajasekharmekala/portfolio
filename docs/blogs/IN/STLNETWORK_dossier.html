STL Networks builds and runs fibre networks, data centres and managed IT services, mostly for Indian government bodies and telecom carriers, and was split out of Sterlite Technologies in 2025[10]. 3. Business model and segments

STL Networks sells design, build and operating services for digital infrastructure. The company site lists six service lines: data centre services, cybersecurity, fibre deployment, cloud, managed services and network services, serving government, telecom, defence, healthcare, mining, energy and transport[17]. It reports as a single segment, internet software and services, with sales of ₹1,475 Cr in FY24, ₹1,180 Cr in FY25 and ₹959 Cr in FY26[18]. A rating agency describes it as an end-to-end executor of complex, mission-critical projects. Fibre route km, home passes and data-centre counts are N/A: no recorded source supports them[14]. Management has said it delivered 47 data-centre projects worth more than ₹4,000 Cr[12].

3.1 How it earns money

Most revenue is project revenue: the company wins a tender, builds, bills on milestones and sometimes earns a smaller managed-service tail. Project revenue is lumpy. The quarterly sales series shows the swing, from ₹355 Cr in June 2024 to ₹181 Cr in March 2025, back to ₹335 Cr in December 2025, then ₹176 Cr in June 2026[3]. An order book is the value of signed contracts not yet delivered; a book-to-bill ratio is order book divided by annual revenue. The last published figure is above ₹6,500 Cr at June 2025, or more than 5.5 times FY25 revenue, but it is more than a year old and a current figure was not found[14][32].

4. Industry and competition

The company works in three markets. One is government broadband, such as BharatNet. Another is data-centre and cloud builds for public bodies and firms. The third is telecom network services. Demand follows public spending plans and carrier budgets, so revenue arrives in steps. A rating agency expected healthy growth from the second half of FY26 through FY27[14]. It pointed to BharatNet Phase III, the Smart Cities Mission and Digital India. The results so far run the other way. FY26 sales fell 18.7% and TTM sales are down 7%[2]. An AI-written page cites a global telecom outsourcing market growing 9.8% a year. That figure is unverified and is not used in any estimate[19].

5.2 Standalone and consolidated differ

The standalone Indian company earned ₹42 Cr, ₹31 Cr and ₹−26 Cr in FY24, FY25 and FY26 on sales of ₹1,303 Cr, ₹1,060 Cr and ₹851 Cr[9]. Standalone ROCE is 4.26% against 0.48% consolidated[9][1]. Even the better standalone figure is below the 15% floor, so the choice of view does not change the gate.

The number

Return on capital employed (ROCE) of 0.48%, consolidated.

How it was calculated

ROCE is operating profit after depreciation, divided by capital employed (equity plus borrowings). It was read from Screener, not recomputed from raw statements.

The inputs

Equity ₹801 Cr (98 + 703) plus borrowings ₹934 Cr gives about ₹1,735 Cr of capital; FY26 operating profit ₹39 Cr less depreciation ₹37 Cr is only ₹2 Cr before other income, consistent with a sub-1% result[1][4].

The range

From 0.48% (consolidated) to 4.26% (standalone)[9]. Both are far below 15%.

If the number is off

Even if ROCE were 10 times higher, at about 5%, the gate would still fail. The verdict does not depend on this number being exact.

What this means for an owner: the profit statement is the first reason for the rejection. Cash flow, covered next, shows whether the accounts hide anything worse or better.

How to read the columns. Operating cash flow (CFO) is cash made by the business before investment. Investing (CFI) is spending on assets, and financing (CFF) is borrowing and repayment. Free cash flow (FCF) is CFO less capital spending.

6.1 Working capital: the real pressure point

Debtor days are 377, so customers take about a year to pay; on FY26 sales that equals roughly ₹990 Cr of receivables (959 × 377 / 365)[1]. Payable days of 1,117 show the company also stretches suppliers, so the cash conversion cycle is a negative 653 days on paper[6]. A negative cycle normally helps a business, but here it signals large unpaid supplier balances, which turn into a cash call whenever customers are slow. The rating agency noted a working-capital lock-up in a few projects and delays and disputes, and said management expected a significant part to clear by the first half of FY27[14]. The first half of FY27 ends on 30 September 2026; no filing read confirms a release, and Q1 FY27 interest of ₹36 Cr was the highest on record[3].

The agency’s own FY25 figures were an interest cover of 1.0 times and net debt multiple of 9.7 times (up from 4.7 times), with free cash of ₹79.2 Cr at September 2025 and annual term-debt repayments of ₹36 to ₹37 Cr in FY26 and FY27[14]. These are credit-agency numbers and use a different definition from the Screener debt-to-equity above. The banks still rate the company A-/Stable, at both India Ratings and Infomerics[14][16]. That is investment-grade-adjacent, and it explains why lenders keep funding the business; it does not make the equity cheap or safe.

What this means for an owner. The balance sheet has used up financial slack: debt has more than doubled while equity fell. Any new contract must convert to cash before it can help the stock. The next chapter asks who is running that conversion and who owns the shares.

12.1 Target, stop and asymmetry

The number

Asymmetry of 0.73 for a tactical entry at the close of ₹46.35, against a floor of 3.5.

How it was calculated

Asymmetry = (target − price) / (price − stop). The target is the 52-week high; the stop follows the tactical rule: below the lowest of the 20-day swing low and the 20/50-day averages, less 0.5 ATR[29].

The inputs

Price 46.35; target 56.50; lowest of swing low 33.63, SMA20 45.68 and SMA50 34.13 is 33.63; minus 0.5 × 2.55 = 1.275 gives a stop of ₹32.355[26]. Reward = 56.50 − 46.35 = 10.15 (+21.9%). Risk = 46.35 − 32.355 = 13.995 (−30.2%). Asymmetry = 10.15 / 13.995 = 0.73.

The range

A target of 3.5 times risk would need a price of 46.35 + 3.5 × 13.995 = ₹95.33 (+105.7%), which no earnings-based anchor supports. A pullback entry at the 20-day average (₹45.68) gives risk 13.33 and reward 10.82, or 0.81.

If the number is off

Even an optimistic target of ₹65 would give (65 − 46.35) / 13.995 = 1.33. The floor is not met under any reasonable target, so the result does not depend on exact inputs.

13. Fatal flaws and red-team case

A red-team case argues the opposite of the verdict as hard as the evidence allows. Here the opposing view is that the RailTel L1 bid, a possible BharatNet and data-centre revival and A-/Stable ratings from two agencies show a turnaround about to start, with the chart in Stage 2 and relative strength of 87.6%. That case is real, but it fails on four points.

Fatal flaw 1: the business does not cover its interest. TTM operating profit of ₹43 Cr against interest of ₹122 Cr is 0.35 times cover[2]. A ₹250 Cr contract at the company’s 4.5% margin would add about ₹11 Cr of operating profit (249.8 × 4.5%), less than a tenth of the interest bill.

Fatal flaw 2: cash has not followed profit and receivables are a year long. CFO was negative in FY25 and FY26 and debtor days are 377[5][6]. The agency’s expectation of a first-half FY27 release has no confirming filing[14].

Fatal flaw 3: L1 is not an order. No purchase order was received at the time of the report[11][12]. The market reaction on the filing day is not used as evidence.

Fatal flaw 4: price is 9.16 ATR above support in a down-trending market. Risk to a disciplined stop is 30.2% against 21.9% upside[26][27].

The hinge claim. The single claim on which a bullish reversal would hinge is that locked working capital releases and operating cash flow turns positive in FY27. If true, interest cover improves and the debt-to-equity trend reverses. Nothing read today proves it; the rating agency only forecasts it. Other risks: management turnover (new CEO in June 2026), institutional exit, a holder base now dominated by retail, an unconfirmed order book, tender-price competition, and an unreported promoter pledge (N/A)[7][19][32]. For an owner, the red-team case lands on the hinge claim, and the plan in chapter 14 is built around watching it.

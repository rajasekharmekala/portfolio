What the company does

SKF India is a bearing maker. A bearing is a part that lets a shaft turn with very little friction, so a wheel or an engine part can spin for years without wearing down. Screener describes the company as a leading supplier of rolling bearings, seals, mechatronics and lubrication systems, and says it uses SKF Group technology.[1] Vehicle makers buy most of its output, and the same products are sold as spare parts after a vehicle leaves the factory.

The company changed shape in 2025. Effective 1 October 2025, its Industrial business was demerged into a separate listed company, SKF India (Industrial) Limited.[1] Shareholders received one share of the new company for each share they held.[1] What remains is the automotive business, which the company's results note describes as a single segment of bearings and related components.[11] This is why the old figures and the new figures are hard to compare, and chapter 3 explains how we handled that.

The Q1 FY27 call gives the current mix. Original equipment sales (OEM, meaning parts fitted to new vehicles) were about 62 percent of sales in the quarter. The vehicle aftermarket, which is replacement parts, was about 20 percent. Exports were 8 percent, and a line the call calls SKF Industrials was about 10 percent.[13] The transcript spells the first item as "owings", which we read as OEM. Management also agreed that growth broadly mirrors the OEM production data, so vehicle output drives this business.[13]

The top team is changing too. Mukund Vasudevan resigned as managing director, effective the close of business on 31 August 2026. Sujeeth Pai moves from whole-time director to managing director from 1 September 2026, subject to shareholder approval.[11] The CFO role changed in May 2026, and the August call was given by CFO Mayank Holani.[15][13] The FY2025-26 annual report is listed by aggregators, but we could not read its text, so FY26 figures here come from the fetched Screener and directors' report pages.[27][25]

The promoter is also changing name. On 25 March 2026 the promoter entity was renamed from SKF Interim AB to SKF Vertevo AB, and the company said the change had no impact on shareholding or promoter status.[29] The group's Annual and Sustainability Report 2025 was published on 6 March 2026.[30] On 7 September 2026 AB SKF's board proposed distributing and listing its automotive business, SKF Vertevo, subject to an extraordinary general meeting on 1 October 2026.[31] The 65th annual general meeting of SKF India was held on 14 August 2026.[32] We could not confirm from our sources whether that group distribution affects SKF India's own shares, so we treat it as an open question for the next filing and not as evidence either way.

Terms in this chapter. A demerger is a split in which one company moves a business into a new company and gives the new shares to its existing shareholders. OEM means original equipment manufacturer, the business that builds vehicles and fits parts to them. The aftermarket is the replacement parts business that serves vehicles already on the road.

Why the business is strong or weak

The main question is whether the company earns good money on the capital it uses, and whether that is holding up. The test we use is return on capital employed (ROCE), which is the profit a company earns on the money invested in its business. A high number means each rupee invested earns more. Our rule asks for ROCE of at least 15 percent. On the group, or consolidated, basis the FY26 figure is 24.2 percent.[1] On the standalone basis, which covers the parent company alone, it is 13.3 percent.[2][3] We use the consolidated figure because the group's cash and profit figures use the same basis. The weak spot is the vehicle aftermarket. The managing director described that business as having "plateau or a small decline in our revenue", and said the next five months carry a different plan to recover volume.[13] Capacity is also tight. An analyst put loading at about 93 percent, and the CFO answered "Yes" to that point. Tight capacity means growth needs more investment before it can be sold.[13]

Ownership is stable. The promoter group holds 52.58 percent of the shares, and the shareholding data from three sources agree on that figure.[1][5][6] Screener records none of the promoter shares as pledged, which means none are used as collateral for loans.[4] One aggregator shows the promoter group at 48.50 percent, a figure we could not match in the other sources, so we do not use it.[7] Foreign portfolio investors hold 6.89 percent and domestic institutions hold 27.17 percent.[1] Trendlyne's credit ratings page for the company shows no rating entry, so the company's borrowing is not independently rated in the sources we could read.[18]

Our reading is that the business is sound on returns and debt, and weaker on trend. The next hard evidence is the Q2 FY27 result, and the sources we could read do not give its date.

What the price is doing

The price tells us whether the market is willing to pay for the business today. We use the ledger, a code-built record of prices and indicators for the 9 October 2026 session. The momentum result is the frozen output of the momentum gate code, and we do not re-derive it here.

Three terms come first. The relative strength index (RSI) is a 14-day measure of how fast the price has been falling or rising, scaled from 0 to 100. Below 30 is called oversold, which means the stock has fallen unusually fast. It does not mean a bounce is due. The average true range (ATR) is the typical daily price swing over 14 days, here ₹41.48, and the rules use it to measure distance from support. Moving averages smooth the price: the 50-day average is the mean close of the last 50 sessions, and the 200-day average is the same over 200 sessions.

A base is a sideways stretch in which a stock trades in a fairly flat range before it moves on. Stage 2 is the rising phase, when the price is above a rising 50-day average and that average is above the 200-day average. Stage 4 is the falling phase, when the price is below falling averages.

The 50-day average was ₹1,547.98 twenty-one sessions earlier, and the 200-day average was ₹2,055.58 sixty-three sessions earlier, so both are falling (ledger). The RSI is 28.0, which is in the oversold zone, and volume is 0.56 times its 20-day level (ledger). That is the picture of Stage 4.

The momentum gate is the code check that every candidate must pass. It has two routes. Test A asks whether a stock is already in an uptrend. Test B asks whether it is starting to trend up from a base. A stock that fails both gets no allocation, however good the business. The result here is NO_MOMENTUM.

No target is set. Analysts' median is ₹1,862.50 and Trendlyne's view is ₹1,820.[16][17] We show them for context, but our rule needs a target we can check ourselves, so neither counts. Without a checkable target the asymmetry floor fails, and the name cannot be bought.

Re-test the name when the close is above ₹1,531.36 for three sessions and the 50-day average has risen for three weeks (ledger). Until then the verdict stays REJECTED.

Record the trend as broken and pause re-testing if the close falls below ₹1,351.7, the core stop reference, because the falling phase would then have extended past the current swing low.

Re-underwrite after the Q2 FY27 results if revenue growth is at or near 20 percent and the margin is 11 to 12 percent.[13][14] Those results can change the business case, but the rejection stands until the trend passes.

Re-test the asymmetry floor once a three-year median P/E and a forward EPS are published from a source we can cite, because without them no target can be anchored.

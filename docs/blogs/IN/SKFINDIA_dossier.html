1. What the company does

In plain words: SKF India Limited makes bearings for vehicles and sells most of them to vehicle makers. This chapter says what it sells, who pays, and what the October 2025 demerger left in this ticker. A bearing is a ring with balls or rollers that lets a wheel or shaft turn with little friction.

The company runs three plants (Pune, Bengaluru and Haridwar), employs 933 people and works with 330 distribution partners [1]. Its range covers wheel-end units, the bearing sets around a wheel hub, gearbox and driveline bearings, and bearings for electric motors [2] [3]. Management says more than 90 percent of its automotive products are made in India [2].

Vehicle makers (OEMs, short for original equipment manufacturers) bought about 62 percent of Q1 FY27 sales, the aftermarket network 20 percent, exports 8 percent and SKF India (Industrial) 10 percent [3]. Inside the vehicle-maker share, two-wheelers were 54 percent, passenger vehicles 31 percent and commercial vehicles 15 percent [3]. The top five customers are close to half of sales and the top ten about 70 percent. Bajaj is the largest in two-wheelers, with Mahindra and Tata large in passenger vehicles [2].

FY26 revenue from operations was ₹21,295.9 million (₹2,129.6 crore) against ₹18,453.4 million, up 15.4 percent [4]. Much of that rise is not the core business. Manufactured goods grew from ₹17,040.7 million to ₹17,152.2 million, up 0.7 percent. Traded goods, bought in and resold, grew from ₹966.0 million to ₹3,152.1 million, and ₹709.4 million came from services to SKF Industrial under a temporary arrangement [4]. Management says the Industrial trade 'will go away' over time [2].

In Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹5,877.9 million, up 27.1 percent on the re-presented ₹4,625.0 million, and profit before tax before exceptional items was ₹837.8 million [5]. Q3 FY26 was the first quarter the company reported for the vehicle business alone [6], and Q4 FY26 revenue was ₹5,945.4 million [7]. The call opened with a warning that prior-period figures were taken from the 'erstwhile combined entity' and 'may involve some assumptions by the management pertaining to the demerged industrial undertaking' [3]. So the 27.1 percent is a comparison against a rebuilt base, not against a filed year.

What the demerger did for a shareholder

A holder of one SKF India share before 15 October 2025 now holds one SKF India share and one SKF India (Industrial) share [4]. The drop from ₹5,008.40 to ₹2,266.10 on the ex-date is therefore not a loss; it is the Industrial value leaving this ticker [10]. Industrial shares were first described as listing 'by November 2025' and listed on 5 December 2025 [13] [4].

Both companies plan to invest: the automotive business ₹4,100 to 5,100 million by 2030, the industrial business ₹8,000 to 9,500 million [13]. Management later gave about ₹500 crore for 2026 to 2028 for the automotive business, with ₹170 to 200 crore in the current year [2] [3] [14]. The first plan was stated in million rupees to 2030 and the second in crore to 2028, so they are different horizons and not a revision.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Demerger: a company splits into two and each shareholder gets shares in both. Record date: the day that fixes who gets the new shares. Ex-date: the first day the old share trades without them.
  • Continuing and discontinued operations: accounting labels for the business kept and the business split off. Re-presented: earlier periods rewritten on that basis.
  • BAPA: a bilateral advance pricing agreement, a deal between the company and India's tax board that fixes the price of dealings between related companies.
  • Promoter: the controlling owner. Normalised: with one-off items removed.
  • TIER_0 is the zero-size tier used for a rejected name.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

In plain words: the business is strong where it is close to its customers and has no debt, and weak where its margin fell after the split and part of its sales depend on SKF's industrial company. This chapter weighs both with the filed evidence.

Where the strength is

The balance sheet carries no borrowings. At 31 March 2026 equity was ₹13,294.7 million, lease liabilities were ₹29.1 million and cash and bank balances were ₹2,999.0 million [4].

The customer base is close to the vehicle makers. Management says its share of two-wheeler electric-vehicle bearing business is 'around' half, and it has won a wheel-end programme from a large passenger-vehicle maker that starts in Q4 of calendar 2028 [2] [3]. Demand is strong: SIAM (the vehicle makers' body) reports domestic sales up 18.2 percent in July to September, and in April to June they were up about 21.4 percent on our unit-weighted sum of its four segments (chapter 2B shows the working) [15] [16]. Q1 FY27 product sales grew about 22 percent on volume, against that industry pace [3].

Margin recovered in the latest quarter. Profit before tax before exceptional items was 14.3 percent of revenue in Q1 FY27, against 9.0 percent in Q4 FY26 [5]. Management says the Q4 dip came from one-off items and the figure 'has now returned to a very normal level largely' [3].

Where the weakness is

Margin is lower than before the split. Profit before tax before exceptional items was 19.2 percent of revenue in FY25 on the re-presented base and 12.3 percent in FY26 [4]. Management told investors in May that 'around 11 to 12 percent' was the near-term level [2]. Peers report operating margins of 17 to 19 percent: NRB Bearings 17.2 percent and Timken India 18.5 percent in Q1 FY27 [17] [18]. An investor on the May call said disclosure had dropped since the demerger, and management promised to improve it [2].

Growth quality is mixed. FY26 manufactured goods rose 0.7 percent; traded goods and temporary service income did the rest [4]. The aftermarket is flat to lower: management says 'it's kind of plateau or a small decline' and that discounts were given [3]. Capacity is tight: management said it is 'short of little capacity' and buys from Industrial [2], while the annual report prints a capacity use of 63.3 percent for FY26 [4]. The two statements cannot both describe the same capacity, and the filing does not reconcile them.

Customers are concentrated. The top five are close to half of sales, and two-wheelers are about a third of total sales on our arithmetic (54 percent of the 62 percent that goes to vehicle makers) [3] [2]. A slowdown at one or two makers would show quickly.

Demand outlook for vehicles and for industry

SIAM's release for July to September 2026 shows domestic vehicle sales up 18.2 percent to 83.88 lakh units, with passenger vehicles up 30.7 percent, commercial vehicles 28.7 percent, three-wheelers 23.6 percent and two-wheelers 15.2 percent [16]. Two-wheelers, 54 percent of this company's vehicle-maker sales, grew slowest of the four. SIAM expects growth to stay positive in the second half on the festive season, new launches, infrastructure spending and fleet replacement [16]. Its June release said vehicle costs were lower after the GST cut and that commodity costs remain a pressure point [15]. Our inference is that part of the growth is a one-time step down in vehicle prices, so the second half may grow less than the first.

The industrial side matters to this company through trade, not through its own sales. SKF India (Industrial) grew revenue 18.3 percent in Q1 FY27 but earned an EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) margin of 9.09 percent against its own target of 16 to 19 percent, and Timken India grew 15 percent [19] [18].

Governance, the parent and related-party payments

The promoter holds 25,992,059 shares, 52.58 percent of 49,437,963, and the holding has not changed in any quarter from December 2023 to September 2026 [12]. The immediate and ultimate parent was AB SKF until 22 December 2025 and is SKF Vertevo AB (formerly SKF Interim AB) since [4]. AB SKF's board proposes to distribute Vertevo's shares to its own shareholders and list Vertevo on Nasdaq Stockholm, expected on 1 December 2026, subject to approval at a shareholder meeting on 1 October 2026, before our session; we found no report of its outcome [11]. SKF announced the name SKF Vertevo on 26 February 2026 [20]. That would leave the Indian promoter owned by a separately listed automotive group. We found no statement of what it means for royalty terms, supply or the 52.58 percent stake, so continuing leakage and neutral listing economics are open gaps, and the absence of a readable primary pledge statement is a gap too.

Payments to the parent are not small. Royalty and trademark fees were ₹1,102.7 million in FY26 (₹890.5 million to AB SKF and ₹212.2 million to SKF Vertevo) against ₹1,322.8 million in FY25 [4]. These figures cover the whole company until 30 September 2025 and the filing does not split them. Against whole-company revenue of ₹37,633.9 million and ₹49,199.2 million they are 2.93 percent and 2.69 percent [5] [8]. Management says there is 'no change in any royalty or trademark fees from the earlier settlement' [2]. The company also earned ₹372.2 million of technical service income from AB SKF [4].

Trade with SKF Industrial is large and affects the balance sheet. At 31 March 2026 receivables from Industrial were ₹2,669.1 million of ₹7,167.7 million, and payables to it were ₹3,092.5 million, including ₹1,118.7 million for the interim service arrangement [4]. Industrial was 6 percent of sales in Q4 FY26 and 10 percent in Q1 FY27 [2] [3]. Management says this is 'not a priority for growth' and 'is expected to come down' [3], but the share rose in the latest quarter.

Tax is a known dispute area. In March 2026 the company signed a bilateral advance pricing agreement with the Central Board of Direct Taxes on dealings with its ultimate parent for FY13 to FY21; the extra tax was ₹614.8 million including ₹72.8 million of interest [4]. Pledge: we found no pledge in the readable evidence. Trendlyne shows 0.0 percent in every quarter, and the annual report text has no pledge statement. The one regulatory no-encumbrance declaration we found names SKF India (Industrial), so we do not rely on it [12] [21].

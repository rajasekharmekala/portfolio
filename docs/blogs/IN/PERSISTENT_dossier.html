Overview

Persistent Systems Limited is an Indian digital engineering and enterprise modernisation company listed on the National Stock Exchange and BSE. It designs, builds and operates software, cloud, data and artificial-intelligence systems for enterprise clients. The business is closer to an engineering workshop than a recurring-revenue software publisher: people, specialised skills and delivery execution convert contracted work into revenue.[1]

FY26 consolidated revenue was ₹147,484.5 million, up 23.5% in INR, while PAT was ₹18,651.2 million, up 33.2%. The company reported 15.6% EBIT margin and a 24th sequential quarter of growth. Those figures describe a strong operating franchise, but they do not override the market-timing rule because the stock is in Stage 3 and the broader market is in Downtrend.[2]

The underwriting date is 2026-10-08, the latest completed NSE session supplied for this run. Company and business model

The company and business model explain what Persistent sells, who pays for it and which resources must scale for growth to remain profitable. This framing matters because a services company is valued through delivery quality and repeat enterprise work rather than only through a product-style recurring-revenue metric. The history and operating identity below connect the business description to the later financial tests.

History and operating identity

Persistent was founded in Pune and grew from product engineering into a global digital engineering partner. Its work spans software product development, cloud migration, data and analytics, customer experience and intelligent automation. This matters because clients buy a blend of domain context and engineering capacity; the relationship can deepen when the vendor moves from a project to a platform or managed capability.

The FY26 annual-report theme, “Where AI Ambition Meets Engineering Execution,” describes the company’s strategic position without proving a moat by itself. The useful test is whether AI offerings are embedded in paid client work, repeat bookings and better economics. FY26 TCV of $600.8 million and ACV of $445.1 million provide evidence of demand conversion, while the 15.6% EBIT margin shows that growth has not yet required a collapse in operating profitability.

Persistent reports more than 27,500 employees across 18 countries and serves sectors such as banking, financial services and insurance, healthcare and life sciences, and software and hi-tech. A service company’s main capacity is skilled labour, so utilisation, attrition, pricing and offshore mix deserve as much attention as headline bookings.

Takeaway for you: the investment case rests on repeat enterprise engineering demand and disciplined delivery, not on a single AI product. The next subsection tests whether the reported growth is broad enough to support that case.

Risk register and bear cases

A risk register lists the ways the thesis can fail and attaches observable warning levels. It matters because a good historical record can coexist with valuation, collection or execution risk. Each bear case below names what would change the underwriting, so monitoring remains concrete rather than emotional.

Bear case: valuation compression

Valuation compression means the market pays a lower multiple for each rupee of earnings. At roughly 45–46x earnings, Persistent needs continued growth and margin delivery to justify the price. If revenue growth slows toward the sector’s mature rate or EBIT margin retreats from 15.6%, the multiple can compress even while PAT remains positive. The warning trigger is a repeated quarter of weaker growth together with an OPM below 17%. That combination matters because a lower denominator in the valuation equation and a lower multiple can reduce the share price even when the company remains profitable. A later re-entry needs both renewed growth evidence and a target anchor that compensates for this multiple risk. A multiple reset can be abrupt because valuation is a market expectation, not an audited asset. The trigger is therefore meaningful before the income statement is visibly weak.

Bear case: receivables and delivery capacity

Receivables and delivery capacity are the two operating constraints that can turn reported growth into weaker cash. Debtor days rose to 83 in FY26, while a people-led model depends on hiring, utilisation and retention. A larger project mix or customer concentration can create working-capital stress before the P&L shows it. The warning trigger is debtor days above 90 or cumulative CFO/PAT below 0.70x. A services firm can carry a large contract for months before collection, which means reported profit can look healthy while cash is tied up. Persistent therefore needs both delivery staffing and billing discipline; either one can become the binding constraint. A verified fall in debtor days would improve the case. Collection timing can become a balance-sheet issue before it becomes a profit issue, so a rising debtor-days trend deserves early attention. The trigger is a sustained move above the 90-day line.

Bear case: AI theme without monetisation

AI platforms, partnerships and client wins can improve positioning, but thematic language is not a revenue line. If AI-related work remains pilot-heavy or requires heavy investment without ACV, margin or retention evidence, the premium narrative weakens. The warning trigger is two reporting periods without measurable AI-led bookings, client expansion or margin contribution.

These bear cases do not contradict the financial PASS; they define what would invalidate it. They also show why the technical rejection is useful: it prevents a good story from being converted into a poor entry while the market is weak.

What this means for you: monitor the three triggers alongside the chart. A close above the 50-DMA is necessary for Tactical Momentum, but it is not sufficient if cash, margins or monetisation deteriorate.

Why the gate is intentionally strict

A Tactical Momentum strategy is not a ranking of the best companies. It is a timing system for participating in established markup phases. The distinction matters because strong fundamentals can coexist with a topping chart: a business may compound value while the stock spends months correcting an earlier premium. The Stage 2 requirement is designed to reduce that timing mismatch.

Persistent’s present numbers illustrate the separation. ROCE of 34.4%, ROE of 27.2%, 20.9% three-year sales CAGR and 26.5% PAT CAGR describe a company that deserves serious research. Yet ₹5,468 is below the 50-DMA of ₹5,511.94, volume is 0.77x its 20-day average, delivery is 27.71%, and the market is in Downtrend. These facts point in different directions, so the framework gives the explicit hard gate priority rather than averaging the signals into a score.

The same discipline avoids two common errors. The first is buying a falling stock because it is a good company; the second is assuming that relative strength of 20.44% means the stock is immune to a market correction. Persistent may be a leader, but a leader can still lose its 50-DMA. The evidence needed is a repair, not an argument.

A future report should preserve the 2026-10-08 baseline and show what changed. If the price reclaims the 50-DMA, state whether the 50-DMA remained rising. If a breakout occurs, state its volume multiple and whether the breakout held for subsequent sessions. If earnings change the target anchor, state the EPS source, P/E history and the exact arithmetic. If none of those facts changes, the rejection remains the correct action.

Use this rule: the report is conservative by design. It keeps a financially strong name visible without turning visibility into exposure. The bridge to an investable decision is a verifiable Stage 2 repair, a valid support map and a target that clears 3.5:1 asymmetry.

How to read the evidence without overreaching

Reported revenue and PAT are historical facts, while the market price is a forward-looking auction. The gap between them is where underwriting errors occur. When a company reports a good quarter, investors may immediately pay for several good quarters; when the chart weakens, the price may be discounting a future slowdown that is not visible in the last annual report. A sound dossier keeps both clocks visible.

Persistent’s FY26 release gives a clean historical record: revenue of ₹147,484.5 million, EBIT of ₹23,034.7 million and PAT of ₹18,651.2 million. The same release reports a one-time New Labour Codes impact of about 0.6% on EBIT and 0.5% on PAT. That disclosure helps interpret margins, but it does not justify removing every cost from the valuation calculation. The cleanest approach is to show the reported number and mention the disclosed item.

The stock’s 52-week position also needs careful language. Being 17.14% below the high can mean a normal consolidation after a strong run, or the start of a larger reversal. The 50-DMA relationship supplies more information than the distance from the high: price below a rising 50-DMA is a repair problem, not automatically a bargain. RSI 51.51 confirms neither panic nor a forced rebound.

The latest delivery ratio reinforces this caution. Delivery measures the quantity carried into demat accounts relative to total traded quantity; a low 27.71% reading means the day had a comparatively larger trading component. It does not identify every trade as speculative, but it reduces the evidentiary weight of that day as long-term accumulation.

Relative strength deserves the same balanced treatment. A 63-day excess return of 20.44% is useful because it says Persistent has outperformed the benchmark over the measured window. It cannot prove a new uptrend because relative strength is a ratio, and both the numerator and benchmark can change. The correct use is to keep the name near the top of the monitoring queue while waiting for the stage test.

Annual reports explain audited history and notes; results releases provide the current quarter’s headline numbers; investor presentations explain bookings and strategy; exchange filings capture formal events. A claim that appears only in an informal summary is weaker than a claim tied to one of those source classes.

## Chapter 1: Forty-One Years of Brick, Salt-Pan Land & Cycle Survival

> **Core Investor Question:** How did the Ajmera family accumulate 100+ acres in Wadala and Kanjurmarg, how did the company survive the 2018–2021 NBFC and COVID real estate winter, and what drove the 5.1x profit breakout from FY21 to FY26?

Every enduring real estate company is defined by when and how it acquired its land. Incorporated in 1985 and listed on both the Bombay Stock Exchange under code 513349 and the National Stock Exchange under the ticker AJMERA, Ajmera Realty and Infra India Limited is the public flagship of the Ajmera Group, an engineering and construction house founded by the late Chhotubhai Ajmera and his brothers across Gujarat and Maharashtra. In the 1970s, 1980s, and 1990s. At the same time, most Mumbai builders competed for small single-building plots in crowded south and western suburbs, the Ajmera family pursued a far bolder township philosophy. They bought huge, contiguous parcels of marshy, peripheral, or industrial land along Mumbai's emerging transport corridors. Places that skeptics dismissed as too far or too difficult to build upon. And then constructed the roads, water networks, parks, and schools necessary to create self-contained urban communities.

That patient vision produced iconic Mumbai neighborhoods: Shastri Nagar in Andheri West, Yogi Nagar in Borivali West, and, most famously, the one-hundred-acre Bhakti Park and Ajmera i-Land township in Wadala East, built on former salt-pan and industrial land that today sits minutes away from the Eastern Freeway, the Mumbai Trans Harbour Link Atal Setu, the Monorail, and the Bandra-Kurla Complex. Alongside Wadala, the group accumulated a contiguous fifty-five to sixty-six acre estate in Kanjurmarg East and expanded during the 1990s and 2000s into Bengaluru's Electronic City technology corridor, Ahmedabad, Pune, and international ventures in Bahrain and the United Kingdom.

### Twelve Years of Financial History: From the 2020 Debt Peak to the 5.1x Profit Breakout

If we trace Ajmera Realty's twelve-year financial record from fiscal 2015 through the first half of fiscal 2027, the company's evolution falls into three distinct eras. In the first era, from fiscal 2015 through fiscal 2018, Ajmera operated as a slow-moving traditional builder monetizing incremental towers at Wadala and Bengaluru. Annual sales grew from 165 crore rupees in fiscal 2015 to 370 crore rupees in fiscal 2018, operating profit rose from 45 crore rupees to 138 crore rupees at a 37 percent operating margin, and net profit reached a pre-pandemic high of 84 crore rupees, or 4.64 rupees per share, with return on capital employed climbing from 6 percent to 14 percent. Yet beneath those strong margins, balance-sheet velocity was sluggish: inventory turnover stretched across thousands of days, and total debt nearly doubled from 268 crore rupees in fiscal 2015 to 515 crore rupees in fiscal 2018.

The second era, spanning fiscal 2019 through fiscal 2021, brought a perfect storm. In late 2018, the collapse of IL&FS froze non-bank lending to Indian real estate developers, just as the new RERA regime required builders to lock 70 percent of customer collections inside dedicated construction escrow accounts. As Ajmera borrowed to fund upfront project approvals and construction, consolidated debt surged to 863 crore rupees in fiscal 2019 and peaked at 968 crore rupees in March 2020 against just 638 crore rupees of shareholder equity. A heavy 1.52 times debt-to-equity ratio. When the COVID-19 pandemic struck in fiscal 2020 and fiscal 2021, annual sales stalled at 344 crore and 347 crore rupees, interest expense swallowed 66 crore and 58 crore rupees per year, operating margins fell nine percentage points to 28 percent, and net profit plunged 63 percent to a trough of 31 crore rupees in fiscal 2021, or 1.70 rupees per share, with return on capital employed back down at 6 percent.

That crisis catalyzed the third era, from fiscal 2022 to the present, led by Director of Corporate Affairs Dhaval Ajmera and Chief Financial Officer Nitin Bavisi. First, they accelerated monetization of the zero-cost Wadala land bank through high-rise luxury projects like Ajmera Manhattan One and Two, where realizations exceeded 25,760 rupees per square foot. Second, rather than buying expensive new land with debt, they pivoted to asset-light housing society redevelopments and joint development agreements across Mumbai suburbs like Vikhroli, Bhandup, Bandra, Versova, and Borivali, alongside fast-turning mid-market apartments in Bengaluru. The results transformed the income statement and balance sheet simultaneously. Consolidated revenue more than tripled from 347 crore rupees in fiscal 2021 to 1,090 crore rupees in fiscal 2026 and 1,247 crore rupees over the trailing twelve months ended September 2026. Ajmera Realty survived and emerged stronger for two reasons. First, its core Wadala and Kanjurmarg township land banks carried zero acquisition debt. ## Chapter 3: Follow the RERA Escrow Cash — Pre-Sales, Ind AS 115 Recognition & The ₹100 Unit Economics Waterfall

> **Core Investor Question:** Why did Ajmera report ₹224 Cr of pre-sales and ₹190 Cr of collections in Q2 FY27 while recognizing ₹320 Cr of P&L revenue, how does RERA escrow cash flow work, and how much profit remains from ₹100 of sales on legacy Wadala land versus a JDA?

One of the most frequent mistakes investors make when analyzing an Indian real estate stock is confusing operational pre-sales with income-statement revenue. Look at Ajmera Realty's second quarter of fiscal 2027: operationally, the company sold 72,883 square feet of carpet area for a booking value of 224 crore rupees and collected 190 crore rupees in cash from buyers. This brings first-half pre-sales to 370 crore rupees and first-half cash collections to 363 crore rupees, following record full-year fiscal 2026 collections of 1,103 crore rupees. Yet on the consolidated income statement, second-quarter revenue was 320 crore rupees and first-half revenue was 639 crore rupees. More than 70 percent higher than first-half pre-sales. Why do those numbers diverge so sharply?

### Sales Channels, RERA Escrow Accounts, and the 25 Percent Recognition Gate

The journey of a rupee inside Ajmera Realty begins at the sales gallery. Roughly 55 to 65 percent of apartments and commercial offices are sold through registered RERA channel partners and independent property consultants. At the same time, the remaining 35 to 45 percent come from direct site walk-ins at the Wadala experience center, digital campaigns, corporate leasing teams, and referrals from past Ajmera buyers. When a buyer books an apartment at Ajmera Manhattan or Ajmera Solis, they pay a 10 percent booking advance and then make installment payments tied to physical construction milestones. Excavation, plinth, podium, every few reinforced concrete floor slabs, brickwork, plumbing, and possession.

Under India's RERA law, 70 percent of every customer check is locked inside a dedicated project escrow bank account and can only be drawn down to pay for construction and land approvals on that specific site, verified by an independent architect, engineer, and chartered accountant. The remaining 30 percent flows to the company's general account to pay broker commissions, marketing, corporate salaries, taxes, and debt reduction. Because six of Ajmera's eight active projects are already 76 to 95 percent sold, every floor slab poured on site triggers automatic customer billings. Which is why first-half cash collections of 363 crore rupees matched 98 percent of new sales bookings.

Under Indian accounting standards, however, Ajmera cannot record a single rupee of those customer bookings on its profit-and-loss statement until a project crosses a strict 25 percent execution and sales threshold. While a building is in early shoring or excavation, all customer payments sit on the balance sheet as customer advances under Other Liabilities, and all construction and interest costs accumulate inside Work-in-Progress inventory. The moment a project crosses the 25 percent gate, its accumulated revenue and cost pool enter the income statement all at once, and subsequent revenue is recognized in proportion to physical construction progress.

We saw this exact mechanism in action across the first two quarters of fiscal 2027. In the first quarter, Ajmera Solis in Vikhroli crossed its revenue-recognition threshold for the first time with 86 percent of its inventory sold. Because Solis had used a high-cost private-equity loan during its initial acquisition phase. A loan management promptly paid down using customer collections. The accumulated interest inside Solis's cost pool hit the income statement all at once, causing quarterly finance costs to jump from 22 crore rupees to 30 crore rupees even though total company debt fell by 57 crore rupees.

Chapter 1. What RNIT actually does

RNIT AI Solutions sells software and services for identity checks, office workflows and reporting. Its clearest evidence of work is in government education and skills programmes. It is also trying to reuse its technology for private firms. RNIT reports a single segment. Its accounts do not show revenue or profit by product, or split it between public and private clients. FY2026 annual report, management discussion and segment disclosure.

Identity and facial recognition: the event is not the outcome

RNIT describes facial recognition systems (FRS) for staff attendance, identity checks and beneficiary workflows. It offers them via mobile, browser, CCTV and its Faceify device. An attendance system must link a face match to a roster, rules and a dashboard. Only then can an institution act on the result. RNIT presents that full workflow and its reports as the product, not just the face-matching algorithm. May 2026 investor presentation, company overview and product portfolio.

Management says it has processed more than 3 billion facial templates to date. It claims capacity for 15 million face identifications a day. It also says it has served or enabled more than 300 departments and corporate ecosystems. These claims are not independent audit results. Templates are not unique people; capacity is not actual daily volume. “Served or enabled” does not establish 300 paying customers. The goals of 5 billion templates and 50 million daily capacity are not delivered figures. Rejection rates, uptime, active users and renewal economics would help test reliability and profit. May 2026 investor presentation, accomplishments.

Education: from a running portal to additional work orders

Education offers a clearer example than the total template count. On 13 May 2026 RNIT said it had rolled out AMAAA for Andhra Pradesh's State Board of Technical Education and Training (SBTET AP). AMAAA stands for Academic Monitoring & Administrative Automation Application. RNIT said the portal served about 1,00,000 students and staff. That is its own report at that date. It is not a verified count of monthly active users or paying accounts. 13 May 2026 AMAAA rollout filing.

On 9 June 2026 the board awarded two new modules for the existing platform. One manages exams, including pre- and post-exam work, question-paper controls and results. The other manages affiliation, including applications, inspections and approvals. About 4,00,000 students fall within the broader scope, the filing says. This is not proof that they already use the new modules. Exam charges are per student per semester or year. The orders also provide for three years of post-deployment operations and maintenance. The exam order is Tier 2 (₹1 crore–₹10 crore). The affiliation order is Tier 1 (up to ₹1 crore). Both bands include GST under this filing's classification. Neither is an exact contract value or cash collected. 9 June 2026 SBTET work-order filing.

The work extends an installed portal into exams and institutional approvals. If the modules work and remain in use, they could embed RNIT more deeply in daily tasks. But extra setup, support and security costs could absorb the benefit. The orders offer a chance to deepen the relationship. They do not establish future adoption, margin or renewal. SBTET work orders.

NIA and APSSDC: usage is the economic hinge

NIA, or Neural Intellectual Assistant, is RNIT's conversational AI platform. Management says it uses custom large and small language models. It is designed to work in several languages and domains. These are company claims, not independent proof of model quality. They do not prove RNIT owns every underlying component or beats its rivals. For skills, RNIT describes mock interviews, tailored feedback and skill-gap checks. It does not guarantee a job. FY2026 annual report, managing director's message; May 2026 investor presentation.

On 22 September 2026 Andhra Pradesh State Skill Development Corporation (APSSDC) gave RNIT an award for a multilingual interview and skills platform using NIA. The work covers design, development, integration and maintenance. Its scope is up to 20 lakh users/interview sessions. Some students may have two attempts. The figure is neither a distinct-person count nor proof of completed sessions. English, Hindi and Telugu are planned from go-live. The system is to link with the Naipunyam portal and assess skills against stated occupational and qualification frameworks. The filing schedules setup, integration and deployment within 15 working days of the award, subject to dependencies and approvals. It describes a five-year service agreement from signing. It does not prove launch or five years of service. 22 September 2026 APSSDC award.

RNIT will bill per minute per user. Payment depends on actual use and satisfactory delivery. The filing places the project in Tier 7: above ₹25 crore including GST. It gives no exact amount or guaranteed minimum collection. The disclosed value band can still mean modest early revenue if use, interview length or acceptance is low. Inference and support costs matter too. Watch for a signed agreement, launch status, accepted paid minutes, realised price and cash receipts. The maximum scope alone cannot show what RNIT will earn. APSSDC award, terms and value-band annexure.

How an award becomes cash

RNIT lists usage-based SaaS, long-term platform work, maintenance and enterprise subscriptions as business models. These are possible contract types, not a recurring-revenue breakdown. Its FY2026 policy says fixed-price maintenance can be recognised over the service period. It may instead use percentage of completion, depending on the service pattern. Other fixed-price contracts use percentage of completion based on estimated effort or costs. A contract award, recognised revenue and cash receipt can thus occur at different times. May 2026 presentation, business models; FY2026 annual report, revenue recognition policy.

Chapter 2. Competition, moat and learning resources

RNIT competes in face matching, AI interview practice, government workflows and enterprise software integration. These are distinct markets. A cloud API is not a full state programme. A general-purpose AI assistant is not a government contract. Each offers a possible substitute for part of RNIT's work. That does not prove either provider bid against RNIT or could replace it at once.

Where the advantage may sit

Amazon Rekognition offers APIs to compare faces and search stored face metadata. Face matching is therefore available outside RNIT. AWS sells a component; its cited docs do not show it running the same Andhra Pradesh programme. RNIT's possible edge is the full system. That includes local workflows, institution onboarding, links to other systems and support. An existing relationship may also open the way to new modules. These features could make replacement harder. The sources do not prove exclusive rights, switching costs, bid-win rates or better face matching. AWS developer documentation: detecting and analysing faces; SBTET work-order filing.

AI interview practice has substitutes too. Google describes using Gemini Live for real-time mock interviews and feedback. This is a general-purpose option. It is not proof of an equivalent APSSDC deployment or similar quality in Indian languages. RNIT's proposed package combines Telugu, Hindi and English voice use with occupational-framework mapping and the Naipunyam link. Its edge will rest on accepted outcomes, cost per paid minute, assessment quality and renewals. A chatbot alone is not enough. Google's interview-preparation guide, 11 December 2025; APSSDC award.

A credible moat needs more evidence than deployment claims. Look for repeat wins after competitive bids, customer retention and independent benchmarks. Comparable contract economics and revenue left after support and model costs also matter. The SBTET additions suggest a deeper relationship. They do not by themselves prove a lasting moat. RNIT names large technology firms, niche providers and startups as competitive threats. SBTET orders; FY2026 annual report, opportunities and threats.

Privacy, error and delivery risk

Identity systems hold sensitive personal data. Failed matches can also exclude people or identify them wrongly. A 2019 NIST study found demographic differences in many tested face-recognition algorithms. Results varied widely by algorithm and task. NIST did not test RNIT's system, so the study does not establish RNIT's error rates. Ask for false-match and false-non-match rates for each deployment. Ask too about subgroup tests, liveness controls, manual options and ways to correct errors. A high template count cannot establish dependable service on its own. NIST, face-recognition demographic-effects study.

Both FRS and NIA warrant privacy checks. Who is responsible for data collection, and on what legal basis? What are the retention and deletion rules? Ask about encryption, access controls, incident response and independent security tests. Also ask whether voice, interview or biometric data is reused to train models. These are diligence questions, not claims of a breach or non-compliance. RNIT's annual report names cybersecurity, data protection, changing rules, project delivery and competition as risks. The cited disclosures do not establish the controls or independent audit results for each deployment. A serious incident, strict bid rule or material failure rate could raise costs and put renewal at risk. That remains true even if the technology otherwise works. FY2026 annual report, management discussion and risks.

5. Investment horizons and scenario discipline

The evidence supports a commercially meaningful software franchise, but it does not yet establish that new contract awards will turn into reliable cash at the pace implied by the current valuation. The decision is to reject RNITAI for a new shortlist allocation, not to assert that it is a broken business. The hinge claim

The hinge is whether projects and usage produce recurring cash that RNIT can collect, at an acceptable return on capital. The APSSDC award terms tie payment to actual usage and satisfactory delivery. A large award value therefore cannot by itself prove paid usage or cash collection. The September results show an operating cash outflow and higher receivables. They do not show which customers owe the amounts or how old the debts are. Evidence against the cautious stance would include sustained positive operating cash flow and a lower receivable burden. It would also include documented contract acceptance, paid usage and a valuation with enough margin of safety. Positive quarterly earnings alone do not settle the claim. Longer collection delays, dilution or failure to deliver contracts would weaken the thesis further. The action plan keeps this test separate from the decision on the existing holding.

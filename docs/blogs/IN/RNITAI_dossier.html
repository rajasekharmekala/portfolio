1. A Corporate Shell Reborn: Autopal, CIRP and the Operating Reset

RNIT AI Solutions Limited’s legal identity predates its software business. Its corporate identification number embeds 1985, and until February 2025 the listed entity was Autopal Industries Limited. The Autopal legacy was associated with automotive lighting and components. The FY2025 Board’s Report states that operations changed from “Auto Components & Equipments” to “Computers – Software & Consulting,” including software development, IT consulting and data analytics. This matters because the shell’s age is not the age of its current products, customer relationships, software assets or cash flows. The lighting lineage explains corporate history; it does not establish continuity of technology economics. [2] [4]

The operating activity entered through the merger came from RNIT Solutions & Services Limited, described in FY2025 as engaged in IoT, AI, mobile solutions and application development. Raja Srinivas Nandigam, then Managing Director and CEO of that company, became Managing Director of the listed entity. The combination joined a distressed listed shell with a distinct technology business and its leadership. Do not treat the name Autopal as proof that the listed company had already built its present AI platform, or that the acquired operation inherited four decades of lighting-sector relationships. The annual report explicitly describes a change in the nature of business. [2]

Tack Innovations, an operational creditor, initiated CIRP under section 9 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code. On 23 September 2024 the NCLT Jaipur Bench approved a resolution plan jointly submitted by Raja Srinivas Nandigam and Vivek Kumar Ratakonda. The same order approved a scheme merging RNIT Solutions & Services into Autopal, with an appointed date of 1 April 2023. This was both a corporate rescue and an operating-business combination. The appointed date governs accounting treatment; the NCLT order and later Board handover mark legal and managerial events. Keep those dates separate rather than implying continuous operation or control. [2]

An implementation and monitoring committee oversaw the plan. A new Board assumed control on 8 October 2024, and the name change to RNIT AI Solutions Limited became effective on 17 February 2025. FY2025 is identified as the first annual report after relisting and CIRP; FY2026 is the first full year under the new name. FY2025 reported ₹3,222.59 lakh operating revenue and ₹720.94 lakh PAT; FY2026 reported ₹5,149.88 lakh and ₹1,201.73 lakh. These are actual operating results, but only two post-reset audited years give a meaningful performance record. [1] [2] [4]

3. How the Work Becomes Cash: Procurement, Delivery and Working Capital

RNIT sells software development, AI design, digital platforms, consulting, support and maintenance. The audited revenue policy describes time-and-material, fixed-price, milestone, subscription and managed-service arrangements. These formats distribute delivery and collection risk differently. FY2026’s auditor made revenue recognition a key audit matter, citing performance obligations, percentage-of-completion estimates, unbilled contract assets and customer acceptance. You should treat award, recognized revenue, acceptance, invoice and cash as separate stages. A signed scope establishes opportunity, not collected economics. [1]

Public-sector opportunities are visible through named notices. APSSDC’s NIA announcement and the Higher Education agreement identify customers and defined scopes including design, development, integration, deployment and maintenance. The notices establish public-sector engagements, but not every bid step, channel structure, reseller role, procurement conversion or renewal. The annual report does not disclose bid pipeline, commissions or system-integrator dependence. Describe the route only where filings support it: a named public buyer, a specified software program and implementation obligations. [5] [6]

Current filed pricing examples are more specific than a generic SaaS label. The executed Andhra Pradesh Higher Education SLA charges by attendance authentication over a two-year agreement; Telangana’s FRS rate contracts operate under a SaaS model but do not publish a per-user tariff. The 22 September 2026 APSSDC NIA award bills per minute per user, ties payment to actual usage and satisfactory delivery, provides a five-year services term from signing, and is classified by RNIT as Tier 7 (above ₹25 crore inclusive of GST). The tier is a company classification, not guaranteed cash; actual interviews, acceptance and invoicing determine the outcome. [6] [11] [43] A November 2025 Telangana Social Welfare filing similarly described usage-based SaaS consideration for attendance across 268 institutions; it did not disclose the tariff. [44]

Delivery can blend RNIT’s own work with outside components. The accounting policy says integrated contracts may contain subcontractor services and third-party equipment or software. RNIT assesses whether it controls goods or services before transfer; acting as agent means reporting revenue net, acting as principal means reporting gross. This distinction can affect growth and margins even when underlying project value is similar. The reports do not name vendors, hardware sources, hosting providers or supply-chain concentrations. Do not assume all recognized revenue represents a proprietary software license. [1]

Acceptance is a cash gate. Fixed-price maintenance can be recognized ratably or by percentage of completion depending on the service pattern; other fixed-price contracts use effort or cost incurred relative to estimates. The auditor says it tested contracts, timesheets, milestone confirmations, customer acceptance and unbilled revenue. That is not an adverse finding; it maps the judgments. Monitor contract-asset ageing, billing milestones, customer sign-off and post-period collections. Deployment that is active but not accepted may delay billing or change revenue timing. [1]

Management says SaaS and operations-and-maintenance revenue supported FY2025, and the audited policy recognizes subscription and managed-service contracts. Yet there is no SaaS revenue share, contract-duration profile or recurring-revenue reconciliation. Do not assume every customer pays by user, month or usage event. Public programs may be milestone or fixed-price arrangements; a cloud product could bill differently. The source does not provide tariffs. Track subscription revenue and renewals only when reported, and avoid turning “SaaS” into a claim that the majority of revenue is recurring. [1] [2]

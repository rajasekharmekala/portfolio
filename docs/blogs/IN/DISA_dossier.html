Disa India Limited

Disa India Limited is an Indian capital-goods company supplying foundry systems: moulding machines, sand preparation equipment, surface-preparation machines and related environmental-control systems. The company is a focused subsidiary of the global DISA group and is majority controlled by DISA Holding A/S and DISA Holding AG. The business has attractive reported returns and negligible borrowings, but a small-cap industrial stock must be bought only when cash conversion and price structure agree.

Identity and business

Disa India sells engineered foundry equipment rather than a recurring consumer product. A foundry turns molten metal into components; its moulding and sand systems determine throughput, dimensional consistency and reject rates. Disa's offer combines machine hardware with process know-how, which can make replacement and service relationships more valuable than a simple equipment catalogue. Company descriptions identify moulding machines, sand mixers, sand plants, Wheelabrator surface preparation and environmental systems as the core portfolio.[1] [17] [18]

The economic customer is an industrial foundry serving automobiles, engineering, infrastructure and other component makers. Capital spending therefore moves in waves: when foundries add capacity or automate, order intake rises; when end-market utilisation falls, customers defer large projects. The company has a useful niche, but the niche is still exposed to industrial capex timing and imported technology or group-company dependencies.

The investment question is not whether Disa makes a real product. It does. The question is whether its high return profile is durable after a soft quarter and whether the market price already discounts that quality. FY2026 sales were ₹425 crore and net profit ₹54 crore in the fetched standalone table, while TTM sales were ₹410 crore and profit ₹51 crore.[2] That combination says the franchise is profitable, but near-term acceleration is not yet evidenced.

History and ownership

Disa India is tied to the international DISA group, whose technology and product architecture give the Indian subsidiary a credible technical parent. Promoters held 74.82% in every quarterly observation from December 2023 through September 2026 in the retrieved shareholding data.[2] [14] Such stability can support long planning horizons and technology transfer, but it also leaves a relatively small public float.

High promoter ownership changes the market mechanics. A good result can move the price sharply because fewer shares trade freely; a disappointing result can also widen the exit spread. The number of shareholders rose from 4,070 in December 2023 to 4,856 in September 2026, while public ownership was 16.85% at the latest observation.[2] The result is a concentrated ownership structure that deserves a liquidity discount rather than an automatic premium.

Corporate history and governance should be read through the annual reports, exchange announcements and related-party notes, not through brand familiarity. The official investor-relations archive lists reports through FY2025-26, and BSE hosts the filed PDFs.[3] [6] [7] The next diligence step is to reconcile group purchases, technical-service charges and any customer concentration disclosures in those notes.

Unit economics and operating engine

For Disa, an order is a project, not a unit sold from a shelf. Revenue recognition depends on engineering, fabrication, installation and acceptance, so working capital can rise before cash arrives. Gross margin is not separately exposed in the public summary, making operating margin and debtor days the practical observable checks. FY2026 operating margin was 16%, close to the 15% reported in FY2024 and FY2025.[1] [2]

The company benefits from design capability, installed-base familiarity and group technology. Those are soft barriers: a customer cannot change a moulding line casually because the change can interrupt production and require process requalification. Yet a high share of imported or group-sourced components could compress margins if currency, freight or transfer-pricing terms move against the subsidiary. The annual-report notes and related-party schedules are therefore part of the moat test.

Service, spares and repeat orders can make project economics less binary, but the retrieved public tables do not disclose a clean service-revenue split, order book, book-to-bill ratio or installed-base utilisation. Those items are marked N/A rather than inferred. Until management discloses them, the underwriting should rely on reported cash and margins, not an imagined annuity.

Financial record

Sales grew from ₹262 crore in FY2023 to ₹329 crore in FY2024, ₹390 crore in FY2025 and ₹425 crore in FY2026; the fetched Screener series gives a three-year sales CAGR of roughly 18%. Net profit increased from ₹30 crore to ₹43 crore, ₹50 crore and ₹54 crore over the same years, with a three-year profit CAGR of roughly 24%.[1] This is the strongest part of the case: Disa has compounded from a small base while preserving a mid-teens margin.

Returns are also above the hard floor. Reported ROCE is 26.6% and ROE is 19.7%, versus the framework's minimum ROCE of 15%. Borrowings were ₹0 crore in FY2026 after ₹2 crore in FY2025, so debt-to-equity is effectively 0.00x on the displayed balance sheet.[1] [2] A clean balance sheet reduces financial fragility, but it does not remove order-cycle risk.

TTM sales of ₹410 crore and TTM profit of ₹51 crore are below the FY2026 annual numbers. The decline is not fatal by itself, because industrial deliveries can be lumpy, but it raises the burden on the order book and the next two quarters. A pass requires renewed sales growth without debtor days moving still higher; otherwise the apparent earnings quality is less valuable than the historical CAGR suggests.

Cash-flow forensic

Cash from operations was ₹39 crore in FY2023, ₹56 crore in FY2024, ₹33 crore in FY2025 and ₹13 crore in FY2026 in the fetched consolidated series.[1] The three-year cumulative CFO/PAT calculation using FY2024–FY2026 is (56+33+13)/(43+50+54) = 102/147 = 0.69x, just below the 0.70 hard floor. Using FY2023–FY2025 produces (39+56+33)/(30+43+50) = 128/123 = 1.04x. The latest three-year window therefore fails narrowly, and the difference is economically meaningful.

Debtor days rose to 61 in FY2026 from 40 in FY2025 and 33 in FY2024; inventory days were 112 and the cash-conversion cycle was 89 days.[1] A debtor-day increase is like a shop selling more on credit: reported sales can rise while cash remains in customers' hands. The balance sheet has enough equity to absorb a year of working-capital investment, but repeated deterioration would turn a strong return profile into a funding problem.

Free cash flow was negative ₹15–16 crore in FY2026 despite positive profit because investing and working-capital movements absorbed cash. The annual reports must be read for the exact capex and receivable bridge. The underwriting conclusion is conditional: the cash gate is WATCH/FAIL depending on the prescribed three-year window, not a clean pass.

Market position and valuation

The verified 9 October 2026 close is ₹11,385, with a reported market capitalisation near ₹1,656 crore, 52-week range ₹10,414–₹14,499, P/E about 30.7–30.9, book value around ₹2,076–₹2,098 and dividend yield about 1.76%.[1] [2] At 30.7 times earnings, the market is paying for quality and scarcity. That valuation can work if profit compounds near the historical rate, but it leaves less room for a weak order cycle or a cash-conversion miss.

The stock is down 17% over one year and its three-year price CAGR is negative 6% in the retrieved Screener history.[1] The mismatch between long-run profit compounding and recent price performance is a warning, not a bargain signal. It may represent a reset, or it may be the market discounting slowing growth; only verified technical structure and order evidence can separate those cases.

A target anchored to forward EPS times a verified three-year median P/E is not deterministically available from the accessible sources. A mechanical target would therefore be false precision. The correct action is to set target and asymmetry to N/A until forward EPS, median multiple and a stop based on ATR are sourced.

Fatal-flaw red team

Flaw one — receivables become the growth engine. If debtor days remain near 61 or rise, sales growth can consume cash. The trigger is two consecutive reporting periods above 60 days or latest-window CFO/PAT below 0.70x. In that case the thesis changes from “efficient niche compounder” to “working-capital-funded project seller.”

Flaw two — capex cycle and valuation compress together. At roughly 31 times earnings, a single-digit profit-growth year can produce a falling multiple and a falling EPS estimate at the same time. The trigger is TTM profit below FY2026 while P/E remains above 28x. The correct response would be to reject rather than average down.

Flaw three — technical structure is already broken. The stock's one-year return is negative and the technical ledger failed. If verified data shows price below a falling 200-DMA or a death cross, the market is not offering a support-aware entry. The trigger is any close below structural support by more than the prescribed stop rule; until that level is calculated, capital stays uncommitted.

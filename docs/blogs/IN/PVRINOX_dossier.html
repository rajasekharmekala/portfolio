What the company does

PVR INOX sells admission, food and beverage, advertising and related entertainment experiences through a screen network. The economic model is unusual: a substantial portion of revenue is variable with attendance, while rent, staff, maintenance and lease obligations create a high operating-cost floor. A good film slate therefore lifts margins disproportionately; a weak slate can make a healthy footprint look structurally impaired. The merger that created the combined platform also made scale a strategic asset, but scale does not remove content volatility. The company annual-report archive and merger disclosures establish the business perimeter.[2]

Unit economics

Three levers matter most. First, occupancy converts a fixed screen schedule into paid seats. Second, average ticket price captures format mix, geography and premiumisation. Third, spend per head in food and beverage can expand contribution without requiring another screen. Advertising is a fourth lever but is more cyclical. The right question is not whether revenue grows; it is whether incremental attendance and pricing cover film-rental, rent and operating costs after the cash cost of expansion. The annual-report operating review describes the revenue streams and cost base.[4]

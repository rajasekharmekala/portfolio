1. What the company does

Some terms recur in this report, so they are defined once here. RSI, the relative strength index, is a gauge from 0 to 100 of how fast a price has moved, and a reading below 30 is called oversold. ATR, the average true range, is the typical daily price swing in rupees. EBITDA stands for earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, which is operating profit before those four items. ROCE, return on capital employed, is yearly profit before interest and tax as a share of the capital in the business. EPS, earnings per share, is profit divided by the number of shares. FCF, or free cash flow, is cash from operations less capital spending. A price-to-earnings ratio is the share price divided by a year of earnings per share. The gross margin is sales less the cost of goods, as a share of sales. The operating margin is operating profit as a share of sales. The debt-to-equity ratio is borrowings divided by shareholders' equity. Guidance is what management says it expects.

Some chart and order terms recur as well. Stage 2 is a rising trend and Stage 4 is a falling one. A base is a stretch where the price moves sideways after a fall. A pivot is the price level at the edge of a base. A breakout is a close above that level. A retest is a later return to a level to see whether it holds. A limit order is an order to buy at a stated price or lower. The entry zone is the price range where we would place one. Asymmetry, also called reward-to-risk, is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop, and this report calls it the gain-to-loss ratio.

KRBL Limited mills, ages, packs and sells rice, mostly basmati, the long-grain aromatic rice grown in northern India. It calls itself India's first integrated rice company: it buys paddy (rice in the husk) from farmers, stores and ages it, mills it, and sells it under brands, the best known being India Gate.[6] The company was incorporated in 1993 by Anil Mittal, Anoop Gupta and Arun Gupta, and Mr Mittal still leads the company as chairman and managing director.[6][3] CARE puts installed milling capacity at 207 tonnes an hour, and the company also makes by-products such as bran oil, furfural and de-oiled cake and sells some electricity.[6] In FY26 about 71.6 percent of standalone revenue came from domestic rice sales and 25.5 percent from exports, which means two different customers pay KRBL: Indian households and shops, and importers abroad.[4][5]

Domestic customers buy packs through distributors, shops, modern trade chains and online platforms. India Gate's market share at the June 2026 exit was 37 percent in general trade, 38 percent in modern trade and 41 percent in e-commerce, on the company's own measure.[1] Management says the brand leads in about 75 percent of Indian states, but that its share in traditional trade fell by about 2 percentage points from a year earlier.[3] Export customers are mostly in the Middle East. The annual report says the Gulf region took 59 percent of KRBL's international basmati revenue in FY26, Europe 11 percent and North America 8 percent.[4] Middle East buyers take close to three quarters of all Indian basmati exports, so KRBL is not unusual in this respect, but it means a shipping or payment problem in that region reaches KRBL's sales within weeks.[3][7]

The money is made in the gap between what KRBL pays for paddy and what it charges for aged rice, less the cost of holding the stock. Rice improves with age, so KRBL holds a large inventory: ₹2,944 crore at 30 June 2026, which is 389,000 tonnes of rice and 71,000 tonnes of paddy.[3] That is why working capital (the money tied up in stock and unpaid bills, net of what the company owes suppliers) is the main use of capital here: the five-year table in chapter 3 shows 329 inventory days at March 2026.[2] The annual report says stock is valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value, with cost fixed on a moving weighted average, and the auditors treat inventory valuation as a key audit matter.[4] A weighted average blends old, cheap purchases with new ones, so when paddy prices rise the profit on each sale can look unusually high for a while before the new, dearer crop works through the stock.

Policy sets the terms of trade. The APEDA list of government notices, read to 30 September 2026, shows no basmati export restriction among its 2026-27 entries, so we do not treat export policy as a live catalyst.[8] Basmati going to the European Union needs an inspection certificate under a 2019 notification.[9] The United States cut its reciprocal tariff on India from 25 percent to 18 percent in February 2026, after KRBL's November 2025 call had described a 50 percent levy on Indian rice, and on the August 2026 call an analyst described the 18 percent duty as settled while management said the US market is stable for KRBL.[10][11][3] The United States is a small market for KRBL, so the tariff matters less than the Middle East.[11]

  • Ageing means storing rice for months so the grain lengthens and firms when cooked. It is why KRBL holds so much stock.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The strength of KRBL is a brand with a large share, a buying network that reaches the farm, and a balance sheet with no real debt. India Gate holds 37, 38 and 41 percent of packaged basmati sales in general trade, modern trade and e-commerce, with a lead of about 10 percentage points over the next brand online.[1][3] Borrowings were ₹170 crore against equity of ₹5,807 crore at March 2026, which is a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.03, and the June 2026 presentation shows cash and investments of ₹1,841 crore with no net bank borrowing.[2][1] ICRA reaffirmed its AA(Stable) rating on ₹1,540 crore of working-capital limits on 21 October 2026, and CARE put FY26 interest cover at 108.8 times.[16][6] Because it can fund a large crop purchase from its own cash, it is not forced to sell stock at a bad moment, as the chairman said on the August call.[3]

The weakness is that the earnings are those of a commodity processor. The profit from each sale depends on the gap between paddy cost and rice price, and that gap has moved a lot: the operating margin before other income was 24 percent in FY18, 16 percent in FY22, 12 percent in FY25 and 15 percent in FY26.[2] Over three years sales grew 4 percent a year and profit fell 3 percent a year, and return on equity for the last three years was 11 percent.[2] Growth is also uneven by channel: modern trade rice volume grew 6.5 percent in Q1 FY27[3] but management says its share in parts of modern trade needs work, and the traditional-trade share fell about 2 points.[3] The brand is strong, but it is not widening its lead everywhere.

Capital allocation has one item that does not fit the rice story. The FY26 annual report records ₹40,694 lakh (about ₹407 crore) added to investment property, which the report says is not related to the rice business.[4] That is about 7 percent of equity, put into land that earned only ₹79 lakh of rent in FY26.[4] Other income also matters. Interest, investment gains and similar items made ₹70 crore in FY26 and ₹64 crore in the June 2026 quarter alone.[2][3] Chapter 3 tests how much of profit that is.

The legal and governance conditions

The Enforcement Directorate (ED) registered a case in 2014 and filed a criminal complaint in 2021 alleging an offence under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act against KRBL, its Dubai subsidiary KRBL DMCC and one of its joint managing directors, over USD 24.62 million said to have passed through a customer in the AgustaWestland helicopter case between 2008 and 2010.[17] The joint managing director was arrested in January 2021, according to a news report from the time.[18] The company's note says the case is pending before a special court at the stage of serving summons on remaining accused, with the next hearing set for 15 September 2026 (the outcome is not verified in our record), and that management has found no adjustment or extra disclosure necessary.[17] The statutory auditors do not agree that the matter is closed: they have qualified their opinion on both the standalone and the consolidated FY26 statements, saying that with the investigation pending they cannot comment on any adjustment that may be required, and they qualified the June 2026 limited review the same way.[4][17] Land worth ₹1,532 lakh at Dhuri in Punjab is attached by the ED.[4]

A qualified opinion is not a finding of fraud, and the 2025 AZB review found no irregularity affecting the financial statements in relation to the director's observations. It did not address the ED case and is not judicial clearance of it.[15] It does mean that the auditors are unable to vouch for one item, and it has lasted for years, so the legal condition cannot clear within the next two quarters. On the August call the finance chief declined to take questions on the matter because it is before the court.[3] This condition, not the price chart, is why the status in this report is deferred. Flaw 1: the margin may not hold while the new crop costs more

The weakest assumption behind the central earnings is that the operating margin stays near 14.5 percent while paddy costs more. A year ago Pusa 1509 paddy opened at ₹2,500 to ₹3,200 a quintal, and by February 2026 the 1121 variety traded at ₹4,300 to ₹4,700.[3] A Times of India report of 18 October 2026 puts Pusa 1509 near ₹4,000, up from about ₹3,000 last season.[7] KRBL's stock at 30 June carries rice at about ₹63.8 a kilo (₹2,481 crore over 389,000 tonnes) and paddy at about ₹40.3 a kilo, both our arithmetic from the call.[3][5] Because stock is costed at a moving weighted average, older and cheaper purchases may have helped the June quarter and may not be there later.

1. What the company does

In plain words: ARAPL builds robotic welding lines and parking systems on fixed-price projects. This chapter covers how it earns, its size and trading, and the terms we use.

Affordable Robotic & Automation Limited, called ARAPL here, designs, builds and programs robotic welding lines for vehicle plants, and builds automated multilevel car parking systems. Its plant is at Wadki near Pune, a site of 3,50,000 square feet with more than 400 staff[3]. It is not a new listing. The shares began trading on the BSE SME platform on 4 June 2018 after a Rs 22.79 crore issue at Rs 85, and the company later moved from the SME platform to the main boards of the BSE and NSE (the annual reports give FY25, 2023 and 2024 in different places, and we could not confirm the date)[21][6]. Our NSE price history starts on 23 October 2024, so it holds 494 sessions.

The company wins turnkey projects at fixed prices. A customer orders a line or a parking system, the company designs and builds it, and it books the sale when the goods are dispatched, not as work proceeds[2]. Revenue is therefore lumpy. Consolidated net revenue was Rs 117.67 crore in FY26 against Rs 162.56 crore in FY25, and the four quarters of FY26 ran from Rs 18.77 crore to Rs 50.93 crore[1][16][24]. Named customers include Honda, Tata Motors, Mahindra, Bajaj and Piaggio for welding, and Lodha, Ruparel and Columbia Asia for parking[3]. The annual report gives no customer shares.

A third line is Humro, a warehouse robot business held through the subsidiary ARAPL RaaS Private Limited. RaaS means robots as a service: customers lease the robot and pay over time. ARAPL holds 83.54 percent of it. A term sheet of 9 April 2026 offered up to Rs 48 crore of equity from Sai Green Projects Private Limited in tranches, and by 13 August the first tranche of Rs 24 crore had been received[3][18][10]. The company says Humro has 16 robots deployed, 8 in trials and Rs 36 crore of three-year lease contracts[3][10].

2. Why the business is strong or weak

In plain words: the engineering is real and the cost reset worked, but sales are lumpy, customers pay slowly and the promoter funds the company.

The case for the business is real engineering skill, repeat customers and a cost reset. A secondary summary of a November 2025 filing said 80 percent of its order book was repeat business[3][19]. In FY26 it chose margin over volume. Material cost fell Rs 42.33 crore and staff cost fell Rs 11.99 crore. EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) before other income went from minus 2.0 percent of revenue to plus 11.8 percent[3][1]. The parent made a profit in each of FY24 to FY26: Rs 6.07 crore, Rs 5.99 crore and Rs 6.96 crore[3][5][6][17].

The case against it is the cash behind that profit, the small size and the funding. Revenue fell 27.6 percent in FY26. The June 2026 quarter was Rs 11.03 crore, down 41 percent[3][9].

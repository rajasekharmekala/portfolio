Chapter 1

PCBL Chemical Limited began in 1960 as Phillips Carbon Black Limited. Today it is India's oldest and largest carbon black maker and a flagship arm of the RP-Sanjiv Goenka (RPSG) Group. Its roots lie in a joint venture between US energy major Phillips Petroleum Company and Kolkata-based Duncan Brothers & Co. Ltd., part of the Goenka family's holdings. Output started on June 5, 1962, at Durgapur, West Bengal, with 14,000 metric tonnes per annum (MTPA) of capacity. At that time, Indian tire makers relied heavily on imported fillers. Over the next six decades, Phillips Petroleum exited and the Goenka family consolidated control under the RPG and later RPSG Group. The firm renamed itself PCBL Limited in 2021 and later PCBL Chemical Limited to reflect its push beyond rubber-grade carbon black.

PCBL's stock cycles track three clear eras of capital allocation. In its first fifty years, it grew as a domestic rubber-grade carbon black supplier tied to Indian truck and car tire demand. In 1996, it added plants in Kochi (via Carbon and Chemicals India Ltd.) and Palej, Gujarat (via Gujarat Carbon Ltd.). In 2009, it built a large coastal plant at Mundra, Gujarat. Because carbon black reactors produce combustible tail gas, PCBL added co-generation power units at its sites, reaching ~122 MW of green power. Still, in the early 2010s, high debt, Chinese dumping, and volatile Carbon Black Feedstock (CBFS) prices kept margins weak.

PCBL spent FY15–FY23 building a 17%–25% ROCE cash-generative carbon black franchise with modest debt, then levered up nearly 5x in FY24 to buy Aquapharm—compressing consolidated ROCE to 8% in FY26.

The second era (FY18–FY23) showed what tight execution can do in a cyclical business. From FY15 to FY17, management cut gross debt from ₹1,220 crore to ₹758 crore while anti-dumping duties curbed cheap Chinese and Russian imports. As plant use rose, operating margins jumped from 6% in FY15 to 17% in FY19 and 19% in FY21. Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) peaked at 25% in FY19 and stayed at 16%–18% through FY23. During those years, PCBL built the Sushila Goenka R&D Centre at Palej and shifted mix toward higher-margin specialty blacks for plastics, inks, and coatings. It kept debt below ₹1,030 crore even as sales more than doubled from ₹2,558 crore in FY18 to ₹5,774 crore in FY23.

The third and current era began in January 2024, when PCBL pivoted from organic growth to a large debt-funded buyout. Through a new unit, Advaya Chemical Industries Limited (merged January 1, 2025, and renamed Aquapharm Chemical Limited), PCBL bought 100% of Pune-based Aquapharm Chemicals Private Limited for ~₹3,800 crore EV. On paper, the deal looked strong. Aquapharm was India's largest and a top-two ex-China maker of phosphonates. It served global leaders like Procter & Gamble, Unilever, Henkel, and Ecolab from plants in India, the US, and Saudi Arabia, posting FY23 sales of ₹2,045 crore and EBITDA of ₹417 crore (20.4% margin).

In practice, the Aquapharm deal reshaped PCBL's risk profile. Gross borrowings jumped nearly fivefold from ₹1,029 crore in March 2023 to ₹4,983 crore in March 2024 and ₹5,571 crore in March 2025. Debt eased to ₹4,989 crore in March 2026 and stood at ₹5,083 crore in September 2026. At the same time, Aquapharm's profits dropped due to weak US oilfield demand, soft European chemical orders, and Chinese price pressure. Aquapharm posted just ₹1,443 crore in sales and ₹162 crore in EBITDA (11.2% margin) in FY26. With core carbon black spreads also tight, consolidated net profit fell 54.5% YoY from ₹435 crore in FY25 to ₹198 crore in FY26. ROCE compressed to 8% (7.75% latest), and both CRISIL (January 2026) and ICRA (February 2026) cut PCBL's AA rating outlook from Stable to Negative.

Historical Failure-Mode & Base-Rate Twin: Rain Industries Limited (India / Europe (2012–2024))

Shared Vulnerability: An Indian industrial carbon producer (calcined petroleum coke) used heavy debt to acquire Belgium-headquartered Rütgers NV for €702 million (~₹4,900 crore) in 2012–2013 to gain European coal-tar pitch and specialty chemical scale. While the combined entity achieved vertical integration, the sticky acquisition debt amplified cyclicality whenever raw material (GPC/coal tar) spreads compressed or European industrial demand slowed.

Historical Numbers & Base Rate: Gross debt remained structurally elevated for over a decade, net-debt-to-EBITDA spiked above 3.5x–4x during industrial downcycles, and consolidated ROCE repeatedly compressed into single digits whenever carbon spreads normalized, forcing management into multi-year debt-prepayment mode rather than high-ROIC expansion.

How PCBL Compares Today: In carbon and industrial chemicals, acquiring an overseas specialty platform with debt does not immunize consolidated returns from cyclicality; if the acquired asset's EBITDA dips simultaneously with core spreads, fixed interest and depreciation charges crush net profit and ROCE.

The Corporate Identity Shift: Utility Carbon to Leveraged Multi-Chemistry

From FY18 to FY23, PCBL earned 16%–25% ROCE by running coastal plants hard, selling tail-gas power, and funding specialty lines from cash flow. That model was easy to underwrite.

By taking ₹3,800 crore of debt for Aquapharm while funding a 90,000 MTPA Tamil Nadu plant and Nanovace R&D, PCBL traded a clean balance sheet for complex multi-country risk. The key test is whether Aquapharm and Nanovace can earn back their cost of capital.

A look at each plant shows how steady PCBL's organic buildout was before 2024. After Durgapur opened in 1962, the 1996 buys of Kochi and Palej gave PCBL coastal and western access at low cost. Mundra opened in 2009 around export container lanes and captive tail-gas power. Even the 2021–2024 Tamil Nadu program—adding automated reactors with zero-liquid-discharge controls—was paced with internal cash flow. By contrast, the January 2024 Aquapharm deal caused a sharp jump in capital intensity. Fixed Assets surged from ₹1,968 crore in March 2023 to ₹6,892 crore in March 2024, adding heavy goodwill and intangibles to the balance sheet.

Promoter actions during this leveraged phase offer one helpful signal. Unlike weak acquirers that pledge shares or dilute at distressed prices, RPSG Group entities (Rainbow Investments, Quest Capital Markets, and STEL Holdings) put in fresh equity via convertible warrants across 2024–2025. That raised promoter ownership from 51.41% (Dec 2023–Sep 2025) to 53.38% (Dec 2025–Sep 2026). Domestic funds (DIIs) also lifted their stake from 5.61% (March 2024) to 13.72% (September 2026), while retail ownership fell from 35.66% to 26.57%. Strong backing buys time, but it does not change the math: ₹5,083 crore of debt must be paid from operating cash flow.

Rubber carbon black provides scale and 97%+ plant utilization, while specialty black, captive power, and Aquapharm are meant to supply the margin engine—though Aquapharm is currently operating well below its historical 17%–20% EBITDA margin.

Rubber-grade carbon black is the volume core, making up ~88.7% of PCBL's 618,956 MT sales in FY26. Made by burning heavy aromatic oils (CBFS) in high-heat reactors, rubber black makes up 25%–30% of a modern tire by weight. It gives tires tensile strength, wear resistance, and heat control. A radial truck tire cannot run at highway speeds without grade-specific carbon black. PCBL sells to Indian tire majors and global OEMs, splitting FY26–H1 FY27 volume roughly two-thirds domestic and one-third export. In Q1 FY27, total carbon black volume reached 153,530 MT [7], [8]. In Q2 FY27, PCBL cut low-margin spot exports 18% QoQ to 41,337 MT against 92,843 MT domestic (134,180 MT total) [6], [7].

Specialty carbon black is PCBL's main organic margin driver. With capacity expanded to 112,000 MTPA (including 20,000 MTPA added at Mundra), PCBL sold 69,635 MT of specialty grades in FY26 (~11.3% of carbon black volume, with Q4 FY26 reaching 19,000 MT, up 26% YoY). Unlike tire blacks, specialty grades ship in smaller lots for non-rubber uses. They add jetness and UV protection to black HDPE pipes, tint car paints and inks, and give controlled conductivity in high-voltage power cables. Winning approval for a single specialty grade takes 12 to 24 months, creating much higher switching costs than standard ASTM rubber grades.

Aquapharm Chemical Limited marks PCBL's move outside carbon chemistry. It runs plants in India (Pune/Mahad/Pirangut), the US (Texas), and Saudi Arabia (Jeddah) with ~130,000 MTPA base capacity plus 38,000 MTPA of brownfield additions. Aquapharm makes three product lines. First, organophosphonates (HEDP, ATMP, DETPMP under Aquacid) stop scale buildup in boilers, cooling towers, and desalination membranes. Second, green chelates (GLDA, HEIDA, IDS) replace banned phosphates and EDTA in dishwasher pods and laundry liquids for P&G, Unilever, and Henkel. Third, oilfield chemicals (imidazolines, quats, scale inhibitors) protect oil and gas wells from mineral scaling. Roughly 50% of Aquapharm's sales come from North America, 20% from Europe, 17% from India, and 13% from the rest of the world.

Finally, PCBL is building a battery and conductive materials arm for EVs and grid storage. Beyond Energia conductive grades and a planned 1,000 MTPA super-conductive line, PCBL signed a tech deal with China's Ningxia Jinhua Chemical for a 5,000 MTPA Acetylene Black plant. Acetylene Black is an ultra-pure additive used in LFP/NMC lithium-ion cathodes and high-voltage cables. At the same time, majority-owned Nanovace Technologies Ltd. (with a Palej pilot plant and US patents) is developing nano-silicon anode materials with 5–6x the energy density of graphite. While aligned with India's EV cell push, these battery units are pre-commercial and remain long-term options rather than near-term profit drivers.

Why Segment Mix Matters More Than Headline Revenue

Because carbon black prices pass through crude and CBFS moves, headline sales can swing without showing true volume health. For example, FY26 sales dipped 3% to ₹8,189 crore even though carbon black volumes hit a record 618,956 MT.

On the other hand, Aquapharm gets 50% of its sales from North America and 20% from Europe. That exposes PCBL to US rig counts and European factory slowdowns that have no link to Indian tire demand.

Chapter 3

PCBL's supply chain is built around two physical facts of carbon black. First, it is bulky to ship long distances relative to its value. Second, its main input—Carbon Black Feedstock (CBFS), a heavy oil from refining and coal tar—makes up 65%–70% of operating costs. So PCBL placed its main Indian plants near deep-water ports and tire hubs. Mundra and Palej in Gujarat serve western auto hubs and Arabian Sea shipping lanes. Kochi sits near southern rubber belts and port berths, while Durgapur serves eastern industry. The automated Tamil Nadu complex sits right in the Chennai auto belt (Apollo, MRF, Michelin, JK Tyre) with easy coastal export access.

Western tire and coatings buyers demand just-in-time delivery rather than 45-day sea transit. To serve them, PCBL built warehouses in Antwerp, Belgium, and leased railcar fleets in the United States. Aquapharm's supply chain relies on yellow phosphorus, phosphorus trichloride (PCl3), and specialty amines across Mahad and Pirangut (India), Texas (USA), and Jeddah (Saudi Arabia). Local plants in Texas and Jeddah shield part of Aquapharm's US and Middle East volume from ocean freight spikes. However, Indian exports to the US and Europe still face container rate and tariff swings.

Core carbon black unit economics rebounded strongly in H1 FY27 after management sacrificed low-margin spot exports, whereas Aquapharm's EBITDA margin remains stuck near 10%—roughly half its pre-acquisition level.

A key trait of carbon black economics is the gap between formula contracts and spot timing. About 70%–75% of domestic rubber black sells on quarterly formula contracts that pass through CBFS moves with a one-quarter lag. When crude and CBFS drop fast—as in parts of FY26—two pressures hit at once. First, PCBL takes inventory losses on higher-cost CBFS in its tanks; second, spot rivals running cheaper fresh oil undercut export prices, pulling blended EBITDA/t toward ₹13,500/t. When management holds price discipline—as in Q2 FY27, when it cut low-margin exports 18% QoQ—underlying EBITDA/t snaps back to ₹17,500/t (and ₹19,800/t with a ₹2,300/t US tariff refund).

Working capital is the other quiet driver of unit economics. When crude falls, lower input prices shrink the rupee value of receivables and inventory, freeing cash. Between FY25 and FY26, PCBL cut debtor days from 78 to 62 and inventory days from 80 to 65 while holding payable days near 92. That cut the Cash Conversion Cycle from 57 days to 35 days and pushed Working Capital Days to -40 days. It explains why FY26 generated ₹1,576 crore of operating cash flow even as net profit fell 54.5%. Investors must note that this cash tailwind reverses when crude oil prices rally.

Read EBITDA per Tonne Ex-One-Offs and Normalize Working Capital

Do not treat one quarter's headline EBITDA/t or 158% CFO/EBITDA conversion as a steady state. In Q2 FY27, ₹2,300/t of the ₹19,800/t reported EBITDA/t came from a one-off US tariff refund.

Likewise, ₹851 Cr of FY26 Free Cash Flow got a boost from falling input prices freeing working capital. When crude rises, inventory absorption will pull CFO/EBITDA back toward 70%–85%.

Co-generated green power is the quiet stabilizer inside PCBL's conversion-cost structure. Every tonne of carbon black manufactured in a furnace reactor yields high-calorific tail gas; rather than flaring it, PCBL routes that gas into captive steam turbines totaling ~122 MW across Durgapur (30 MW), Palej (12 MW), Kochi, Mundra, and Tamil Nadu. This self-generated electricity covers plant parasitic loads—primarily high-pressure air blowers, pelletizers, and bag-house filtration—and exports surplus megawatts to state grids. Because tail-gas power carries near-zero marginal fuel cost once the reactor is running, it acts as a structural ₹1,500–₹2,000 per tonne cost buffer relative to unintegrated smaller producers.

Chapter 4

The global carbon black market—~15 million MTPA, with over 70% used in tires and rubber—is regional because low bulk density makes long ocean hauls costly. In India, the market is a tight oligopoly led by PCBL Chemical (~35%–40% share), Birla Carbon India, Himadri Speciality Chemical, Continental Carbon India, and Epsilon Carbon. High capital cost, emission permits, and 12–24 month OEM tire approval cycles keep out new entrants. Even so, pricing power in standard rubber grades faces two caps. Large tire buyers (MRF, Apollo, CEAT, JK Tyre, Balkrishna) hold strong buyer power, while Chinese coal-tar producers and re-routed Russian supply periodically dump excess tonnage.

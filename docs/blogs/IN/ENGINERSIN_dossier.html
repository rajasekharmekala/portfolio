Chapter 1

Engineers India Limited (EIL) started on March 15, 1965, as a joint venture between the Government of India and Bechtel International Corporation of the United States [4]. Its core goal was to build local engineering skill so India would not rely on foreign firms for oil refineries. In 1967, EIL became a wholly owned Government of India firm [4]. It soon began work on the Madras Refinery project. In 1969, it moved into petrochemicals with aromatics and olefins plants at Vadodara for IPCL [4]. Over the next forty years, EIL became the technical backbone of India's energy build-out. It designed offshore platforms starting with Bombay High North in 1979 [4]. It engineered the 1,800-kilometre Hazira-Bijapur-Jagdishpur (HBJ) gas pipeline for GAIL in 1986 [4]. In 1993, it also commissioned India's first 1.2 million TPA hydrocracker unit for IOCL at Baroda [4].

Market access and state status arrived in steady steps. In 1994, EIL formed its wholly owned inspection unit, Certification Engineers International Limited (CEIL) [4]. It won Mini Ratna status and listed on the BSE and NSE in 1997, then ran a Follow-on Public Offer (FPO) in 2010 [4]. On June 24, 2014, the Government of India gave EIL Navratna status [4]. That step gave the board more freedom over joint ventures and investments [4]. That same year, EIL won its largest overseas job: Project Management Consultancy (PMC) and Engineering, Procurement, and Construction Management (EPCM) work for the 650,000 BPSD Dangote Refinery and Petrochemical Complex in Nigeria [4], [8]. Today, the Government of India holds a 51.32% promoter stake under the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas (MoPNG) [1].

EIL runs an asset-light core business, yet it has also put surplus cash into minority stakes and joint ventures. In 2015, EIL teamed up with National Fertilizers Limited (NFL) and Fertilizer Corporation of India (FCIL) to form Ramagundam Fertilizers and Chemicals Limited (RFCL) [7], [18]. EIL took a 26% equity stake (₹491 crore total equity investment) to revive a gas-based urea plant in Telangana [7], [18]. In 2020, EIL bought a 4.37% stake in Numaligarh Refinery Limited (NRL) for ₹838.41 crore [4], [18]. Over the past ten years, EIL's sales and margins moved through four clear cycles [1], [19]. Domestic refinery upgrades, lumpy turnkey EPC work, and global order wins drove those swings [1], [19].

EIL's cycles show that sales volume alone rarely drives returns. Margins expand sharply only when high-margin Consultancy outpaces low-margin Turnkey work.

The gap between the FY18–FY20 upcycle and the FY21–FY25 plateau shows EIL's core tension. In FY19 and FY20, BS-VI refinery upgrades lifted consolidated sales from ₹1,824 crore to ₹3,237 crore [1]. However, most of that extra volume came from Turnkey EPC contracts [1]. As Turnkey work grew, operating profit margin (OPM) fell from 24% in FY18 to 14% in FY20 [1]. OPM sank further to 9% in both FY23 and FY24 [1]. Consolidated sales stayed stuck between ₹2,913 crore and ₹3,330 crore for five straight years [1]. That flat stretch explains why Screener shows a modest 4.55% five-year sales growth rate [1].

The shift in FY26 and H1 FY27 moves EIL back toward its highest-return engine. Consolidated sales jumped 27% in FY26 to ₹3,928 crore [1]. OPM doubled from 9% in FY24 to 18% in FY26, reaching 21% on a trailing twelve-month basis [1]. Fresh orders hit ₹7,978 crore in FY26 and ₹8,100 crore in FY27 to date [15], [19]. Those wins pushed the standalone order book to a record ₹20,421 crore by September 2026 [15], [19]. Consultancy makes up 75% of that backlog [15], [19]. The 2018–2020 BS-VI wave relied on low-margin Turnkey pass-through billing. By contrast, today's cycle rests on multi-year overseas EPCM contracts [10], [18], [19]. Key wins include Dangote's Train-2 refinery expansion (~US$360 million) and a four-train fertilizer plant in Nigeria [10], [18], [19]. EIL also won an Ethiopian fertilizer complex (₹286 crore) and a greenfield refinery job in Mokowe, Kenya [10], [18], [19]. At home, EIL is expanding into data centers, airports, and non-ferrous metals [10], [18], [19]. For an owner, EIL is entering a multi-year phase backed by engineering fees rather than construction pass-through billing. Chapter 2 breaks down each segment in detail.

Chapter 2

Engineers India Limited reports two main segments under Ind AS 108: Consultancy & Engineering Projects, and Turnkey Projects [13], [15]. Turnkey Projects include Lump-Sum Turnkey (LSTK) and depository-basis work [13], [15]. Both units serve energy, petrochemical, fertilizer, metal, and infrastructure clients, yet their economics differ sharply [15], [19]. In Consultancy & Engineering, EIL sells design know-how, Front-End Engineering Design (FEED), Project Management Consultancy (PMC), and EPCM man-hours [6], [15]. Only EIL's fee enters revenue, yielding segment margins of 22% to 31% [15], [20]. In Turnkey Projects, EIL handles equipment buying, fabrication, and site erection [15], [20]. Equipment and contractor costs flow through EIL's sales line, lifting revenue while keeping normal margins at 4% to 8% [15], [20].

That 17% margin spiked due to a 38% Q3 FY26 margin from project closeouts and change-order settlements [19], [20]. One real project from each unit shows how the two models work on site. In Consultancy & Engineering, EIL's top overseas job is the Dangote Refinery and Petrochemical Complex in Nigeria [10], [18]. In FY26–FY27, that mandate grew to add a Train-2 EPCM job (~US$360 million / ₹3,000+ crore) and a four-train fertilizer plant [10], [18]. EIL also won a ₹286 crore PMC/EPCM fertilizer contract in Ethiopia [10], [18]. On EPCM jobs, EIL runs feasibility studies, writes engineering packages, vets global vendors, and oversees site crews. The client pays suppliers and builders directly, while EIL bills only for engineering hours and oversight [15]. As a result, EIL carries low working-capital and material-cost risk, and segment margins stay at 24%–30% [15].

In Turnkey Projects (LSTK and depository work), EIL acts as prime contractor for state firms like IOCL, BPCL, HPCL, or ISPRL [5], [15]. Work covers refinery revamps, tankage terminals, and institutional buildings [5], [15]. On a turnkey package, EIL designs the unit, buys long-lead gear, hires site crews, and commissions the plant [15]. Because equipment and material costs pass through EIL's income statement, sales jump during peak site work [15], [20]. However, operating margins sit at 4%–7% during early work and only jump when final change orders settle at closeout [15], [20].

Oil refining and petrochemicals still drive most sales, yet EIL has built new verticals in infrastructure, metals, water, and clean energy [5], [10]. Recent wins include completing the Reserve Bank of India's Greenfield Data Centre and Training Institute at Bhubaneswar [10]. EIL also serves as Independent Engineer for Noida International Airport and advised on the Shri Ram Temple Complex at Ayodhya [10]. In metals, it won a project management job for Hindustan Copper's ₹7,189 crore mine expansion [10]. It is also pursuing nuclear balance-of-plant work and coal gasification studies backed by a ₹34,000 crore state viability policy [15], [18]. In clean energy, EIL is building a 5 TPD compressed biogas (CBG) plant [15], [18].

Two equity-accounted stakes and one subsidiary complete the group structure [3], [7]. Certification Engineers International Limited (CEIL), a 100% subsidiary, handles third-party audits, safety checks, and offshore certification [7]. Ramagundam Fertilizers and Chemicals Limited (RFCL) is a 26% joint venture with NFL and FCIL running a 3,850 TPD urea plant in Telangana [7], [15], [18]. After years of early losses, RFCL turned profitable in H1 FY27, adding ₹63 crore to EIL's consolidated profit (₹42 crore in Q1 and ₹20 crore in Q2) [7], [15], [18]. Finally, EIL's 4.37% stake in Numaligarh Refinery Limited (NRL) brings steady dividends, including ₹21 crore in Q2 FY27 [15], [18]. For an owner, group profit now blends high-margin fees, cyclical turnkey work, and steady JV and dividend income. Chapter 3 traces how this mix turns into unit cash flow.

Each segment carries a distinct risk profile for shareholders. In Consultancy, EIL bills for engineering hours, design reviews, and site supervision [6]. Fixed overhead stays stable, so incremental revenue drops straight to operating profit [15][20]. In Turnkey EPC, EIL buys equipment from third-party vendors and manages site subcontractors [6][19]. Margins stay thin at 4% to 8%, and unexpected site delays can quickly erode profits [20]. Watching the revenue split between these two engines explains most quarterly earnings surprises [15][19].

Geographic diversification also reshapes segment margins. Overseas consultancy jobs in Nigeria and the Middle East often carry higher billing rates per man-hour than domestic PSU contracts [8][15]. As international work scales toward 55% of the ₹20,421 Cr backlog, blended Consultancy realization improves [15][26]. However, overseas execution demands local visa compliance, foreign-currency hedging, and strict milestone sign-offs [11][17]. Monitoring regional revenue split alongside segment mix helps track this margin tailwind [14][15].

Chapter 3

EIL wins work on two distinct tracks: domestic Public Sector Undertaking (PSU) jobs and global competitive tenders [3], [15]. At home, EIL holds deep ties with state energy majors IOCL, BPCL, HPCL, ONGC, GAIL, and NRL [3], [15]. It wins PSU orders through competitive bids and strategic nominations, such as underground crude oil caverns for ISPRL [3], [15]. EIL has engineered 20 out of 23 refineries and 11 out of 12 mega petrochemical complexes in India [3], [15]. That track record puts EIL at the table during early feasibility and licensor-selection studies, years before construction tenders open [3], [15].

Overseas, EIL cannot rely on PSU ties and must win on technical score and price [15]. To serve global clients, EIL runs an engineering hub in Abu Dhabi for ADNOC and Gulf national oil firms [3], [10]. It also opened an office in Saudi Arabia backed by a long-term services agreement (LTA) process with Saudi Aramco [3], [10]. Procurement and sales teams sit in London, Milan, and Shanghai [3], [10]. In Africa, EIL used its ten-year Dangote tie to win repeat multi-thousand-crore jobs in Nigeria and Ethiopia [3], [8], [10]. Government of India Lines of Credit (LoC) also helped EIL win refinery work in Mongolia and power and NGL oversight in Guyana [3], [8], [10].

EIL's supply chain relies on skilled people rather than heavy plants [3]. As of March 31, 2025, EIL employed over 2,600 staff, including more than 2,300 engineers and technical experts [3]. That bench delivers roughly 3.5 million design-office man-hours and 1.3 million site-management man-hours each year [3]. Teams work across New Delhi, Gurugram, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Vadodara, and Abu Dhabi [3]. On Consultancy jobs, EIL's main inputs are internal engineers, design software, and R&D licensor ties [3], [6]. On Turnkey (LSTK) jobs, EIL manages external equipment makers, civil builders, and inspection teams, aided by subsidiary CEIL [7]. Because EIL owns no heavy yards or crane fleets, it avoids fixed depreciation drag. Still, it faces vendor delays, slow client sites, and contractor disputes on turnkey packages [13], [15].

Operating cash flow hit ₹8.12 per ₹100 (₹319 crore), and low capex of ₹1.71 (₹67 crore) left ₹6.42 in free cash flow (₹252 crore) [1].

Chapter 4

EIL competes in a concentrated EPCM and turnkey industry where technical skill, safety records, and balance-sheet strength block new entrants [3], [17]. In Indian hydrocarbon consultancy, EIL faces global peers like Technip Energies, Worley, Wood, and Toyo Engineering [22]. It also competes with local private firms such as L&T Hydrocarbon Engineering and Tata Consulting Engineers [22]. In non-hydrocarbon infrastructure, water, and metals, EIL meets state peers MECON and RITES alongside private EPC firms. Pure construction builders fight on thin labor and material spreads. By contrast, EIL wins at the front end: feasibility studies, process licensing, FEED, and owner's engineer PMC [3], [6]. Switching costs are high at that stage, so technical pedigree matters more than the lowest fee [3], [6].

EIL's competitive moat rests on three durable pillars [3], [10]. First is deep domestic refinery incumbency: EIL has worked on 20 of India's 23 refineries, totaling over 200 MMTPA of capacity [3], [5]. It has also engineered 11 of 12 mega petrochemical plants, 248 offshore platforms, and 5.33 MMT of underground crude caverns [3], [5]. Second is proprietary process IP: EIL's Gurugram R&D Centre has built over 40 process technologies, often with IOCL-R&D, BPCL-R&D, and IIP [3], [10]. As of FY26, EIL holds 58 granted patents with 74 applications pending [3], [10]. Third is balance-sheet strength: EIL carries almost zero debt (₹17 crore in FY26) plus ₹1,606 crore in investments and large cash reserves [1], [17]. That cash lets EIL post multi-billion-dollar sovereign bank guarantees that leveraged EPC rivals struggle to back [1], [17].

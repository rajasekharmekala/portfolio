2 What the company does

Aarti Pharmalabs makes chemical ingredients that other companies turn into medicines and nutritional products. The main product family is xanthine derivatives, which are caffeine-type compounds used in beverages and pharmaceuticals. Screener describes the company as an "internationally recognized manufacturer of generic API & Intermediates, Xanthine derivatives, and offers CDMO /CMO services," with six manufacturing plants and three R&D centres.[1] The company's own website describes the same activities: the development of Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients (API) and New Chemical Entities, API intermediates, and Xanthine Derivatives for clinical testing and commercial production.[34] An API, or active pharmaceutical ingredient, is the chemical that does the work in a medicine. A CDMO is a contract manufacturer that makes drugs for another company under its own process and quality system. The plain version is that the company sells raw chemicals, sells the service of making them, and in one case sells the service of developing and making a drug for someone else.

Demerged from Aarti Industries in October 2022, the company was previously its wholly owned subsidiary.[1] That history matters for one of the risks in section 8. The demerger has produced a tax and stamp-duty dispute, and the company now carries its own balance sheet and borrowing.

The business in three parts

The three product lines behave differently, so the mix shapes the results. Xanthines are the largest. In the June 2026 quarter they were about 57 percent of turnover, with the highest quarterly sales the company has recorded, according to the earnings-call transcript.[13] The same summary puts API and intermediates at about 160 crore rupees and CDMO and CMO at about 37 crore, or 7 percent, for the quarter.[12] Xanthine prices move with raw material costs and with the supply from other producers, so this line gives the largest swings in profit.

The company has been adding xanthine capacity. A source on the quarter reports production capacity rising to 9,500 metric tonnes from about 6,000, a 50 percent increase that was commissioned.[12] The two printed capacity figures, 6,000 and 9,500 tonnes, imply a rise of about 58 percent, so the source's percentage is quoted as its own figure. The company has also sought environmental clearance to take API and intermediate capacity at Tarapur from 14,688 tonnes a year to 45,641.52 tonnes a year. That application is not an approval, and the report does not count the capacity as available.[23] Management said in the June quarter that it had completed a couple of expansion projects.[35] What this means is that the company is buying growth with capital, and the growth is only worth something if the plants run full at good prices.

Ownership and the register

Ownership tells you who is in control and whether the people running the company have their own money at stake. The promoter group held 42.86 percent of the shares in the June 2026 quarter, down from 46.46 percent in March 2024.[1][20] A falling promoter stake is not a breach of any rule, but it means the group has been selling or diluting over two years.

Pledging is the more important ownership question. When a promoter pledges shares as collateral, a fall in the price can force a sale the company did not choose. Trendlyne reports that the total pledge stands at 11.08 percent of promoter holdings as of the June 2026 quarter.[20] A separate filing summary describes a March 2026 pledge of about 3.08 percent of the total share capital, as collateral for a financing of 75 crore rupees.[19] The report does not judge the purpose of the loans. It records that 11.08 percent of the promoter stake is pledged, and that this sits alongside a falling price trend.

Demand context

Demand for these products depends on two things: the number of medicines and beverages that use them, and the number of producers able to supply them. The company itself says the competition for many of its xanthine products is concentrated in China, which means Chinese supply sets much of the price.[13] Management said in the call that prices had "come to lower level from the peak," and that realisation per kilogram would fall while volumes rose.[13] The report does not have a market-size figure for xanthines that it can cite to a primary source, so demand is treated as an open question rather than a number.

6 Layer 3 entry posture

A support level is a price where buyers have stepped in before, and moving averages serve that role here. The ladder reads as follows: within 0.5 ATR of support is the full-size band, within 1.0 ATR is the standard band, and within 1.2 ATR is a reduced band that accepts only a limit order. A limit order is an instruction to buy only at a set price or better. Beyond that is the no-buy band, where the rule says to wait for a pullback, a retest or a new base.[33]

8 Fatal flaws and red team

A red-team review asks what would make the decision wrong, and it puts the strongest honest case against the report's own conclusions. The four points below are the risks a careful reader should weigh. Each has a number attached, so it can be checked.

Fatal flaw one: the return on capital is below the floor and falling

Return on capital is the measure that most directly decides whether a company creates value from the money invested in it. A reading of 10.7 percent means that every rupee of capital earns about 11 paise of operating profit, before tax and interest. The ratio fell from 17 percent in FY25 to 11 percent in FY26, as profit dropped from 272 to 175 crore.[1] The standalone accounts show a 32 percent fall in profit for the year.[9] The new plants add capital ahead of the profit they will earn, so the ratio may stay low for some time. The report cannot say when it will recover, only what level would pass.

Fatal flaw two: the cycle is in the price of xanthines

More than half of the quarter's revenue came from one product family, and its price is set in part by supply from other producers. The June 2026 quarter benefited from pricing that management called exceptional, and it reported that prices had come off the peak.[12][13] A business whose profit is set by one commodity-like price can post a strong quarter and a weak year, and the FY26 result shows that pattern already. The rules use three-year totals for this reason, and the three-year return is still below the floor.

Fatal flaw three: the capital programme is growing faster than the cash it earns

Borrowings rose from 413 to 754 crore in one year, and interest cost rose from 27 to 47 crore. Free cash flow has been negative for two years.[1] The company has a 149 crore intermediate-block project to fund through internal accruals and borrowings, and it is also expanding its Tarapur capacity.[15][23] On the reported equity of 2,124 crore, the debt limit of 0.70 would be reached at about 1,487 crore of borrowings, roughly double the current level. The debt test still passes, but the room is shrinking as the programme proceeds.

Fatal flaw four: a pledge and a legal claim on the company's cash

Promoter pledges stand at 11.08 percent of promoter holdings as of June 2026, and a March 2026 pledge was reported as collateral for a 75 crore rupee financing.[20][19] The company also received a stamp-duty and penalty order of 59.5 crore rupees dated 29 August 2026, linked to the 2022 demerger, and has filed a writ petition in the Gujarat High Court.[18][1] That amount is about 34 percent of FY26 profit. The company has filed a writ petition against the order, and the outcome is not known.[1] If the order were paid in cash, it would reduce the room for the capital programme. The report does not assume an outcome.

The red team's view of the rejection

The strongest honest case against the rejection is this. The latest quarter is the best in the company's recent record. Sales rose about 39 percent and profit about 52 percent year on year, and CRISIL has reaffirmed the company's rating at AA-/Stable on 9 October 2026, with a stable outlook.[16][7] The company guides to 15 to 18 percent revenue growth over the next three to four years (StockFin note, dated 1 June 2026).[10] On that view, the return will rise as the new plants fill, and the rules are applied too early. The reply is that the rules ask for the return the business earns today, not the return it expects. One quarter does not move a three-year record that is still below the floor, and the new capacity has not yet earned anything. A stock that passes the return test in a later filing can be re-run then. Until it does, the rule stands.

The hinge claim

The claim on which the verdict most depends is the ROCE reading. The consolidated header shows 10.7 percent, the standalone header 11.4 percent, and the FY26 ratio row 11 percent.[1][2] All three are below the 15 percent floor. The falsifier is a reported consolidated ROCE of at least 15 percent in a later filing, which would clear the floor on the rule's own definition. A second, independent claim is that no verified target reaches the 3.0-to-1 core floor of 1,431.79 rupees. Clearing that alone would not clear the momentum gate.[32]

9 Verdict, action and triggers

The verdict is REJECTED. The return on capital fails the 15 percent floor. The asymmetry test fails as FAIL_ASYMMETRY, because no verified target reaches the floor. The momentum gate fails in code as NO_MOMENTUM. Under the rules, a Layer 1 failure rejects the stock, and Layers 2 and 3 are not evaluated. The strategy bucket is REJECTED, because neither the core nor the tactical bucket is allowed while Layer 1 fails.

What this means for you

For a reader deciding whether to add new money to AARTIPHARM, the answer today is no. The rules give three reasons, each with a number: a return of 10.7 percent against a floor of 15, a reference reward of 0.67 to 1 even at the 52-week high, against a floor of 3.0 and with no verified target, and a momentum gate that is 4.86 ATR from its base against a limit of 1.2. The report does not cover any decision about an existing holding, which the portfolio rules govern separately. The levels in the triggers below are reference levels that mark where the trend would break. They are not orders.

  • Trend review level: a close below the 50-day average of 848.15 on two consecutive sessions. The Stage 2 posture would then fail.

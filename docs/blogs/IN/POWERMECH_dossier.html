Power Mech Projects builds and services power plants in India, and it also does civil work, plant maintenance and coal mining.[1][3] This report rejects the stock for now, because the chart is in a topping stage, the company turned only 59 paise of every rupee of profit into cash over three years, and only three of five operating tests pass.[1][6][7] The business is sound: debt is low, the order book is large, and the shares sell at 19.3 times earnings against a three-year median of 29.2.[1][2][3] The rating changes if the price closes back above its falling 50-day average and the company shows real cash collection in the half-year numbers. 1. What the company does

Power Mech Projects was founded in 1999 and is based in Hyderabad. It sells skilled people, heavy equipment and project management to the owners of power plants and other large sites.[1][3] Its customers include BHEL, the Adani group, JSW, Vedanta, state power companies, Indian Railways and coal miners.[3][4][9] A plant owner pays Power Mech either to build part of a plant, to keep a running plant healthy, or to dig coal.

The business has five lines. Erection, testing and commissioning (ETC) means fitting boilers, turbines and generators and switching them on. Civil work means foundations, cooling towers and coal-handling structures. Operation and maintenance (O&M) means running and repairing plants under contracts of three to five years. Engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) is a turnkey job; the company now takes balance-of-plant (BOP) packages. The newest line is mine developer and operator (MDO), where it builds a coal mine and then runs it for 25 years or more.[3][4]

In the June 2026 quarter the company booked ₹1,624 crore of revenue. Civil work gave ₹796 crore, about 49% of the total. O&M gave ₹431 crore, about 27%, and mining gave ₹84 crore, about 5%.[3][9] Those shares are our arithmetic on the reported figures. The mix matters because the lines earn different margins. Management says O&M earns 18% to 20%, while the whole company earned an operating margin of 12.3% in FY26. The operating margin is the share of revenue left after operating costs.[3][9]

Most of the money goes out to others. In FY26 the company paid ₹3,612 crore of contract execution costs, which is about 59% of its ₹6,107 crore total revenue. Employee cost was ₹755 crore and materials ₹869 crore.[3] So the firm runs a large site operation with thin spreads. A small slip in cost or a late payment moves profit a lot, which is why cash collection gets so much attention in this report.

How the mining line works

In mining, Power Mech acts as developer and operator for a state-owned miner. It builds the mine infrastructure, removes the overburden (the soil and rock above the coal), digs and delivers the coal, and is paid a fee per tonne for 25 years or more.[4] Two contracts are running. The Tasra mine for SAIL has a value of ₹9,294 crore. The Kotre Basantpur Pachmo (KBP) mine for Central Coalfields has a value of ₹30,300 crore.[4] Coal production at KBP began in November 2025, and a coking-coal washery at Tasra is due in December 2026.[4]

These contracts are why the order book reads ₹55,398 crore with mining and only ₹16,229 crore without it.[3] The mining work is large but slow: management expects ₹500 crore of mining revenue in FY27 and ₹1,100 to ₹1,250 crore in FY28.[9][24] It also takes capital. The two contracts call for about ₹1,680 crore of investment by the company (₹330 crore plus ₹1,350 crore, our sum), against equity of ₹2,519 crore.[1][4]

Who owns the company

The promoter group holds 58.42% of the shares as of September 2026, and the founder, Sajja Kishore Babu, holds 24.01% himself. None of the promoter shares is pledged to lenders.[16] Promoter holding was 60.40% in December 2023 and has stayed between 58.29% and 58.41% since the middle of 2024.[1] Mutual funds hold 20.17%, led by the HDFC Infrastructure Fund at 8.19%.[16] Foreign funds hold about 6.3%. The number of shareholders fell from 79,426 to 69,526 in the September quarter.[1] This is a clean register: no pledge, long-run founder control and steady institutional holders.

Overseas work is small at 4% of revenue, split between plant maintenance in the Gulf and a long contract at the Dangote complex in Nigeria.[9] The Middle East conflict has raised the cost of steel, gases and diesel, and management says its price-variation clauses return most, but not all, of that cost after a delay of a quarter.[9]

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Order book: signed work not yet billed. Power Mech reports it with and without MDO, because the 25-year mining contracts are large but pay slowly.[3]
  • ETC: erection, testing and commissioning of plant equipment. BOP: balance of plant, the pipes, pumps and structures around the boiler and turbine.
  • O&M: operation and maintenance contracts for running plants. MDO: mine developer and operator contracts. EPC: a turnkey build contract.
  • CFO/PAT: cash flow from operations divided by profit after tax. A value of 1.0 means profit arrived as cash.
  • Certification: the customer's sign-off on work done. Until it happens, the company cannot bill, and cash stays tied up.[8]

2. Why the business is strong or weak

Power Mech is one of India's largest independent power-plant service firms. It says it holds about 16% of the Indian power O&M market and looks after 36.25 GW of plants at home and more than 42 GW worldwide.[3][4] It has erected equipment for about 84 GW of power capacity, including the first two ultra-mega power projects at Mundra and Sasan.[4][8] CARE Ratings rates its bank debt A+ with a stable outlook, and counts a track record of more than 27 years as a strength.[8]

Where the business is strong

The first strength is skill that takes years to build. The company says it has about 4,000 engineers and 45,000 to 50,000 workers, and that its bids for power work succeed roughly 60% to 65% of the time.[9] Private plant owners such as Adani and JSW buy schedule certainty, and management says they prefer firms that have delivered before.[9] That is a real if narrow edge. It protects share, but it does not let the firm set prices.

The second strength is a large and growing book of work. The executable order book, which leaves out the mining contracts, was ₹16,229 crore at the end of June 2026 and ₹17,317 crore by late September.[3][4] That is 2.7 times FY26 revenue of ₹6,062 crore on the June figure (our calculation).[1][3] The O&M backlog alone rose to ₹3,322 crore. New O&M wins keep arriving, including a ₹549 crore five-year contract for a 2x600 MW Adani plant at Tuticorin.[1][9][23]

The third strength is the mining option. The mining order backlog still to be executed was ₹39,253 crore in September 2026, on two MDO contracts that run 25 years or more with state-owned counterparties.[4] CARE notes that the mining fee is set in advance and that margins should rise as production peaks from FY28.[8] Management expects MDO revenue of ₹500 crore in FY27 and ₹1,100 to ₹1,250 crore in FY28.[9][24] If that happens, the mix shifts toward longer, steadier income.

Where the business is weak

The first weakness is dependence on a few big buyers. Management put the unfinished work at BHEL at about ₹5,300 crore, at Adani at about ₹2,400 crore and at Vedanta at ₹1,551 crore.[9] Together that is ₹9,251 crore, or 57% of the ₹16,229 crore executable book (our calculation). A delay in BHEL tendering, which management admits is happening, slows the whole order flow.[9]

The second weakness is slow cash. Customers must certify work before the company can bill, and that takes time. Debtor days were 93 in FY26.[1][6] CARE counts 268 days of gross current assets and 165 collection days, and says unbilled revenue and advances to subcontractors are the cause.[8] The Uttar Pradesh water programme is the clearest case: management says about ₹900 crore of that order is waiting for state funds to restart.[11]

The third weakness is that new businesses carry new risks. Capital commitments rose from ₹92 crore to ₹1,016 crore in a year.[6] The company is building a coal washery and a road project at the same time.[4][8] On 15 October the company issued a ₹469 crore guarantee for its subsidiary KTMPL.[1] CARE expects term debt to rise in FY27 and calls execution risk for developer projects significant.[8]

Fourth, the company has been hit by events it does not control. A West Bengal order for battery storage worth ₹1,563 crore was cancelled in FY26.[11] A change in royalty rules cut the margin of its KRBM royalty-monitoring contract from 14% to about 10%.[9] These are not signs of a poor firm. They show how thin the cushion is when a single contract changes.

Competition and pricing power

Power Mech rarely competes with the large equipment makers; it usually works for them. Management tracks about 58,000 MW of thermal orders placed with BHEL and L&T in three years. BHEL took the bulk of the orders, about ₹2.41 lakh crore, and L&T took ₹42,523 crore.[9] When BHEL wins a plant, Power Mech bids for the erection, civil or balance-of-plant part. The price is set by a bigger buyer, so the firm earns a contractor's margin of about 12%.[3]

Price protection is partial. Most contracts carry price-variation clauses, but the indices update with a delay, and work done after the contract period is settled through claims.[9] In mining, the company was the lowest bidder for an NMDC contract, but NMDC re-tendered it because of a pricing issue.[9] These examples show a firm that wins on delivery and price discipline, not on pricing power.

Demand for the next five years

India plans to add at least 80 GW of coal-based capacity by 2031-32, according to the Ministry of Power.[22] The company's own deck uses the Central Electricity Authority plan that lifts coal capacity from 231 GW to 291 GW by FY32.[4] Each new unit needs civil work, erection and, once running, maintenance. That is the demand the company is built to serve. The risk is timing: orders have been slower than the plans, and BHEL packages have been delayed.[9] What this means for an owner: the demand is real, the question is whether the company can convert it into cash at its current working-capital intensity. Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Management gives clear targets on every call, which makes it easy to check them. We read the May 2024 and May 2025 calls and compared each target with the later filed result.[13][15] The pattern is consistent. The debt and FY26 margin promises were kept; the FY25 margin was narrowly missed. Revenue, order and mining promises were not kept, and the gaps were large.

Revenue guidance, which is management's official forecast, has been too high two years running. In May 2024 management set a 30% growth goal for FY25, about ₹5,500 crore.[15] In August 2024 it narrowed the goal to 25% to 30%.[14] The company reported ₹5,234 crore, up 24.4%.[3] In May 2025 it set ₹6,500 crore for FY26.[13] In February 2026 it cut the figure to ₹6,100 to ₹6,200 crore and then to about ₹6,000 crore.[12] The reported figure was ₹6,062 crore.[3] The cut came in time, which is honest, but each year the first number was 5% to 7% too high.

Order targets were missed by more. The FY25 order target was ₹12,000 crore and the company won about ₹6,350 crore (the slide says ₹6,347 crore and the May 2025 call said ₹6,437 crore).[4][13][15] The FY26 target was ₹10,000 crore and it won ₹7,210 crore, which is 72%.[5][11][13] The cancelled battery order of ₹1,563 crore explains more than half of the FY26 gap of ₹2,790 crore.[11] The mining forecast was also far off: management assumed ₹300 crore of MDO revenue in FY25 and reported ₹84 crore.[13][15]

The FY27 plan is still open. Management guides revenue of ₹7,300 crore, an operating margin of 12.5% and ₹12,000 crore of orders.[9][10] June-quarter revenue grew 26%, but the margin was 10.78% and orders were ₹1,864 crore, or 15.5% of target.[3][9] Given the record, we plan on a lower revenue figure than management does.

Four red lines ban the stock outright if crossed: debt-to-equity of 1.20 or more, interest cover under 2.0, a pledge of 20% or more of the promoters' shares, and cash conversion under 0.40 with no turnaround.

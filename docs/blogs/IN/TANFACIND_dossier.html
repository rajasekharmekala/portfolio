AHF and ordinary diluted HF: different grades, different evidence

Anhydrous hydrofluoric acid (AHF) contains no water or has water tightly controlled; it differs from diluted aqueous HF. TANFAC’s AHF expansion was commissioned in October 2024, increasing capacity from 14,850 to 30,000 TPA for about ₹100 crore. The FY26 capacity disclosure lists 30,000 TPA HF capacity and 95% utilisation. AHF is supplied in bulk ISO containers and used as an input in refrigerants and other fluorinated chemicals. In an illustrative buyer journey, a chemical producer receives a controlled bulk input and incorporates it into its own downstream manufacturing. The customer and its exact internal process are not identified. [1][2][3][11][33]

Management gave a Q1 FY27 AHF realization of ₹275–₹280 per kilogram and said AHF contracts are typically monthly or quarterly rather than long-term. Raw-material cost pass-through generally lags by 30–45 days. This makes the quoted realization a useful dated reference point, not a current guaranteed price, a margin disclosure or a forecast. Management later said AHF gross profit per tonne remained broadly stable in H1 FY27 even though gross-margin percentage was lower amid higher realizations, sulphur, new-product ramp-up and power, fuel and freight. These statements describe the operating backdrop, not a separately reported AHF profit line. [3][33]

Ordinary diluted HF is made to a required concentration and application; TANFAC lists it separately from solar-grade DHF. The company’s product and presentation materials include glass etching among end-market contexts, but they do not provide a product-specific customer list, price, volume or revenue share. An illustrative glass processor might specify a diluted grade for an etching or cleaning step and require consistent deliveries. Do not turn this archetype into an actual TANFAC customer claim, and do not equate ordinary diluted HF with solar-grade material merely because both are aqueous HF products. [12][33]

TANFACIND: Scenarios, Judgment & the Proof Required

Core Investor Question: At ₹3,415.95, does TANFAC offer enough growth and execution evidence to justify the market's expectations, and what observable proof would change the answer?

Decision in one sentence

At the verified 6 November 2026 close of ₹3,415.95, TANFAC is a credible operating-growth story but not yet a proven cash-compounding story. Our judgment: WAIT over 1–12 weeks; WAIT FOR PROOF over 3–18 months; WATCHLIST over 3–10 years, and STAY AWAY from new money until free cash flow turns positive and growth converts into cash. The 8 November stock-snapshot quote of ₹3,431.90 differs slightly from the exact-date market ledger; all price comparisons here use the 6 November close. [24][25]

The central analytical divide is timing. The Bull sees integrated assets, high HF utilization, first-mover solar DHF, contracted growth and a large HFC-32 opportunity. The Bear sees a single site, unreported product economics, declining H1 margins, negative FY26 FCF, execution risk and a valuation that discounts success before it is visible. Our conclusion recognizes the assets but requires proof through shipments, margin, cash conversion and capex discipline.

Bull steelman: integration plus contracted adjacency

TANFAC's case starts with an operating platform. Fluorspar and sulphur feed an integrated chain from sulphuric acid and oleum to HF and downstream products. FY26 sulphuric acid/oleum capacity of 100,000 TPA ran at 101%; HF capacity of 30,000 TPA ran at 95%. Captive power with waste-heat recovery, domestic sulphur sourcing, fluorspar suppliers in several countries and access to Chennai port support manufacturing continuity. These figures show a scaled base with high utilization in the core acid streams, rather than an untested project company. The strongest version of the Bull is that integration and process discipline can support reliable supply and retain more value as mix moves downstream. [1][3][33]

Solar-grade DHF is the differentiated opportunity. TANFAC reports trace elements below 10 ppb and use in solar-wafer texturing, cleaning and etching; it describes itself as India's first or only such platform. Capacity increased from 20,000 TPA at FY26 close to 25,000 TPA by the 2 November 2026 presentation after a ₹47 crore internally funded debottleneck. The named orders—₹732 crore ex-GST from Kredence Electronics Materials India and ₹336 crore ex-GST from Krishna Organics, both through FY2028-29—give ₹1,068 crore of disclosed visibility. The releases say no additional capex is required to execute those orders. A Bull can reasonably argue that local supply, quality qualification and disclosed multi-year demand provide a bridge to scale. The counterweight is that contract price formulas, product margins and current coverage of the expanded capacity are unpublished. [4][8][9][13][33]

HFC-32 could be a second step-change. The 20,000 TPA facility was expected to commission in Q3 FY27 according to the 2 November deck. Company slides describe supply arrangements covering about 65% of capacity. The January Japanese-customer release names 7,500 TPA of fluorinated chemicals for seven years from 1 January 2027, with estimated annual value ₹337.5 crore and 37.5% of planned 20,000 TPA downstream capacity; that release does not identify the product as entirely HFC-32. Other dated disclosures refer to an MNC MoU and an air-conditioning/commercial-refrigeration agreement. The Bull's thesis is that a commissioned plant, contracted take-up and management-described formula-based economics can lift customer reach and revenue. The confirmation required is output shipped and cash contribution—not capacity or contract value alone. [2][3][5][7][33]

Bear steelman: the operating proof is behind the valuation

The Bear does not need to deny demand. It questions whether growth will create enough incremental profit and cash. FY26 revenue was a record ₹711.1 crore, yet EBITDA declined to ₹112.1 crore from ₹128.9 crore and PAT to ₹70.1 crore from ₹88.1 crore; EBITDA margin was 15.8%. H1 FY27 revenue grew 21.2% to ₹417.7 crore, but EBITDA grew 5.6% to ₹60.6 crore, margin fell to 14.4% from 16.6%, and PAT declined 1.6% to ₹35.9 crore. Q2's 13.3% EBITDA margin remained below the prior-year quarter's 16.2%, despite higher revenue and EBITDA. Management cited higher realizations, sulphur, new-product ramp and power/fuel/freight. This is the crucial test: do new volumes improve economics, or dilute them while absorbing capital? [1][33]

FY26 CFO of ₹43.4 crore less ₹82.4 crore of PPE/CWIP cash purchases produced FCF of negative ₹39.0 crore; CFO was 61.9% of PAT. The H1 balance sheet also needs a careful definition of net debt because borrowings, cash, investments and other bank balances are separately presented. QIP and preferential proceeds bolster liquidity but issue new equity; they do not demonstrate internally generated cash. FY26 fluorspar and sulphur costs were ₹313.4 crore and ₹104.6 crore, respectively, and management described a 30–45-day pass-through lag for AHF raw-material costs. High utilization does not prevent margin volatility, working-capital needs or cost overruns. [1][3][33]

The risk set is concentrated and incompletely observable. TANFAC reports one Fluoro-Chemicals segment and operates one Cuddalore site. It does not disclose product-level profits or map every displayed customer logo to a product, period and revenue share. Specialty inorganic fluorides utilization was 41% in FY26. HFC-32 project cost estimates changed by date and scope: the Q2 deck showed ₹360 crore committed and ₹231 crore spent by 30 September, while Q1 cited ₹390 crore and ₹315 crore committed and earlier materials described an approximately ₹495 crore package. These should be reconciled, not silently added. One site's commissioning or operating interruption could matter, while external readers cannot fully assess customer concentration or contract protection. [1][2][3][4][5][33]

Regulation makes HFC-32 a growth opportunity with a finite transition question. R-32's GWP is around 675–677: lower than R-410A, but still an HFC and neither zero-emission nor immune from policy change. U.S. HFC phasedown guidance and EU Regulation 2024/573 are relevant transition signals. Honeywell's U.S. arrangement with Arkema expands access to lower-GWP HFO blends; GFL in India is a larger, more diversified fluorochemical group whose FY26 report includes R-32; Arkema's European Forane 32 page markets R-32 for air conditioning and heat pumps. These parallels establish competitive context, not direct peer economics or an imminent demand cliff. The Bear's strongest claim is that long-lived capital should account for substitution and policy risk. [27][28][30][31][32]

Our view: strong assets, insufficient cash proof

The Bull is strongest on the physical platform, high utilization in HF and sulphuric streams, named solar-DHF orders, and the possibility that HFC-32 increases scale. The Bear is strongest on margin conversion, free cash flow, project-cost ambiguity, concentration and valuation. We side with the Bear on entry discipline, not with a claim that the business has no future. At 101.77x TTM earnings in the TradingView snapshot, versus differing vendor estimates around 107x, TANFAC needs sustained delivery. Comparisons with larger peers are screening context only; business mixes and vendor definitions are not identical. The reverse-DCF in the evidence pack is explicitly an expectations test: even assuming 8–12% normalized FCF margin, it implies 30.67–36.62% revenue CAGR for ten years under its stated assumptions. The point is not that the model is a price target; it is that execution must be exceptional relative to today's price. [23][24]

Our independent conclusion is conditional: keep the company on a watchlist, but demand observable milestones before committing new capital. First, confirm HFC-32 commissioning and saleable output, then compare actual shipment and utilization with the disclosed agreements. Second, test whether margin improves after the H1 pressure, separating product mix and ramp costs from durable price or input changes. Third, reconcile project spend and scope against dated disclosures. Fourth, follow operating cash flow, working capital and capex together; a rising PAT line without positive FCF is not enough. Fifth, track delivery of the ₹1,068 crore solar orders and request evidence of economics, not just order headlines. These are decision rules, not predictions that every milestone must arrive by an exact quarter. [2][3][8][9][33]

What changes the verdict

Upgrade evidence: management confirms commissioning and the next results show sustained HFC-32 output; customer arrangements translate into shipments and cash; solar DHF contracts progress against capacity; margins recover for explained and repeatable reasons; project cost is reconciled; FCF turns positive after capex. Downgrade evidence: commissioning delay or low utilization, margin deterioration, rising working capital without collection, further capex escalation, loss or repricing of contracted volume, or evidence that policy transition materially shortens the addressable HFC-32 runway. A single quarter can prompt review, but should not by itself establish a long-term trend. [2][3][5][7][8][9][27][28][33]

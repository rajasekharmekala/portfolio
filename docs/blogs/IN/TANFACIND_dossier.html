TANFAC makes hydrofluoric acid and downstream fluorine chemicals at one Cuddalore site. Its solar-grade diluted HF line now has contracted orders, while a 20,000-tonne HFC-32 project is the next major earnings test. The chart is in Stage 2, but the share price sits above the support band and at roughly 105–112 times trailing earnings. 1. What the company does & day-to-day vertical operations

TANFAC Industries is a fluorochemical manufacturer based in Cuddalore, Tamil Nadu. It is a joint-sector company promoted by Anupam Rasayan and Tamil Nadu Industrial Development Corporation. Its product list spans anhydrous HF, diluted HF, solar-grade DHF, sulphuric acid, aluminium fluoride, potassium fluoride, potassium bifluoride and specialty complexes. Company filings describe the Cuddalore site as the manufacturing base; they do not publish a segment revenue split or a full product-level profit bridge. That limits how precisely an investor can model each line. [24] [25]

The platform starts with fluorspar, sulphur and other inputs that must arrive on time and meet tight quality controls. The company says it sources fluorspar from a range of countries and discusses sulphur-price swings on calls. The broad operating sequence is inbound receipt, safe storage, chemical conversion, product finishing, laboratory checks, packaging or bulk transfer, then customer dispatch. TANFAC does not disclose a proprietary process flow or unit cost by product, so this account stays at the operating level the company reports. [1] [3]

Hydrofluoric acid is the central intermediate. AHF and dilute HF feed direct industrial demand and downstream products. Captive HF also supports solar-grade DHF and the planned HFC-32 refrigerant line. The value-chain benefit is real only when a downstream line earns more after conversion, utilities, depreciation and customer qualification than the acid it consumes would earn elsewhere. Management describes backward integration as a source of supply security, but the filings do not give a per-tonne transfer price or return on capital by product. [3] [8]

Solar-grade DHF is a high-purity product sold to solar-cell and wafer manufacturers for silicon surface treatment, cleaning and etching. TANFAC states that its product is designed for very low trace impurities, and identifies wafer texturing, cleaning and etching as uses. That makes batch consistency, contamination control, packaging and customer qualification central to the sale. A customer must validate the chemical in its line; repeat volumes depend on yield and process compatibility, not just a published specification. [29]

The legacy acid and fluoride lines serve a broader industrial set. AHF goes in ISO containers for refrigerants, fluorine compounds and petrochemical uses. Diluted HF and aluminium fluoride are supplied for customer-specific industrial applications. Sulphuric acid is both a saleable product and part of the site’s chemical system. The public product pages describe specification and application, but they do not give utilization or profit by product. The investor has to read the capacity data alongside total margins rather than mistake a long catalogue for a broad earnings base. [28] [30] [31]

For a new product, the commercial work begins well before full-rate output. TANFAC must qualify customer samples, agree purity and packaging, secure shipment requirements and establish a contract or order. Long-term supply terms can reduce the risk of an empty plant, but they do not guarantee take-or-pay cash flow unless the filed contract says so. The public filings describe several arrangements as supply contracts or orders; customer names and full formula terms are not always public. We treat the company’s contracted volume claims as management disclosures, not as proof of margin or payment quality. [13] [14] [16]

The daily operating risks are unusually tangible for an acid and fluorine site: feedstock quality, corrosion, storage, process safety, power, environmental controls, planned shutdowns and logistics. The annual report describes formal safety and environmental systems. This matters because a single-site interruption can affect every vertical, while a customer switching a qualified chemical may require another approval cycle. The company does not disclose a separate-site backup or detailed insurance recovery for a prolonged shutdown. [4] [24]

The working-capital loop runs from imported and domestic inputs, through production and laboratory release, to dispatch and customer collection. FY26’s reported working-capital cycle was 91 days, eight days better than the prior year. That remains a meaningful use of capital for a company funding projects at the same time. Strong orders improve utilization visibility; they do not remove receivable, inventory or shipment timing risk. [3] [7]

Where the value is created

TANFAC is trying to move from merchant acid sales toward higher-value grades and fluorinated products. Higher-purity DHF has qualification and contamination barriers. HFC-32 adds a larger market and a new product set, but also adds commissioning risk, startup yield, gas-handling requirements and fresh customer acceptance. The company has already raised capacity in HF, but the next margin step depends on downstream plants running at saleable quality and converting contracted volume into cash. [3] [8]

This is an asset-heavy chemical business. Maintenance and safety spend protect uptime; capex has added depreciation and diluted near-term margins. Growth is not automatically value creating: the relevant test is incremental after-tax return on the funds invested. TANFAC has not yet published HFC-32 operating data, realized prices, customer-level contribution margins or a cash-payback bridge. Until it does, the project should be valued as an opportunity with execution risk, not a completed profit stream. [3] [9]

2. Competitive moat, industry structure & external learning hub

TANFAC’s edge is its larger HF base, plant experience and approved high-purity products. The company reports about 30,000 tonnes per year of HF capacity, with FY26 utilization at 95%. Its solar DHF line is the first domestic supply position claimed by management. High purity and repeat approvals can make a switch harder. They do not stop a customer from choosing another source on price, quality or supply. [3] [4] [9]

That edge has limits. TANFAC is smaller and more concentrated than the large listed fluorochemical groups. SRF sells refrigerants, propellants, industrial chemicals and fluoropolymers. Gujarat Fluorochemicals has a wide fluorine product range. Navin Fluorine has a larger mix of specialty products and advanced chemistry. Their size, product mix, research spend and end markets differ. We compare business breadth, not share valuation. [36] [37] [38]

HFC-32 has a different test. Management says the project is supported by agreements for about 65% of capacity, with cost pass-through and a fixed EBITDA contribution on the contracted share. That can speed market entry and reduce raw-material volatility, but the remaining capacity and future renewals will face spot pricing and supply competition. Customers are not named in the Q1 call, so independent concentration analysis is not possible. [1] [8]

Fluorspar supply and sulphur prices are key outside risks. Management reported that sulphur moved sharply during Q1 and said pass-through contracts take 30–45 days to catch up. That delay can cut reported margin before prices reset. The 15.3% Q1 operating EBITDA margin shows that pass-through does not prevent near-term volatility. [1] [2]

HFC-32 has a lower global-warming impact than some older blends. It is still an HFC, so future rules and other refrigerants can limit demand. We have not used a long-run quota path in the valuation because the company has not disclosed a product-level demand forecast. New electronic-grade chemicals also need customer approval and investment. [1] [8]

Competitive set: different scale, overlapping chemistry

SRF is a broad peer in refrigerants and fluorochemicals, with more sites and a wider product set. Gujarat Fluorochemicals combines refrigerants, fluoropolymers and other fluorochemicals. Navin Fluorine has a larger research-led mix of specialty and advanced products. TANFAC may grow faster from a smaller base, but it depends more on one site, a few large projects and a smaller group of customers. Plant execution and customer approval matter more here than a broad demand headline. [36] [37] [38]

We do not compare peer ratios because the businesses, fiscal periods and project costs differ. Better tests are product range, customer mix, plant use, cash collection, research and the ability to fund growth without repeated share sales. The reported ₹250 crore QIP improves funding flexibility, but it also increases the share count and raises the bar for incremental returns. [17] [18]

Five operating indicators give a cleaner view than a generic revenue-growth target: HF utilization, solar-grade contract coverage, HFC-32 commissioning, margin recovery after raw-material shocks, and cash conversion. A sixth indicator is specialty-fluoride utilization, which shows whether the existing product base is gaining traction. TANFAC reports most of these at company level; it does not publish unit economics by vertical. [3] [7] [8]

The growth plan now has three steps: run the solar-grade DHF capacity at its intended rate, complete and ramp HFC-32, and turn the downstream pipeline into products with repeat orders. The first step is in execution; HFC-32 remained under construction as of the cutoff. Revenue guidance is ambitious, while Q1 profit was lower year on year. The evidence therefore supports a growth option, not yet a proven earnings inflection. [1] [3]

Is the company reaching its potential & are the CEOs walking the talk?

The HF expansion provides one completed promise to audit. In February 2023, TANFAC announced an increase from 14,850 to 29,700 tonnes per year and gave a 15–18 month implementation window. On 7 October 2024 it announced the capacity expansion, roughly 20 months after the announcement. That is about two months beyond the upper end of the original window, despite management describing the project as on time on the FY26 call. Once commissioned, the HF line reached 95% utilization in FY26. The record is mixed: a modest schedule miss, followed by strong operating use. [3] [7] [9]

The solar DHF build is a better execution result. TANFAC’s FY25 report expected capacity to reach 20,000 TPA within the next financial year; company notices record phase commissioning in June and October 2025. The FY26 annual report confirms both phases and an order book of ₹1,068 crore through FY29. That meets the stated window and supports real loading, although sales, margins and cash conversion still need to be shown by quarter. [6] [11] [12] [4]

HFC-32 is not yet a delivered commitment at this cutoff. The Q1 FY27 call kept the commissioning target at the end of Q3 FY27, with about 65% of capacity under long-term arrangements. We cannot score timing, first-pass quality, ramp rate or project return until a later filing reports production and customer deliveries. The November 3 Q2 call falls after this report’s 30 October market cutoff and has not been used. [1] [20]

In the Q1 FY27 call, Chief Executive Officer Hemango Gupta said, “our focus has shifted from commissioning to ramping up production” [1]. That makes DHF throughput and customer execution the next proof points. President and Chief Financial Officer N. R. Ravichandran said, “pricing contracts generally operate on a cost pass-through mechanism” [1]. The contract design cushions input swings over time, but it does not remove the 30–45 day recovery lag or the margin pressure already visible in Q1.

6. Key risks, thesis breakers & bear case

The bear case starts with price. A company can deliver revenue growth and still disappoint if margins, cash conversion or per-share returns fall short. TANFAC’s current valuation leaves little room for a commissioning delay or a lower HFC-32 netback. The strongest counterpoint is that the market may already be discounting high growth; that is why waiting for support alone does not solve the fundamental risk. [1] [3] [25]

The thesis rests on one hinge: can the expanded HF base feed qualified downstream products at attractive incremental returns without a long period of low utilization or working-capital strain? If HFC-32 is late, if contracted volumes are conditional, or if pass-through terms lag input costs, the promised earnings slope flattens while depreciation and equity dilution remain. [1] [3] [17]

A high multiple can unwind faster than earnings grow

The live earnings base is around ₹33.35 per share on Screener, with negative TTM EPS growth in the consumer snapshot. At roughly 105–112x trailing earnings, a lower exit multiple could offset operating progress. The valuation assumes a high probability that new plants work well. [25] [26]

Project cost and timing are not fully reconciled

The current disclosures cite ₹395 crore and ₹495 crore project costs. The larger number may include utility and other value-added scope, but no reconciliation is present in the reviewed filings. The original HF expansion also slipped beyond the outer implementation window. Each new project therefore needs a dated cost-to-complete and a commissioning milestone, not just a target quarter. [1] [3] [9] [15]

Margins depend on raw materials, ramp and pass-through lag

Sulphur and fluorspar move with global supply, shipping and currency. TANFAC says customer contracts pass costs through, yet the Q1 call described a 30–45 day lag and reported 15.3% EBITDA margin. At a premium valuation, even temporary margin pressure can move earnings estimates. [1] [2]

Customer and site concentration are hard to measure

The company does not identify every HFC customer or publish a customer concentration table. Its major manufacturing base is Cuddalore. Any prolonged outage, delayed customer qualification or loss of a large contract could weigh heavily on a small earnings base. Order values over multiple years should not be read as guaranteed annual sales. [3] [13] [16]

Equity raising helps funding, but dilutes per-share returns

The ₹250 crore QIP reduced reliance on debt and was intended to fund strategic projects. Shareholders accepted dilution before the main investment had reached full output. If incremental return on capital trails the existing business, the balance sheet can be safer while per-share value still falls. [18] [19]

Fluorochemical rules and substitution change demand

HFC-32 has a near-term role in air conditioning and refrigeration, but long-run refrigerant choice depends on regulation, equipment design and lower-GWP alternatives. Solar-grade DHF depends on continued customer expansion and competitive pricing. Neither market should be extrapolated in a straight line from current contracts. [1] [3]

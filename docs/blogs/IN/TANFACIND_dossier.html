Chapter 1

Tanfac Industries Limited (TANFAC) was incorporated on December 20, 1972 [1]. It was formed as a joint-sector enterprise between TIDCO and the Aditya Birla Group via Birla Group Holdings [1][2]. The founders aimed to build domestic self-reliance in inorganic fluorine chemistry [2]. The company established a 60-acre integrated manufacturing complex inside the SIPCOT Industrial Complex at Cuddalore, Tamil Nadu [2][3]. Commercial production commenced at the site in March 1985 [2][3]. Situated near Cuddalore Port, the site was engineered around a foundational chemical loop [2][4]. The plant reacts imported acid-grade fluorspar with captive sulphuric acid [2][4]. This process yields Anhydrous Hydrofluoric Acid (AHF), Aluminium Fluoride (AlF3), and various inorganic fluorides [2][4]. The joint-sector structure combined state industrial infrastructure with private-sector process engineering [1][5]. TIDCO retained a ~26% anchor shareholding, while the Aditya Birla Group provided commercial oversight [1][5].

For its first three decades, Tanfac operated largely as a cyclical bulk-chemical manufacturer [4]. Its revenues depended on primary aluminium smelting and commodity acid spreads [4]. That legacy model suffered a severe structural stress test between FY2012 and FY2016 [5]. China controlled global fluorspar mining and export quotas during this period [5]. Chinese producers aggressively flooded the Indian market with underpriced Aluminium Fluoride [5]. They enjoyed vertically integrated access to domestic fluorspar reserves and major scale advantages [5]. Consequently, they landed finished AlF3 at Indian ports below Tanfac's imported raw-material conversion cost [5]. Tanfac could not pass through fluorspar costs against dumped Chinese product [5]. The company reported a net loss of ₹24 crore in FY2012 [5]. Operational losses continued through FY2014, including ₹5.6 crore in nine-month losses on ₹114.8 crore turnover through December 2013 [5]. Distress peaked in FY2015, when sales reached ₹120 crore with operating profit of just ₹8 crore, representing a 7% margin [5][6]. Interest expense was ₹10 crore, producing a -₹5 crore net loss [6]. Accumulated reserves fell to -₹10 crore against ₹69 crore of borrowings [5][6].

Tanfac evolved from a distressed, debt-laden Aluminium Fluoride producer in FY15 into a net-cash, specialty-fluorine platform under Anupam Rasayan, though recent quarters show cyclical margin compression returning during heavy capex.

The 2012–2015 crisis forced a permanent strategic pivot [5]. Bulk Aluminium Fluoride could not compete against Chinese state-backed scale [5]. The Aditya Birla Group therefore explored exiting its ~25% stake as early as 2013 [5]. It held exploratory talks with Navin Fluorine International and Gujarat Fluorochemicals [5]. Both peers possessed stronger fluorspar sourcing alliances, including GMDC partnerships [5]. Although no sale closed, management initiated an internal restructuring [4][5]. The team deemphasized low-margin AlF3 and reallocated reactors toward merchant AHF [4][5]. They expanded higher-value specialty fluorides like Potassium Fluoride (KF), Potassium Bifluoride, and Boron Trifluoride complexes [4][5]. This operational discipline delivered results between FY2018 and FY2021 [6]. Revenue climbed from ₹162 crore in FY18 to ₹222 crore in FY19 [6]. Operating margins expanded from 13% in FY18 to 24% in FY19 [6]. Borrowings fell from ₹43 crore in FY18 to ₹12 crore in FY19 and reached zero by March 2020 [6]. Tanfac entered the post-pandemic chemical upcycle with positive reserves of ₹73 crore in FY21 and zero debt [6].

Even during that turnaround, Tanfac's operating history exhibited classic chemical cyclicality. Revenue contracted 26% to ₹165 crore in FY20 and dropped 10% to ₹148 crore in FY21 [6]. Pandemic industrial disruptions and softer demand drove those declines [6]. Top line then surged 116% in FY22 to reach ₹320 crore [6]. Operating profit hit ₹76 crore, representing a 24% margin, while net profit reached ₹53 crore [6]. Across FY23 and FY24, revenue plateaued near capacity ceilings at ₹375 crore and ₹378 crore respectively [6][7]. The legacy 14,850 MTPA AHF plant operated near full utilization during this phase [6][7]. Meanwhile, management engineered its next brownfield expansion [7]. This recurring pattern shows sharp multi-year upswings followed by plateaus or 10%–26% cyclical pullbacks. Therefore, Tanfac must be evaluated across full chemical cycles rather than single-year earnings peaks.

The defining corporate inflection arrived in March 2022 [1][3]. Anupam Rasayan India Limited (ARIL) acquired the Aditya Birla Group's 24.96% equity stake [1][3]. ARIL assumed joint management control alongside state partner TIDCO [1][3]. Global agrochemical and pharmaceutical innovators required backward integration into hydrofluoric acid and potassium fluoride [2][3]. That integration helped ARIL secure complex fluorination contracts [2][3]. For Tanfac, the partnership provided growth capital, institutional governance, and an anchor downstream customer [1][2]. Over the next four years, Tanfac executed three major capital expenditure programs [7][9][11]. First, it doubled AHF capacity from 14,850 MTPA to 29,700 MTPA in October 2024 for ~₹100 crore [7]. Second, it commissioned India's first Solar-Grade Diluted Hydrofluoric Acid (DHF) lines in June and October 2025 [9][10]. That project added 10,000 TPA on an AHF basis, or 20,000 MTPA diluted [9][10]. Third, the board approved a ₹495 crore downstream expansion in January 2026 [11][12]. This investment is anchored by a 20,000 TPA HFC-32 (R-32) refrigerant gas plant [11][12].

In addition, single-site operations in Cuddalore require rigorous environmental vigilance [28][30]. The complex remains under active oversight by the TNPCB and the National Green Tribunal [28][30].

Hydrofluoric acid and its direct derivatives—now bolstered by Solar-Grade DHF—generate ~85% of Tanfac's revenue, while captive 100,000 MTPA Sulphuric Acid underpins the core reaction.

1. Anhydrous & Diluted Hydrofluoric Acid (AHF / HF): AHF is the foundational building block for fluorine chemistry. Tanfac manufactures AHF inside rotary kilns at Cuddalore [2][4]. The reaction combines acid-grade fluorspar with concentrated sulphuric acid and oleum [2][4]. This process releases hydrogen fluoride gas [2][4]. The gas is condensed into 99.9%+ pure anhydrous liquid [4]. Alternatively, it is absorbed into water for diluted 40%–70% grades [4]. In October 2024, Tanfac doubled AHF capacity from 14,850 MTPA to 29,700 MTPA [7][10]. That ~₹100 crore brownfield expansion reached ~95% utilization in FY26 [7][10]. AHF serves as both a merchant chemical and captive feedstock [4][10]. It feeds Solar DHF, Potassium Fluoride, and the future HFC-32 facility [10][12]. High kiln utilization is the primary driver of fixed-cost absorption at Cuddalore [4][10].

Concrete End-to-End Example (AHF): Consider an Indian agrochemical intermediate manufacturer synthesizing a fluorinated herbicide [2][3]. The buyer could be co-promoter Anupam Rasayan or a Gujarat-based formulator [2][3]. The customer cannot safely import bulk AHF in small batches [3]. AHF boils at 19.5°C and causes severe deep-tissue chemical burns on contact [3]. The customer therefore contracts with Tanfac for scheduled deliveries of 99.95% pure AHF [2][3]. Tanfac manufactures the acid at Cuddalore using imported fluorspar and captive acid [2][4]. It loads the product into specialized pressure-rated stainless-steel ISO tanks [4]. Dedicated hazardous-cargo transport carries the containers to the customer's plant [4]. Tanfac bills on formula-linked monthly or quarterly contracts tied to fluorspar and sulphur indices [4].

2. Solar-Grade Diluted Hydrofluoric Acid (DHF): Tanfac commissioned this product line in two separate phases [9][10]. It installed 5,000 TPA on an AHF basis in June 2025 [9]. A second 5,000 TPA followed in October 2025, reaching 20,000 MTPA diluted [9][10]. Tanfac thus became India's first and sole domestic producer of ultra-pure Solar-Grade DHF [9][10]. Industrial HF is commonly used for steel pickling and glass frosting [4]. By contrast, solar-grade DHF requires parts-per-billion purity control [9]. Contaminants like iron, sodium, copper, and nickel must stay near zero [9]. Trace metal ions diffuse into silicon wafers during etching and ruin photovoltaic conversion efficiency [9].

Concrete End-to-End Example (Solar-Grade DHF): In November 2025, Tanfac executed a 3.5-year commercial agreement [14]. The supply contract is worth approximately ₹732 crore excluding GST through FY2028–29 [14]. The partner is Vadodara-based Kredence Electronics Materials India Pvt Ltd [14]. Kredence supplies high-purity wet chemicals to domestic solar photovoltaic cell manufacturers under the PLI scheme [14]. Indian cell manufacturers previously imported ultra-pure DHF from China or East Asia [14]. Those shipments faced 4–6 week freight lead times, working-capital lockup, and contamination risks [14]. Tanfac now processes captive AHF through multi-stage sub-boiling distillation at Cuddalore [14]. The acid is diluted with ultra-pure deionized water inside fluoropolymer-lined systems [14]. Cleanroom technicians verify parts-per-billion purity using ICP-MS testing [14]. Tanfac delivers certified batches domestically to Kredence without requiring new capital expenditure [14].

3. Specialty Fluorides & Aluminium Fluoride (~15% of Revenue): Tanfac upgrades basic HF into solid and complexed fluorides [4][10]. These products include Potassium Fluoride (KF), Potassium Bifluoride (KHF2), and Boron Trifluoride (BF3) complexes [4][10]. The company also manufactures Aluminium Fluoride (AlF3) [4][10]. Together with surplus acid, these lines contribute roughly 15% of revenue [10]. Specialty fluorides involve lower tonnage than bulk HF [10]. However, they serve high-value pharmaceutical, agrochemical, and petrochemical applications [4][10]. Strict batch purity and moisture control generate superior unit realizations compared to commodity AlF3 [4][10].

Concrete End-to-End Examples (Specialty Fluorides & AlF3): Consider Potassium Fluoride applications [4]. A pharmaceutical manufacturer in Hyderabad requires a Halex reaction to synthesize a drug intermediate [4]. This process swaps a chlorine atom for fluorine on an aromatic ring [4]. Standard HF is too corrosive for glass-lined reactors [4]. The customer instead purchases anhydrous spray-dried KF from Tanfac [4]. Tanfac neutralizes HF with potassium carbonate and dries it to strict moisture limits [4]. In catalyst markets, petrochemical producers buy Tanfac's BF3-etherate or BF3-phenol complexes [4]. These compounds function as Lewis acid catalysts for hydrocarbon resins and fuel additives [4]. Finally, primary aluminium smelters in Odisha purchase dry AlF3 powder from Tanfac [4][5]. The powder lowers melting points and enhances electrical conductivity in molten cryolite cells [4][5]. Tanfac keeps AlF3 production volumes capped because margins remain lower than specialty fluorides [4][5].

4. Sulphuric Acid & Oleum: Tanfac operates a 100,000 MTPA Sulphuric Acid plant at Cuddalore [10]. The facility operated at ~101% capacity utilization in FY26 [10]. Roughly two-thirds of this acid is consumed captively inside the HF rotary kilns [4][10]. Tanfac sells surplus Sulphuric Acid and high-strength Oleum (H2SO4·SO3) to regional buyers across South India [4][10]. Burning elemental sulphur is an exothermic chemical process [4]. The acid plant generates byproduct high-pressure steam during operation [4]. This byproduct steam reduces external power purchases across the Cuddalore complex [4].

Concrete End-to-End Example (Sulphuric Acid / Oleum): A textile dye manufacturer in Tamil Nadu requires 20%–23% Oleum for sulphonation [4]. Hauling fuming acid from western India involves high freight costs and transport hazards [4]. The customer procures merchant Oleum directly from Tanfac in lined road tankers [4]. Deliveries take place within a 300-kilometer radius of Cuddalore [4]. This regional arrangement minimizes transportation expense on heavy acid [4]. Concurrently, it provides Tanfac with a reliable regional outlet for surplus production [4].

5. Pipeline Growth Leg — HFC-32 (R-32) Refrigerant Gas: Tanfac approved a ₹495 crore downstream chemical expansion in January 2026 [11][12]. The centerpiece is a 20,000 TPA HFC-32 plant at Cuddalore targeted for late Q3 FY27 [11][12][20]. HFC-32 is a lower-GWP refrigerant replacing R-410A in residential air conditioners [11][12]. The plant reacts captive AHF with methylene chloride [11][12]. This process captures downstream margins rather than relying solely on merchant acid sales [11][12]. However, the business will compete against established incumbents such as SRF and Gujarat Fluorochemicals [4][12]. It must also manage pricing competition from Chinese export suppliers [4][5].

Concrete End-to-End Example (HFC-32 Pipeline): Commissioning is scheduled for late FY27 [12][20]. Domestic air-conditioner OEMs and distributors in Chennai or Pune will source bulk ISO tanks of R-32 gas from Tanfac [12][20]. They will charge inverter split air-conditioner compressors with this refrigerant [12]. This domestic supply will replace imported Chinese R-32 and blended R-410A cylinders [12].

Chapter 3

Tanfac operates a direct B2B industrial distribution model [4]. It maintains zero retail or consumer-facing sales operations [4]. The company supplies over 400 industrial customers across India and ten export destinations [4]. Anhydrous Hydrofluoric Acid is an acutely toxic, corrosive substance classified as UN 1052 [4]. Therefore, it cannot be shipped through standard commercial transport or third-party freight [4]. Tanfac relies on specialized stainless-steel ISO tank containers for bulk deliveries [4]. It uses fluoropolymer-lined drums for ultra-pure Solar DHF and lined road tankers for Oleum [4]. Dedicated hazardous-cargo carriers handle overland movement [4]. This transport complexity creates natural geographic barriers against potential competitors [3][4]. Concurrently, proximity to Cuddalore Port streamlines bulk discharge of imported fluorspar and sulphur [3].

Co-promoter Anupam Rasayan India Limited represents an important strategic partner [1][3]. ARIL acquired a 24.96% stake in March 2022 [1][3]. That transaction secured captive HF and Potassium Fluoride for its agrochemical pipeline [1][3]. Despite this linkage, related-party sales to Anupam Rasayan remain modest [21]. Transferred volumes typically account for 1.88% to 3.57% of annual revenue [21]. All transactions undergo Audit Committee review under SEBI LODR arm's-length pricing rules [21]. Over 95% of total sales flow to independent domestic and export accounts [14][21]. These customers include long-term partners like Kredence Electronics Materials [14]. Arm's-length pricing discipline protects minority shareholder value as Anupam Rasayan expands fluorinated custom synthesis [3][21].

Three globally traded commodities dictate Tanfac's unit input costs [4][20]. These feedstocks are acid-grade fluorspar, elemental sulphur, and potassium carbonate [4][20]. China dominates worldwide acid-spar mining and processing [22]. Chinese environmental curbs or mining crackdowns routinely trigger global price spikes [22]. Elemental sulphur prices also escalated during FY26 [20][23]. Consequently, Tanfac's raw material and packing costs rose 47.4% year-over-year [20][23]. Total material expense increased from ₹307.60 crore in FY25 to ₹453.47 crore in FY26 [20][23]. Customer supply contracts reset prices on monthly or quarterly lags [20]. Rapid input increases therefore compress gross margins during transitional periods [20]. Gross margin dropped from 45.20% in FY25 to 37.46% in FY26 [20]. Operating EBITDA margin compressed further to 11.84% in Q2 FY27 despite record revenue of ₹230.48 crore [8][20].

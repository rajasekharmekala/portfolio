Chapter 1: How the Business Got Here — From Joint-Sector Acid Maker to Fluorine Platform

To understand Tanfac Industries Ltd (TANFACIND), we must start on the Coromandel Coast in Cuddalore, Tamil Nadu. The company began in 1972 as a joint venture between Tamil Nadu Industrial Development Corporation (TIDCO) and the Aditya Birla Group (via Grasim Industries and Birla Group Holdings) [1][2][7]. It started plant output in the 1980s inside the SIPCOT Industrial Complex at Kudikadu, Cuddalore [2][7][15]. Tanfac had a clear import-replacement goal: supply Anhydrous Hydrofluoric Acid (AHF) and Aluminium Fluoride (AlF3) to India's growing aluminium smelters and chemical plants [2][7][15]. Consumers never see Tanfac's products, yet heavy industry cannot run without them. Primary aluminium pots, stainless steel pickling lines, oil alkylation units, and agrochemical plants all rely on fluorine molecules [2][3][26]. Very few plants hold the permits and skills to make and handle these acids safely [2][3][26].

For nearly four decades under Birla and TIDCO control, that industrial role carried a hard ceiling. Tanfac sold basic AHF and AlF3 into merchant markets [1][15]. Global fluorspar swings, Chinese export surges, and local smelter cycles whipsawed its earnings [1][15][16]. The audited 12-year record from FY2015 to FY2026 makes this clear. In FY2015, Tanfac posted ₹120 crore in sales and ₹8 crore in operating profit (7% OPM) [1]. It paid ₹10 crore in interest on ₹69 crore of debt, ending with a ₹5 crore net loss and negative reserves of ₹10 crore [1]. FY2016 brought little relief. Sales edged up to ₹133 crore, operating profit stayed at ₹9 crore (7% OPM), and net profit was ₹0 crore [1]. A sharp upcycle in FY2018–FY2019 lifted sales from ₹162 crore to ₹222 crore and margins from 13% to 24% [1]. ROCE peaked at 78% as debt fell from ₹43 crore to ₹12 crore [1]. Yet FY2020 and FY2021 pulled sales right back down to ₹165 crore and ₹148 crore [1]. Throughout that legacy era, Tanfac acted as a classic price-taker. It earned strong cash in tight markets, yet could not compound capital steadily without specialty downstream products [1][2].

The 2022 Inflection: Aditya Birla Exits, Anupam Rasayan Enters

Everything changed in early 2022 when the Aditya Birla Group exited its non-core fluorochemicals stake [7]. Anupam Rasayan India Limited, a major Indian custom-synthesis and specialty chemical firm, bought a 24.96% controlling stake via a Share Purchase Agreement (SPA) [6][7]. It also launched a mandatory 26% SEBI open offer alongside co-promoter TIDCO [6][7].

For Anupam Rasayan, Tanfac was a key backward-integration prize rather than a passive financial bet [4][10]. Fluorine chemistry adds high value to modern farm and drug active ingredients [4][10]. Owning a coastal HF plant at Cuddalore with full green permits gave the group instant molecule security [4][10]. It avoided a five-year wait for new hazardous-chemical approvals [4][10].

The post-2022 shift happened in three clear waves that reshaped both profits and the balance sheet [1][4]. First, Tanfac wiped out its old debt. From FY2020 to FY2024, it ran debt-free and built a ₹67 crore investment cushion [1]. Second, the post-COVID fluorine upcycle met Anupam Rasayan's commercial network. Sales jumped 116% in FY2022 to ₹320 crore, then reached ₹375 crore in FY2023 and ₹378 crore in FY2024 [1]. In FY2025, sales surged 47% to ₹557 crore, with ₹131 crore in operating profit (24% OPM) and ₹88 crore in net profit [1]. Third, management pivoted from merchant acid toward captive scale and higher-value downstream fluorochemicals [4][11].

Chapter 2: Inside the Molecule Factory — Segments, Customers & Unit Economics

In its audited Ind AS filings, Tanfac reports one main segment: "Fluorine-Based Chemicals / Specialty Chemicals" [1][5]. It does not publish a product-by-product revenue table [1][5]. Still, plant disclosures and industry data reveal a clear revenue split. Hydrofluoric Acid (HF) and its direct derivatives bring in roughly ~85% of sales, depending on cycle spreads [24]. Specialty Fluorides and allied inorganic chemicals add the other ~15% [24]. Every product starts from one integrated chemical tree at Cuddalore. Imported acid-grade fluorspar (CaF2) reacts with captive sulphuric acid (H2SO4) in rotary kilns to form Anhydrous Hydrofluoric Acid (AHF) [2][3][8]. From there, the acid branches into merchant AHF, ultra-pure solar-grade DHF, Aluminium Fluoride, potassium salts, and new fluorocarbons [2][3][8].

Segment-by-Segment Real-World Walkthroughs

1. Anhydrous Hydrofluoric Acid (AHF) & Diluted HF (29,700 MTPA): Picture a mid-sized Indian agrochemical or drug intermediate maker producing fluorinated herbicides or API building blocks. They cannot buy HF on spot exchanges or store it in plain steel drums. AHF boils at 19.5°C, burns human tissue on contact, and eats through glass. Buyers order bulk AHF directly from Tanfac in lined road tankers or T14 ISO tanks [2][26]. At Cuddalore, Tanfac reacts imported acid-spar with captive sulphuric acid in hot kilns [4][15]. It scrubs and distills the gas to >99.9% purity, then bills on formula-linked B2B contracts tied to landed fluorspar and sulphur prices [4][15]. Capacity doubled to 29,700 MTPA in October 2024, giving AHF both merchant sales and captive feed for every downstream line [11].

2. Solar-Grade Dilute Hydrofluoric Acid (10,000 TPA, AHF basis): Indian solar PV makers scaling TOPCon or heterojunction (HJT) cell lines face a strict hurdle [3][12]. Every silicon wafer needs wet-chemical etching and oxide removal using ultra-pure Dilute HF [3][12]. Metallic impurities in that acid must stay in parts per billion (ppb) [3][12]. Until recently, Indian solar cell makers imported this grade at high freight cost and delivery risk. Tanfac started Phase 1 (5,000 TPA) in June 2025 and Phase 2 (5,000 TPA) in October 2025 [3][12]. It became India's first local maker of Solar-Grade DHF [3][12]. By purifying its own AHF through multi-stage distillation and clean packaging, Tanfac wins a local import-replacement niche with much stickier client ties than basic acid [4][12].

3. Aluminium Fluoride (~15,600–16,500 MTPA): A primary aluminium smelter running high-current Hall-Héroult pots needs Aluminium Fluoride (AlF3) non-stop [2][15]. It lowers the melting point of the cryolite-alumina bath and keeps electric current flowing smoothly [2][15]. Tanfac reacts HF with alumina hydrate to make dry, free-flowing AlF3 powder [2]. Yet global smelters buy AlF3 through tight annual tenders pegged to Chinese export rates [15][16]. As a result, this legacy line carries thinner spreads and sharp price cycles [15][16].

4. Specialty Fluorides & Organics (KF, KHF2, BF3 Complexes, IBAP — 8,400+ MTPA): An API plant making ibuprofen needs Isobutyl Acetophenone (IBAP) [2][4][24]. Meanwhile, a fluorination plant—including co-promoter Anupam Rasayan—needs dry Potassium Fluoride (KF) as a halogen-exchange agent [2][4][24]. Tanfac makes KF and Potassium Bifluoride (KHF2) by mixing HF with potassium hydroxide, then drying and sizing the salt to strict moisture limits [2][24]. It also sells Boron Trifluoride (BF3) complexes and sodium silico-fluoride [2][24]. These specialty salts earn higher prices per tonne than bulk AlF3. They also deepen Tanfac's share across farm, heavy-water, brazing, and drug supply chains [24].

5. Captive Sulphuric Acid & Oleum (~100,000 MTPA) and Future R-32 / Japanese Contract (20,000 MTPA): Tanfac burns imported molten or granular sulphur in its captive contact plant at Cuddalore [2][24]. This makes sulphuric acid and oleum to feed its HF kilns, while surplus acid goes to local buyers [2][24]. Looking to January 2027, Tanfac is building a ₹495 crore, 20,000 MTPA downstream plant for HFC-32 refrigerant gas and specialty fluorinated intermediates [8][9]. Already, 7,500 MTPA (37.5% of capacity) is locked into a 7-year Japanese supply deal worth ₹337.5 crore per year (₹2,362.5 crore total ex-GST) [8][9]. That deal turns raw HF capacity into steady, multi-year export sales.

To see why downstream upgrading lifts unit returns, follow the value chain per tonne of fluorine atom. In legacy Aluminium Fluoride (AlF3), Tanfac reacts HF with alumina hydrate for smelters [2][15][16]. Prices track global benchmarks where Chinese excess supply often caps spreads [2][15][16]. In Potassium Fluoride (KF) and Potassium Bifluoride (KHF2), mixing HF with potassium hydroxide yields high-purity reagents [2][24]. Farm active makers, drug plants, heavy-water units, and brazing shops pay much more per tonne of HF consumed [2][24]. Likewise, Isobutyl Acetophenone (IBAP) links Tanfac straight into painkiller API chains like ibuprofen [2][24].

In Solar-Grade DHF (10,000 TPA), the moat shifts from reaction chemistry to trace cleaning [3][12]. Silicon wafer fabs running TOPCon and HJT lines need etching acids free of metal ions that hurt cell output [12]. Client testing takes months of batch checks and clean-pack audits [4][12]. Once a domestic solar maker approves Tanfac's Cuddalore stream, switching costs rise sharply [4][12]. Next, the 20,000 MTPA R-32 / fluorinated chemicals plant moves Tanfac into organic fluorocarbons [4][8][9]. Pairing captive AHF with chlorinated inputs serves HFC-32 cooling demand under Kigali rules, while locking 7,500 MTPA into the 7-year Japanese export deal [4][8][9].

Customer Concentration, Qualification Cycles & Pricing Formulas: Across its ₹711 crore FY2026 sales base, Tanfac serves over 100 industrial clients [10]. Still, client loyalty varies widely by line. In legacy Aluminium Fluoride (AlF3), smelters run annual or half-year tenders tied to landed Chinese quotes, leaving low switching costs [15][16]. In Anhydrous HF, buyers in Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, and Gujarat rely on Tanfac's tanker fleet for just-in-time supply [26]. Storing bulk toxic liquid HF on-site brings steep safety and insurance costs [26]. In Solar-Grade DHF and the Japanese chemical stream, testing takes months of trace-impurity checks, building multi-year lock-in [4][9][12].

By-Product & Co-Product Synergy: Inside an integrated HF site, waste handling and co-products shape unit margins [2][18]. Reacting fluorspar with sulphuric acid leaves calcium sulphate (anhydrite/gypsum) as a solid by-product alongside HF gas [2]. Managing gypsum disposal, scrubbing tail gases, and reusing heat from the ~100,000 MTPA sulphuric acid plant cuts net cost per tonne of AHF [2][24]. Meanwhile, Boron Trifluoride (BF3) complexes and Potassium Bifluoride (KHF2) earn higher revenue per fluorine atom [2][24]. Tanfac serves small-batch drug, nuclear/heavy-water, and brazing clients without adding heavy kiln tonnage [2][24].

Chapter 3: Logistics, Fluorspar Sourcing & Where Every ₹100 of Sales Goes

Tanfac sells strictly B2B industrial direct. It has no retail dealers, middlemen, or consumer channels [2][10]. The company serves over 100 domestic and global industrial buyers across farm chemicals, drugs, solar PV, aluminium, and fluorochemicals [10]. Its off-take splits into two tracks. Part of its AHF and Potassium Fluoride goes to co-promoter Anupam Rasayan India Limited as a captive fluorination input [4][10]. Under Ind AS 24 related-party rules, these deals run at arm's length in the normal course of business with Audit Committee approval [4][10]. The rest goes to third-party domestic contracts and export buyers [4][10].

Anhydrous Hydrofluoric Acid is toxic, fuming, and corrosive, so shipping it is a tough engineering moat on its own [26]. Tanfac's SIPCOT Cuddalore plant sits along Tamil Nadu's coast near Cuddalore Port and deep-water hubs at Karaikal, Chennai, and Ennore [10][26]. Domestic loads travel in lined road tankers made with rubber, HDPE, or special alloys [26]. Each tanker carries pressure-relief valves, shut-off valves, and GPS tracking [26]. Export loads of HF and derivatives move in T14-rated lined ISO tank containers that meet International Maritime Dangerous Goods (IMDG) rules [26]. This hazmat fleet builds a strong regional moat. Foreign rivals cannot cheaply ship liquid AHF into southern India without ISO tank fleets and port permits.

On the inbound side lies Tanfac's biggest supply risk: 100% import reliance for acid-grade fluorspar [15]. India lacks commercial reserves of high-grade fluorite [15]. Local HF makers must import acid-spar from China, South Africa, Morocco, Thailand, and Vietnam [15]. Tanfac spreads orders across Africa and Southeast Asia and runs a ~100,000 MTPA captive sulphuric acid plant on-site [4][15]. Still, landed fluorspar costs swing with Chinese export quotas, ocean freight, and USD/INR rates [4][15]. Working capital also shifted sharply in FY2026. Debtor Days improved from 65 to 57 days, and Inventory Days fell from 112 to 79 days [1]. Yet Days Payable shrank from 48 days to just 21 days [1]. That pushed the Cash Conversion Cycle to 115 days (versus 43 days in FY2022) and pulled CFO-to-EBITDA down [1].

Chapter 4: The Fluorine Oligopoly — Regulatory Barriers vs. Cyclical Traps

India's fluorochemicals sector is an oligopoly built on two hard facts: extreme chemical hazard and scarce mineral supply [15][20][21]. Hydrofluoric Acid (HF) turns into a toxic, tissue-burning gas above 19.5°C [26]. Getting MoEFCC and state pollution permits for a new HF plant takes years of public hearings, safety studies, and waste-treatment engineering [18][19][26]. Tanfac's 54-year-old coastal site inside SIPCOT Cuddalore creates a real entry barrier [2][10][26]. It already holds Consent to Operate (CTO) permits, captive sulphuric acid, ISO 9001/14001/45001 tags, and port access [2][10][26]. Yet that same hazard brings sharp operating and legal risk. Tanfac had a fatal April 2004 sulfuric acid tank accident and a May 2021 sulfur tank burn case [17][18]. The Cuddalore industrial belt also draws regular scrutiny from the Tamil Nadu Pollution Control Board (TNPCB) and National Green Tribunal (NGT) over air VOCs and Uppanar River rules [17][18][19]. After nearby unit leaks in SIPCOT Cuddalore in 2025, oversight across the entire estate grew even tighter [19].

Against India's Tier-1 triad—Gujarat Fluorochemicals (GFL), SRF Limited, and Navin Fluorine International (NFIL)—Tanfac is a nimble mid-sized specialist, not a giant [20][21][25]. GFL owns a captive fluorspar mine in Morocco and leads in fluoropolymers (PTFE/PVDF) and EV battery chemicals [21]. SRF holds huge scale in refrigerants (HFC-32, HFC-134a) and captive AHF [25]. NFIL runs ~20,000 MTPA AHF at Surat plus a 40,000 MTPA AHF plant at Dahej, alongside a high-margin pharma/agro CDMO arm and a fluorspar JV (Swarnim Gujarat) [20]. By contrast, Tanfac runs 29,700 MTPA AHF at one Cuddalore site with zero captive mines [11][15]. Its upstream spreads therefore stay more exposed to imported acid-spar swings [11][15]. Tanfac counters with niche speed: India's first local Solar-Grade DHF plant (10,000 TPA) and captive sales to Anupam Rasayan [4][12]. Entering HFC-32 (R-32) via its ₹495 crore, 20,000 MTPA plant puts Tanfac straight against SRF and GFL's scale [8][9][25]. That makes the 7,500 TPA Japanese pre-contract vital to protect plant load [8][9][25].

Chapter 1

Vijay Gupta founded SoftTech Engineers Limited in Pune in June 1996 [1][18]. He is an M.Tech alumnus of IIT Bombay (1986) who previously worked at Crompton Greaves and Godrej & Boyce [18]. His first commercial product, STRUDS (Structural Design and Detailing Software), served Indian civil and structural engineers [18]. It helped them comply with Indian Standard (IS) building codes [18]. STRUDS built SoftTech's reputation across domestic design consultancies for fifteen years [18]. In 2011, the company sold the STRUDS intellectual property to UK-based CSC [18]. That sale marked SoftTech's first big pivot: leaving desktop structural tools to focus on municipal e-governance and automated plan scrutiny.

The second act began in 2005 with the launch of AutoDCR (Automated Development Control Regulations) [18]. Indian urban local bodies faced major delays checking architectural CAD drawings by hand [16][17]. Engineers had to verify complex municipal bye-laws, floor-space-index (FSI) rules, and setback norms [16][17]. AutoDCR automated this geometry check against encoded local rules, cutting subjectivity and approval times [16]. Early wins at Pune Municipal Corporation (PMC) and Greater Mumbai (MCGM) gave SoftTech a strong GovTech foothold [16][17]. Over the next decade, the firm added PWIMS for state public works departments and Opticon ERP for private contractors [3][4].

Concrete End-to-End Example: How AutoDCR / CivitPERMIT Works in Mumbai & Pune

Picture an architect filing a 20-storey residential tower plan in Pune (PMC) or Greater Mumbai (MCGM) [16][17]. Under the old manual system, the architect submitted physical paper blueprints [16][17]. Municipal engineers then checked setbacks, stair widths, fire-tender access, and Floor Space Index (FSI) by hand [16][17]. Reading hundreds of pages of Development Control Rules took months and invited subjective disputes [16][17].

With AutoDCR (now CivitPERMIT), the architect drafts the plan in CAD/BIM using set layer rules and uploads it online [16][20]. SoftTech's geometry engine parses every polygon and checks room sizes, parking ratios, and FSI against local rules [16][20]. It flags rule errors on the drawing within minutes and routes clean files to issue demand notes and final permits [16][20]. SoftTech earns an upfront setup fee from the city plus a pay-per-use fee per square foot or application [3][4].

Beyond municipal permits, SoftTech has expanded into two newer verticals. First is CivitINFRA for large central and state agencies [3][9]. Key wins include the Airports Authority of India (AAI) and the ₹92.95 Cr JNPA AI Digital Twin project [3][9]. Second is CivitTDR / eTDR, a digital registry for Transferable Development Rights in metro cities [3]. It scaled from 0% to 3.2% of standalone sales in H1 FY27 (and 5.3% in Q2 FY27) [3]. Meanwhile, private construction ERP (CivitBUILD, formerly Opticon) and global BIM bids (CivitTWIN, Finland, Middle East) remain small [3]. CivitBUILD was just 2.8% of H1 FY27 mix, and consolidated PAT fell 4.9% YoY after an MEA order delay [3].

A close look at the subsidiary footprint shows how concentrated the core engine remains [2][3]. The group lists nine domestic and global subsidiaries across Finland, Germany, Dubai, the UK, Singapore, the US, and India [2]. Yet standalone parent sales of ₹128.30 Cr generated 96.5% of FY26 consolidated revenue (₹132.90 Cr) [2]. The other subsidiaries added just ₹4.60 Cr of sales while cutting consolidated PBT by ₹4.06 Cr [2]. Inside India, however, CivitPERMIT's rollouts across Maharashtra, Delhi (DDA), Jammu & Kashmir, and 18 states offer a strong base for eTDR and digital twins [3][16][17]. Converting that domestic municipal base into steady pay-per-use cash remains management's core operational test [3].

Chapter 4

SoftTech sits between two distinct software markets [4][16]. First is GovTech / Urban e-Governance, covering municipal permits, TDR registries, and public works [4][16]. Second is AECO Construction Tech, covering BIM, digital twins, and contractor ERP [4]. In India, urban local bodies have digitized plan approvals under Ease of Doing Business, Smart Cities Mission, and AMRUT [16][17]. Globally, the AECO market is much larger and led by well-funded giants. Key rivals include Autodesk, Bentley Systems, Trimble, Schneider Electric's RIB Software, Procore, and US civic-tech leader Accela [13].

SoftTech's moat is narrow, local, and procedural [3][4]. It is strongest in Indian municipal plan scrutiny (AutoDCR / CivitPERMIT) and weakest in private ERP (CivitBUILD) and foreign markets [3][4]. Why has AutoDCR held its ground across 1,500+ cities and 18 states for two decades? Indian Development Control Rules (DCR) are hyper-local, change often, and carry many geometry exceptions [16][17]. Global CAD leaders like Autodesk and Bentley supply the drafting canvas, not the Indian rule engine. Meanwhile, large Indian IT services firms rarely chase ₹5–20 Cr municipal rule-engine deals that require 15-year upkeep.

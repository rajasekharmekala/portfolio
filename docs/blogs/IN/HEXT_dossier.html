1. What the company does

Hexaware Technologies is an IT services company. Its engineers build, modernise and run software for banks, insurers, hospitals, drug makers, manufacturers and professional-services firms. It was incorporated in 1992, employs 34,506 people in more than 30 countries and says it serves more than 30 of the Fortune 500 companies.[6][1] In CY25, the year to 31 December 2025, revenue was US$1,537.4 million, or ₹13,430 crore.[13][2] The company reports on a calendar year. The year to March 2026 that many Indian companies call FY26 does not exist here: the latest audited year is CY25, and the latest filed quarter is Q2CY26, the three months to 30 June 2026.[18][6]

Carlyle, the US private equity firm, bought control in 2021 through a holding company called CA Magnum Holdings.[20] The February 2025 listing was a pure offer for sale of about ₹8,750 crore, so Carlyle received the money and Hexaware received none.[20] CA Magnum still holds 74.30% of the shares, and the exchange filings mark none of them as pledged.[21] The holding company has borrowed heavily against its own structure, which Chapter 3 explains, and that matters because a controlling owner with debt can become a seller.[19][22]

A client signs a contract and pays in one of three ways. It pays for engineer time (time and materials), for a fixed price for a defined job, or for a running fee to operate a service. On fixed-price contracts revenue is booked as work is completed, and the auditor lists this as a key audit matter because it depends on management's estimate of effort still to come.[18] In Q2CY26, IT services made up 85.2% of revenue, BPS (business process services, such as claims handling and customer support) 11.4%, and licences and other items 3.3%.[6] Employee costs were ₹22,664 million, or 58.9% of the ₹38,452 million of revenue by our division, so profit depends on how many paid hours each employee bills to a client.[6]

Two operating numbers show how well that works. Utilisation, the share of available hours billed to clients, was 84.8% in Q2CY26. Part of the rise from 82.6% comes from a new method that excludes people working on platforms, which added about 80 basis points.[6][7] Voluntary attrition, the share of staff who quit in a year, was 11.2%.[6] Hexaware reports in rupees but sells mostly in dollars. The rupee averaged 94.86 per dollar in Q2CY26 against 85.27 a year earlier, which is why rupee revenue grew 17.9% while dollar revenue grew only 6.1%.[6][7] Hedge and translation losses of about $8 million also hit profit in the quarter.[9]

The top five clients gave 25.3% of revenue over the last twelve months, the top ten gave 35.7%, and 34 clients each pay more than $10 million a year.[6] One US government-sponsored enterprise (GSE) client has been a drag: revenue from it fell about 40% to 45% over the four quarters to March 2026, and management now calls it stable.[12][9] Thirteen of the top 20 clients cut their vendor lists in five quarters, which management puts at four to five times the usual pace.[9] IT services demand follows client technology budgets, so it slows when clients fear a downturn. CY25 revenue grew 7.6% against a long-term ambition of low-to-mid-teens growth.[13][15]

AI is both the product and the threat

Management launches one new AI-based service a month, sells a service called Zero License that maps the business logic inside a rented software platform so the client can move to a system built for it with AI, and runs agent and engineering platforms named Agentverse and Zerovity.[9][26] On 8 October 2026 it became a Preferred Partner in Anthropic's Claude network.[26] The Q4CY25 investor deck says AI "will also be dampener to the existing business", which is the honest summary: clients can now buy the same code with fewer hours, so each hour Hexaware sells is worth less unless volume rises.[15]

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Constant-currency (CC) growth is revenue growth measured as if exchange rates had not moved, so it shows demand rather than currency swings.
  • EBIT margin is operating profit before interest and tax divided by revenue. Hexaware changed to reporting EBIT from Q1CY26.[16]
  • Utilisation is the share of available working hours that IT staff spend on work billed to clients.
  • Attrition is the share of staff who leave in a year. The figure here counts only people who quit.
  • DSO (days sales outstanding) is the number of days of revenue that clients owe, billed and unbilled. Fewer days means cash arrives sooner.
  • OCF (operating cash flow) is cash collected from running the business after tax, before capital spending.
  • GSE means a US government-sponsored enterprise, a very large mortgage-related client that Hexaware does not name.
  • Rebadging means taking over a client's staff and contract so that the people become Hexaware employees on day one.
  • CQGR is compound quarterly growth rate, the steady growth per quarter that reaches a target over several quarters.
  • TCV and ACV are total and annual contract value. Hexaware does not publish them.[9]

2. Why the business is strong or weak

This chapter asks whether Hexaware is a strong or weak business. For an IT services firm, strong means a clean balance sheet, steady cash, growth at least level with peers, healthy margins and clients that are not too concentrated. The short answer is that Hexaware is a sound business that grows more slowly and earns lower margins than its listed peers. The paragraphs below give the evidence, starting with what is strong.

The first strength is the balance sheet. Hexaware has no bank debt: borrowings were zero at the end of CY25 and CY24, after a ₹2,930 million short-term loan was drawn and repaid within CY24.[18] The only debt-like items at June 2026 were lease liabilities of ₹6,830 million and an earn-out of ₹5,135 million still owed to sellers of acquired firms, against cash and fund investments of ₹16,596 million.[6] Return on capital employed (ROCE) has stayed between 27% and 30% for five years, and Screener shows 30.1% now. StockAnalysis shows 25.2% on its own definition, so we treat the range as 25% to 30%.[3][1][30] Operating cash flow over the last twelve months was 124.7% of profit, and the three-year figure is 1.36 times.[6][18][3]

The second strength is where growth comes from. In Q2CY26, healthcare and insurance revenue grew 18.9% on a year earlier in dollars, manufacturing and consumer 17.9% and banking 10.8%.[7] IT headcount rose for the twelfth quarter in a row. The sales team also reports a new deal type: a client runs a short contest and picks one vendor as its strategic AI partner. Two were won, but deal sizes are small, in the range of several hundred thousand dollars.[9] On 8 October 2026 Hexaware said it had more than 1,100 Claude-certified professionals and joined Anthropic's partner network.[26] Growth of this kind is real, but it is not yet large enough to move a company of $1.5 billion of annual revenue.

The weakness is speed and profit per rupee of sales. Constant-currency revenue growth was 6.3% on a year earlier in Q2CY26 and 3.2% in Q1CY26, and management cut its CY26 growth guide from at least 7.6% to 6% to 7% in July.[6][11][8][12] Reported EBIT margin was 13.6%, against an adjusted 14.7% a year earlier.[6] On Screener's measure, Hexaware's operating margin is 15% against 17% to 19% for four listed peers, and trailing sales growth is 13% against 42% at Coforge, 27% at Persistent, 15% at LTM and 14% at Mphasis.[2][32][33][35][34] Part of the gap comes from acquisitions, different year-ends and different margin definitions, so we do not treat it as a like-for-like ranking. It does show that Hexaware is the slower and lower-margin name in this group.

Three structural risks sit on top of that. First, client concentration: the ten largest clients bring 35.7% of revenue, and one GSE client lost about 40% to 45% of its revenue in four quarters.[6][12] Second, travel and transportation revenue fell 14.5% on a year earlier because airline clients cut budgets, and two Middle East deals started later than planned.[7][9] Third, AI changes what clients are willing to pay for. Management says AI will create new growth but will also dampen the existing business, and 13 of the top 20 clients consolidated their vendor lists in five quarters.[15][9] Each of these shows up as slower revenue rather than a loss, which is why the guidance cut matters more than any single quarter's profit.

The moat, in plain words

In IT services a moat is rarely a patent. It is the cost to a client of switching vendors in the middle of running its core systems. Hexaware's evidence for that cost is mixed. The top-five client share was 25.0%, 25.7% and 25.9% in CY23 to CY25 and 25.3% for the last twelve months, which says clients stay.[15][6] Against that, one large client cut its spend by about 40% to 45%, and Hexaware publishes no contract value or order book, so we cannot measure how much future revenue is already signed.[12][9] A moat that cannot be measured counts as narrow in our scoring.

Where Hexaware stands against its rivals

Reuters ranks Hexaware as India's tenth-largest IT firm by revenue.[25] Among listed mid-sized rivals it is the cheapest on earnings after LTM: Screener shows a trailing P/E of 24.6 for Hexaware against 21.5 at LTM, 25.9 at Mphasis, 40.8 at Coforge and 49.5 at Persistent.[1][35][34][32][33] The discount to the two fast growers is explained by slower growth, and the premium to LTM is hard to justify on margin alone. Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Walk-the-talk means checking what management said it would do against what it did. The CY25 margin guide was for EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization). We compared guidance from the November 2025, February 2026, May 2026 and July 2026 communications with the filed numbers. The record is mixed. The margin guide for CY25 was met at the low end, and the CY26 margin band is on track so far. The growth floor was broken in less than three months, and the acquisition record carries a large write-back.[17][15][12][9]

The sharpest example is the CY26 growth guide. In February 2026 management expected CY26 revenue growth to be better than the 7.6% of CY25. On the 7 May call the CEO said: "At a minimum, we are reaffirming what we said last time, the floor of growth of 7.6%."[15][12] On 29 July the guide became 6% to 7%, including 50 basis points from a rebadging deal. Management gave two reasons: three or four deals ramped later than planned, and travel and transport got worse than it expected.[8][9] The CEO resigned five weeks later, on 2 September.[23]

Capital allocation is the second test. Hexaware paid ₹8,268 million for acquisitions in CY24 and ₹7,452 million in CY25, and another ₹2,787 million in the first half of CY26.[18][6] In CY25 it also reversed ₹3,820 million of earn-out it had expected to pay on an earlier purchase and wrote off ₹1,696 million of customer relationships under the same note.[18] An earn-out reversal means the acquired business earned less than the price assumed. The reversal itself loses no cash, but it is evidence about how well the buying was priced. Dividends are the clearer positive: the company paid ₹11.50 a share for CY25, a 51.1% payout, and declared ₹9.50 on 20 October 2026.[19][4]

Momentum and turnaround inflection gate

The momentum gate runs three tests on the frozen prices, and the exact thresholds come from the portfolio rules. Test A asks whether the stock is in a Stage 2 advance now. It needs the price above the 50-day average, the 50-day above the 200-day, and the 50-day not falling over 21 days. It also needs a 200-day average that is flat or rising over 63 days, a 63-day return that beats the Nifty 50, and support within 1.6 ATR. Test B asks whether the stock is entering a Stage 2 advance from a flat floor. It needs the 50-day average to have fallen no more than 0.8% over 21 days, a 28-day return that beats the index, and a price within 1.5 ATR of the pivot, the trigger level for a move up. It also needs a volume dry-up ratio of 0.92 or less and session volume of at least 0.95 times the 20-day average. Test C asks whether a beaten-down stock is turning up from a floor that holds. It needs the price above the 63-day low with the 20-day swing low holding within a 2.0% tolerance. It also needs the price at or above the 20-day average with a positive 28-day relative strength or two-week gain, support within 2.0 ATR, and an RSI of 32 or more. A stock needs to pass one test. A name that fails all three is not bought, however good the business is.

7. What we would do, and when

Plain-English key. WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT means do not buy now: place a limit order lower, and only if conditions are met. Core is the portfolio bucket for steady compounders, and its rule is that the reward must be at least twice the risk, written 2.0 to 1. The support ladder measures how far the price sits above its nearest support, in units of ATR. Up to 0.6 ATR is the FULL_SIZE band, up to 1.2 is the STANDARD band, up to 1.6 is limit-order-only, and anything above is NO_BUY. TIER_3 is the third of four conviction tiers. Its risk budget is 50 basis points, which means we size the position so that being stopped out costs 0.5% of net worth, subject to a cap of 3.5% of net worth in the position. REJECTED means we drop the name from the shortlist.

The decision is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT. The rule is the Core asymmetry floor of 2.0 to 1. At ₹569.35 the ratio is 0.37, because our central value of ₹612 is only ₹42.65 above the price while the stop at ₹454.60 is ₹114.75 below it.

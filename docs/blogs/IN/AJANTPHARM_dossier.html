Terms used in this report

This chapter explains each technical and financial word once, with an everyday comparison, so the later chapters can use the words without stopping to define them. The words appear in the order the framework needs them.

ROCE stands for return on capital employed. It asks how much operating profit a business earns for each rupee tied up in factories, machines and working stock. A figure of 34.5 percent means the business earns about 35 paise a year on every rupee it uses. [1]

RSI stands for relative strength index. It is a gauge from 0 to 100 of how quickly a price has recently risen or fallen, and readings below 30 are called oversold. The 14-day RSI for Ajanta is 34.9, which is near that line but not below it. [1]

ROE stands for return on equity. It measures profit against the money the shareholders have put in, and it is the shareholder's view of the same question. Here it is 27.1 percent. [1]

Debt-to-equity is borrowings divided by the capital owned by shareholders. A value of 0.06 means borrowings are about six paise for each rupee of owners' capital. The framework's limit for a non-lender is below 0.70.

Cash from operations is the cash the business brings in from running its activity, before money spent on new equipment. Comparing it with reported profit shows whether profit is backed by cash that has actually arrived. The three-year ratio of cash to profit is the framework's cash test. [1]

Operating margin, shortened to OPM, is the share of each sales rupee left after the costs of making and selling the product. In FY26 it was 28 percent. [1]

P/E, or price-to-earnings, is the share price divided by profit per share. A P/E of 36.7 means investors pay ₹36.70 for each rupee of a year's earnings per share. [1]

ATR stands for average true range. It is the typical daily price swing over the last 14 sessions, and it works as a ruler. A stop placed one ATR away leaves room for an ordinary day's movement. The ledger ATR for this session is ₹101.70, about 3 percent of the price.

Stage 2 is the rising phase of a stock's cycle, when the price holds above averages that are themselves rising. Stage 3 is the topping phase that often follows, when the rise stalls and the averages flatten. Stage 4 is the falling phase, when the price breaks below averages that are heading down.

Base is a sideways stretch in which a stock trades inside a narrow range before its next move, like a car idling at a junction. A breakout is a close above the top of that base, ideally on heavier trading. The base low used in this report is the lowest price over the last 63 sessions, ₹3,301.00.

Pivot is the price level a stock has to clear to confirm a breakout, usually the top of its base. The ledger does not record a top-of-base price, so the momentum gate measures distance from the base low instead.

Stop loss is the price at which a holding would be sold to cap the loss. It is set below the level at which the trend counts as failed, so the sale happens before the loss grows. Entry zone is the price range in which the rules allow a buy, normally close to a support level. When a stock is outside its entry zone, the rule is to wait rather than chase it.

Gross margin is the share of sales left after the direct cost of the product itself. It shows how much the product earns before the costs of running the company. EBITDA stands for earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. It is the profit from running the business before the bank, the tax office and the wear-and-tear charges are deducted, so it shows how strong day-to-day operations are on their own.

Guidance is the company's own forecast of what it expects to achieve in the coming period. It is useful because it is the management's stated view, and it is judged against what the company later delivers.

Measured move is a price target found by adding the height of a base or range to the level it breaks out from. Chart readers use it to estimate how far a move might run. The framework accepts it as a target anchor when no earnings anchor is available.

Asymmetry is the ratio of expected upside to possible downside: how many rupees of gain the rules expect for each rupee at risk. A ratio of 3.0 means three rupees of expected gain for each rupee of risk to the stop.

Pledge means a promoter has offered shares as security for a loan. If the loan is not repaid, the lender can sell the shares, so a high pledge is a risk to the shareholder even when the business is sound.

Form 483 is a list of observations that a US Food and Drug Administration inspector writes at the end of an inspection, describing possible departures from good manufacturing practice. A VAI classification means the agency found deficiencies that it considers voluntary to correct. It sits between the milder NAI outcome and the serious OAI outcome, which can lead to regulatory action.

The 50-day average is the average closing price over the last 50 sessions, and the 200-day average is the same over 200. A price above a rising line is in an uptrend, and a price below a falling line is in a downtrend. The framework reads these lines as reference levels, not as predictions.

How the framework works for this company

The framework is a set of hurdles that a company must clear in a fixed order. Layer 1 holds the hard floors: returns on capital, the level of borrowing, the quality of profit as cash, the shape of the price chart, the momentum test and the upside-to-downside ratio. A failure at any of these ends the review with a REJECTED verdict, and the later layers are not read. Layer 2 would score three to five operating measures for the business, and Layer 3 would decide the entry posture.

Each candidate is tested for a strategy bucket. CORE_ALPHA is for businesses expected to compound over three to five years. It needs a return on capital of at least 15 percent, with a preference above 18 percent, and either a fresh base breakout or a Stage 2 trend. TACTICAL_MOMENTUM is for shorter moves of three to twelve months, and it needs a Stage 2 trend that sits above rising 50-day and 200-day averages. Ajanta is tested under CORE_ALPHA, which matches the business profile.

The framework never lets a good business excuse a bad chart. That is why Ajanta can pass every financial floor and still be rejected. The later chapters show each floor in turn, starting with the financial tests and then the chart.

What the company does and how it earns

Ajanta is an Indian specialty pharmaceutical company. Its own description is that it develops, manufactures and markets specialty finished dosage medicines [1]. Its India business sells branded generics to doctors, who prescribe them for long-term conditions. This matters because a doctor who has prescribed a medicine for years is slow to switch, so the revenue is repeatable. The company's India business grew 24 percent in the June 2026 quarter, to ₹509 crore [3]. Dividing ₹509 crore by the quarter's revenue of ₹1,625.96 crore gives about 31 percent, while the source states 32 percent, so the share is shown as roughly a third of revenue. The therapy mix is cardiology at 37 percent, ophthalmology at 29 percent and dermatology at 22 percent [3].

The second engine is the United States. US generics sales were ₹487 crore in the June 2026 quarter, up 57 percent, and they made up 30 percent of revenue [3]. A generic is a copy of a medicine whose patent has ended, and the first company to launch one can earn a high share of sales for a time. The growth came from two launches in the quarter, and management says it expects the growth to slow as competition and price cuts arrive [4].

The rest of the revenue comes from Africa and Asia. Africa branded sales were ₹295 crore, up 30 percent [3]. Asia fell 16 percent, to ₹255 crore according to one report [2], and the company blames Middle East supply disruption for delayed dispatches [3]. A second report gives the Asia figure as ₹225 crore [3], so the rupee amount is not settled; the percentage fall is the same in both.

The quarter to June 2026 was strong. Revenue was ₹1,625.96 crore, up 24.82 percent, and profit after tax was ₹334.22 crore, up 30.89 percent [2][16][20]. Adjusted EBITDA, which excludes the foreign-exchange loss, was ₹454 crore, up 21 percent, at a margin of 28 percent [3]. The two foreign-exchange items sit in different lines. A loss inside operating costs lowers reported EBITDA, and the adjusted figure adds it back; reported EBITDA margin was about 26 percent [2]. The profit line was then helped by other income, which included a separate foreign-exchange gain of ₹50.5 crore. That gain equals about 15 percent of the quarter's reported profit [2]. That gain is not part of the operating business, so it is the reason the profit growth overstates the operating growth.

Asymmetry and target anchoring

The upside-to-downside ratio compares how far the price could rise with how far it could fall before the stop is hit. The formula is the base target minus the close, divided by the close minus the stop. A target without an anchor is treated as unverified, and an unverified target fails the floor. The rule is strict because a target chosen to look attractive can make almost any stock appear to have room to run.

The framework accepts two kinds of anchor. The first is a forward earnings forecast multiplied by a three-year median price-to-earnings ratio. The second is a technical measured move from a base. Ajanta does not publish a forward earnings-per-share figure that could serve as the first kind of anchor, and the company gives revenue guidance only. The search for a company earnings figure found none, so it is recorded as not published [4]. The measured move is therefore the anchor. It is built from the ledger: the 52-week high of ₹3,796.00 plus the height of the base, which is the high minus the 63-session base low of ₹3,301.00. The height is ₹495.00, so the base target is ₹4,291.00. The bull reference uses twice the height, which gives ₹4,786.00, and it is shown for context only.

Fatal-flaw red team

The red team asks what would make the verdict wrong, and the answer should be sharp. Three fatal flaws were tested against the evidence. Each is a reason a buy could fail even if the business keeps performing well.

Flaw one: the trend has already turned

The first flaw is that the Stage 3 reading is the start of a longer decline. The price is below its 50-day average, the Nifty 50 is in Stage 4, and only about one stock in five in the breadth pool is above its 50-day average. If the market keeps weakening, Ajanta could test the 200-day average at ₹3,126.09 and then the Core stop at ₹3,024.39. The rules would then have already rejected the name, so the flaw does not make the verdict wrong. It makes it more likely to stay right for longer.

Flaw two: the first-quarter profit is flattered and the US growth will slow

The second flaw is that the headline profit growth of 30.9 percent is partly non-operating. The company booked a ₹50.5 crore foreign-exchange gain, and management says US generics growth will drop to mid-single digits for the rest of the year [4]. Cash generation also weakened in FY26. Cash from operations was ₹529 crore against ₹1,157 crore a year earlier, so the operating conversion in the latest year is 0.50 [1]. The answer to this flaw is that the three-year cash test still passes, and management's US guidance of mid-single digits signals a sharp slowdown from the first quarter rather than a repeat of it. Those points carry weight, but they do not rescue a stock that fails the chart.

Flaw three: ownership and regulatory overhang

The third flaw is that the promoter's shares are pledged as security. The pledge was 17.94 percent of promoter holdings in March 2026 [6], 22.53 percent in June and 20.65 percent in September [5][8]. A release of 1,732,792 pledged shares was reported on 25 June 2026 [10]. The record does not give the pledged share count for each quarter, so it does not show whether the June rise in the percentage came from new pledges or from a fall in the promoter's own holding over the same period [5]. On the regulatory side, the Paithan plant received a Form 483 with five observations after an inspection in April 2026 [11][12][13][14], and a later VAI classification was reported in July [15]. The company also says the impact of an August 2025 income-tax search on the quarter is not ascertainable [2]. A pledge that falls is the better sign, but the pledge has stayed above 17 percent of promoter holdings in each of the last three quarters, and the tax matter has no number attached yet.

What would make the bear case wrong is a clean run of Stage 2 signals: a close above the 50-day average on heavy volume, relative strength turning positive, and a pledge that keeps falling. The record does not show any of these yet, so the flaws stand, and the verdict stands with them.

Invalidation levels and what we would do

An invalidation level is a price or event that ends a view. For a rejected stock, the relevant levels are the ones that would reopen the case and the ones that would confirm the rejection. Each rule below names the number that triggers it, so a reader can check the level against the next ledger.

A close above ₹3,541.88, the 50-day average, with the 200-day average still rising, would begin to reopen the case. The momentum gate would then be run again on the new close, and the Core test would be checked for Stage 2. The case reopens for the asymmetry floor only if a rebuilt target and stop restore a ratio of at least 3.0, since the ratio is about 1.45 at ₹3,541.88 on the current target and stop.

A close below ₹3,126.09, the 200-day average, would confirm that the long-term support has failed. The Core stop of ₹3,024.39 would then be the next line to watch, since a close below it ends the Core structure.

A close above ₹3,341.04 would take the upside-to-downside ratio below the Core floor of 3.0 on the current anchor, even if the chart improved. A new anchor would be needed before the ratio could pass again.

A new US FDA classification of official action indicated at Paithan, an adverse outcome of the tax search, or a rise in the pledge above the June level of 22.53 percent would each be a reason to re-underwrite the business before any other step.

What we would do is nothing, for now. No allocation is made while the verdict is REJECTED, and no buy is considered until the technical floor passes. If the price closes above ₹3,541.88 on heavier trading, the next step is to re-run the momentum gate on the new close. If the price closes below ₹3,126.09 first, the case is weaker still, and the report would be reviewed with the new price before any further action.

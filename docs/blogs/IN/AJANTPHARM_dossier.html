1. What the company does

Ajanta Pharma Limited is a Mumbai drug maker listed on the NSE as AJANTPHARM and on the BSE as 532331[1]. It was incorporated in 1979 and calls itself a specialty pharmaceutical formulation company, which means it turns active ingredients into finished tablets, creams and eye drops rather than making the raw chemicals[15][3]. It runs seven manufacturing plants in India, two of them approved by the US drug regulator, and a research centre in Mumbai[3][11]. The portfolio is more than 600 products, and the annual report says nearly half hold a first-to-market position in their markets[11].

The company earns money in four ways, and the difference between them is the main thing to understand. A branded generic is an off-patent medicine sold under the company's own brand name, pushed to doctors by a field force of medical representatives, so the buyer chooses on trust and repeat prescriptions rather than lowest price. Ajanta does this in India (30.6 percent of FY26 sales), in Asia (21.7 percent) and in Africa (15.9 percent), with over 6,000 medical representatives[5][15]. A US generic is the same kind of medicine sold without a brand to wholesalers and pharmacy chains. The buyer chooses on price and supply, so margins erode when competitors arrive. That business was 28.8 percent of FY26 sales, up from 22.8 percent a year earlier, and it was 30.1 percent in the June 2026 quarter[5][3].

The mix matters because the two kinds behave differently. Cardiology is 37 percent of India sales, ophthalmology 29, dermatology 22 and pain 10, all long-term conditions, and Ajanta's India price rises match the market's, so growth comes from volume and launches[2]. The US business is lumpier. Management has described part of it as limited-competition products, now expects price erosion and some loss of share, and says its customers pay more slowly[10][2][4]. Branded generics were 74.0 percent of sales in FY25, 68.2 percent in FY26 and 65.5 percent in the June 2026 quarter, so the company is becoming more US-dependent. CARE puts the US at about 29 percent of FY26 revenue against 23 percent a year earlier[5][3][15].

The Africa institution line sells to aid agencies through tenders and is volatile. Semaglutide, a weight-loss and diabetes drug, is the new piece: Ajanta signed an in-licensing deal with Biocon in December 2025 to market it in 26 countries across Africa, the Middle East and Central Asia, with approvals expected in late 2026 or early 2027[9][2][20]. No revenue from it is in our numbers.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • EIR is the establishment inspection report that the US drug regulator issues after an inspection, with a classification such as VAI (voluntary action indicated).
  • Stage 2 is a rising price trend above both the 50- and 200-day averages, and Stage 4 is a falling trend below both, with the 50-day also under the 200-day.
  • A base is a quiet stretch of sideways trading after a fall, and a pivot is the price level that the stretch must rise through to confirm a new advance.
  • Asymmetry is the ratio of the gain to the target to the loss to the stop; reward-to-risk means the same thing, and we also call it the gain-to-loss ratio.
  • A stop loss is a pre-set price at which a position is sold, a limit order is an order that fills only at your price or better, and an entry zone is the band of prices where buying is planned.
  • A promoter pledge is a promoter's use of their shares as collateral for a loan. If the loan is not repaid or the share price falls, the lender can sell the shares.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

A company is strong when it keeps earning high returns on the money it puts in. It is weak when that rests on one product, one customer or one regulator's goodwill. Ajanta's returns are high. Return on capital employed was 34.5 percent on Screener's trailing figure and 33 percent on the company's FY26 figure, return on equity 27.1 percent, and borrowings are ₹260 crore (about 0.06 times equity, including leases) against ₹4,527 crore of equity[1][5][11]. CARE Ratings reaffirmed its AA+ (stable) rating in August 2026 and measured interest cover at 99 times, so solvency is not the issue[15].

India: the strongest engine, with a hole in cardiology

In the twelve months to June 2026, IQVIA (the data firm that tracks prescriptions) shows Ajanta's India brands growing 15 percent against 11 percent for the market. Volume growth was 40 percent above the market and new launches 75 percent above it. The lead has widened from 11 versus 9 percent in December 2025 and 13 versus 10 in March 2026[2][3][6]. Cardiology, 37 percent of India sales, is the exception: Ajanta grew 9 percent against 15 for the market, and 5 against 13 in December. Management says IQVIA undercounts its cardiology sales. We cannot verify that and score the reported figure. India is guided to grow mid-teens for FY27, and Q1 sales grew 24 percent[3][2].

Asia and Africa: a missed guide in Asia, a strong Africa, and a logistics problem

Asia was guided to grow in the mid-teens for FY26 and grew minus 1 percent. The June 2026 quarter fell 16 percent to ₹255 crore because Middle East supply disruption delayed shipments. Management says the sales slipped into the September quarter and that demand is not the constraint[10][5][2]. Africa branded sales grew 15 percent in FY26 and 30 percent in Q1, helped by a larger and now more productive field force. Management expects the rest of FY27 to run a little below Q1's 30 percent[5][2]. Over the last four quarters the two regions together grew only 4.5 percent (₹2,055 crore against ₹1,966 crore), the figure the Layer 2 scorecard uses[7][6][3]. Semaglutide is the one new lever, and management says it is too early to size, because launches are two years away and the Indian market for the drug has plateaued[20][2].

United States: high returns today, a normalisation guide, and a tariff schedule

US sales rose 49 percent in FY26 to ₹1,557 crore and 57 percent in Q1 FY27 to ₹487 crore, and management says "we have a dominating market share" for most products and, asked about 20 to 25 percent, said "20% and above also"[5][2]. It guides FY27 to mid-single-digit growth, which with Q1 at ₹487 crore implies about ₹1,148 crore for the last three quarters at 5 percent growth (₹1,179 crore at 7 percent) against ₹1,247 crore a year earlier, a fall of roughly 6 to 8 percent that management ties to competition, price erosion and share loss[2][5][3]. The FDA inspected the Paithan plant from 13 to 21 April 2026 and closed with a Form 483 carrying 5 observations. The company later received the EIR classified as Voluntary Action Indicated (the regulator record, limited to the filings)[16][17]. On 21 July 2026 the US President announced that imported generics would stay duty-free for two years from 1 August 2026, then face a 100 percent tariff from August 2028 and 200 percent from August 2029 under Section 232. Management sees a lot of uncertainty[26][2].

Margins, research spending and the balance sheet

The gross margin, which is revenue minus the cost of materials and goods, rose from 74.7 percent in FY24 to 77.0 in FY25 and 78.3 in FY26 on our arithmetic from the audited statements. Management guides about 78 percent for FY27[12][11][2]. EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) was ₹1,395 crore in FY26, 25.6 percent of sales as reported, and ₹1,498 crore (27.5 percent) after adding back ₹103 crore of mark-to-market forex losses. The FY27 guide is about 27 percent excluding forex. Research spending rose 57 percent to ₹252 crore, about 5 percent of sales[5][2]. The balance sheet shows ₹103 crore of cash, ₹560 crore of current investments and ₹212 crore of borrowings, of which ₹207 crore are working-capital loans taken, per CARE, by the US subsidiary. Net cash is about ₹451 crore on our arithmetic. Capital spending is guided to ₹400 crore in FY27, ₹300 crore of it to expand the Pithampur plant[11][15][2].

Governance: a promoter family with outside interests, and a tax search

The promoters are four brothers. Two, Yogesh and Rajesh Agrawal, run the company and, management says, have no pledged shares, while the other two pledge shares to borrow for their own new businesses, which management says have nothing to do with Ajanta's borrowing[4]. A company clarification says a section of the promoter group that controls Lenexis Foodworks is independently acquiring Restaurant Brands Asia, and a news summary says the 2026 pledges secure debentures of Lenexis and Inspira Realty 2, companies outside Ajanta (secondary). [19][23]. On 9 June 2026 the Ravi Agrawal Trust sold 34.5 lakh shares (2.76 percent), taking the promoter holding from 66.25 to 63.48 percent (news report, secondary)[25][14]. Separately, the Income Tax Department searched offices and plants from 19 August 2025. The company received a notice for April 2019 to November 2025, filed its return on 6 April 2026 reporting no undisclosed income, says the effect is not ascertainable, and its auditors added an emphasis-of-matter paragraph[18][11]. What this means for an owner: the operating company is strong and conservatively financed. Its shareholders are borrowing against its shares, which concerns control and supply rather than Ajanta's own debt. A filed return reporting no undisclosed income is the company's own return, not an official clearance. Both are reasons to wait for facts, not to doubt the products.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Before you read

Glenmark Pharmaceuticals sells medicines in more than 80 countries, and India, the United States, Europe and emerging markets each carry a large share of sales[10]. We rate the stock WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT, because it passes our debt, pledge and cash rules and scores 3.5 out of 5 on operating checks, but the 27 October close of ₹2,265.70 gives a reward-to-risk (gain to target divided by loss to stop) of only 0.19 to 1[37][38]. We would be wrong if the Indore warning letter escalates or the September-quarter margin falls below 20%.

1. What the company does

Glenmark is a Mumbai-based drugmaker with 11 factories across four continents and sales of about US$2 billion a year. Management expects more than 60% of FY27 revenue to come from branded products, which doctors prescribe by name[10]. Its reported revenue for FY26 was ₹16,983 crore[8][29]. The business has three layers: branded medicines in India and emerging markets, generic copies of off-patent drugs in the United States and Europe, and a research arm that licenses new antibodies to larger companies.

In India, patients buy Glenmark medicines at pharmacies on a doctor's advice. Glenmark sells through distributors in a three-tier system, which means sales recorded by the company (primary sales) can differ from what pharmacies sell (secondary sales). It ranks 13th in the Indian market with a 2.37% share and has 11 brands in the top 300[4]. It is second in skin care, third in breathing medicines and fourth in heart care[4]. About 60% of its India sales come from long-term (chronic and sub-chronic) therapies, which repeat month after month[16].

In the United States, Glenmark sells generic tablets, creams, injectables and inhalers to wholesalers, pharmacy chains and hospital buyers. It has 225 products approved for sale and 53 applications waiting at the US regulator, of which 26 challenge a brand's patent[3]. Prices in this market keep falling as more copies arrive, so the company tries to win on harder-to-copy products. The first generic version of Flovent HFA 44 mcg gives it 180 days with no other generic competitor[3]. Most of its injectables are sold through partners and the company calls them commodity products that will take time to matter[4].

Europe and emerging markets add about 37% of sales. In Europe, branded products are about 30% of revenue today and management wants about 60% within five years[4]. RYALTRIS, a nasal spray for allergies, is sold in 57 markets and has earned about US$300 million in cumulative sales[2][10]. It is the company's first global brand. Management says its peak sales should reach US$250 to 300 million[10].

The research arm, Ichnos Glenmark Innovation (IGI), builds antibodies that attach to several cancer targets at once. In July 2025 it licensed its lead drug, ISB 2001, to AbbVie for US$700 million upfront, up to US$1.225 billion of later milestone payments and royalties on sales[26]. That one deal changed Glenmark's reported profit, cash and debt, so most of this report is about separating the deal's effects from the day-to-day business.

How the money flows, and the one-time AbbVie income

Glenmark spends about 7% to 8% of sales on research and development, and says that rate will continue[4]. IGI alone spends about US$70 million a year[10]. After the AbbVie deal, IGI no longer needs money from the parent. Management and India Ratings describe the upfront payment as funding IGI for the next three years[13][16]. That is shorter than US$700 million divided by US$70 million a year, which would be ten years. The filings do not explain the gap, so we use three years as the company's stated horizon and not as a limit on the cash.

The accounting is unusual. AbbVie paid US$700 million in September 2025[13]. Glenmark booked about US$525 million as revenue in the September 2025 quarter, about US$35 million in the second half of FY26, and plans US$70 million in each of FY27 and FY28[13][16]. Because the schedule matches the income to IGI's spending, the remaining income is roughly equal to IGI's yearly cost. The company reports it inside North America revenue[1][3]. No milestone payment has been received yet[4].

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) is operating profit before interest, tax and the write-off of past purchases. It shows what the business earns before the cost of its funding.
  • EPS (earnings per share) is the profit that belongs to one share. Share prices tend to follow it over several years.
  • ROCE (return on capital employed) measures the profit earned on each rupee of capital used. Screener's version is flattered here by one-time income.
  • Free cash flow, or FCF, is cash from operations minus spending on plants and equipment.
  • net debt is borrowings minus cash. A negative figure means the company holds more cash than debt.
  • debt-to-equity is borrowings divided by shareholders' funds. A figure above 1.20 is a red line in our rules.
  • gross margin is revenue minus the direct cost of making the product, as a share of revenue. operating margin is operating profit as a share of revenue.
  • price-to-earnings is the share price divided by the profit per share. A lower figure means each rupee of profit costs less.
  • guidance is a forecast that management gives about its own results. Chapter 2B checks how often it came true.
  • working capital is the money tied up in stock and unpaid customer bills, less what the company owes suppliers.
  • capital expenditure is spending on plants, equipment and long-lived assets such as licensed products.
  • Stage 3 is the topping or pullback phase of a share price, and Stage 4 is the downtrend. Stage 2 is a rising phase.
  • RSI (relative strength index) is a 0-to-100 gauge of how fast a price has risen or fallen. ATR (average true range) is the typical size of one day's price swing, in rupees.
  • A base is a flat resting area on a price chart, and the pivot is the price where a breakout from it would start.
  • The entry zone is the price range where we would buy. A stop loss is the price at which the idea is wrong and we sell. A limit order fills only at your price or better.
  • asymmetry (also called reward-to-risk) is the gain to our target divided by the loss to our stop.
  • A warning letter is a formal US FDA notice that a plant breaks manufacturing rules. A Form 483 lists inspector observations, and VAI (voluntary action indicated) is a lighter inspection result than OAI (official action indicated).

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The strongest part of the business is its Indian franchise. In the quarter to June 2026 its secondary sales grew 18.1% against 12.2% for the whole market, and 14.3% against 10.0% over the year to June[3][4]. Glenmark rose from 15th place in the Indian market in FY22 to 13th in FY26, and from 8th to 4th in heart care[10]. Eleven brands each sell more than ₹100 crore a year[10]. Because these brands are prescribed by habit, growth in them tends to repeat. Management says India should grow 12% to 15% a year from here[4].

The balance sheet is the second strength. At 31 March 2026 the company had no bank borrowings and held ₹1,176 crore of cash and bank balances[7]. India Ratings upgraded its bank loans to IND AA+ with a stable outlook in September 2026, citing net leverage of negative 0.13 times and interest cover of 21.9 times[16]. CRISIL's rating stood at AA with a positive outlook[17]. A company with no debt can ride out a bad year in the US without being forced to sell assets.

The third strength is product difficulty. Inhalers, nasal sprays and nebulizer medicines are harder to copy than tablets. Glenmark has the first approved generic of Flovent HFA 44 mcg and a nasal-spray version sold over the counter[3]. In India it launched the world's first nebulized triple therapy for chronic lung disease, which is now growing quickly[3][4]. RYALTRIS had more than US$100 million of sales last year, with secondary sales growing about 50%[9]. These products give pricing power that plain generics lack.

Where the moat is thin

Margins are the first thin spot. EBITDA margin was 20.03% in the June 2026 quarter, below the 21% to 22% guided for FY27[5][9]. Management said cost pressure from the Middle East conflict, shipping and raw materials would last for at least two more quarters[4]. The company had also promised about 23% from the second half of FY26. The second half actually delivered 21.3%, which is our calculation from the Q3 and Q4 results[11][14][8].

Cash conversion is the second. Trade receivables rose from ₹3,342 crore to ₹4,963 crore in FY26, a rise of 48.5%, and debtor days went from 92 to 107[7][29]. Management blamed the end of early collections from distributors, delays in the Middle East and currency effects[9]. Ind-Ra's own measure of the working-capital cycle rose from 91 to 100 days[16]. If customers pay more slowly, the cash cushion thins even when profit looks fine.

US generics are the third. The company has been named in more than 35 antitrust complaints about generic drug pricing[24]. It provided ₹323 crore for the direct-purchaser class and ₹278 crore for the state attorneys general in FY26[1]. It then settled with Humana for US$15.28 million and with the states for US$29.628 million over five years[23][24]. Management says two more plaintiff groups remain and that all known cases are already provided for[4].

Regulatory risk at the factories

The US regulator wrote to Glenmark's Indore plant in July 2025 with a warning letter. It cited failed dissolution tests, late stability testing and unvalidated test methods[20]. The plant had first been classed OAI (official action indicated) in May 2025[21]. The same letter listed earlier warning letters for a plant in Himachal Pradesh in 2019, the Goa plant in 2022 and the Monroe plant in North Carolina in 2023, and said these showed weak management oversight[20]. The Himachal Pradesh plant was put on an import alert in October 2022[20].

There is progress. Monroe received a VAI result, which allows commercial production to restart, in November 2025[22]. Goa was inspected in June 2026, ended with six procedural observations and no data-integrity finding, and received its inspection report with a VAI result in September 2026[19][18]. We found no public notice that the Indore warning letter has been closed. India Ratings lists the resolution of pending US FDA issues as a key item to watch[16].

India has its own risks. About 17% of India sales fall under price controls, and a GST tax order of ₹16.05 crore plus interest and penalty is under appeal[16][25]. Neither is large. They matter only if a similar order is applied to other years.

Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

The second half of Layer 2 asks one question. When management gave a number, did it deliver? Glenmark has made several clear promises since 2022. The balance-sheet promises were kept. The margin and revenue-timing promises were not.

3. The numbers, in plain words

Five years of figures show a company that grew revenue from ₹12,305 crore to ₹16,983 crore, lost money in FY24, and then reported its best profit ever in FY26[29]. The FY26 profit is the problem. It is a blend of three one-time effects: US$560 million of AbbVie licensing income, ₹2,266 crore of exceptional charges, and a one-time hit to India sales when distributors cut stock after the GST change[16][7][12].

Screener's headline figures miss that blend. They show a return on capital of 39.8%, profit growth of 134% over the last year and a price-to-earnings of 20.9[29]. None of the three is reliable. The 20.9 does not match reported profit: market value of ₹63,944 crore divided by TTM profit of ₹1,798 crore gives 35.6. Screener's multiple appears to use a profit figure before exceptional charges, which we could not confirm.

How it was calculated

1. What the company does

Welspun Corp Limited is the flagship listed company of Welspun World, a group whose other listed company, Welspun Living, is a promoter-group company.[3] It is one of the largest makers of large-diameter steel line pipe in the world. It also makes ductile iron (DI) pipes, stainless steel bars and pipes, thermo-mechanically treated rebars, and Sintex water tanks and plastic pipes.[1] It serves customers in more than fifty countries from plants in India, the United States and Saudi Arabia.[3] CARE Ratings puts global line-pipe capacity at about 2.02 million tonnes a year, of which 1.32 million is in India and 0.70 million in the US, plus 0.5 million tonnes run by an associate in Saudi Arabia.[28]

Large-diameter line pipe is the steel tube that carries gas, oil or water over long distances. It is made by three main routes: longitudinally submerged arc welding (LSAW) for the thickest walls, spiral welding for water and lower-pressure gas, and electric resistance or high-frequency welding (ERW and HFIW) for smaller diameters.[4] Buyers are pipeline owners, so demand follows how many pipelines get built, and CARE says oil and gas make up more than half of the company's revenue.[28]

The US plant at Little Rock, Arkansas matters most to the story. The new HFIW mill there has been commissioned and management says it is stable and about to start executing orders.[5] A second mill, a $150 million LSAW line announced in August 2025, is promised for the end of calendar 2026.[33][8] Management says US demand is booked through FY28, with talks under way for FY29, and gave a rough split of its US order portfolio of about 75 percent for Gulf Coast LNG export lines and 25 percent for gas lines that feed data centres.[5]

In the June 2026 quarter, line pipe sales (India and the US) rose 6.0 percent to 193 thousand tonnes, DI pipes rose 6.2 percent to 69, TMT rebars rose 17.5 percent to 47, and stainless bars and pipes fell 24.1 percent to 6.3 thousand tonnes.[4] The filings give volumes and not profit by product, so the profit mix is N/A here.

How the money is made: steel in, pipe out

More than 85 percent of the raw material cost is hot-rolled coil and plate, and the company limits its price risk by tying up steel with suppliers when it bids for a project and confirming the steel order as soon as it wins.[28] On the June call management said the steel is completely hedged against the order book, which is why it is willing to give a full-year profit guide.[5] That is a pass-through of cost, not a promise of the same margin each quarter. A steel price move after the hedge, or a supplier that cannot deliver for FY29 at a fixed price, is a risk the call itself raised.[5]

Profit per tonne shows what the US mix does. CARE puts operating profit per tonne for the pipes and steel business at ₹11,031 in FY24, ₹11,922 in FY25 and ₹14,413 in FY26, and credits the US line-pipe business.[28] Management says US EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) a tonne has historically been close to $300 and is 'slightly more' now in what it calls an exceptional scenario, so $300 is the level to underwrite for the long run.[5] The June quarter's 18.5 percent margin is therefore a peak reading, not a normal run rate.

The order book: how much is signed, and when it ships

The presentation of 24 July 2026 put the global order book at about ₹24,750 crore, measured to 22 July.[3][4] The spoken call transcript says ₹25,750 crore, so the written figure of ₹24,750 crore is the one used here and the difference is disclosed in the run notes.[5] Three orders followed. A $1.8 billion order for pipes made in the US, about ₹17,200 crore, was announced on 21 August for delivery between FY28 and FY29.[29] A $412.5 million order for HFIW pipe, about ₹4,000 crore, followed on 25 September for FY28 and FY29, and the company-stated order book became $4.7 billion.[30] On 23 October, the session date, the press reported a ₹5,500 crore order for FY29 and an all-time high order book of $5.25 billion.[31] The 23 October order is an overlay (its time against the close is not verified) and is used in no gate or calculation.

Nearly all of this book ships in FY28 and FY29, after the new plants start, so FY27 is largely sold, FY28 and FY29 depend on execution, and the price already assumes a lot of both.

US tariffs, anti-dumping and the regulatory moat

A US proclamation under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act imposes 10 to 50 percent duties on steel, aluminum and copper articles and their derivatives from all countries, effective 6 April 2026.[34] Management says the Section 232 duties are 'by statute of law' and cannot be rolled back, and that 'the regulatory challenge is more for the importers rather than domestic producers'.[5] That is the company's view. The dossier checked only the proclamation text, so whether a given rate applies to line pipe is not verified. No anti-dumping order covering Welspun's own US products was found in the sources used, so that point is N/A.

In Saudi Arabia, asked about the anti-dumping investigation into imported DI pipe, management said 'there is a significant progress, which is happening'. The outcome is not yet public in the sources used.[5] The risk on the other side is the one that matters for a buyer today. A market that is protected by tariffs and booked through FY28 is also a market where one policy change, one delayed plant or one cancelled project moves the whole earnings path.

The newer businesses: stainless, DI pipes and Sintex

Welspun Specialty Solutions (WSSL) makes stainless bars and seamless pipes. The company calls it the only integrated Indian producer from steel melting to finished pipe, and holds about 55 percent of it.[4][28] Its EBITDA rose 52 percent in FY26, but its June-quarter volume fell about 24 percent.[7][4] DI pipes have been weak: CARE describes significant underperformance offset by the US line pipes. Management has shifted to exporting pig iron and DI pipe because Indian water-scheme spending is slow.[28][5]

Sintex, the water-tank and plastic-pipe brand, is being built out. Distributors rose from 356 to 545 and retailers from 20,000 to 37,000 between June 2024 and June 2026, but the domestic market is muted.[4][5] An analyst asked about negative Sintex margins and management answered 'please do not see Sintex margins as negative' without a number, so Sintex profit is N/A here.[5]

2. Why the business is strong or weak

Welspun Corp is a strong operating business at a high price. The hard evidence for the strength is the balance sheet, the delivery against its own guidance and the order book. The evidence for the weakness is that the profit is tied to a handful of US projects, that a large slice of reported profit is not repeatable, and that the stock has already moved on the news.

What is strong

Return on capital employed (ROCE, the profit a business earns on the money tied up in it) was 22.9 percent on Screener's measure. The company's own figure was 22.3 percent for FY26 and 23.1 percent for the June quarter, annualised.[1][4] Net cash, meaning cash and investments less borrowings, was ₹2,336 crore at 30 June 2026 after ₹834 crore of capital spending in the quarter, up from ₹1,627 crore at March.[3][7] CARE rates its non-convertible debentures AA+ with a stable outlook and calls liquidity strong, with ₹3,766 crore of cash and investments at March 2026.[28]

The competitive edge is hard to copy quickly. Management says a new US plant takes at least 18 to 24 months to build, and that approvals from major buyers take 'weeks, not months' after commissioning.[5] CARE agrees that the quality demands create high entry barriers.[28] The company also has plants in the US and Saudi Arabia, which sit inside the two markets that are building local capacity, while its Indian line-pipe mills export 150,000 to 200,000 tonnes a year.[5]

What is weak or fragile

First, quality of the headline profit. June-quarter profit after tax was ₹1,046 crore, but ₹548 crore of it was a one-time gain on selling part of the stake in its Saudi associate EPIC. Without it profit was ₹499 crore.[3][4] The sources do not reconcile to the rupee: ₹1,046 less ₹548 is ₹498 crore, the company states ₹499 crore, and Screener's net profit of ₹1,048 crore includes minorities. We use the company's ₹499 crore for recurring profit and do not capitalise disposal gains into recurring earnings. This is the fourth such gain since FY24 (₹105 crore in FY24, ₹378 crore in the December 2024 quarter and ₹477 crore of exceptional items in the March 2025 quarter came before it), so reported earnings per share (EPS, profit divided by shares) overstates what the businesses earn, and Screener's trailing price-to-earnings of 27.3 is lower than the 35.8 on our adjusted earnings.[11][12][13][1]

Second, concentration. Oil and gas are more than half of revenue, the largest orders are US gas lines for FY28 and FY29. The company said on the July call that it had no FY29 order book yet (the August to October orders now reach into FY29). Management also says the steel plan for FY29 is still being agreed with suppliers.[28][5] Third, the Indian business is soft: management calls domestic demand muted, DI pipes are under severe pressure and Sintex is being funded through the wait.[5] Fourth, insiders sold: on 26 August 2026 a promoter-group company and the chief executive sold about 63 lakh shares, around 2.4 percent of the equity, at an average ₹2,275.30. The promoters' share count fell from 131,188,603 to 125,188,603 between the June and September filings.[32][17][16]

Flaw 1: margins and mix are near a peak

The June quarter's 18.5 percent EBITDA margin and the US profit per tonne that management calls 'an exceptional scenario' are both above the long-run levels the company itself quotes, which is $300 a tonne in the US.[4][5] If margins normalise as new US competitors or imports arrive, or as the buyers who are 'intelligent' renegotiate, the central margin of 15.4 percent is too high. The −100 basis point stress takes the ratio from −3.86 to −4.95.

Persistence is the opposite risk and is also stressed. If the June margin of 18.5 percent held for all of FY28 the target would be ₹2,326.60 and the ratio −0.49. Even the most generous margin does not turn the ratio positive at an 18.82 multiple, which is the main reason the deferral does not depend on a margin view.

Flaw 2: execution and concentration

FY28 earnings depend on a mill that is not yet running (the US LSAW line), a Saudi plant whose date management now gives as quarter 3 of FY27 against the Q2 it gave earlier (our reading: about a quarter later, although management says nothing has slipped). A steel supply plan for FY29 that management says is still being agreed.[5] The key-project slip stress takes revenue to ₹22,500 crore and margin to 14.0 percent and gives EPS of 76.49, a target of ₹1,439.60 and a ratio of −7.44. Policy is the other side: the Section 232 duties are a proclamation, and the company's argument that they cannot be rolled back is its own view.[34][5] More than half of revenue is oil and gas, and the US order book is mostly two years deep.[28] That depth is a strength until a project is delayed or cancelled.

Flaw 3: earnings quality and insider selling

Four one-time gains since FY24 mean reported profit was better than recurring profit in FY24, FY25 and the June 2026 quarter. The market's 27.3 times trailing multiple is 35.8 times on adjusted earnings.[1] The normalised-earnings stress is exactly that: replacing reported trailing earnings per share of 87.55 with 66.81 raises the multiple paid by 31 percent.

Promoters and the chief executive sold about 2.4 percent of the equity on 26 August at ₹2,275.30, in the same weeks as the $1.8 billion order. The Board has approved buying 51 percent of a captive power company from Welspun Living, a promoter-group company, for ₹67.6 crore.[32][6] Neither is evidence of a problem, but both are the kind of event a buyer at a record price would want to watch.

Flaw 4: cash conversion leans on customer advances

Net working capital is negative because US customers pay advances to book capacity, and the finance chief expects that to continue.[8] When the advances are used up as pipes ship and no new advances arrive on the same terms, operating cash flow falls even if profit does not. Other current liabilities include customer advances but also other items, so their movement is a broad proxy for advances and not a measure of them.

The FY24 year shows the reverse: other current liabilities fell ₹2,664 crore and operating cash flow was only 1.15 times profit.[10] What this means for you: the 1.29 reported reading is helped by advances, the 1.05 reading without working-capital swings is the cleaner guide. A fall in advances would pull cash conversion toward that lower figure while the post-spending reading of 0.52 stays the weak point until the plants are built and paid for.

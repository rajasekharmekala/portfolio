Before you read

ITC Limited is India's largest cigarette maker, with standalone gross revenue of ₹80,867 crore in the year to March 2026 (consolidated revenue from operations was ₹89,913 crore) [4][3]. This book concludes REJECTED: the share is in a Stage 4 downtrend that fails the recovery test, and reward-to-risk (the potential gain for each rupee at risk) is 1.01 to 1 against a 2.0 floor [35]. The business is sound. It has a 38.9% return on capital, almost no debt and a 5.6% dividend yield, so this is a price and timing verdict [14]. The verdict is wrong if the cigarette tax shock fades faster than feared, and the first test is the Q2 result on 2 November 2026 [14].

1. What the company does

ITC was founded in 1910, is run from Kolkata, and is listed on the NSE and BSE. Screener's company summary calls it the largest cigarette manufacturer and seller in the country, and lists four operating segments: FMCG cigarettes, FMCG others, paperboards-paper-packaging, and agri [14]. In the year to March 2026 the parent company (standalone accounts) reported gross revenue of ₹80,867.49 crore and earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA), which is profit before the accounting and finance adjustments, of ₹25,208.22 crore [4][5]. The consolidated accounts add subsidiaries such as ITC Infotech and Surya Nepal, so they are larger: revenue from operations was ₹89,913.33 crore and Screener's operating profit was ₹27,306 crore, and Chapter 3 uses that consolidated set [3][14][1]. Hotels are no longer inside ITC. The hotels business was demerged into a separate company, ITC Hotels Limited, with effect from 1 January 2025, and ITC now keeps a 40% stake in it while the other 60% went to ITC shareholders [7][10][13]. ITC Hotels is separately listed (NSE: ITCHOTELS, BSE: 544325), so an ITC shareholder owns hotels twice: directly in ITC Hotels shares received in the demerger, and indirectly through ITC's 40% [11].

The most important fact about ITC is who pays and how much of the payment is tax. Smokers pay for cigarettes, and cigarettes are the profit engine: in FY26 the cigarette segment produced ₹21,051 crore of the ₹25,139 crore total segment result, or 83.7% (my arithmetic on the filed segment table) [5]. From 1 February 2026 the tax on a cigarette changed. The Annual Report says the goods and services tax (GST) moved from 28% of transaction value to 40% of retail sale price, together with a steep rise in excise duties after the compensation cess was phased out [6]. Press reports put the new excise at about ₹2,050 to ₹8,500 per 1,000 sticks depending on stick length, on top of the 40% GST; the Annual Report gives no per-stick figures, so treat that range as secondary [27][33]. Because excise is included in reported gross revenue, gross revenue now overstates what ITC keeps: in Q1 FY27 standalone gross revenue was ₹26,794 crore but net revenue, after excise, was ₹16,812 crore [2].

The second business is FMCG others: packaged foods such as Aashirvaad atta, Sunfeast biscuits, Bingo! snacks and YiPPee! noodles, plus personal care, Classmate notebooks and incense sticks. FY26 segment revenue was ₹24,210 crore and segment result was ₹1,803 crore, a result of about 7.4% of revenue and an EBITDA margin of about 10.0% [5]. The company also owns a group of digital-first and organic brands (Yogabar, 24 Mantra, Prasuma, Meatigo and Mother Sparsh) with an annualised revenue run-rate of about ₹1,500 crore in Q1 FY27, and a new fresh-food cloud-kitchen business whose gross merchandise value, the total value of food sold, grew 90% year on year [1]. This business is growing at double digits, but its margin is about half of what pure-play staples peers earn, a gap Chapter 3 quantifies.

The third and fourth businesses are the quiet ones. Agri trades and processes wheat, leaf tobacco, spices, coffee and seafood, earning a thin operating margin of about 4.4% in Q1 FY27 according to ICICI Securities, and it also supplies wheat to the atta brand and leaf tobacco to the cigarette business [13][1]. Paperboards, paper and packaging makes board for cartons, specialty paper and flexible packaging. It is cyclical and was hurt by cheap imports and high wood prices in FY25, then recovered: Q1 FY27 segment profit rose 38% on 9% revenue growth [1]. The group also owns ITC Infotech (information technology services) and Surya Nepal, which are consolidated but small next to cigarettes [1].

Put together, ITC is a cash machine with a growing but low-margin side business. The cigarette cash pays a large dividend (₹14.50 per share for FY26, a cash outflow of ₹18,167.57 crore) and funds the push into foods and paper [4]. Four measures describe the machine. Return on capital employed (ROCE) is the profit earned on each rupee the business uses, and it was 38.9% [14]. Earnings per share (EPS) is profit divided by the number of shares, and it was ₹16.51 consolidated for FY26 [14]. Free cash flow (FCF) is the cash left after capital spending, and it was ₹16,332 crore [14]. Debt-to-equity is borrowings divided by shareholders' money, and it was about 0.03, which means almost no debt [14]. The model works while cigarette profit stays stable. It is under pressure now because the tax reset cut cigarette profit by 35% in the first full quarter, which is the story the rest of this book weighs against the share price.

How a packet of cigarettes becomes ITC profit

Think of a retail price of 100 rupees. Before February 2026 a large part went to tax, and ITC kept the rest. Cigarette segment profit equalled about 71% of net revenue in Q1 FY26 and about 63% in Q1 FY27, according to ICICI Securities [13]. Both figures are very high for a consumer product. The tax reset changed the sum. Excise duty on ITC's consolidated books was ₹10,408.93 crore in Q1 FY27 against ₹1,634.56 crore a year earlier, more than six times the year-earlier level [3]. ICICI Securities puts the cigarette tax hike at about 35% [13]. ITC cannot pass all of that on at once, because a rise that large on top of a pack price would push a smoker to a cheaper product. That 35% is a tax figure and is separate from the 35% fall in segment profit. The company raised prices in stages, about 22 to 23% in the first quarter, and further rises came in September [13][23].

The words that matter for the rest of the book are these. Net revenue is revenue after excise. Segment result is the segment's profit before interest and tax. Illicit trade is untaxed or tax-evaded cigarettes sold outside the legal system. Downtrading is a smoker switching to a shorter or cheaper stick. ITC says the legal cigarette industry holds only 10% of all tobacco use in India, against a global average of 90%, and it estimates that illicit cigarettes already cost the exchequer about ₹23,000 crore a year, equal to about one-third of the legal industry [6][4]. That is the company's argument that higher tax drives smokers out of the taxed market and not out of smoking, and it is the main risk the verdict has to weigh.

What changed in the structure during 2025 and 2026

Three structural changes matter. First, the hotels demerger took effect on 1 January 2025, so FY25 net profit includes a one-off exceptional gain of ₹15,163 crore on the demerger and is not a clean starting point for growth rates [7]. Second, ITC bought growth in foods. Sresta Natural Bioproducts (24 Mantra Organic) was merged in during FY26, Ample Foods (Prasuma, Meatigo) and Mother Sparsh were added, and on 28 September 2026 ITC bought the remaining 52.5% of Sproutlife Foods (Yogabar) for about ₹645 crore, taking it to 100% [4][7][14][30]. Third, ITC agreed on 31 March 2025 to buy the Century Pulp and Paper undertaking of Aditya Birla Real Estate for ₹3,498 crore in cash; the Competition Commission cleared it on 16 December 2025 and the forest ministry approved the land transfer on 18 March 2026, and press reports say it completed in September 2026 [6][28][29]. These deals use cash and add capacity, which matters for the dividend question in Chapter 2B.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Gross revenue: what customers and the government are charged in total, including excise duty.
  • Net revenue: gross revenue minus excise duty, which is the revenue ITC keeps.
  • Segment result: a business segment's profit before interest and tax.
  • Excise duty: a tax charged on cigarettes when they leave the factory; from February 2026 it sits on top of 40% GST.
  • Illicit trade: cigarettes sold without paying the full tax.
  • Downtrading: a buyer switching to a cheaper or shorter product.
  • Demerger: splitting a company so that one business becomes a separate listed company owned by the same shareholders.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

ITC is strong where it matters most and weak in the places investors want it to improve. The strength is a pricing franchise in cigarettes that has survived a tax rise the company calls unprecedented [1][6]. It is backed by a net-cash balance sheet and a tobacco-to-foods supply chain that rivals cannot copy quickly. The weakness is dependence: one product line earns about four-fifths of segment profit, and its tax is set by the government, not by the market. A good business can be a poor holding at the wrong price, and the evidence below separates the two.

Competitive position in cigarettes

ITC's own Annual Report describes an integrated 'seed-to-smoke' chain, which means it develops leaf tobacco with farmers, processes it, makes the cigarette and distributes it, all in-house [6]. A retail-share estimate in a Q2 preview puts ITC at 75% to 80% of the legal cigarette market. It puts Godfrey Phillips near 12% and VST near 8%. This is a secondary source, not an ITC statement [23]. The test of a franchise is what happens when price rises sharply. ICICI Securities reports that ITC's cigarette volume fell only about 5% in Q1 FY27 even after price rises of 22 to 23%, against its own expectation of an 8 to 10% fall [13]. Prabhudas Lilladher estimates ITC's volume fall since Q3 FY26 at about 9%, against 13 to 15% for peers [31]. ITC does not publish cigarette volumes in the results documents I read, so these are brokerage estimates, not company data. Both point the same way: ITC is losing less volume than its peers.

The counter-evidence is also real. A retail-channel report cited by a trading-education site put the whole industry's volume drop at about 20% in April 2026. It flagged premium king-size sticks as the hardest hit, because their price per stick rose by 25 to 40% [32]. That figure is for April only and rests on an unverified press report. I treat it as a warning about the worst month, not as a run-rate. ITC itself says Q1 strategy and outcomes are 'tracking well in line with internal plans'. It also says it made over 30 portfolio interventions. But it gives no volume number to test that claim against [2][1].

FMCG others: scale without margin yet

In Q1 FY27 FMCG others revenue rose 12% (16% excluding staples) and segment profit rose 21%. The segment's EBITDA margin was 10% excluding the acquired Sresta business, up 55 basis points. As reported, it was 9.7% [2][1]. The company had to absorb a sharp jump in fuel, edible oil, soap noodles and packaging costs from the West Asia conflict. It cushioned the jump with inventory cover and commodity hedges [1]. The structural problem is the level, not the direction. Hindustan Unilever's operating margin on Screener is 22 to 24% in recent quarters. Britannia's is 16 to 20%. ITC's packaged foods earn about 10% at the EBITDA level [17][19][2]. ITC is spending to build distribution. It runs 12 integrated manufacturing and logistics hubs and an electronic ordering platform covering over 8 lakh outlets [4]. So the margin gap is partly an investment. It also means FMCG others cannot replace cigarette profit soon.

Agri and paper: the low-margin cushions

Agri is volatile. Q1 FY27 segment revenue fell 17% to ₹8,082 crore and segment profit fell 18%. West Asia disruptions delayed export orders and wheat timing shifted. Value-added products such as spices and fruit and vegetables grew strongly [2][1]. Paper is recovering. A minimum import price on virgin multi-layer paperboard has applied since 22 August 2025 and slowed cheap imports. Wood costs eased. Q1 FY27 segment profit rose 38% with margin up 200 basis points. An anti-dumping duty on Indonesian supply awaits finance ministry approval [4][1]. With Century Pulp and Paper added, ITC becomes India's largest integrated paperboards and paper business, according to press reports. Sources differ on the capacity total: 12.8 lakh tonnes in one and about 1.5 million tonnes in another [28][29]. Both segments diversify earnings, but together they earned ₹2,293 crore of FY26 segment result against ₹21,051 crore for cigarettes [5].

Who owns the shares, and who is selling

ITC has no promoter group: promoter holding and pledged shares are both 0% in the September 2026 pattern, so the usual pledge red line is not triggered [16]. Foreign investors owned 43.26% in December 2023 and 33.22% in September 2026. Domestic institutions rose from 41.98% to 49.81% over the same period. Mutual funds alone rose from 9.66% to 17.02% [14][16]. British American Tobacco (BAT), the largest holder, cut its stake to 22.9% in a May 2025 block sale at ₹413 per share [26]. I could not verify any later change from a primary filing. Treat 22.9% as the last confirmed figure. On 8 October 2026 GQG Partners sold about 36.51 crore shares, 2.91% of the company, at ₹257.35, and domestic mutual funds and Fidelity bought them [25]. Block sales near the 52-week low show how supply from large sellers can cap a rebound even when the buyers are strong.

3. The numbers, in plain words

This chapter reads five years of consolidated numbers and checks them against ITC's filings. Screener's 'sales' line is net of excise duty, and I confirmed it by arithmetic. FY26 revenue from operations in the filing is ₹89,913.33 crore and the excise expense line is ₹11,044.93 crore, which leaves ₹78,868 crore, the Screener figure [3][14]. The Q1 FY27 check works the same way: ₹29,523.30 crore less ₹10,408.93 crore is ₹19,114 crore [3][14]. I use the filing where the two sources differ and say so. This matters because the newspaper headline for Q1 FY27 reads 'revenue up 28%', while the like-for-like figure, net of excise, is down 11% [1][14].

Five-year sales growth is lower than it looks. Net sales were ₹60,645 crore in FY22 and ₹78,868 crore in FY26. But FY22 and FY23 include the hotels business and FY24 onward do not. So the FY23 to FY24 fall of 4.2% is a demerger effect [14][7]. From FY23 to FY26 net sales grew at about 3.6% a year (my arithmetic; Screener prints 4%). Net profit grew at about 2.6% a year (Screener prints 3%) [14]. For a company that pays out most of its profit, that is a slow-growth profile. It is not a compounder in the growth sense.

FY25 net profit of ₹35,052 crore includes ₹15,163 crore of exceptional gain on the hotels demerger, which is not repeatable earnings [14][7]. Without it, profit has been flat at roughly ₹20,000 to ₹21,000 crore for four years. It was ₹19,477 crore in FY23 and ₹20,751 crore in FY24. It was about ₹20,562 crore in FY25 on Crisil's adjusted basis, and ₹21,018 crore in FY26 [14][12]. The operating profit margin (OPM, operating profit as a share of net sales) has held in a narrow 34 to 37% band over the same years [14].

The trailing twelve months to June 2026 show the first break. Net sales were ₹76,488 crore (-3% over the year). Operating profit was ₹25,682 crore and OPM was 34%. Net profit was ₹20,184 crore, which includes a ₹406 crore one-off gain from consolidating Sproutlife [14][2]. The quarterly table shows the cause. Q1 FY27 OPM was 27% against 32% a year earlier and 39% in the March 2026 quarter [14][1]. The March quarter included two months of the new tax regime, and ITC calls it a transition quarter. The sources I read do not explain why its margin was so high. So I do not treat 39% as a run-rate [4].

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate in Chapter 5 rests on a weakest assumption. This chapter names it, says what evidence would show it wrong, and says when that evidence arrives. It also states the strongest case against the verdict and the one claim that, if false, breaks the whole analysis.

Weakest assumption in the earnings estimate

The base case relies on cigarette profit per stick recovering to its pre-tax-reset level by the March 2027 quarter. ICICI Securities says ITC must take a further 12 to 13% price rise over two quarters to get there, and that volume could dip more in Q2 before it recovers [13]. Prabhudas Lilladher expects volume and margin pressure to last two to three more quarters [31]. The first checkpoint is the Q2 FY27 result on 2 November 2026 [14]. If the cigarette segment result is not above Q1's ₹3,341 crore, the recovery is stalling. A volume fall of about 8% is the warning level, because that is the low end of the 8 to 10% fall ICICI Securities had expected for Q1. A fall of 10% or more is the KPI 1 FAIL line, and there the recovery path is wrong. A second checkpoint is the Q3 FY27 result, expected in late January 2027 (date not announced).

Weakest assumption in the multiple

The base case holds the multiple at 16.69 times, which assumes no further de-rating. The risk is tax policy: the government raised the statutory National Calamity Contingent Duty (NCCD) rate on tobacco from 25% to 60% from 1 May 2026, but kept the effective duty at 25% through a notification [27]. That leaves room for the government to raise the effective tax by notification without new legislation. If it does, every earnings estimate in this book is too high and the multiple falls. The evidence would be a notification changing the effective NCCD rate. It can come at any time, which is why I treat it as a bear-case risk and not a timed event. For scale, a fall to 14 times on FY28E earnings per share of ₹16.31 would give about ₹228, which is 12% below the close.

Weakest assumption in the dividend

The total return case adds a 5.6% dividend yield. FY26 dividends cost ₹18,167.57 crore against free cash flow of ₹16,332 crore, and consensus FY27E EPS of ₹14.48 is below the ₹14.50 total dividend per share [4][14][35]. ITC has ₹28,774 crore of cash on ICICI Securities' count, so it can pay for a year or two from its balance sheet [13]. It also spent about ₹4,143 crore on the paper and Yogabar deals in H1 FY27 [28][30]. The interim dividend decision (last year's ₹6.50 was declared on 29 January 2026, with the Q3 results) will show whether the board intends to hold ₹14.50 per share [9]. At a ₹12 dividend the yield would be 4.6%, still above the large staples peers but a visible step down from 5.6%.

The red-team bear case

The strongest case against owning ITC is that the market is correct to cut it. ITC itself says illicit cigarettes are already about one-third of the legal industry, and the tax rise widens the price gap between legal and illegal products [4]. A retail-channel report put April industry volume down about 20%, and Prabhudas puts peer volume falls at 13 to 15% [32][31]. Consensus FY27E EPS has fallen 9.3% in 90 days, foreign ownership has fallen from 43.26% to 33.22% in nearly three years, and large holders (BAT in 2025, GQG in October 2026) are selling blocks [35][14][26][25]. The chart agrees: Stage 4, below all three averages. On this reading the right price is the bear value of ₹217.20 or lower, 16.3% below the close, and the shares can drift to it with no news at all.

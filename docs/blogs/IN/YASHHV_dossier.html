1. Origin, Corporate Evolution & Past Business Cycles

Core Investor Question: How did a Vadodara-based electrical component workshop founded in 2002 evolve into India's only independent condenser-graded transformer bushing specialist, and how did it survive past power-sector cycles?

1.1 Origins in Vadodara and the 2008 Leadership Inflection

Yash Highvoltage Limited was incorporated in 2002 in Vadodara, Gujarat, inside western India's transformer manufacturing cluster [1][14]. For its first six years, it supplied conventional oil-impregnated paper (OIP) transformer bushings to the domestic market. At that time, utilities trusted European multinationals like ABB (now Hitachi Energy) and Trench/HSP for extra-high-voltage equipment. The turning point came in 2008, when first-generation entrepreneur Keyur Girishchandra Shah took over management [1]. Shah brought over 25 years of technical and commercial experience in high-voltage insulation [1]. Instead of treating bushings as commodity metal and porcelain, he refocused Yash on condenser-graded transformer bushings.

External validation of this technical pivot arrived quickly. In 2009, Yash completed its first export shipment, proving an Indian workshop could meet global tolerances [1]. By 2011, the company commissioned a new manufacturing bay in Halol, Vadodara [1]. In 2012, it won utility approval from NTPC and built its first 16 kA High Current (HC) generator step-up bushing [1]. These milestones set a clear playbook for two decades. Yash finds critical bottlenecks where Indian OEMs import from Europe or Japan, builds in-house testing, wins utility approval, and captures repeat orders.

1.2 The Swiss Technology Partnership and the Pivot to Dry-Type RIP/RIS

Between 2013 and 2019, Yash made a strategic pivot that reshaped its unit economics and moat. Traditional OIP bushings hold mineral oil inside a porcelain shell. When an OIP bushing fails under stress, pressurized oil can ignite and cause a severe transformer fire. Global utilities began shifting to dry-type, fire-resistant Resin-Impregnated Paper (RIP) and Resin-Impregnated Synthetic (RIS) bushings. In 2013, Keyur Shah signed an agreement with Swiss pioneer MGC Moser Glaser AG to bring RIP technology to India [1].

In 2016, Yash became the first Indian MSME to localize dry-type RIP/RIS assembly under MGC's technical collaboration [1]. During 2016–17, Yash built the world's first 52 kV 6,300 A Hybrid RIP & OIP bushing for PGCIL's STATCOM substations [1]. In 2017, it launched India's first OIP condenser bushing with silicone-polymer composite insulators [1]. Two years later, in 2019, it delivered India's first localized RIS transformer bushing [1]. This technology push happened during a steep downturn in Indian power capex, when leveraged OEMs like EMCO and Vijai Electricals faced distress. Yash stayed asset-light, avoided EPC contracting, and focused on high-margin retrofit orders and utility approvals. It exited the 2012–2018 slump with a clean balance sheet and a multi-year qualification lead as the grid cycle turned.

Business Cycle Sensitivity: Why Bushings Lag—and Outlast—Transformer Booms

Unlike raw-material-heavy conductors or distribution transformers, condenser bushings are late-cycle, specification-locked components. Utilities take 8–10 years to approve a bushing vendor. Once approved, repeat order rates exceed 96% [1].

Even when new transformer capex slows, over 50,000 installed Yash bushings need retrofit and replacement over a 30-year service life. This installed base softens cyclicality compared to greenfield EPC contractors [1].

Yash's engineering moat rests on capacitive-grading precision across all four segments [1]. 3.2 Supply Chain, Import Dependency & Working Capital Cycle

Yash's bill of materials relies on electrical kraft and crepe paper, aluminum foil, epoxy resin, insulators, and copper or aluminum conductors. Historically, its biggest supply-chain bottleneck has been imported RIP capacitive cores [1]. Over 95% of RIP cores assembled in India come from Europe and China [1]. That dependency creates 12-to-16-week lead times, ~18–20% combined import duty and freight friction on core costs, and a steep currency mismatch: in FY26, foreign-exchange outflows totaled roughly ₹130.6 crore (~55.5% of revenue) against only ₹17.2 crore in export earnings, leaving a ~₹113.4 crore net FX deficit. Crucially, Yash's Swiss technology partner and former shareholder MGC Moser Glaser—whose equity stake Yash bought back in 2024—holds an Indian core-supply exclusivity window running only until mid-2027 with no non-compete bar, creating a firm operational deadline for Jarod's in-house core ramp.

That bottleneck explains why FY26 Cash Flow from Operations (CFO) trailed PAT. Revenue jumped 57% to ₹235.1 crore, so Yash raised buffer inventory from ₹29.7 crore (Mar-25) to ₹66.7 crore (Mar-26) [1][2]. That build pushed the FY26 Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) to 81 days and absorbed ₹51.9 crore in working capital [1][2]. High-voltage condenser bushings sit at a tight bottleneck in power transmission. Every power transformer needs 5 to 7 bushings with zero substitute [1]. Yet fewer than 12 independent condenser bushing makers exist worldwide, and Yash is India's only independent specialist [1]. Globally, the bushing market should grow from $1.8 billion in 2023 to $3.0 billion by 2033, a 5.1% CAGR [1]. Dry-type RIP share rose from 30% in 2021 to 35% in 2023 and could reach 45% by 2030 [1].

In India, the Central Electricity Authority's National Electricity Plan (Transmission) 2023–2032 targets a ₹9.15 lakh crore outlay [15]. That plan supports 458 GW of peak demand and 500+ GW of renewables, lifting substation capacity from 1,251 GVA to 2,342 GVA [15]. Meanwhile, the US grid faces 2+ year lead times on large transformers as 40 million aging units meet AI data-center demand [1].

Yash makes bushings for power transformers. Sales and orders grew, but its new RIP/RIS line still needs customer tests. At ₹1,208.05 on 30 Oct, the share was 12.29% above its 50-day average; our view is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT. Consider a limit only after support holds; below ₹1,039.72, review the thesis.

1. What the company does & day-to-day vertical operations

Yash Highvoltage Limited (BSE SME: 544310. trading symbol YASHHV) designs and manufactures transformer bushings at its Gujarat operations. The company is not an insulator-maker in the generic sense implied by its older queue label. Its core product is a condenser-graded electrical component. It routes a transformer conductor through a grounded tank wall. It controls electrical stress around that opening. The product range goes to transformer OEMs, utilities and service customers. Each order must match its project specification, voltage class, current rating, pollution environment and approval status. The company’s own reports describe a specialist manufacturer with domestic utility approvals and exports. Those claims are issuer-sourced and have not been independently tested customer by customer. FY26 annual report [1] company overview [7]

The existing OIP line serves oil-impregnated paper bushings, including high-current products. The expansion targets localized RIP/RIS dry-insulation cores and a wider high-voltage range. In an OIP design, crews wind condenser paper and conductive grading layers around a conductor. They dry and oil-impregnate the assembly. It is then sealed into a porcelain or composite outer body and an oil-end assembly. RIP/RIS uses resin-impregnated or synthetic insulation. These systems reduce oil dependence. They can also change fire, maintenance and installation needs. Yash lists products across these designs and voltage/current specifications. But the available public material does not provide a consistent model-by-model cost curve or yield series. OIP products [9] RIP/RIS products [10] product overview [8]

Operationally, this is an engineered-to-order business. A customer or transformer OEM supplies an electrical specification and interface drawings. Engineering confirms dimensions, creepage distance and capacitance grading. It also checks the conductor, terminals, insulation design and required type and routine tests. Production needs specialized winding and drying equipment. It also needs controlled impregnation or resin processes, close assembly tolerances and in-house high-voltage tests. The issuer describes oil handling, winding, assembly and laboratory capabilities on its infrastructure and quality pages. Standards such as IEC 60137 specify insulation coordination and test requirements for bushings. The report does not assume every Yash model has every approval. infrastructure [11] quality and testing [12] IEC 60137 [38]

Final acceptance is critical because a bushing failure can damage the transformer and interrupt a high-value asset. Routine and type tests can measure capacitance and dissipation factor. They can check partial discharge, power-frequency withstand and impulse strength. Each customer may require separate approval before serial supply. A tested reference base can reduce friction for repeat orders. But supplier qualification is not permanent protection: utilities can qualify alternatives, and any new localized design must reproduce electrical and reliability performance. The company says its existing approvals and installed base help it win. Its investor materials also say the greenfield line is still progressing through prototype and customer approvals. quality and testing [12] FY26 annual report [1] H1 presentation [2]

Sales are mainly tied to transformer production, utility capex, replacements and retrofit work rather than recurring software-like revenue. The H1 FY27 presentation reports a ₹760 crore order book at 30 September. Management expected to execute it over 15–18 months. The backlog offers visibility, but it is not recognized revenue. Delivery slots, customer inspections, dispatch, acceptance and collection determine conversion. Management reported a 63-day cash-conversion cycle at 30 September, versus 81 days for FY26. This is a useful efficiency measure. Track it through the next two half-years. earnings call transcript [3] H1 presentation [2]

Yash also has a US sales-representation presence and acquired Sukrut Electric in 2025. The H1 presentation says the USA agreement spans 22 states, while the October call described agreements extending to close to 27 states. Those descriptions do not reconcile and are not treated as active customer coverage. Exports represented only 5% of H1 revenue. So a US commercial footprint is an option, not proof of a major export earnings stream. The call said the new factory had begun invoicing through assembly with imported cores. Localized RIP product sales were still expected from Q4 FY27. H1 presentation [2] earnings call transcript [3] leadership team [17]

The public filings do not provide customer-level concentration, product-level contribution margins, line yields, warranty claims, or separate realized prices by voltage class. Management said price and profitability vary by the combination of voltage and current ratings rather than rising mechanically with kilovolts. Track aggregate EBITDA, order book, cash conversion and on-time delivery. Qualification milestones and the share of localized products also matter. Without customer concentration and order cancellation disclosure, backlog quality remains less transparent than the ₹760 crore headline suggests.

Where the operating leverage comes from

Revenue can rise through higher units, a richer voltage/current mix, price realization and faster throughput. The first three are visible in the company’s discussion. The fourth is constrained by bottleneck equipment, testing capacity, skilled winding and assembly labor, component sourcing and customer approval timing. The company says it spent ₹106 crore of a planned ₹220 crore greenfield capex by 30 September 2026. Depreciation rose during the half-year, with further charge expected as the plant is commissioned. Output ramp therefore needs to outrun fixed-cost absorption for the margin increase to persist. [2][3]

Localized RIP/RIS cores are the key vertical integration hypothesis. If local manufacturing reaches repeatable yield and customer approval, it could shorten imported-core lead times and improve gross margin. The counter-case is that resin systems, tooling, test reliability and utilization may take longer than planned; the facility can add depreciation before it adds saleable volume. The near-term test is not capex completion by itself but localized commercial shipments, repeat orders and stable warranty/quality performance. [2][3][10]

2. Competitive moat, industry structure & external learning hub

The end market has a durable investment driver. New generation, grid reinforcement, replacement demand and large-load connections require transformer capacity. Each transformer uses bushings. The IEA’s 2025 grid-supply report describes pressure on transmission-component lead times and supply chains. But it does not quantify Yash’s addressable market or prove that bushing shortages accrue to this issuer. Management’s description of a seller’s market should therefore be treated as a cycle view, not as an underwriting assumption. IEA grid-supply report [39] earnings call transcript [3]

The moat rests on product approvals, engineering and field references. It also depends on reliable products across voltage and current classes. Yash reports a large global installed base and high repeat-order share. Check these claims against repeat-customer counts. Also review warranty history and utility approvals. The barrier is meaningful but not exclusive. Global competitors have longer histories and larger engineering and test teams. Customers can use more than one supplier when designs are qualified.

The main competitive vulnerability is execution at scale. The established Halol unit was already near 90% utilization. So a strong backlog creates both pricing leverage and risk of delivery bottlenecks. Local RIP/RIS manufacturing is intended to expand capacity and reduce imported-core dependence. Yet it adds a new process, a new facility and a qualification cycle. The investment case depends on proving this new production system. It must not weaken current delivery or margins.

Competitive set and position

Hitachi Energy, GE Vernova, Siemens Energy and Trench are useful technology and customer-alternative references. But they are not clean valuation peers. Each reports broad electrical-equipment businesses and does not publish a comparable stand-alone bushing income statement. Their product pages confirm that established global vendors compete across transformer bushing designs, applications and voltage classes. Yash is smaller, India-based and specialized. A direct margin or multiple comparison would mislead. Scale, geography, product mix and in-house engineering differ. [34–38]

The investable edge, if sustained, is speed and focus in a constrained niche: specialized products, an installed base, customer approvals and incremental capacity. Evidence is strongest for the order book and current profit. Public evidence is weaker for customer concentration, market share and profit by product. The public record supports a credible specialist position, not a defensible claim that Yash is the only viable source for global OEMs.

It requires backlog to convert into shipped and collected bushings, the greenfield line to pass qualification, and high current profitability to survive a larger depreciation base. The reported operating measures below are useful because they connect those steps; several remain issuer-reported rather than independently reconciled.

Management’s evidence is mixed but constructive. The order book increased. The existing plant is highly loaded, and cash conversion improved. US revenue remains small. The localized RIP line has not reached its stated commercial production window. The latest call gave no full-year revenue or margin figure.

Is the company reaching its potential & are the CEOs walking the talk?

Yash listed on the BSE SME platform in 2024, so a two-to-three-year public-company record is limited. Its latest annual report and October 2026 call provide a short but checkable sequence: a ₹220 crore greenfield plan, ₹106 crore spent by September, imported-core assembly already invoicing, and localized production still targeted for Q4 FY27. There is no basis to claim a long history of fulfilled multi-year public guidance.

Keyur Shah, Chairman and Managing Director, said the localized facility was progressing with “No unpleasant surprises.” The call placed localized production of 245 kV RIP bushings in Q4 FY27. It placed 420/550 kV localization in Q4 FY28 or by mid-2028. These are future targets. No localized commercial shipment or qualified 420/550 kV design had been demonstrated by the cutoff.

Darshan Thakkar, Executive Director–Finance, said management “might want to still maintain the PAT margin in spite of a higher depreciation.” The comment is a margin intention. It is not a quantified FY27 forecast. The CFO listed in the FY26 annual report did not join the H1 FY27 call. This dossier therefore attributes the answer to the finance director. Full-year revenue and EBITDA margin guidance were not committed on the call.

The old watchlist entry said ₹2,000 crore revenue by FY31 and a sevenfold increase. That statement is not supported by the latest primary sources. In the October call, the CEO discussed approximate peak revenue of ₹1,000 crore around FY30–31; the presentation states 3×–4× growth within five years of full-scale operations. We replace the old queue claim with the lower, qualified ambition and treat it as a management aspiration rather than a forecast.

3. Financial trajectory & valuation sanity check

The business expanded quickly from FY23 to FY26, with revenue rising from ₹90.33 crore to ₹235.16 crore and PAT from ₹10.87 crore to ₹37.32 crore. Profit growth outpaced revenue as EBITDA margin improved to 25.67%; ROE, however, fell as the balance sheet and equity base expanded. H1 FY27 extended the margin trend, but acquired activity and a new capacity cycle complicate a straight-line extrapolation.

At the cutoff close, issued-share market capitalization was about ₹3,611 crore. Reconstructing a trailing period from FY26 plus H1 FY27 less H1 FY26 gives ₹330.06 crore revenue, ₹91.86 crore EBITDA and ₹59.82 crore PAT. These are arithmetic analyst estimates from reported periods, not a company-published TTM statement. That implies about 60.4× TTM P/E and 38.3× EV/TTM EBITDA after subtracting management’s ₹95 crore net-cash figure; the resulting multiples leave little room for a missed ramp.

Medium-term catalyst pipeline & variant perception

The market may be pricing a durable shortage and a step-up in addressable products before the new production system has demonstrated yields. The order backlog and existing line use are real. Localized products and large-voltage approvals are not yet achieved. If localization lowers costs and improves export sales, earnings can rise. Customer preference for imported cores or slow approvals could dilute returns.

The demand case should not be confused with customer access. A 22-state representation agreement in the investor presentation and a roughly 27-state description on the call do not establish signed purchase commitments in those states. H1 revenue was 95% domestic and 5% export. The next report should show export order and revenue mix, named-customer concentration, repeat-order share and conversion by voltage class.

6. Key risks, thesis breakers & bear case

The bullish view is not hard to state: specialist components, constrained supply, an expanding order book, high existing utilization and a newly funded capacity plan create a plausible multi-year growth runway. The more important question is what can break that chain. At roughly 60× trailing earnings, the shares leave little room for execution slippage or normalized margins.

New plant and product qualification risk

A greenfield plant adds depreciation, commissioning work and quality risk before it adds dependable output. H1 presentation lists equipment progress and prototype work; management expected localized 245 kV production in Q4 FY27 and localized 420/550 kV in Q4 FY28 or by mid-2028. Customer approvals are a separate step from facility approval. A delayed test, yield problem or repeat failure can push revenue out while capex remains spent.

The new line’s output is intended to replace imported cores. Until a material share of orders ships on localized product and returns cash at a stable margin, the cost-savings thesis is unproven. Track commercial invoices, warranty claims, yield, customer audits and incremental EBITDA rather than commissioning photographs.

Cycle, backlog and customer concentration

A ₹760 crore backlog is valuable only if it is executable at quoted prices and collected on time. The available investor materials do not break out the largest customers, order cancellation clauses, firm versus framework orders or the order book by product and geography. A small number of large OEMs can create volatility if schedules change or a customer delays acceptance.

Transformer demand can remain strong while component margins normalize: competitors can add capacity, customers can dual-source, and the current seller’s market can ease. Management itself says mix and margin depend on capacity and project selection. A decline in new orders, book-to-bill below one, extended receivables or lower EBITDA per unit would weaken the thesis.

Valuation and dilution

At about 60.4× reconstructed trailing PAT, investors are paying for continued growth before the new line contributes. A move from 55× to 30× on flat fully diluted earnings cuts the illustrative value to roughly ₹565; even good operating performance may not prevent a falling share price if the exit multiple contracts. This is the central reason for a support-only posture.

The Sep-2026 filing shows 832,177 warrants and 1,067,700 outstanding ESOPs. Full conversion would increase the current share count by 6.36%. The value of any warrant exercise proceeds can offset dilution partly, but it does not remove per-share growth dilution; verify strike prices, vesting and conversion status from subsequent filings.

Margin persistence and working capital

H1 EBITDA margin reached 28.1%, aided by volume, price realization and cost efficiency according to management. The finance director also said depreciation from the project would rise materially in H2 and described maintaining PAT margin as an intention. Higher depreciation, imported component costs, the acquired Sukrut business and working-capital needs could reduce PAT conversion even if revenue rises.

Cash conversion improved to 63 days, but one half-year can be distorted by billing and dispatch timing. The risk is a growing order book that absorbs cash through inventories and receivables. The next annual report and cash-flow statement should be reviewed for cumulative operating cash flow versus PAT, capex returns and receivable days.

Market and liquidity risk

The benchmark breadth regime is Downtrend, with 19% of the Nifty 500 above its 50-day average and eight distribution days. YASHHV is a BSE SME security with lower liquidity than large-cap names; a wide spread can make a stop-market assumption unreliable and limit execution during stress. Do not calculate position size from theoretical risk alone without checking market depth and portfolio concentration.

The hinge claim is that Yash can turn the ₹220 crore facility into qualified, repeatable localized RIP/RIS output before the current order backlog and pricing cycle fade. If the next two reporting cycles show delay, cash deterioration or margin compression, the long-term narrative loses its near-term earnings support.

Chapter 1

Ajmera Realty & Infra India Limited (ARIIL) traces its operational roots to 1968 [1][2]. The late Chhotalal S. Ajmera—alongside brothers Rajnikant, Natwarlal, and Shashikant Ajmera—founded the Ajmera Group in Mumbai [2][3]. They incorporated the listed flagship in March 1985, originally under the name Percolated Steels [21]. Over nearly six decades, the group grew from a mid-income contractor into a Mumbai-centric developer [1][3]. It has delivered over 20.3 million sq ft (msf) across more than 155 projects and 46,000+ homes [1][3][6]. Its first major breakthrough came in 1975 at the Ajmera Housing Complex in Pimpri, Pune (100,000 sq ft) [2]. Next came Shanti Nagar in Mira Road, a 265-acre township of 1,500 buildings and 17,000 flats [2][3]. That project cemented the family's reputation for mass-scale execution in affordable and mid-income housing [2][3].

The group forged its enduring balance-sheet anchor in 1996 at Bhakti Park in Wadala East [2][5]. Management later unified and rebranded that site as Ajmera i-Land, a 100-acre mixed-use township in central Mumbai [4][5]. The enclave features a 25-acre private garden, a sports academy, and the iconic IMAX Dome Theatre [4][5]. Unlike peers that bid aggressively at peak-cycle textile mill auctions in the 2000s, Ajmera assembled its Wadala footprint decades ago at historical cost [5][11]. Through the 2000s and 2010s, it added marquee i-Land phases. These included Ajmera Aeon (launched 2009, OC in 2018), Ajmera Zeon, Ajmera Villows (launched 2012 across 4 acres for 800 families and 52 villas), and Ajmera Treon [2]. The company also expanded into Bengaluru's Electronic City—launching Infinity (2006), Stone Park (2014, 13.8 acres), Annex (2017), and Nucleus (2018)—alongside Ahmedabad (Casa Vyoma) and overseas JVs in Bahrain and London [2][6][7].

Ajmera survived the 2016–2021 Indian real-estate winter without solvency distress, then used its near-zero-cost Wadala land bank and an asset-light redevelopment pivot to scale PAT 5.1x from FY21 to FY26.

Between FY16 and FY21, Ajmera's results mirrored India's broader real estate stagnation [8]. Demonetisation (2016), RERA restructuring (2017), GST transition, and the 2018 IL&FS liquidity freeze starved mid-tier developers of wholesale credit [8]. Consolidated revenue stalled in a ₹277–382 crore band for six straight years (FY16–FY21) [8]. Meanwhile, borrowings swelled from ₹334 crore in FY16 to a peak of ₹968 crore in FY20 [8]. Interest expense climbed from ₹31 crore to ₹66 crore, consuming over 60% of operating profit in FY20–FY21 [8]. Net profit fell from ₹84 crore in FY18 to ₹33 crore in FY20 and ₹31 crore in FY21 [8]. Operating cash flow bled ₹-116 crore in FY18 and ₹-345 crore in FY19 during heavy construction, before recovering to ₹201 crore in FY21 on completed handovers [8].

The post-2021 upcycle marked a structural inflection. Third-generation leaders now steer the business under Rajnikant S. Ajmera (Chairman & MD, civil engineer, former CREDAI/MCHI president) [1][3][9]. Manoj Ajmera serves as MD, Sanjay Chhotalal Ajmera as Whole-time Director, and Dhaval Ajmera leads corporate affairs and capital markets [3][10]. Together, they articulated a '5x growth' roadmap [9][10]. Rather than buying land outright, management pivoted to four project buckets: monetizing owned legacy land (Wadala and Kanjurmarg), society redevelopment, SRA projects, and JV/JDA partnerships [11]. In late 2024 (FY25), Ajmera raised ₹225 crore via a preferential allotment of 31.64 lakh shares [8][12]. That issue diluted promoter holding from 74.20% to 68.23% (68.56% by Sep 2026) [8][14]. Combined with strong collections, it cut debt-to-equity from 1.13x in FY21 to 0.53x in FY26 and 0.44x by September 2026 [8][10][14].

The Overseas Detour: UK & Bahrain Capital Trap

During the 2010s, Ajmera deployed capital into international JVs: Ajmera Corporation UK Ltd (London) and Ajmera Mayfair Global Realty W.L.L. (Bahrain, 60% subsidiary) [1]. While domestic Mumbai real estate boomed post-2021, that capital stayed trapped overseas for years. Management began UK repatriations in FY23 (₹14 crore initial tranche, targeting ₹55 crore in FY24) [11][13]. It also exited Bahrain with a down payment and inventory entitlements, targeting ~₹330 crore of cumulative asset monetization and repatriation over FY26–FY27 [11][13].

Management cautions that consolidated EBITDA margins swing quarter-to-quarter with revenue-recognition mix [11]. Chapter 3

Ajmera's go-to-market engine pairs an entrenched channel-partner (broker) network across MMR and Bengaluru with a strong direct-referral base [1][3]. Five decades of delivering 46,000+ homes built that referral pool [1][3]. Management briefs channel partners weeks ahead of RERA launches through soft-marketing previews [10][14]. That playbook drove 40%+ launch-month sell-through (~₹700 crore) at Manhattan II and 84% absorption in 60 hours at Solis Vikhroli [10][14][18]. Existing homeowners in Bhakti Park and Electronic City also refer buyers for new phases, cutting mass-media ad spend [1]. Brokerage commissions typically run 2% to 4% of booking value, paid against milestone collections.

On the execution side, Ajmera acts as an engineering-led project manager rather than relying solely on turnkey EPC contractors [1][21]. Chairman Rajnikant Ajmera's civil-engineering background anchors that approach [1][21]. In FY26, the company capitalized Mivan aluminium formwork ('slidings') across high-rise sites [8][10]. That investment lifted net Property, Plant & Equipment from ₹35 crore to ₹60+ crore and total Fixed Assets from ₹77 crore to ₹106 crore [8]. Mivan formwork accelerates slab cycles and cuts finishing defects [10]. Steel, cement, and ready-mix concrete still expose margins to commodity inflation. Management also flagged potential Q4 FY27 water shortages in Mumbai as an operational risk [14]. In redevelopment projects, Ajmera absorbs tenant transit rent and corpus obligations until OC handover.

Unit economics follow strict RERA escrow rules: 70% of customer collections must enter project-specific bank accounts [1][14]. Developers can withdraw those funds only against certified percentage-of-completion milestones for land and construction [1][14]. Under Ind AS 115, P&L revenue recognition waits until a project crosses key gates: roughly 25% construction cost incurred, 10%+ collections, and core approvals [11][22]. That rule creates a 4-to-5-quarter lag between pre-sales bookings and reported revenue [11][22]. In FY26, collection efficiency improved to 65% (₹1,103 crore collected on ₹1,701 crore pre-sales, up from 60% in FY25) [10][14]. Weighted average cost of debt also fell from 12.22% in Q3 FY25 to 11.15% in FY26 and 10.75% by Q2 FY27 [10][14][22].

Chapter 4

India's urban housing market has consolidated steadily since RERA launched in 2017 and the 2018–2020 NBFC credit crunch [1][13]. Market share has shifted decisively toward listed, well-capitalized developers [1][13]. In the Mumbai Metropolitan Region (MMR), Ajmera Realty competes in two distinct arenas [10][11]. In mid-premium and luxury townships (Wadala, Sion, Kanjurmarg), it faces Tier-1 giants like Macrotech Developers (Lodha), Godrej Properties, Oberoi Realty, and Raymond Realty [10][11].

This dossier answers one question about Narayana Hrudayalaya Ltd (NH), listed on the National Stock Exchange of India: should a buyer put new money into the shares now, and if not, what would have to change first? The answer comes from a fixed process, not from a view about how good the hospitals are.

The process has three layers. Layer 1 is a set of hard gates. A gate is a yes-or-no test with a number attached. If any gate fails, the stock is rejected and the later layers are not run. That rule keeps two questions apart: whether a company is a good business, and whether its shares are a good purchase on this date.

Layer 2 is a scorecard of three to five operating measures for the type of business. Layer 3 is the entry posture. It measures how far the price sits from its nearest support level, in units of ATR, the average true range: the typical daily price swing, averaged over the last fourteen sessions.

Several technical terms recur. A 50-day moving average is the average closing price over the last 50 sessions, and a 200-day moving average is the same measure over 200 sessions. A base is a sideways stretch in which a stock stops falling and builds a floor. A breakout is a move above the level that has capped the price. Stage 2 is the markup phase, when the price sits above a rising 50-day average that is itself above the 200-day average. Stage 4 is the reverse, a markdown below falling averages. Stage 1 is the basing phase that comes before a markup.

The momentum gate is a separate test in the scoring rules. It requires either an established uptrend with relative strength, or a confirmed breakout from a base. Relative strength is how far a stock's return has beaten its benchmark over a set window, here the Nifty 50, the main index of large listed Indian companies. A sound business that fails this test is not bought.

Two more terms matter for the numbers. ROCE, return on capital employed, is the operating profit a company earns on all the money tied up in it. Debt-to-equity is borrowings divided by shareholders' equity. The rules also test cash-to-profit, the operating cash flow divided by reported profit, over three years, because a profit that never turns into cash is weaker than it looks.

The verdict is REJECTED. The debt test fails, the technical test fails in both buckets, the target cannot be verified, and the code momentum gate fails. The financial returns and the cash test pass. 2 What the company does

NH runs hospitals. Its network, as described by Screener, is 55 healthcare facilities across India, with a presence in the Cayman Islands and the United Kingdom. Of these, 20 are hospitals and heart centres in India, and the group reports 5,955 operational beds and a total capacity of 6,269 beds, on Screener's count [1]. Screener describes the company as engaged in "providing economical healthcare services", and its cardiac and multi-speciality network is built on that model. An analyst on the August 2026 call put it this way: "if you compare your ARPOB with other competitors, it is significantly lower" [12].

ARPOB, average revenue per occupied bed, is the revenue a hospital earns from each bed that is actually in use. It is the main lever on hospital income besides how full the beds are. A low ARPOB can mean a lower price for each service, or a case mix weighted towards cheaper treatments. The analyst's point is that the company can grow revenue by raising ARPOB, not only by adding beds.

The business in three parts

The company has three operating parts, and each behaves differently. The core is the Indian hospital network. In the June 2026 quarter, Indian revenue grew 17.0 percent year on year, which the company attributes to higher patient footfall and more complex procedures [10]. The Cayman operation is a hospital and a set of adjacent businesses. The new Camana Bay hospital in George Town was built for more than US$100 million and has a 70,000 square foot footprint [27]. Cayman revenue grew 38.9 percent year on year in the June 2026 quarter [10]. The UK business is smaller and was described as stable, with 2.1 percent sequential growth [10].

The Cayman adjacencies are where the losses sit. The company's own presentation summary, as printed on an aggregator page, gives a Cayman EBITDA loss of US$3.7 million for the quarter [11]. EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization: operating profit before borrowing costs, taxes, and the wear on buildings and equipment. On the August call, management said the Cayman hospital had "come back to its earlier levels" and was "operating at optimal margins", while the insurance losses had narrowed sequentially [12]. Those are management statements, not audited segment figures, and they are the reason the margin trend in section 3 needs watching.

Capacity and the projects that cost money

Beds are the engine of growth, and each new hospital costs cash long before it earns. The June 2026 presentation reports a current bed capacity of 5,775, with a stated aim of more than 7,600 beds by FY30 [7]. Screener's count of 5,955 operational beds is a different measure, and neither source explains the gap in one line, so this report uses the presentation's 5,775 as the base for the growth path. An aggregator summary of the presentation puts planned additions at 1,205 beds for FY27 to FY30 [11]. The construction phase is what drives the debt picture, which section 3 covers in detail.

Several openings are already visible in the filings. KS-Narayana Hospital in Banashankari, Bengaluru, with 110 beds, began commercial operations on 5 October 2026 [1] [17]. The company also signed an operation and management agreement for a 100-bed Mission of Mercy Hospital in Kolkata, under which commercial operations may take up to twelve months to start [1] [23]. A 100-bed Southwest Bangalore project was expected to come on stream during FY27, and an aggregator summary of the call gives its start as the end of the second quarter [12] [13]. An operation and management deal earns a fee rather than owning the building, so it adds revenue without the same capital cost.

Ownership and the register

Ownership shows who controls the company and who is buying. Promoters held 63.27 percent in June 2026, down from 63.85 percent through most of the prior two years. Foreign institutions held 9.40 percent and domestic institutions 9.05 percent in the same quarter [1]. A high promoter stake usually means the founders have their own money at risk. It also means a minority of the shares trades freely, so the price can move a long way on modest volume.

Pledging is the second ownership question. When a promoter pledges shares as collateral, a falling price can force a sale. No fetched source gave a promoter pledge figure for NH in this run, so pledge status is N/A and is not treated as clean.

Financial floors

The financial floors ask whether the business earns enough on its capital, carries a manageable debt load and turns its profit into cash. Each one protects against a different way of losing money. A low return means the business is not using its capital well. A high debt load means a bad year can force a rescue sale of equity. Weak cash conversion means reported profit may be a paper figure.

NH passes two of the three. The return on capital is 16 percent, just above the floor of 15 percent, and it has been falling for three years [1]. The cash test is 1.54, which is comfortably above 0.70 [1]. The debt test fails on the gross measure. Debt-to-equity is 1.29, because borrowings of 5,858 crore sit against net worth of 4,537 crore [1]. The company's own net measure of 0.42 at June 2026 would pass [7]. The rule is written as debt-to-equity, so this report applies the gross measure and records the net figure as the main dispute. What follows is that the debt gate will stay under pressure while construction continues, because management expects debt to rise for two to three years [12] [13].

Technical posture

A stock in Stage 1 is still building a base. The tactical bucket needs Stage 2. The core bucket accepts Stage 1 only after a confirmed breakout. NH is in Stage 1, which the ledger labels Basing [32].

The 50-day average has fallen 4.3 percent over the last 21 sessions, from 1,935.6 to 1,852.2 [32]. That fails the tactical rule, which requires a rising 50-day. It also fails the core rule, because the price has not broken out of its base. The 63-day low is 1,680.2 and the price sits 0.24 ATR above it, so the stock is near the bottom of its range rather than at the top of a breakout [32].

Two parts of the technical floor do pass. The stock is not in Stage 4, and the 50-day average has not yet fallen below the 200-day, which would be a death cross. Asymmetry and target anchoring

Asymmetry compares the reward a buyer could make with the loss a buyer would take if the stop is hit. The rule defines it as the target minus the price, divided by the price minus the stop. The rules set a floor of 3.0 for the core bucket and 3.5 for the tactical bucket. The target is not free to choose. It must come from forward earnings multiplied by a three-year median price-to-earnings ratio (the share price divided by earnings per share), or from a measured move on the base (the distance a stock is expected to travel after breaking out, judged from the height of its base). If the target cannot be verified, the gate is FAIL_ASYMMETRY.

NH fails this gate on verification. The forward earnings are available: a consensus of 52.48 rupees for FY27 and 69.42 for FY28 on one aggregator, and a median of 51.70 and 68.70 on another [18] [21]. The three-year median price-to-earnings ratio is missing, and no historical P/E series for NH could be verified from the sources available to this run [26]. Investing.com and GuruFocus, which carry historical multiples, returned HTTP 403 errors when fetched, and CompaniesMarketCap publishes no median [31]. A base measured move cannot be computed from the ledger alone, because the ledger gives the 63-day low but not the base high. The target therefore cannot be anchored, and the gate is FAIL_ASYMMETRY.

The broker targets are not an allowed anchor. The median of ten analysts is 2,150 rupees and the average is 2,194.67 [21]. Those numbers would clear both floors if they could be verified, which means the failure is one of verification rather than value. The rules do not accept a broker target in place of the anchor, so the report records the gap rather than using the number.

The momentum gate

The momentum gate is the code test that decides whether money may be allocated at all, however good the business. It has two routes. Test A asks for an established uptrend with leadership against the benchmark. Test B asks for a stock that is entering momentum from a base with rising relative strength and volume. A stock must pass one of the two. NH fails both [32] [33].

Test A fails on two conditions. The structure fails because the price is below a 50-day average that has fallen from 1,935.6 to 1,852.2. Relative strength also fails. Over 63 sessions, NH trails the Nifty by 9.33 percentage points, against a minimum of zero [32]. The 200-day average is not falling, and the price is not extended from its support, so those two conditions pass. Test B fails on three conditions. The 50-day average has declined by 4.31 percent over 21 sessions, against a limit of 0.5 percent. Relative strength over 28 sessions is negative at minus 4.14 percent, so it is not rising. Volume expansion is 0.61 times, against a minimum of 1.0 [32]. The base pivot test (whether the price sits at the level a base must clear to break out) and the volume dry-up test both pass. What follows: the momentum gate blocks any allocation until relative strength turns positive and volume expands on a breakout.

8 Fatal flaws and red team

A fatal flaw is a fact that would stop an investment even if every other measure looked good. A red-team review is a deliberate attempt to argue the opposite of the verdict and see whether it survives. The flaws found in the evidence come first, and then the verdict is tested against the strongest case for buying.

Fatal flaw one: borrowing is rising faster than equity

Borrowings rose from 2,428 crore to 5,858 crore in one year, while net worth rose from 3,626 crore to 4,537 crore [1]. The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 0.67 to 1.29 in that year. Management expects the debt figure to keep rising for two to three years while the projects are built [12] [13]. A rising gross debt load with falling returns is the combination that turns a good operator into a fragile one if construction slips or occupancy lags.

Fatal flaw two: the trend is still down

The price is in Stage 1 and below a 50-day average that is falling [32]. Over one month the stock is down 10.5 percent and over two weeks 9.1 percent, and it sits 19.2 percent below its 52-week high of 2,093.3 [32]. Relative strength against the Nifty is negative over both 28 and 63 sessions [32]. A stock falling against its benchmark has to find a reason to turn, and none of the fetched sources gives a date for one.

Fatal flaw three: the target cannot be verified

The asymmetry gate needs a target that can be checked. The forward earnings exist, but the three-year median multiple does not, and the base measured move needs a base high the ledger does not give [18] [32]. Without a verified target the reward side of the trade is unknown, and under the rules an unknown reward is a failure.

Fatal flaw four: margins and returns are falling

The operating margin fell from 22 percent a year earlier to 17 percent in the June 2026 quarter [1]. The return on capital fell from 26 percent to 16 percent over two years [1]. An analyst asked whether the margin decline was temporary, because of integration costs, and management answered that the Cayman hospital had returned to its earlier margin levels [12]. That answer covers one hospital. A falling return is measured across the whole group in the reported numbers, and those are still going the other way.

The red team's view of the rejection

The strongest case for buying runs as follows. The business is cash-rich: the three-year cash test is 1.54, and FY26 operating cash flow was the highest in the table [1]. The debt is funding beds that are opening now, including 110 beds in Bengaluru in October 2026 [1]. The company's own net debt measure is 0.42, which would pass the gate [7]. The credit rating is AA with a stable outlook [14]. June-quarter consolidated sales were up 78 percent on a year earlier [1]. The analysts' median target of 2,150 rupees implies a large gain from here [21]. On that view, a rejection on the gross debt measure is too strict.

The answer is that the verdict does not depend on the debt gate alone. Even if the net measure were accepted and the debt gate passed, the technical floor would still fail in both buckets, the momentum gate would still block any allocation, and the asymmetry gate would still have no verified target. Three separate tests would have to change. The red team's strongest point is that the gross measure may be unduly strict, and the report says so. That point alone does not reverse the verdict.

The hinge claim

The verdict holds as long as any one failing gate stays failed. It would only flip if the debt basis, the trend and the target all changed together. Three things would have to change together: the debt basis, the trend (technical and momentum), and the target. The debt basis is the least fixed of the three, because management expects borrowing to keep rising for two to three years [12] [13]. The trend is the nearest of the three to changing. It needs the 50-day average to turn up, the price to reclaim it, and relative strength to turn positive [32]. Even a trend recovery would not flip the verdict alone, because the asymmetry gate still has no verified target. The verdict is therefore firm in its present form, and the next re-run should test all three together.

9 Verdict, action and triggers

The verdict is REJECTED. The rule that produces it is the Layer 1 rule: a failure of any hard gate rejects the stock and stops the rest of the process. Here the debt gate fails at 1.29 against a ceiling of 0.70, the technical gate fails in both buckets, the asymmetry gate fails on the missing target, and the code momentum gate fails on both of its tests [1] [32]. Layers 2 and 3 are not run, and the stock is not assigned to a strategy bucket, because neither bucket is open while Layer 1 fails.

What this means for you

No money should go into NH on this evidence. The stock is not a current holding, so there is no position to hold, trim or exit. The practical meaning is that the shares are not a candidate for a new purchase until the gates pass on fresh data. Waiting is not a lapse in discipline here. The price has fallen 10.5 percent in a month, but the trend has not yet shown it has stopped [32], and buying a falling base before it turns is the mistake this process exists to prevent.

Archive and re-entry

Under this process, a Layer 1 failure moves NH from the active watchlist to the archive sheet, where its failed metrics are kept as the audit trail. The watchlist row already records the rejection and sets a re-review date of 27 December 2026 [34]. Re-entry means the full Layer 1 is run again on new ledger data and new filings. Reaching a single price level does not restore the name on its own.

Why the verdict is REJECTED and not deferred

QUALIFIED_DEFERRED is used when a candidate passes the hard gates but ranks below its bucket's capacity. NH fails hard gates, so it cannot be qualified. The business itself is not weak on return and cash, but the debt, the trend and the target each fail on their own, and any one of them would reject the name. The triggers below show the levels and outcomes that would bring it back for evaluation.

  • Trend review level: a close below 1,680.2, the swing low, on two consecutive sessions. The stock would then be making a new low inside the base.

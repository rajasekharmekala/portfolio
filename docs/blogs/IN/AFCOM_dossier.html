Air Cargo Payload Chemistry: Density, Volumetric Weight & Pallet Architecture

In air cargo logistics, pricing is governed by the International Air Transport Association (IATA) volumetric weight rule: 1 cubic metre equals 167 kilograms (or 6,000 cubic centimetres per kilogram). Shippers are billed on chargeable weight, defined as whichever is higher between actual gross physical weight and dimensional volumetric weight. Electronics sub-assemblies and automotive wiring harnesses typically exhibit high density, allowing Afcom to maximize the Boeing 737's structural payload of 21 tonnes without 'cubing out' (filling the physical volume before reaching the weight ceiling).

Conversely, e-commerce parcels and pharmaceutical packaging frequently exhibit low density, filling the main cargo deck volume while carrying only 12 to 14 tonnes of physical weight. To maximize profitability, Afcom's load planning software actively pairs high-density industrial cargo (such as marine engine replacement parts from Chennai) with low-density pharmaceutical cartons, optimizing both gross payload and usable cubic capacity on every flight.

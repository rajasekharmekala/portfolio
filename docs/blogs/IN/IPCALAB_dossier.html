Before you read

Ipca Laboratories is a Mumbai drug maker that sells branded pills to Indian doctors and generic medicines abroad, and makes many of the bulk chemicals inside them [1]. It is a sound business on an improving path, with profit per share up for three years, borrowings down to ₹809 crore and targets raised in August 2026 [2][3]. But the ₹1,980.80 price at the 27 October 2026 close already pays for much of that good news [4]. So place no order before the 5 November 2026 results, then buy only on a dip at or below about ₹1,946; the planned ₹1,895 to ₹1,936 zone holds only while buyers pay about 36.5 times yearly profit. The view is wrong if the share closes below ₹1,728.84, or if the profit margin slides back toward 20 percent while raw materials get dearer [5].

1. What the company does

Start with who pays. Ipca earns its money from two groups of customers. The first is Indian patients, whose doctors write prescriptions for Ipca's branded medicines. The second is overseas buyers, who purchase finished tablets or the raw drug chemicals themselves. In the year to March 2026 the group sold ₹9,599 crore of goods [2]. A crore is ten million, so that is about ₹96 billion. The company was founded in 1949, runs 16 factories in India and sells into more than 100 countries [6][1]. The company describes exports as nearly half of its income [6]. On the parent company's own numbers, exports of ₹3,129 crore were 43 percent of its ₹7,296 crore of sales [1]. Against the group's ₹9,599 crore the same ₹3,129 crore is about a third, but that is not a group export share [2]. The group's own export total is not shown in the sources we hold, and the company's 'nearly half' appears to be a group figure that also counts Unichem's and other subsidiaries' overseas sales.

The Indian branded business is the profit engine. Ipca sells about 172 brands through 22 marketing teams, which the company calls divisions [1]. It ranks 16th in the Indian prescription market with a share near 2.08 percent, and a field force of about 7,000 sales representatives visits doctors [3]. Pain management brands, such as medicines for arthritis and joint pain, are 53 percent of brand sales, and heart and diabetes medicines are another 17 percent [1]. These are long-term conditions, so patients refill every month and sales are steadier than for a seasonal cough syrup. Malaria medicines, once a bigger line, are now only 2 percent of brand sales [1]. In FY26 the Indian formulations line sold ₹3,817 crore [1].

Exports were ₹3,129 crore in FY26: ₹2,083 crore of finished medicines and ₹1,047 crore of bulk drug chemicals, which the industry calls active pharmaceutical ingredients, or APIs [1]. Europe is the biggest market at ₹885 crore, followed by the Americas at ₹665 crore and Africa at ₹594 crore [1]. Ipca sells in four ways abroad. It sells its own brands with its own sales staff in Africa, Russia and its neighbours, and parts of Asia. It sells generics, which are copies of off-patent medicines, in the United Kingdom under its own label and in the United States through its Unichem unit. It supplies aid agencies on tender, a line that fell to ₹270 crore in FY26 from ₹355 crore [5]. And it sells APIs, where nearly 79 percent of that business is exported [2].

The group is wider than the parent company. Its consolidated results include Unichem Laboratories, a generic drug maker with a large United States business, and subsidiaries in the US, Brazil, Europe and South Africa [2]. Ipca also holds 40.98 percent of Lyka Labs, a listed maker of injectable medicines that is still losing money [1][3]. On 26 June 2026 the board approved merging Krebs Biochemicals, a small related maker of fermentation-based ingredients with ₹26 crore of income, into Ipca for 7 new Ipca shares per 200 Krebs shares [7]. The newest bet is biosimilars, which are near-copies of expensive biological medicines. Management counts 7 candidates in its pipeline, and it signed a licensing deal in June 2026 and a United States technology transfer in September 2025 [3][6][8].

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • average true range (ATR) is the typical daily price swing of a share, in rupees. A larger ATR means the price jumps around more.
  • relative strength index (RSI) is a meter from 0 to 100 of how fast a price has been rising. Above 70 is hot and below 30 is cold.
  • earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA) is profit before interest, tax and the accounting charge for wear on assets. It is a clean read on operating profit.
  • return on capital employed (ROCE) is yearly operating profit divided by the money tied up in the business. It shows how hard each rupee invested works.
  • earnings per share (EPS) is the company's profit divided by its number of shares.
  • free cash flow (FCF) is the cash left after running the business and buying equipment.
  • operating margin is operating profit as a share of sales. Gross margin is sales minus the cost of materials, as a share of sales, and net margin is what is left after all costs and tax.
  • debt-to-equity is total debt divided by shareholders' money. Net debt is debt minus cash held.
  • price-to-earnings (P/E) is the price paid for each rupee of yearly profit. A P/E of 36 means the share costs 36 times one year of profit.
  • cash flow from operations is the cash the business itself generates before financing. Working capital is the cash tied up in stock and unpaid bills.
  • capital expenditure is spending on factories and equipment.
  • guidance is the company's own published forecast. A consensus estimate is the average of analysts' forecasts.
  • dilution is the fall in each owner's slice when a company issues new shares. A buyback is the opposite: the company buys its own shares back.
  • A 50-day moving average is the average closing price of the last 50 trading days, and a 200-day moving average is the same over 200 days. Prices tend to bounce off these slow lines.
  • Stage 2 is the confirmed rising trend on the Weinstein stage scale; Stage 4 is the confirmed falling trend. A base is a sideways resting period before a move, and the pivot is the top of that base.
  • A breakout is a move above the pivot on strong volume. A retest is the pullback that tests that level from above. Chapter 2 now asks whether the business behind the share is strong.
  • A stop loss is the price at which the idea is wrong and the position is sold. A limit order is an order that fills only at your price or better.
  • The entry zone is the band of prices where buying is planned. Asymmetry, also called reward-to-risk, is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop. A measured move is a target taken from the height of a base.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

Ipca is a mid-sized player in a crowded market, and its edge is steady execution rather than size. It ranks 16th among Indian drug sellers by prescription sales, with a share of about 2.08 percent that has hardly moved in a year [5][3]. Holding share while the market grows means Ipca is growing as fast as its rivals. On the February 2026 call management said the Indian market had grown 8.9 percent while Ipca had grown 10.6 percent [10]. In the June 2026 quarter management said the Indian market grew 4.5 percent against Ipca's 8.9 percent in acute illness, and 15.2 percent against Ipca's 17.2 percent in long-term illness [3]. A company that beats its market in both short and long illnesses is doing something right in the field.

The edge rests on three things. First, Ipca's Indian sales lean toward long-term conditions: pain, heart and diabetes medicines are 70 percent of brand sales [1]. Refills make those sales steadier than seasonal ones. Second, Ipca makes many of its own active ingredients, which lets it control cost and supply. It holds European quality certificates for 62 ingredients and has registered 60 generic dossiers in Europe [1]. Third, it has United States approvals: 46 abbreviated drug applications filed, 32 granted and 50 drug master files, after the US regulator lifted an import alert on three of its plants [1]. These approvals take years to earn, and a rival cannot copy them quickly.

The balance sheet is a quiet strength. Borrowings fell from ₹1,363 crore in March 2025 to ₹809 crore in March 2026 [2], against shareholders' money of about ₹8,063 crore. Debt is therefore roughly a tenth of equity. India Ratings rates the bank facilities IND AA+ with a stable outlook, one step below its top grade, and affirmed that grade in both July 2025 and July 2026 [11][12]. In August 2026 management said the parent company had repaid every dollar loan and that its remaining term loans were about ₹193 crore [3]. The ₹809 crore figure is a group total, so it also holds the subsidiaries' borrowings. A company that does not need lenders can keep investing through a weak year. The next two subsections show what the margin gain is made of and where the business is weaker.

What the margin gain is made of

The June 2026 quarter was the best in years, so it is worth taking apart. The group's operating profit margin, which the company reports before currency gains and other income, rose to 22.88 percent from 18.39 percent a year earlier [9]. Management said material cost per rupee of sales fell by 1.35 points, staff cost by 1.5 points and manufacturing and other costs by 1.19 points on a group basis [3]. Most of that came from spreading fixed costs over 21 percent more sales, not from cheaper inputs: on the parent company, savings on material cost were only 0.14 points [3].

Three helpers will not repeat on demand. A weaker rupee added about 5 points to the 21 percent sales growth, because the dollar was about 11 percent dearer than a year earlier [3]. About ₹40 crore of tender shipments due in March slipped into April, which inflated the quarter [3]. And a currency gain of ₹31.57 crore was booked below the operating line [9]. For you as an owner, this means the quarter proves the margin can reach 23 percent, but does not prove it can be multiplied by four. The next subsection turns to the weak spots.

Where the business is weaker

Unichem is the first weak spot. Its operating margin fell from about 12 percent to about 8 percent in FY26 after it lost market share on high-volume United States products [5][10]. Management had spoken of 10 to 11 percent [13]. Unichem also paid a European Commission fine, and its interest on that fine cost ₹58.26 crore in an exceptional item [9]. For FY27 management expects 12 to 13 percent but has not revised that target yet [5][3].

The second weak spot is the related-party web. Lyka Labs, in which Ipca holds 40.98 percent, still has flat sales and negative operating profit, as an investor pointed out on the August 2026 call [1][3]. An analyst also noted on the June call that Ipca had taken an impairment on its Krebs Biochemicals investment [5]. The Krebs merger is priced at arm's length and dilutes the promoter stake only from 44.72 to 44.66 percent [7]. The sums are small, but they show capital flowing to weaker affiliates.

The third is capital use. Inventory days were 353 in FY26 and the cash conversion cycle was 308 days [2]. Return on capital employed is 17.0 percent and the three-year average return on equity is 12.9 percent, while the share trades at 6.23 times book value [2]. The fourth is regulation: a US inspection of the Tarapur API plant in December 2025 ended with a Form 483, which is a list of inspector observations, with three items [14]. We found no public statement on how it was closed. Chapter 2B scores these points; Chapter 3 shows the numbers behind them.

Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

A promise is only worth what it delivers. We read the last two years of management statements and compared them with the results. On margin, management has been conservative: it promised about 18 percent for FY25 and delivered 18.94 percent, then promised about 20 percent for FY26 and delivered 20.72 percent [15][5]. That pattern, promise low and deliver slightly higher, is why the August 2026 raise to 23 percent is credible.

The misses are on sales growth and on Unichem. FY26 sales grew about 8 percent against a promise of 9 to 10 percent, and Unichem earned about 8 percent against 10 to 11 percent [17][5][13]. Management was open about both. It cut the margin promise in August 2025 when Unichem slipped, and it said in August 2026 that it would wait for 'some more quarters' before changing the Unichem target [17][3]. For an owner this means the Indian business is reliable and the subsidiary is the part to doubt.

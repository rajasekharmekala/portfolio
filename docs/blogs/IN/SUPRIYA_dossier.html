1. What the company does

Supriya Lifescience Ltd is based in Mumbai. It makes active pharmaceutical ingredients, or APIs. An API is the chemical in a tablet or injection that does the medical work. The company sells it to drug makers, who add other ingredients and sell the finished medicine. Supriya makes more than 40 APIs at Lote Parshuram, near Khed in Ratnagiri district. Its best-known lines are anaesthetics such as ketamine, antihistamines for allergies, vitamins and asthma drugs.[1][2] It began in 1987 as a partnership, became a public company in 2008 and listed in December 2021.[2][27] Customers are drug makers, both generic and innovator, in more than 120 countries. Exports were 81 percent of sales in the June 2026 quarter and 82 percent in FY26.[1][4]

The company sells each API by the kilogram on a purchase order. It does not sign long fixed-price contracts. So the managing director says it can pass raw-material and solvent price rises on to customers.[3][5] FY26 revenue was ₹827.87 crore, up 18.9 percent. The ten largest customers made up 52 percent of it, down from 58 percent in FY25.[4][2] The filings do not give the share of any single customer. So concentration beyond the top ten is not known.

Here is where a rupee of sales goes. In the June 2026 quarter, each ₹100 of sales paid ₹29.7 for materials, ₹15.0 for staff and ₹30.3 for other running costs. Those costs include power, solvents and freight. That left ₹25.0 of EBITDA. EBITDA stands for earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. It is profit before accounting and finance charges. A year earlier materials took only ₹21.9, and EBITDA was ₹35.6.[2] That jump in materials cost is the core of the margin reset. We test it in Chapter 3.

All production sits at one site. Lote Parshuram has five reactor blocks, labelled A to E. Their reactors can process 932 kilolitres per day. Use was 70 percent in the June 2026 quarter and 74 percent in FY26.[1][2] About 72 percent of June 2026 quarter revenue came from products made from basic chemicals upward. This is called backward integration. It was 76 percent for FY26.[2][5] Backward integration lowers cost and secures supply. But it also ties up stock. Inventory was ₹159.60 crore at March 2026, 35 percent more than a year before.[9]

Three projects carry the next leg of growth. The first is Ambernath, a site for finished medicines and contract manufacturing. It makes tablets, injections and inhalation liquids. It awaits a European inspection in the second half of November 2026.[3][6] Its capital work in progress fell from ₹148.02 crore to ₹35.55 crore in FY26 as the plant came into use.[9] The second is a ten-year vitamin supply deal with DSM. It brought ₹30 to ₹35 crore in FY26 and should reach about ₹60 crore in FY27.[5][8] The third is 80,000 square metres of land at Isambe, Patalganga. The first ₹200 crore phase of new API blocks has started with a boundary wall.[2][3]

Why controlled substances matter to this business

Ketamine hydrochloride is one of Supriya's oldest products. It is a psychotropic substance under India's Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act of 1985, known as the NDPS Act. Each export needs a prior authorisation from the Central Bureau of Narcotics. The managing director says every consignment has its own permit, tied to one customer, one country and one product.[21][3] That rule is a barrier to entry, because few plants are licensed to make and ship such products. It is also a risk. One slip with paperwork can lead to a seizure, an arrest and a court case. That is what happened in 2026, as Chapter 6 shows.

The anaesthetic group was 48 percent of June 2026 quarter sales.[1] The filings do not say how much of it is ketamine. So the exact exposure of sales to controlled substances is N/A: not published.

What the company does not yet do

Finished-medicine sales from Ambernath are small today. The managing director expects the full effect to take three to four years.[5] A large anaesthetic contract-manufacturing deal is described as very close to a signed term sheet. Nothing was signed by 14 August 2026.[3] So the investment case rests partly on projects that have not yet produced revenue. Chapter 2B tests this against management's earlier promises.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

A competitive position is whatever makes it hard for a rival to copy the business. For Supriya it has four parts, and each has evidence. The first is regulatory approval. In February 2026 the Lote plant passed a surprise seven-day inspection by the United States drug regulator. It had one minor observation. It then received an EIR (Establishment Inspection Report) classed Voluntary Action Indicated. That means the regulator found nothing serious enough for official action.[5] The company also lists European, Brazilian, Korean and Chinese approvals.[2] A buyer that has qualified a supplier through such audits does not switch quickly.

The second part is cost control through backward integration. Twenty products are made from basic chemicals upward. They covered 72 percent of June 2026 quarter revenue.[2] The third part is a set of niche molecules where management says India has few makers. This is management's view, and we could not verify the number of rivals.[5][7] The fourth part is a long-term vitamin deal with DSM. It has passed pharma validation and has its own plant.[3] Together these help explain why margin stayed between 28 and 37 percent for four years. Meanwhile the wider Indian bulk-drug market grows only 5 to 7 percent a year.[34][4][28]

The edge is thin in four places. Customers are concentrated, with the top ten at 52 percent of FY26 revenue.[2] One therapy group, anaesthetics, is about half of sales.[1] Everything is made at one site that depends on river water. A delayed monsoon in 2026 cut supply for 15 to 20 days. It cost ₹30 to ₹35 crore of production.[3] And China supplies about 73.3 percent of India's bulk-drug imports. So the cost of key starting materials can move with events outside Supriya's control.[29]

The balance sheet is strong, but the cash record is mixed. Borrowings were nil at March 2026. The company held ₹177.39 crore in cash, bank deposits and investments. Management put its fixed deposits and mutual funds at about ₹150 crore in August.[9][31] Yet over FY22 to FY26 the company spent ₹628.08 crore on plants and land. Cash from operations was ₹576.16 crore. So it used about ₹51.92 crore more than it generated, before the dividend.[9][10][11][12] The high return on capital is real. But it was earned on a balance sheet still being built. Free cash only turned positive in FY25 and FY26.

How Supriya's margins compare with its peers

Screener shows Supriya's operating profit margin at 33 percent over the last twelve months. That is profit before depreciation as a share of sales. Divi's Laboratories, a much larger maker, shows 36 percent. Neuland Laboratories shows 32 percent, Aarti Pharmalabs 22 percent and Granules India 21 percent.[16][17][18][19][20] So Supriya sits near the top of its peer group on margin, with no borrowings, like Divi's. It is much smaller, though. And its June 2026 margin of 25.0 percent shows how fast that lead can narrow when costs jump.

What the evidence adds up to

The moat is real in regulation, integration and licences. It is weaker in customer and site diversification. For an owner, the business can earn high returns for years. But one event, such as a plant stoppage or a licence problem, has an outsized effect on profit. The next chapter turns these ideas into five scored operating measures. Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Management walk-the-talk means comparing what the company said it would do with what it did. We audited the last two years of statements on growth, margin, spending and launches. The record is mixed. The core promises on growth and margin were met. Spending and one product launch ran well past the plan.

On growth, management reaffirmed in May 2025 a target of about 20 percent for FY26. It delivered 18.9 percent.[8][4] On margin it promised 33 to 35 percent. It delivered 35.5 percent.[8][4] On spending, it said in May 2025 that FY26 capital spending would be ₹75 to ₹80 crore. In February 2026 it said only about ₹15 crore remained for the last quarter, after ₹71 crore in nine months.[8][6] The actual figure was ₹152 crore. So the last quarter alone took about ₹81 crore. The company says the year's spending was mainly the Ambernath plant, plus maintenance, smaller projects and land purchases.[5][2][9]

Product launches were mixed. The ADHD product was expected in the fourth quarter of FY26. It arrived a quarter early.[7][6] The contrast-media product was due in the second quarter of FY26. It slipped to the fourth quarter. In May 2026 it moved to the second half of FY27, a delay the company put at two quarters on its August call.[7][5][3] The company is open about the delay. But the miss is large, about five quarters.

The weakest item is the customs case. In August 2026 the managing director called it a two-day technical lapse by the logistics team. She said it was limited to one consignment, with no provision made and no other employee involved.[3] Court reports show the export authorisation expired on 31 May. The weakest assumption behind each estimate

For cash conversion of 0.90, the weakest assumption is that the FY26 build in receivables and stock is temporary. Receivables rose 63 percent and stock 35 percent. The red-team bear case

The strongest case against buying is that three things can go wrong together. First, the narcotics-export case is not a paperwork footnote. A general manager was in judicial custody in August. The company went from the High Court to the Supreme Court. The Supreme Court granted an interim stay that protects directors, officers and officials from coercive action. It does not end the case.[23][25][26][3] Ketamine is a controlled substance with per-shipment permits. A finding against the company, or a pause by the Central Bureau of Narcotics, could hit a product line inside the 48 percent anaesthetic group. The seized consignment, about ₹72 lakh or 0.4 percent of June 2026 quarter revenue, was still booked as a sale. That is small, but it is an accounting judgment worth watching.[3][23]

CONCRETE SEGMENT WALKTHROUGH 1: THE NUCLEAR GENERATOR TRANSFORMER

Engineering the 400 kV Kaiga Nuclear Power Station Step-Up Units

The Customer & The Problem: Nuclear Power Corporation of India Limited (NPCIL) operates the Kaiga Atomic Power Station in Karnataka. In late 2024, NPCIL awarded TARIL the prestigious contract to design, manufacture, test. In addition, commission the generator transformers for Kaiga Units 5 & 6 (700 MW indigenous Pressurized Heavy Water Reactors). A nuclear generator transformer steps up the electrical output produced by the turbine generator (typically generated at 21 kV to 24 kV) directly to the 400 kV national extra-high-voltage transmission grid.

The Engineering & Materials: A nuclear generator transformer is one of the most critical single points of failure in power generation. A trip or insulation fault in this unit immediately scrams the reactor. TARIL engineers designed a 3-phase, 400 kV-class transformer utilizing highest-grade Japanese laser-treated Cold-Rolled Grain-Oriented (CRGO) silicon steel cores, continuously transposed conductor (CTC) oxygen-free electrolytic copper windings. In addition, high-density pressboard insulation.

Manufacturing & Delivery: The core and coil assembly is constructed inside TARIL's climate-controlled clean room at Changodar. Here, humidity is maintained below 0.5% to prevent moisture ingress into the cellulose insulation. The entire assembly is dried using advanced kerosene vapor-phase drying plants before being lowered into a heavy structural steel tank filled with 80,000 liters of inhibited transformer oil. Factory acceptance testing (FAT) includes lightning impulse tests at 1,425 kV peak, power-frequency withstand tests. In addition, partial discharge measurements below 10 picocoulombs. The unit, weighing over 280 metric tonnes when oil-filled, is transported to the Kaiga site via a specialized 24-axle hydraulic puller.

Pricing & Margin Profile: While standard distribution transformers trade at ₹8–12 lakh per MVA, a nuclear-grade 400 kV generator transformer commands ₹25–35 lakh per MVA due to stringent nuclear quality assurance protocols, zero-tolerance warranty liabilities. CONCRETE SEGMENT WALKTHROUGH 2: THE 765 KV SHUNT REACTOR

Mitigating the Ferranti Effect for Gujarat Energy Transmission Corporation (GETCO)

The Customer & The Problem: When transmission utilities construct ultra-long, 765 kV and 400 kV transmission lines to evacuate solar power from the Khavda renewable energy park in the Rann of Kutch to load centers, a dangerous electrical phenomenon known as the Ferranti effect occurs. During periods of low power flow (e.g. night-time or sudden renewable curtailment), the high shunt capacitance of the transmission line causes the voltage at the receiving end of the line to rise significantly above the sending end voltage, risking catastrophic dielectric breakdown of substation switchgear.

The Solution: To absorb this excess capacitive reactive power, utilities install extra-high-voltage Shunt Reactors. In October 2026, GETCO awarded TARIL landmark contracts for 80 MVAR and 125 MVAR 400 kV shunt reactors. Unlike a transformer. This has two or more windings to transfer energy, a shunt reactor consists of a single winding connected directly in shunt across the transmission line to act as an inductive load.

The Physics Challenge: A shunt reactor must maintain a linear inductive impedance up to high overvoltages. This requires an air-gapped core constructed from precision-machined ceramic or non-magnetic radial spacers between core packets, subjected to intense electromagnetic clamping forces to prevent violent 100 Hz vibrational noise and structural fatigue. Only a handful of global manufacturers can design gapped-core shunt reactors capable of passing 5-year vibration and acoustic endurance trials.

Contract Execution: The GETCO orders carry delivery schedules over 24 to 30 months, with milestone payments tied to engineering drawing approval, raw material procurement, factory testing. In addition, site energization.

In the heavy industrial domain, TARIL's competitive positioning in Electric Arc Furnace (EAF) transformers remains an enduring technical moat[1][22]. Steel producers utilizing scrap steel and direct reduced iron (DRI) operate electric-arc furnaces that require massive secondary electrical currents—often exceeding 60,000 to 100,000 amperes—at low secondary voltages (typically 400 to 1,200 volts) to strike the smelting arc between graphite electrodes[22]. TARIL has delivered specialized furnace transformers globally, including a 175 MVA EAF unit to Mexico, a 156 MVA unit to Novoross Steel in Russia. In addition, multiple high-current units across the Middle East and Southeast Asia[1][21].

Beyond central transmission grid substations and nuclear plants, TARIL has engineered deep competitive footholds across specialized industrial and transportation verticals[1][21][22].

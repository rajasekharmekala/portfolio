Before you read

Granules India makes paracetamol, metformin, ibuprofen and ADHD medicines, from the raw chemical to the finished tablet.[1] Most of its sales are in the United States.[1] The business is sound and makes cash, but its biggest tablet plant, Gagillapur, is still under a US FDA Warning Letter, and a reinspection that began on 19 October 2026 has no published result.[2] The verdict is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT: buy only between ₹796.17 and ₹815.94, after the 2 November 2026 results and the inspection outcome, with a stop at ₹753.81.[3] A new Form 483 with data-integrity findings, or a close below ₹753.81, would show this conclusion wrong.

1. What the company does

Granules India is a Hyderabad drug maker. It began as a paracetamol producer and says it pioneered paracetamol active ingredient in India, where low cost became its first durable edge.[1] Paracetamol is the pain and fever medicine sold as Tylenol or Crocin. Today the core products are paracetamol, metformin, ibuprofen and controlled substances such as ADHD tablets.[4]

The company sells at three steps of one chain. The first step is the active pharmaceutical ingredient (API), the chemical that does the work in a medicine. The second is the pharmaceutical formulation intermediate (PFI), a ready-to-press powder or pellet that carries the API. The third is the finished dosage (FD), the tablet or capsule a pharmacy hands over. Finished dosages made 74% of the ₹1,477 crore Q1 FY27 revenue, API 13%, PFI 9% and the new peptide contract business 4%.[1]

Chairman K.P. Chigurupati describes the edge as being able to make the chemistry, turn it into a formulation and deliver it to the most demanding regulated markets with quality and cost discipline.[5] The point for an owner is simple. Making all three steps in-house keeps the cost of each tablet low and protects supply when outside API is short. The weakness is that one failed inspection at a key plant can stop new launches from that plant.

The mix is moving. Complex generics, which are products that are hard to formulate, hard to make or protected by tricky patents, were 39% of finished dosages a year ago and are now 50%.[1] Complex products earn more per tablet. That is why gross margin rose to 65.6% in Q1 FY27 even while raw-material and freight costs went up because of the West Asia conflict.[5]

How the company makes money

Granules earns the difference between what it pays for chemicals, labour and plant, and the price at which it sells API, powders and tablets. In Q1 FY27 gross margin was 65.6%, which means 65.6 paise of every rupee of sales stayed after the cost of materials. After staff costs of ₹255 crore, research of ₹88 crore and other costs, EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) was ₹339 crore, or 22.9% of sales.[1]

Net profit was ₹180 crore, 12.2% of sales and up 60% on a year ago, when a one-time charge of ₹26 crore cut profit.[1][6][7] Research costs 6.0% of sales. Management says it will stay near 5.5% to 6%.[5] The company expects operating margin to stay in a 22% to 23% range, which is the guidance we test later.[5]

Where the plants are, and why one of them matters most

Annual finished-tablet capacity is about 39 billion doses. Gagillapur, near Hyderabad, holds 26.8 billion of that. Genome Valley holds 10 billion, the Virginia plant of Granules Pharmaceuticals Inc (GPI) 1.5 billion, and the Vizag oncology unit 1.1 billion.[1] So about 68% of tablet capacity sits at the one site that has an open US FDA Warning Letter.

The company says it has never stopped production at Gagillapur beyond a couple of days, and that existing products keep shipping.[5] What stopped is the approval of new products made there.[8] Seven of the eight US-inspected sites hold a clean establishment inspection report (EIR), the US FDA's closing paper for an inspection. Gagillapur is the eighth.[1] That is the hinge of this report, and Chapters 2B and 6 return to it.

Who the customers are

The company does not publish a share for its largest customer, and we record that as not published.[4] What it does publish is the region split above. Rating agency ICRA says North America and Europe together bring 85% to 90% of revenue, and that the United States alone gave about 77% in FY2025 and the first half of FY2026.[9] The company ranks first in 10 of its 35 US products and in the top three for 18, by IQVIA data to May 2026.[1]

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • API: active pharmaceutical ingredient, the chemical that does the medical work.
  • PFI: pharmaceutical formulation intermediate, a ready-to-press powder or pellet carrying the API.
  • ANDA: abbreviated new drug application, the filing a generic maker sends the US FDA for approval.
  • Form 483: the list of observations a US FDA inspector hands over at the end of an inspection.
  • Warning Letter: a formal US FDA notice that a plant has serious violations; new approvals from that plant are usually held until it is cleared.
  • OAI and VAI: Official Action Initiated is the worse inspection class; Voluntary Action Indicated means minor issues the company fixes by itself.
  • Complex generic: a copy of a drug that is hard to formulate, hard to make or tied to a hard patent strategy, so fewer rivals compete.
  • MUPS: multiple-unit pellet system, a tablet that breaks into many tiny coated pellets.
  • CDMO: contract development and manufacturing organisation, a firm that makes another firm's product to order (here, peptides).

2. Why the business is strong or weak

A drug maker is strong when it can make the same product at lower cost than rivals, win approvals faster than rivals, and keep regulators satisfied. Granules is strong on the first and improving on the second. The third is the open question.

The balance sheet is the clearest strength. Net debt to EBITDA fell to 0.07x in Q1 FY27 from 0.34x at March 2026 and 0.75x at March 2025.[1] Borrowings of ₹1,512 crore at March 2026 sit against equity and reserves of ₹5,085 crore, a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.30.[3] ICRA upgraded the long-term rating to [ICRA]AA (Stable) on 19 January 2026 and named the paracetamol leadership, new launches and backward integration as strengths.[9]

Where the edge comes from

Backward integration is the first source of edge. Granules makes the API, the intermediate and the tablet in the same group, so it controls cost and supply for its main molecules.[5] Management also points to scale: GPI has risen to 27th among all US generic firms from 74th five years ago, and the company is the fourth largest in controlled substances.[5]

Complex products are the second source. The deck calls Granules the owner of the world's largest single-site MUPS facility, and lists two sole first-to-file products, one of them sodium oxybate extended release.[1] A first-to-file filer can earn 180 days without generic rivals if it wins. Management says 25 ANDAs are awaiting approval with a US market of about $40 billion, branded and generic together.[1]

The third source is controlled-substance quota and reliability. On the Q1 FY27 call the chairman said that many rivals run out of stock on ADHD products because the tablets are hard to make consistently, and that Granules has kept supplying.[5] This is a real barrier, but it depends on keeping regulators satisfied.

Where the business is thin

The first weakness is plant concentration. About 68% of tablet capacity sits at Gagillapur, which received a US FDA Warning Letter on 26 February 2025.[10] The letter said the plant's quality system "does not adequately ensure the accuracy and integrity of data".[10] The FDA inspection narrative described a large number of torn pieces of GMP records found in scrap.[11] These are not cosmetic findings. Data-integrity problems are the kind that take the longest to clear.

The second weakness is price and cost pressure on the older products. On the Q1 call the executive director said Granules could not pass much of the cost rise to customers in Europe, so it held back some supply.[5] Gross margin was kept at 65.6% mainly by cheaper opening inventory and by mix, and management expects cost pressure for the next couple of quarters.[1]

The third weakness is thin return on equity. Return on equity (ROE) was 13.8% and Screener's three-year figure is 13.6%, which it flags as low.[3] Profit grew only 5% a year over three years even though the last twelve months show +36%.[3] The business is improving from a modest starting level. It has not yet shown a long record of high returns.

Policy and cost risks that sit outside the plants

The US tariff on patented drugs took effect under Section 232 on 2 April 2026, but generics and their ingredients were excluded at this time.[12][13] A separate plan announced on 21 July 2026 would keep generics at zero tariff until 31 July 2028, then raise it to 100% and later 200%. That plan is not law.[12] Granules has a US plant at Virginia running at about 70% of capacity, which gives it a partial answer if the plan becomes law.[5]

Raw-material and freight costs rose after the West Asia conflict, and management said it is seeking price increases and cost pass-through where it can.[5] Paracetamol is the largest legacy molecule. Market-research sites quote Indian paracetamol at about $3,050 a tonne in the second quarter of 2026, but they do not give Granules' own selling price, so we make no price claim from them.

A drug maker is judged on a handful of numbers that fit its business. For Granules we picked five. They are how much of its tablet capacity the US regulator has cleared, how steady its gross margin is, how fast its complex products are growing, how well it turns profit into cash, and how dependent it is on one region. Working capital, the cash tied up in stock and unpaid customer bills, is the part of the cash story to watch. We picked each KPI because it decides either the size of revenue (plant clearance, mix), its quality (margin) or the risk of a surprise (cash, concentration).

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate rests on one assumption that could fail. This chapter names that assumption for each estimate, then gives the strongest honest case against buying, the single claim everything hangs on, and the dates when we will learn more.

Weakest assumption in our earnings estimate

We assume the margin holds at 22.5% to 23.5% while raw-material and freight costs rise. Weakest assumption in the reverse DCF

The model assumes a smooth ten years of growth and a 20 times exit multiple. Pharma profits move in steps, with approvals and price erosion in generics, so a smooth line is a simplification. We use the result to compare the price against a range of growth, not to forecast.

Weakest assumption in the scenario probabilities

The citation checker therefore lists research records whose period date is more than 60 days before the report date as EXCEPTION AS-OF lines, and we run it with the stale-as-of allowance. In all, 98 of the 137 found research records carry a period date more than 60 days before 28 October 2026. They are the Q1 FY27 figures, the March 2026 annual data, the Q1 to Q4 earnings-call remarks, the Warning Letter (2025), the ICRA rating (January 2026), broker targets of July 2026 and the July 2026 tariff announcement. We cannot cure this now because the newer filing does not exist. It lowers confidence in the earnings base, so every Chapter 3 and Chapter 5 number is labelled as before Q2. The cure is the Q2 FY27 filing on 2 November 2026, after which the report should be re-underwritten.

Ledger basis. The ledger's reverse DCF profit base of ₹734.33 crore does not match published trailing profit of ₹662 crore. We kept the ledger figure in the Chapter 5 table and added our own calculation with every input cited, and we did not edit the ledger.

Aggregator and AI-assisted sources. Screener and Trendlyne are aggregators and do not count toward the source floor. The KnowYourCompany.ai note is an AI-assisted summary of filings; we use it only for the warrant price, balance payable and share count, which the Economic Times and ScanX notes support in part, and we flag it as the least certain source. The BSE and NSE original filings blocked automatic download (403 or timeout), so promoter-sale and warrant facts rest on press and exchange-derived summaries.

Not published. We found no published share for the largest customer, no segment margin for complex versus other products, and no outcome yet for the Gagillapur reinspection. The research file records each as not_found with three or more queries.

Validator coverage. The repository's validate_report.py in this worktree accepts only the kinds market_intel and portfolio_audit, so it cannot be run for a dossier; the dossier was checked with readability_check.py, citation_check.py and the Research Desk layout check in portal_publish.py. The repository's assemble_report.py auto-glossary step does not fire when a glossary term sits directly inside a paragraph, so the first-use definitions were added by a post-render pass that applies the same wrap-and-expand rule.

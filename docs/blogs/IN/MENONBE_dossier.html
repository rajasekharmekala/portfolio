Start here: the framework and the terms

The verdict is built in layers, the way a building inspector checks a house. Layer 1 is a set of hard gates. Each gate is a floor the company must stand on: return on capital, debt, cash conversion, the share-price trend, and the asymmetry test. If any single gate fails, the company is out, however good the rest looks. Only a company that clears Layer 1 is scored on its operating measures in Layer 2, and only then is it checked for where its price sits against support in Layer 3. Here the company fails Layer 1, so Layers 2 and 3 are shown for information and produce no buy.

Think of a Stage 2 share as one in its growing season. Stage 2 (Markup) is the phase, in Stage Analysis, where a share trades above a rising 50-day average and a rising 200-day average. That is what a healthy uptrend looks like. Stage 4 is the opposite: a decline below falling averages, the winter of the four seasons.

ATR (average true range) is the average daily price movement over 14 sessions. Here it is ₹19.10. Measuring distances in ATRs lets you compare a share that moves ₹5 a day with one that moves ₹50 a day. A distance of 0.5 ATR means the price is half a normal day's move away from a level. RSI (relative strength index) is a speedometer on a 0 to 100 scale; above 70 a share is called extended, and below 30 it is called oversold. Here RSI is 47.96, which is neither.

ROCE (return on capital employed) is the operating profit a business earns on all the money tied up in it, before tax and interest. A figure above 15 percent means the business earns more than the cost of most of its borrowing. EPS (earnings per share) is the company's profit divided by its number of shares. EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) is a profit measure that ignores interest, tax and the wear on assets, and it is used here only where a source reports it.

A base is a sideways pattern a share forms after a fall, and its low point is the bottom of that pattern. The pivot is the price at which a move out of a base would be confirmed, normally near the top of the base. A breakout is a move through the pivot on heavy trading, and a retest is a return to a broken level to check that it now holds as support. A limit order is an instruction to buy only at or below a set price, and an entry zone is the band of prices in which a buyer is willing to start a position.

The asymmetry ratio is the possible gain to the target divided by the possible loss to the stop. A ratio of 3.5 means you can make 3.5 rupees for every rupee you can lose. The Tactical floor is 3.5. The reward-to-risk ratio is the same idea stated as a comparison of what you can win against what you can lose. A stop is a price you decide in advance to exit at if the trade goes wrong.

Operating margin is the share of each rupee of sales left after running costs. Net margin is the share of each rupee of sales left after all costs, interest and tax. Debt-to-equity compares borrowings with owners' funds; below 0.70 means the business is not leaning heavily on debt. Price-to-earnings is the share price divided by the profit per share, so a P/E of 36 means investors pay 36 rupees for each rupee of last year's profit.

Guidance is the revenue or profit range a company's management says it expects, and it is a statement of intent, not an audited figure. A measured move is a technical target found by adding the height of a base to its pivot. Working capital is the money tied up in stock and unpaid customer bills, and rising working capital absorbs cash even when profit grows. A consensus estimate is the average of published analyst forecasts for a company's future earnings.

The momentum test sits beside Layer 1. It asks whether a share is already in an uptrend (Test A) or is forming a base from which a breakout could start (Test B), and whether the price is still close enough to support to be bought without chasing. A candidate needs to pass one of the two routes to be considered on momentum grounds. The gate is computed in code, and the report explains where that code is currently in error.

The verdict labels are these. IMMEDIATE_BUY means the price is at support with a high ratio. ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP means the price is near support and a small pullback is needed. WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT means the price is extended and should not be chased. REJECTED means the company failed a gate. The colour code follows the report palette, and the words carry the meaning, so the colours can be ignored in print. What the company does

Menon Bearings is a manufacturer whose products sit inside the engine. Its core line is bimetal bearings, bushes and thrust washers, which carry the moving parts of a diesel engine. The company's own description, reproduced by Screener, is that it makes critical engine components for high-stress, heavy-duty diesel environments across three segments: Bimetal products, Alkop (aluminium die-casting) and Braking Systems (Screener). The momentum test: Test A and Test B

The momentum test has two routes. Test A asks whether a share is already in momentum: a Stage 2 structure, a 200-day average that is not falling, positive relative strength, and a price not stretched too far from support. Test B asks whether a share is entering momentum from a base: a flat or rising 50-day average, relative strength turning up, the price near a base pivot, volume drying up and then expanding. A candidate needs to pass one of the two routes to be considered on momentum grounds. The gate is computed in code from a frozen price snapshot, and its thresholds are flagged as needing calibration against live data.

The asymmetry test, line by line

The asymmetry gate is the reward-to-risk test. Its formula is simple: the base target minus the price, divided by the price minus the stop. The result is the number of rupees of possible gain for each rupee of possible loss. The Tactical floor is 3.5. The formula is only as good as its target, and the rule insists that the target be anchored to a verifiable source: either forward earnings multiplied by a three-year median price-to-earnings ratio, or a technical measured move from a base. An arbitrary target is not allowed. If the target cannot be verified, the gate is recorded as a fail for asymmetry, whatever the arithmetic says.

Fatal-flaw red team

A red team asks the strongest question against the conclusion and tests whether the conclusion survives. For this company there are seven serious objections. Each is set out with the evidence for and against it, and each is answered or left open. The point of the exercise is not to win an argument; it is to find the one fact that would overturn the verdict, if one exists.

Flaw one: the target has no source, and the record cannot supply one

The most serious objection is that the verdict depends on a target that no source supports, so the gate may be failing for lack of information rather than for lack of value. That is a fair point. The record has no consensus estimate, and MarketScreener's forward cells are blank. The report's arithmetic shows that the company would need forward earnings of about ₹18.87 a share to justify ₹383 at its own three-year median multiple. A reader who believes that the company will earn that much should say so and show the reasoning, because the gate cannot accept a number the record does not contain. The answer to this flaw is that it is real, and it is the reason the verdict is REJECTED rather than WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT.

Flaw two: the volume signal is absent, and the trend is weakening inside the trend

Traded volume over the last twenty sessions is 0.54 times normal, and the price has fallen 6.3 percent over the past month, closing below its 20-day average of ₹297.34. A buyer may read this as a healthy pullback. A seller may read it as distribution, which is the opposite. The regime is Downtrend, with 21.5 percent of the broad pool above its 50-day average and 10 distribution days in the last 25 sessions (ledger). In that regime the editorial posture for a new momentum long is no new momentum longs. The answer is that the volume and regime evidence both point the same way, and both support waiting.

Flaw three: the three-year cash record hides the latest year

The three-year cash conversion of 0.90 passes the gate, but in FY26 alone it is 0.61, and cash from operations was only 36 percent of operating profit. Debtor days rose to 111. A reader who looks at the latest year sees a company whose profit is growing faster than its cash. This is the most credible argument against the company's quality, and it is one the report does not dismiss. The answer is that the gate was passed on its own terms, and that the watch item is real and should be checked at each results date.

Flaw four: one customer and one geography carry the outcome

The company describes a 20-year single-source relationship with John Deere, and the United States, the top market for India's auto-component exports with a 26 percent share in FY26, is where a tariff move would bite (PolicyPulse Media). A loss of the main customer, or a sharp tariff move, would hit the business in a way the three-year numbers would not show. The answer is that these are concentration risks, and the verdict already reflects them through the absence of a verified target. A stronger case for the company would need to show the relationships spread out.

Flaw five: the earnings jump starts from a low base

The June 2026 quarter's profit of ₹14.11 crore is up 67 percent on the year-earlier quarter of ₹8.43 crore (Capital Market). A quarter that starts from a low base can overstate the trend. The answer is that the report does not use the quarter as a trend; it uses the three-year figures, and those show a much slower profit path.

Flaw six: the record itself has defects

The analysis found several defects that a careful reader should know about. The momentum gate's distance function returns the furthest support rather than the nearest, which turns condition A4 into a false failure; the run notes give the script line and the cure. One news account labels the June quarter's profit before tax as profit after tax, which would overstate profit by about 31 percent; the reproduced filing figures confirm the ₹14.11 crore profit after tax and the ₹18.51 crore profit before tax. None of these defects changes the verdict, but each has been disclosed, and the first two are to be corrected before the record is used again.

The promoter group sold 9,96,909 shares between 18 September 2024 and 14 August 2026, and its holding fell to 66.67 percent (Screener). The sale price is not in the verified record, so the report cannot say whether the seller took profit near the top of the rally. The answer is that the sale and the thin holding are exit risks that the verdict already treats as a reason for caution; they are not a separate fail.

Glossary

This glossary repeats the terms defined at the start of the report, so that a reader can find them in one place. Each term is defined where it first appears, in the framework chapter, and the list is short because the report uses the same handful of measures throughout.

ATR (average true range)
The average daily price movement over 14 sessions, used as a unit of distance. Here ₹19.10.
Asymmetry ratio
The possible gain to the target divided by the possible loss to the stop. The Tactical floor is 3.5.
Base low and base pivot
The base low is the lowest price in a 63-session range. The base pivot is the level at which a breakout from the base is confirmed, normally near its top.
CFO/PAT
Cash from operations divided by reported profit after tax, usually summed over three years. The floor is 0.70.
Debt-to-equity
Borrowings divided by owners' equity. The Layer 1 ceiling is 0.70.
Displaced challenger
A shortlisted name that a stronger candidate has replaced in its book.
Forward EPS
Expected earnings per share over the next year. Not published for this company.
Layer 1, 2 and 3
The three stages of the framework: hard gates, operating scorecard and entry posture.
Moving average (SMA)
The average closing price over a set number of sessions. SMA20, SMA50 and SMA200 are the 20-, 50- and 200-day averages.
Promoter pledge
Shares held by the founding group that are offered as security for a loan. A rising pledge is a risk signal.
ROCE and ROE
Return on capital employed and return on equity: profit as a share of the capital or equity that produced it.
Rule stop
The stop-loss price set by the Tactical rule: the lower of the swing low and the 20- and 50-day averages, minus half an ATR.
Stage 2 (Markup)
A share trading above rising 50- and 200-day averages, the healthy uptrend phase.
Support and nearest support
A price level where buying has stepped in before. The nearest support is the one closest below the price.
Volume multiple
Recent average volume divided by the normal average. Below 1.0 means quieter than usual.

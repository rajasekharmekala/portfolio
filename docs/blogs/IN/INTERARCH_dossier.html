1. What the company does

Interarch makes pre-engineered buildings, which are steel frames cut and welded in a factory, shipped to a site and bolted together. The model suits factories, warehouses and large sheds, because the building goes up faster than one made from concrete and site-poured steel. Think of it as a flat-pack kit for a large industrial shed, sold together with the engineering drawings and the erection crew. The company reports installed manufacturing capacity of 221,000 tonnes after the Kheda expansion[23]. Its customers are large industrial groups; the rating agency lists Reliance Industries, the Tata group and the Aditya Birla group among its established clients[4].

The company's legal name changed from Interarch Building Products Limited to Interarch Building Solutions Limited, so older databases may still carry the earlier name[4]. Revenue for FY26 was ₹1,898 crore, up 30.6 percent on the year before[20]. Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹459.65 crore, up 20.7 percent year on year[18]. The order book stood at ₹1,864 crore on 31 July 2026, about 35 percent of it from new-age industries such as data centres, electric vehicles and semiconductors[22].

Where the growth is coming from

Two things drive the next phase of revenue. The first is new capacity: a Gujarat plant that began operations in July 2026[16], and a heavy-structures plant in Andhra Pradesh that is being built in three phases, with Phase 3 due by December 2027[23]. The second is exports. Exports were ₹10 to 12 crore in Q1, roughly 2 to 3 percent of sales[23]. A 76:24 joint venture with the Canadian firm ER Steel is meant to make open web steel joists for North America[16]. For you, the practical meaning is that the company is spending its way into a bigger business, and that spending is the reason the cash numbers in chapter 3 look weak.

What this means for an owner

You own a business whose sales are growing quickly and whose order book is large. What you are paying for is the company turning that growth into cash. Chapter 2 shows that it has grown profit well, but the cash has not followed.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The tests and terms used in this dossier

Several terms recur, so each is defined here before the numbers use it. ROCE (return on capital employed) is the profit a business earns on the money tied up in it, shown as a percentage. The floor here is 15 percent, and 18 percent is the preferred level for a core holding. Debt-to-equity is the money a company has borrowed divided by the owners' money, and a figure below 0.70 is the floor. Operating margin is the share of each rupee of sales left after running costs.

CFO/PAT compares operating cash flow with reported profit over three years. A ratio near 1 means the profit arrived as cash, and the floor is 0.70. Working capital is the cash tied up in stock, unpaid customer bills and supplier credit, and it grows when the company builds work before it is paid for.

Stage 2 means the price is in an uptrend, sitting above a 50-day average and a 200-day average that are both rising. A moving average is the average closing price over a set number of days, and the 50-day and 200-day are the two most watched. Stage 4, defined in the summary, is the downtrend.

A base is a sideways period in which a stock holds a range after a fall. A measured move is an estimate of how far the price can travel after the base is left, found by measuring the height of that base.

ATR (average true range) is the typical daily price swing over fourteen days, so it works as a ruler for distance: a price 1 ATR above a support level has moved about one normal day's range above it. RSI (relative strength index) is a gauge from 0 to 100 of recent buying strength over fourteen days. Readings under 30 are called oversold, and readings over 70 overbought.

The asymmetry ratio is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop, and the floor for a core holding is 3.0 to 1. A price-to-earnings ratio is the share price divided by earnings per share over the last twelve months. Guidance is the outlook management gives for the coming year, and it is an expectation, not a promise.

The holding verdicts are KEEP_COMPOUNDING, ACCUMULATE_ON_DIP, TRIM_PROFIT_BOOKING and REPLACE_UNDERPERFORMER. Each names the rule that produced it. Figures marked as coming from the ledger are the run's internal price record, which is the authoritative price source for this run. Every other figure carries a numbered citation to the list at the end.

The strongest part of the record is the balance sheet and the return on the money tied up in the business. Value Research shows a return on capital employed of 23.36 percent for the year to March 2026, and a return on equity of 16.63 percent[3]. A return on capital of that size is well above the 15 percent floor and above the 18 percent level that marks a core holding. Borrowing is almost nil: the company reports a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.02[2], and the rating agency measured adjusted gearing at 0.02 for FY25[4]. A company with little debt does not have to pay interest out of its earnings, so a downturn hurts it less.

The growth record is also good. Value Research shows three-year compound growth of 19.08 percent a year in revenue and 18.20 percent a year in net profit[3]. I checked the revenue figure against the five-year income statement, where sales rose from ₹1,124 crore in FY23 to ₹1,898 crore in FY26, which works out to about 19.1 percent a year[1].

Where the business is weak

The weak point is margin and cash. Operating margin was 9.15 percent in FY26[1]. Another source shows 9.89 percent for the same year[26], and the two do not reconcile, so I use the lower figure. In Q1 FY27 the margin was 8.58 percent[18], below the 9.3 to 9.7 percent range management set for FY27[18]. The profit line was flat: Q1 net profit was ₹28.25 crore, down 0.47 percent on a year earlier[18]. The sources reviewed do not establish the cause of the flat profit.

Steel is the largest input cost. The rating agency notes that a sharp and unexpected rise in steel prices could erode operating margin, and that the company has passed through only part of such increases in the past[4]. The agency's own downgrade trigger is an operating margin below 8 percent on a sustained basis[4]. The Q1 margin of 8.58 percent is close to that line.

Ownership and valuation

Promoters hold 59.27 percent of the company in the latest filing, the September 2026 quarter[14], against 59.43 percent in June[15]. None of the promoter shares are pledged, according to the StockSena and Trendlyne shareholding pages[2][12]. That matters because a pledged promoter stake can be sold by lenders in a downturn. Arvind Nanda and Gautam Suri are the promoters named in the filings, with holdings of about 29.87 and 22.98 percent respectively[29]. On valuation, the stock trades at 20.91 times trailing earnings[25]. Return on invested capital, calculated on a different basis from the return on capital employed used in the gate, is 15.15 percent[11]. The February reading of return on capital employed was 22.87 percent[27], so the return has been stable. Fourth-quarter FY26 net profit was ₹36.60 crore[19], so the Q1 FY27 profit of ₹28.25 crore, flat on a year earlier, was about 23 percent below the previous quarter.

What this means for an owner

The business earns good returns on the capital it uses and grows quickly. The problem is that growth absorbed cash. The next section puts the cash problem into numbers, because it is the largest weakness in the business and the second gate the holding fails.

5. What could happen next

A catalyst is an event that could move the price, and a results date is the moment the market re-tests the story with fresh numbers. The next few months contain several scheduled checkpoints, and the list below separates the ones that have been confirmed from the ones that have not.

Operating checkpoints already set by management

Management guides to FY27 revenue of ₹2,150 to 2,200 crore, which it describes as maintained after Q1[23]. Hitting that figure needs about ₹563 to ₹580 crore of revenue per quarter for the remaining three quarters, which is 22 to 26 percent above the ₹460 crore delivered in Q1[23]. The Canada joint venture is expected to make open web steel joists for North American buyers. Phase 1 is 4,000 to 5,000 tonnes, which management puts at about ₹70 to 75 crore of revenue at an EBITDA margin near 20 percent[23]. The Andhra heavy-structures Phase 3 is due by December 2027[23].

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Analysts' numbers are useful because they show what the market expects, and dangerous because they are often too kind. This chapter asks where the view in this dossier could be wrong, in either direction.

The forecast gap

The consensus of three analysts, as recorded by S&P Global, has an average target of ₹2,443, a high of ₹2,889 and a low of ₹2,140[10]. Trendlyne's consensus shows the same average of ₹2,443[13]. Consensus earnings per share are ₹87.97 for FY27 and ₹107.17 for FY28[10]. Against FY26 earnings of ₹80.21 per share[1], that is growth of about 10 percent and then about 22 percent. ICICI Direct's August report puts FY28 profit at ₹175 crore, a broker estimate[16], and sets a target of ₹2,300 at 22 times FY28 earnings[16]. Those forecasts assume the margin recovers to 9.5 to 10 percent. Q1 margin of 8.58 percent is the first test of that assumption, and it has not yet been passed[18].

The analysts also disagree. Univest reports a JM Financial buy target of ₹2,150 and an ICICI Securities neutral target of ₹2,000[17], while the ICICI Direct summary carries a buy at ₹2,300[16]. I cannot reconcile these from the sources available, so I weight the consensus range and do not rely on any single target.

The cash risk

The largest risk to the estimates is the cash cycle. The company has been winning larger, milestone-based orders, which tie up cash until work is certified and paid for. Management says it is tightening payment terms and asking for higher advances on large orders[24]. If that works, the cash ratio will improve. If the larger orders keep growing faster than advances, the company can report rising profit and falling cash for another year. This is the central uncertainty of the dossier. It is why cash conversion is the second failed gate, and why the forecasts count for less until cash improves. The verdict itself is triggered by the Stage 4 price trend.

Where the data disagree

Five data points conflict across sources, and each is disclosed in the notes. The debt-to-equity ratio is 0.02 in three sources[2][4][5] and 0.35 in a fourth[6]. The FY25 operating cash flow is ₹53.56 crore in one series and ₹171 crore in another[7]. The FY26 operating margin is 9.15 percent or 9.89 percent[26]. The promoter holding is 59.27 percent in the latest filing[14], while one source reports 48.50 percent[8]. The fifth is the ICICI Securities rating, which is a buy at ₹2,300 in one summary and neutral at ₹2,000 in another, as the forecast section describes. Where a figure drives a gate, I have used the figure that agrees with the independent series and shown the conflict. Only one conflict can move a gate: the FY25 cash figure. On the agreeing series the three-year ratio is 0.35, and with ATS's ₹171 crore it would be about 0.71, against a floor of 0.70, so that one figure would turn the cash gate from FAIL to a narrow pass. The verdict would not change, because the Stage 4 price trend is the independent trigger and a cash recovery alone would not remove it. But the gate label would change from FAIL_CASH_CONVERSION to FAIL_TECHNICAL. The debt test passes at every value in the sources.

What this means for an owner

The forecasts could be right and the stock still underperform, if cash keeps lagging and the market refuses to pay a higher multiple. They could also be wrong and the stock recover, if margin and cash both improve in the next two quarters. The holding verdict is designed for the first case. The mandated anchor, forward earnings times a verified three-year median price-to-earnings multiple, needs history that the cached sources do not contain, so no range on that basis can be set.

If the number is off

The ratio is most sensitive to the stop. A higher stop shrinks the downside and raises the ratio, and a lower stop widens the downside and cuts it. A target that proved too high would lower the ratio by reducing the upside. Even a large error would not change the verdict, because the Stage 4 price trend is an independent trigger that does not depend on this number.

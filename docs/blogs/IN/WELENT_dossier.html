1. What the company does

Welspun Enterprises Limited is the infrastructure arm of the Welspun Group, which is promoted by the Goenka family.[1][12] It bids for contracts from public bodies in three areas: roads, water and wastewater, and tunnelling. In the year to March 2026 its consolidated revenue was ₹3,615 crore, made up of transport at ₹1,425 crore, water at ₹1,240 crore, and tunnelling and rehabilitation at ₹951 crore.[5] The company also owns a 35% share of an oil and gas exploration venture, which has not yet produced revenue and whose gas sales are not expected before FY29.[4][8]

The customers are governments and city bodies. The National Highways Authority of India pays for roads, and Mumbai's civic bodies and state water missions pay for water works.[4][9] Many road projects use the hybrid annuity model, or HAM. Under HAM the developer builds the road, the government pays part of the cost during construction, and the rest comes back as fixed yearly payments called annuities.[10] A second model is build-operate-transfer, or BOT, where the developer runs the asset for a fixed period and earns from users or a government payment. The Pune–Shirur road is the company's newest and largest BOT, a 29-year concession with four years of construction and 25 years of tolling.[9]

The company makes money in three ways. First, it earns a construction margin on contracts that it wins and then passes to regional sub-contractors to build. Management calls this an asset-light model, and the rating agency confirms that most of the building work is outsourced.[6][12] Second, it owns some projects through separate project companies and sells them once they mature. In 2022 it sold six road assets at a combined value of about ₹9,000 crore, and in July 2026 it signed a deal to sell the Aunta–Simaria road in Bihar for about ₹1,000 crore.[6][3][5] Third, it earns fees for running finished water plants over many years, which adds steadier revenue. About 29% of the ₹18,729 crore order book at 30 June 2026 is this kind of operation work.[4]

Two features make this company harder to read than a normal manufacturer. The first is that it is a holding company with many project companies underneath. On a standalone basis, meaning the listed parent alone, the company held ₹1,579 crore of cash and no borrowings at 31 March 2026.[8] On a consolidated basis, meaning the whole group including project companies, it held ₹1,728 crore of cash and ₹1,771 crore of gross debt.[8] The annual report shows that all of the group's long-term debt, ₹1,770.90 crore, sits in subsidiaries.[10] Three debt figures appear for March 2026, and they differ only in what they include. The press release figure of ₹1,771 crore is term loans including the part due within a year. The balance-sheet total of ₹1,971.91 crore adds ₹201.01 crore of other current borrowings, and it is the figure that the borrowings-against-equity red line test uses. Screener's ₹1,987 crore adds ₹14.61 crore of lease liabilities.[8][10][1] This report says which basis each number uses, because the answer changes with the basis.

The second feature is that cash from building a road does not look like cash from a normal sale. A developer spends money while the road is built and receives annuities later. In the accounts, the spending shows up as a growing receivable, which lowers operating cash flow in the building years.[10] The ledger of what the group is owed under road concessions rose from ₹1,020.91 crore at March 2024 to ₹1,949.05 crore at March 2026, before a further ₹40.07 crore due within a year.[11][10] Chapter 3 tests whether that explains the weak cash conversion, or only part of it.

The order book was ₹18,729 crore on 30 June 2026, roughly five times the revenue of the last financial year.[4][5] Water was 55% of it, transport 30%, tunnelling 11% and other work 4%.[4] A large order book gives visibility, but it is not yet revenue, and the company has had to cut its revenue target once in the last year.[16][9]

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The business is strong where it is visible and weak where it is hard to see. It is strong on order cover, operating profit and credit quality. It is weak on turning profit into cash, on delivering revenue on schedule, and on the price now paid for the shares. The paragraphs below give the evidence for each, with the numbers and the basis (consolidated or standalone) stated.

The order book is the first strength. At 30 June 2026 it stood at ₹18,729 crore, which is about 5.2 times the ₹3,615 crore of revenue in the year to March 2026.[4][5] The rating agency, using its own adjusted revenue, puts the cover at 5.7 times at March 2026, up from 4.1 times a year earlier, after the award of the Pune–Shirur road.[12] The annual report says that, leaving out the long-running operation contracts, the work still to be built is about four times annual revenue.[10] That gives the company two to three years of work even if it wins nothing new, but the order book has also shrunk from about ₹20,000 crore in May to ₹18,729 crore in June because revenue was booked and few orders arrived.[8][4]

Where the business is strong

Operating profit is the second strength, and three sources agree on the direction even though they define profit differently. The company reports an EBITDA margin of 22.8% for the year to March 2026, measured on total income that includes other income, against its own target of 18% or more.[8][9] The Screener table of filed data shows an operating profit of ₹746 crore on ₹3,615 crore of sales, or 20.6%, which leaves out other income.[1] The rating agency's own adjusted figure is 16.1%, because it treats some items differently.[12] All three are above the 15% the agency expects over the medium term, so the strength does not depend on which definition is used.

Water is the best-paying business. Its segment result was ₹325.04 crore on revenue of ₹1,239.73 crore, or 26.2%, the highest of the three.[5] Management says this is a technology play: it picks water projects where partners supply specialised know-how, because on a plain water contract the same margin could not be held.[6] The tunnelling subsidiary, Welspun Michigan Engineers, in which the company owns 60.09%, reported revenue of ₹874 crore in FY26 at a 21.1% EBITDA margin, and management says its machines are 100% occupied.[4][6] Competition from larger water engineers is real, so we treat these margins as good but not permanent.

Credit quality is the third strength. CRISIL upgraded the long-term rating to AA with a stable outlook on 21 September 2026, from AA- with a positive outlook.[12] Net debt was only ₹109 crore at 30 June 2026 against a net worth of ₹3,323 crore, and the parent alone held ₹1,660 crore of cash with no debt.[4] The annual report gives an interest coverage ratio of 4.00 times for FY26 and a debt-service coverage ratio of 3.46.[10] Promoters hold 56.13% of the shares and, according to the 30 June 2026 filing, none of their shares are pledged.[13] After shareholder approval on 7 November 2025, a promoter and other investors were allotted 1.9047619 crore warrants at ₹525 each, and ₹250 crore, one quarter of the price, has been received.[5]

Where the business is weak

Cash conversion is the main weakness. The group's contract assets, which are work done but not yet billed, rose from ₹1,181.07 crore to ₹1,318.41 crore in FY26, and trade receivables rose from ₹302.57 crore to ₹509.63 crore.[10] CRISIL measures the cash tied up in current assets in days of sales, and that figure rose from 228 days to 271 days over the year.[12] The agency blames milestone-based payments on water projects and slow collections under the Jal Jeevan Mission, the national rural water scheme.[12] This is the evidence that part of the weak cash flow is genuine working capital stress and not only an accounting classification.

Revenue delivery is the second weakness. Revenue fell 2.2% in FY26 to ₹3,615 crore from ₹3,695 crore, and it fell 8.4% in the June 2026 quarter to ₹774 crore.[8][3] Management blamed supply-chain disruption, a stoppage of construction in Mumbai that lasted several weeks, and labour that left work sites around elections.[6] The company had guided to growth of 15% to 20% for FY26 in May 2025 and cut its revenue target to ₹3,600 to ₹3,700 crore in February 2026.[19][16] It now targets 15% to 20% for FY27 but its managing director says the outcome will be closer to 15%.[6] The rating agency expects 10% to 15% in each of FY27 and FY28.[12]

Concentration and funding are the third weakness. The Pune–Shirur road is about 44% of the order book excluding operation work, and it needs the company to put in about ₹1,500 crore of its own equity.[12] The funding plan relies on the ₹1,000 crore warrant issue, of which ₹750 crore is still to be paid, on selling existing road assets, and on the company's own cash.[12][5] The notice for the 20 August 2026 annual meeting proposed an enabling resolution to raise up to ₹1,000 crore through a placement of new shares.[10] The 1.9047619 crore warrants alone equal 13.8% of the 13.8414 crore shares now in issue, so dilution is a live issue for existing holders.[13] Smaller items add to the list: an oil and gas venture that wrote off ₹48.86 crore in FY26, and two GST show-cause notices, for ₹71.24 crore (Uttar Pradesh, 28 September, for FY2022-23) and ₹15.37 crore (Haryana, 30 September), as summarised by Screener from exchange filings and not read in the filings themselves; the summaries do not say whether any provision was made.[5][1]

How the sector around it is trading

Listed road builders have been marked down hard, and Welspun Enterprises has not. The four road peers in chapter 3 show one-year share price falls of 48% to 58% on their Screener pages, while this company's shares closed at ₹666.90, which is 61.9% above their 52-week low of ₹412.00 and 20.8% below their 52-week high.[21][22][23][24][28] On the same pages the road peers trade at 4 to 10 times earnings, while this company trades at about 25.8 times FY26 earnings at the ₹666.90 close.[21][22][23][24][5][28] The market is therefore paying a premium for the water and tunnelling mix, the order cover and the credit rating. That premium is fair only if cash conversion and revenue growth recover, which is the question the next chapters test.

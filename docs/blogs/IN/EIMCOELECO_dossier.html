Chapter 1

Eimco Elecon (India) Limited was formed in 1974 in Vallabh Vidyanagar, Gujarat. It began as a specialized joint venture between the Elecon Group and the Eimco Mining Machinery division of Envirotech Corporation (USA). Ishwarbhai B. Patel founded the Elecon Group in 1951, and Bhanubhai I. Patel and Prayasvin B. Patel steered it for decades. At that time, Indian underground coal mines relied mostly on manual pick-and-basket labour. Eimco Elecon brought intermediate mechanization to India through pneumatic Rocker Shovel Loaders, electro-hydraulic Side Dump Loaders (SDLs), and Load Haul Dumpers (LHDs). The company went public in 1992–1993 and spent the next three decades serving Coal India Limited (CIL) and Singareni Collieries Company Limited (SCCL). It has built more than 3,800 machines over its history, and 1,065 active machines still run in the field today.

The foreign partnership changed hands as global mining suppliers consolidated. In 1989, Finland's Tamrock Oy bought the Eimco group, and Sweden's Sandvik AB absorbed Tamrock in the late 1990s. Under a 1992 Shareholders' Agreement and a 2011 supplemental pact, Tamrock Great Britain Holdings Limited held a ~25.1% (later 24.68%) co-promoter stake. The Patel family's Elecon group held the ~48.96% Indian promoter stake alongside Sandvik. For over three decades, that tie-up gave Eimco Elecon technical backing, global rock-excavation engineering, and non-compete protection in India. Yet it also placed a strategic ceiling on growth. Eimco Elecon stayed a domestic, single-plant maker in Gujarat with limited export rights and a product line tied closely to Indian coal.

The 2025 Structural Divorce: Sandvik Exits, Independence Begins

On April 23, 2025, Elecon Engineering and Sandvik's Tamrock Great Britain Holdings ended their 1992 Shareholders' Agreement. That step terminated all inter-se promoter rights and obligations. Elecon Engineering then reclassified its 16.62% stake in Eimco Elecon from an associate to a financial asset under Ind AS 109.

In September 2025, Tamrock sold its entire 24.68% stake (14.24 lakh shares) through an Offer for Sale (OFS) and moved to the public shareholder category. Sandvik raised no objection to Eimco Elecon keeping the 'Eimco' brand name. Promoter holding fell from 73.64% to 48.96%, leaving Eimco Elecon an independent Indian company with no subsidiaries or joint ventures.

Understanding Eimco Elecon's past cycles is vital before projecting recent growth forward. From FY2015 to FY2022, India's coal output grew almost solely from opencast pits, while underground coal output fell by roughly 2.9% each year. Because underground coal gear drove over 90% of Eimco Elecon's sales then, revenue suffered repeated air pockets. Sales dropped from ₹196 Cr in FY15 to ₹140 Cr in FY16, bounced to ₹185 Cr in FY19, and then sank to ₹108 Cr in FY20 and ₹84 Cr in FY22 during COVID-19 mine disruptions and tender freezes. Operating margins narrowed to 5% in FY20 and 9% in FY21, and operating cash flow turned negative (-₹10 Cr) in FY22. Unlike leveraged capital-goods peers that went bankrupt in that coal winter, Eimco Elecon kept fixed costs lean and avoided EPC contracting. Management parked surplus cash in mutual funds, keeping the Vallabh Vidyanagar plant intact until the cycle turned.

Two structural shifts drove the recovery after FY22. First, Coal India pushed to reopen closed underground mines using private Mine Developer-cum-Operators (MDOs) and blast-free Continuous Miner (CM) packages. Second, Eimco Elecon's R&D team built higher-ticket CM packages (CM3000/CM3500, shuttle cars, roof bolters, and feeder breakers), 7–10 tonne metal-mine LHDs, and CZM-backed piling rigs. Revenue jumped 105% in FY23 to ₹173 Cr, rose 32% in FY24 to ₹228 Cr, and grew 8% in FY25 to ₹246.5 Cr at a record 22.8% EBITDA margin. Even so, FY26 showed that cyclical swings have not vanished. Tender delays and deferred private orders pulled FY26 sales down 6.4% to ₹230.7 Cr before H1 FY27 rebounded 33.3% YoY to ₹134 Cr. That whiplash—from 105% growth to a 6.4% drop and back to 33% expansion—defines a lumpy, tender-led equipment business.

The Vallabh Vidyanagar Ecosystem & Post-Sandvik Autonomy: Eimco Elecon survived fifty years of mining cycles because of its industrial setup in Vallabh Vidyanagar, Gujarat. Its 15-acre plant houses heavy steel fabrication, precision machining, hydraulic assembly, and flameproof testing under one roof. Management stayed clear of fixed-price EPC mine-development contracts that can destroy capital. Even when annual sales sank to ₹84 Cr in FY22 during the 2015–2022 coal slump, the company refused to take on debt. In September 2025, Sandvik's Tamrock Great Britain Holdings sold its 24.68% stake via an Offer for Sale, ending a 33-year co-promoter era. That exit removed foreign territory and product-line limits, giving Eimco Elecon full freedom over its strategy. Its ₹60.5 Cr FY26 capex—including land at Kanjari and plant upgrades—marks a clear shift from hoarding cash to building capacity as an independent Indian OEM.

Quarter-by-Quarter Cycle Memory (FY21–FY24 Inflection): The quarterly record shows how sharply operating leverage swings when coal tenders resume. In Q2 FY22 (Sep 2021), quarterly sales hit a trough of just ₹11 Cr with ₹1 Cr in operating profit. By Q1 FY23 (Jun 2022), operating profit briefly dipped to -₹1 Cr (-4.3% OPM) on ₹33 Cr of sales. Once Coal India and private MDO orders began shipping, quarterly sales surged to ₹64 Cr in Q4 FY23 (21% OPM) and ₹84 Cr in Q4 FY24 (20% OPM). That swing from near-breakeven quarters to 20%+ operating margins shows why single-quarter trends in either direction often mislead.

Concrete Real-World Example 1: The Blast-Free Continuous Miner Package in an Underground Coal Mine. Picture a private Mine Developer-cum-Operator (MDO) hired by Coal India to reopen a closed underground coal seam in Chhattisgarh. In the past, the mine used drill-and-blast methods with Eimco Elecon 625 SDLs loading blasted coal onto conveyors, which was slow and labour-heavy. Today, Eimco Elecon supplies a complete, homegrown Continuous Miner package. The CM3500 Continuous Miner cuts coal straight from the seam face without explosives using rotating cutter drums. An Eimco Elecon 10-tonne Shuttle Car carries the cut coal to a Feeder Breaker that crushes lumps onto the main belt conveyor. Meanwhile, a Twin-Boom Roof Bolter (TRS) secures the newly exposed mine roof right behind the cut. To move crews through low 1.35m–1.5m seams without diesel fumes, the mine can add Eimco's flameproof, battery-powered MCAR-E (14+1 personnel carrier) and UV-A-BE (10-tonne utility vehicle). Over a 9-year machine life, every shift wears down cutter bits, hydraulic hoses, conveyor chains, and gearbox seals, creating steady spares orders for Eimco Elecon's Bilaspur depot.

Concrete Real-World Example 2: Hard-Rock Piling for Metro & Highway Foundations. Outside coal, consider an infrastructure contractor building elevated metro piers or bridge abutments in hard rock. Imported European piling rigs from Bauer, Soilmec, or Liebherr carry high upfront prices and long wait times for spare parts. Eimco Elecon builds the EC216 / EC267 Piling Rig in Vallabh Vidyanagar on a Hitachi base carrier using CZM (USA) technology. Each rig features Hardox-steel Kelly bars, an anti-shock valve for hard rock, and a MOBA digital system that logs real-time torque and depth. In October 2026, Eimco Elecon widened this foundation line by launching the homegrown DW-220 Hydraulic Diaphragm Wall Grab for deep urban basements, metro stations, and dam cut-off walls.

Spare-Parts Lifecycle Economics & Fleet Annuity Mechanics: Eimco Elecon does not split out equipment and spares revenue in its quarterly filings. Still, its active fleet of 1,065 machines—with 683 coal units (64.1%) and 382 non-coal metal and mineral units (35.9%)—anchors the business. Underground loaders and Continuous Miners run two or three shifts a day in wet, abrasive, and acidic rock. Over a 7-to-9-year working life, a heavy LHD or Continuous Miner package consumes wear parts, hydraulic cylinders, crawler tracks, cutter picks, gearboxes, and electrical assemblies. That cumulative spares spend often equals or exceeds the machine's original purchase price. Coal India, SCCL, and private MDOs face heavy output penalties if a face loader stalls. As a result, getting spares within 24–48 hours matters far more to a mine manager than a small discount on upfront capex. That field reality supports Eimco Elecon's 10 regional depots and cushions margins whenever new-machine tenders slow down.

Electrification, Metal Mining & Import-Substitution Piling Rigs: Beyond coal, Eimco Elecon aims its R&D at two growing adjacencies. First, in April 2026, it launched the flameproof, battery-powered MCAR-E (1.35m height, 14+1 seats) and UV-A-BE (1.5m height, 10-tonne payload, Degree-1 gassy mine certified). These vehicles target India's 8.56% CAGR battery-electric mining shift by cutting diesel exhaust heat and ventilation costs for Hindustan Zinc, Hindustan Copper, and CIL. Second, VP Amit Kumar noted in an October 2026 NBM&CW interview that Eimco Elecon is India's sole multi-size domestic maker of hydraulic piling rigs (EC125, EC216, EC267). Built on Hitachi base platforms with CZM (USA) tech and MOBA monitoring, these rigs were joined in October 2026 by the DW-220 Hydraulic Diaphragm Wall Grab. Local fabrication and fast hydraulic support help Eimco win Make-in-India infrastructure tenders against European imports, though its articulated wheel loaders still face tough Chinese price competition.

Segment Economics Comparison — Coal vs. Metal vs. Foundation Rigs: Each of Eimco Elecon's three verticals carries a different ticket size and margin profile. Standalone coal loaders (SDLs and LHDs) are mature, high-share products with steady spares pull-through across 683 active coal machines. By contrast, a full Continuous Miner package (CM3500, shuttle cars, roof bolter, feeder breaker) is a multi-crore system sale. Commissioning a CM package creates sharp quarterly revenue spikes and locks in high-value cutter-drum and pick replacements. In non-coal metal mining (382 active units at Hindustan Zinc, Hindustan Copper, and UCIL), 7-tonne and 10-tonne LHDs compete directly with Sandvik and Epiroc on uptime. Finally, EC-series piling rigs and the DW-220 diaphragm wall grab serve urban metro and bridge projects, where fast local service offsets European brand prestige.

The Hidden Concentration & Chinese Price-Pressure Reality

Even with piling rigs and metal-mine sales, CRISIL's August 2026 review confirms that 75–80% of Eimco Elecon's revenue still comes from underground mining. That share is down from over 90% before 2022, yet coal mining remains the dominant driver.

In construction wheel loaders (2.3T and 3.5T), management notes fierce price pressure from Chinese OEMs such as Sany, XCMG, and LiuGong. That rivalry limits pricing power outside specialized piling rigs.

India's mining equipment market splits between surface mining and underground mining. Surface mining makes up ~50.5% of the market.

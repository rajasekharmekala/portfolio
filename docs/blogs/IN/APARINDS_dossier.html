1. What the company does & day-to-day vertical operations

APAR is a manufacturer and solution provider. It does not rely on one product. Its reporting segments are conductors, transformer and specialty oils, and power/telecom cables. Daily work combines metal conversion, oil formulation and quality control. It also includes high-voltage testing, export logistics and project delivery. Revenue can rise while volume is flat or lower. Metal prices and product mix can cause that gap. Rupee sales alone do not show throughput or margin quality. [1] [2]

The Q2 FY27 filing reports H1 segment revenue of ₹7,091.59 crore for conductors, ₹3,516.93 crore for transformer and specialty oils, and ₹3,712.70 crore for power/telecom cables. Those are segment figures before the company’s inter-segment elimination; do not sum them and call the result consolidated external revenue. The audited FY26 revenue mix is a useful scale reference, while the current quarter data should anchor the latest operating read. [1] [6]

Conductors: metal conversion plus engineering and execution

A conductor order starts with the customer’s design and project needs. APAR then prepares the metal, makes the specified product, tests it and packs it for delivery. The company says design, manufacturing and testing are in-house, with accredited labs. Its range includes conventional and newer HTLS and AL59 conductors. Reconductoring also needs engineering and site work. That work can matter as much as tonnes sold. [12] [13]

The commercial book is large, but backlog is not revenue. H1FY27 conductor volumes were 110,416 MT, down 8.0% year on year, and Q2 volume was 57,137 MT, down 9.2%. At the same time, APAR reported a ₹12,025 crore pending order book, up 67.2% against H1FY26, and an export share of 51.0%. This combination says demand and booked work are strong while metal-price-led customer pauses and delivery clearances remain constraints on conversion. [2]

Specialty oils: formulation, blending and qualification

The oil business blends transformer oils, white oils, petroleum jelly, process oils and lubricants to meet customer needs. Daily work depends on feedstock quality, recipe control, lab tests, storage, packing and delivery. Technical approval at a transformer maker or industrial customer can support repeat business. APAR describes sites in Rabale and Silvassa in India and Hamriyah in the UAE. It also describes blending and storage hubs in other regions. These are company claims, not third-party capacity audits. [14] [15]

Q2FY27 oil revenue rose to ₹1,819 crore, while volume fell 20.0% to 128,590 KL. EBITDA per KL increased to ₹23,262 from ₹6,517 a year ago. The price/mix and unit-profit jump is substantial, but lower physical volume and volatile base-oil inputs mean investors should avoid extrapolating one quarter’s margin across a cycle. [2]

Cables: engineered products with application-specific qualification

Cable making starts with wire drawing and stranding. The plant then adds insulation and outer jackets, along with product-specific layers. Each cable is tested before dispatch. APAR sells low- and medium-voltage cables, plus products for rail, marine, solar and industry. The company describes two Gujarat plants, accredited cable labs and four e-Beam facilities for polymer cross-linking. These tools support customer approvals. Project timing can still move sales between quarters. [16] [17] [18] [19]

Cable sales rose 22.1% to ₹1,875 crore in Q2 and EBITDA rose 31.9% to ₹207 crore, with an 11.0% segment EBITDA margin. The pending cable order book reached ₹3,012 crore versus ₹1,653 crore a year earlier, with a 32.5% export mix. These are constructive operating signals; the Q2 call also disclosed U.S. DDP shipments still in transit, a timing issue that makes reported revenue lumpy. [2] [4]

2. Competitive moat, industry structure & external learning hub

APAR’s edge comes from its operating system and customer approvals. Reliability, testing, export reach and the ability to fill large orders can help it win work. This is not protection from input-price cycles. Conductors and cables use metal whose price can move fast. Oils depend on feedstock prices. Margins and cash flow depend on pass-through timing and stock control. In its FY26 report, APAR says no single customer in any operating segment made more than ten percent of Group sales. That lowers reliance on one account, but it does not remove project, region or end-market risk. [6]

Polycab, KEI and RR Kabel focus more on wires and cables. APAR also has large conductor and oil businesses. KEC International has more EPC work. The group is not a set of perfect matches. The peer table is a price reference, not a scorecard. APAR’s mix may deserve a premium. The current multiple still needs steady volume and cash. [28] [29] [30] [31] [32]

What could make the competitive position durable

For conductors, in-house design and testing can lower failure risk. Reconductoring work adds engineering and site skills. For cables, customer approvals, labs and e-Beam tools can support product quality. Oil customers may prefer a proven blend over a new supplier. These edges must show up in repeat orders, premium mix, margin per unit and cash. Company claims alone do not prove pricing power. [12] [16] [18] [14]

APAR has scale and exports across its three business lines. Its latest presentation links the company to grid investment and electrification. Scale also adds work: the company must ship orders, collect cash and earn a return on each new plant. Track utilization, order quality, payment terms and returns on new capacity. 6. Key risks, thesis breakers & bear case

APAR has a strong business. The bear case is that its share price assumes H1 margins, orders and profit will turn into cash. Q2 results do not show that yet. Inventory rose from March, operating cash flow was negative, and exports and input prices add risk.

Cash and stock

H1 operating cash flow was -₹862.63 crore. It was +₹168.86 crore in H1 FY26. Inventory was ₹5,055.38 crore at September 30, up from ₹4,152.79 crore in March. Customer bills not yet paid were ₹5,244.74 crore. Growth can use cash for a while. But if stock ages or customers pay late, reported profit may not become cash. The next report should show stock and unpaid bills leveling off against sales. [1]

Margins and input prices

Oil profit per KL rose fast as Q2 volume fell 20%. Better price or mix may explain this. Base-oil prices can change fast, and margins can fall back. Metal prices also affect conductors and cables. Track tonnes sold, profit per unit and gross margin on their own. [1] [2]

Export timing and delivery

The Q2 call says about ₹800 crore in U.S.-bound DDP shipments were still in transit. APAR records sales after delivery. Delays can push sales into a later quarter and tie up cash. U.S. cable sales also fell sharply. Check delivered sales, customer payments and margins. Do not count backlog or cargo in transit as cash. [4] [5]

Price, spending and delivery

APAR trades at 46.9 times trailing earnings, above the selected cable peers. The company plans ₹1,500 crore in new capacity. New plants can add costs and stock needs before sales grow. If new projects earn less or cash stays tied up, the share price can fall even if EPS grows. [3] [1] [38] [39] [40] [41] [42]

What the bear case accepts

The bear case accepts strong orders and H1 profit. It asks if APAR can deliver and collect before oil input prices change, conductor volumes recover, or the share price falls. Until cash flow and order delivery improve, the bear case remains live.

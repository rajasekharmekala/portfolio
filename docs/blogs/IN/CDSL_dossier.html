Terms used in this dossier

Several terms appear again and again below. Each one is a plain measurement, and each has a reason to exist. The reader does not need finance training to follow the argument, but the words must mean the same thing every time they appear. The box below gives the working definition. The first time a term matters to a decision, the surrounding paragraph says what the number is and what the rule does with it.

Return on capital employed (ROCE) asks how much operating profit a company earns on every rupee of capital tied up in the business. A depository earns most of its money from fees and needs little physical capital, so a high ROCE is expected. Cash flow from operations to profit after tax (CFO/PAT) asks whether reported profit turns into cash. A ratio near 1.0 means nearly every rupee of profit arrived as cash in the bank. The Weinstein stage is a four-phase map of a stock's trend: Stage 1 is a flat base after a decline, Stage 2 is a rising uptrend, Stage 3 is a topping phase, and Stage 4 is a downtrend. A DMA is a moving average, the average closing price over a set number of sessions. The 50-day and 200-day averages are the two the rulebook uses. ATR, the average true range over 14 sessions, measures a typical day's price movement in rupees, and is used as a ruler for distance. RSI, the relative strength index over 14 sessions, runs from 0 to 100 and shows how stretched recent buying or selling has been. Asymmetry is the ratio of upside to a verified target against the downside to a stop-loss. The momentum gate is a code-run test that asks whether a stock is in a trend strong enough to hold a position.

ROCE: operating profit divided by capital employed. Above 15 percent is the floor here.

CFO/PAT: operating cash flow divided by profit after tax, summed over three years. Below 0.70 means profit is not turning into cash.

Stage 1 to 4: Stage 1 is a flat base, Stage 2 a rising uptrend, Stage 3 a topping phase, Stage 4 a downtrend.

DMA: the average closing price over 50 or 200 sessions. Price below a falling average is weak.

ATR(14): the typical daily price swing, in rupees, over 14 sessions. The rulebook measures distances in ATR units.

RSI(14): a 0 to 100 gauge of how stretched recent price moves are. Above 70 is stretched, below 30 is washed out.

Asymmetry: (target minus price) divided by (price minus stop). The Core floor is 3.0 and the Tactical floor is 3.5.

Momentum gate: a code test with two routes, A and B. A stock that fails both gets no allocation.

How the verdict was reached

The rulebook is designed so that the most important question is asked first. Layer 1 asks whether the company is financially sound and whether the price trend supports buying it. The financial floors are return on capital (ROCE of at least 15 percent, with more than 18 percent preferred for the Core book), leverage (debt-to-equity below 0.70, with financial lenders excluded), and cash conversion (three-year CFO/PAT of at least 0.70). The technical floor asks whether the stock is in a Stage 2 uptrend for the Tactical book or in a Stage 1 base breakout or Stage 2 for the Core book, with no Stage 4 breakdown and no death cross on heavy volume. The asymmetry floor requires a verified target, built either from forward earnings times a three-year median price-to-earnings ratio, from a technical measured move, or from a discounted cash flow. The floor is 3.0 times for Core and 3.5 times for Tactical. An unverified target counts as a failure.

Any Layer 1 failure ends the process. The rulebook says the action verdict becomes REJECTED and that Layers 2 and 3 are not evaluated. Only a company that passes every Layer 1 floor moves on to a Layer 2 scorecard of three to five operating measures, where at least three must be PASS. Only a company that also clears Layer 2 is placed on the entry-timing ladder in Layer 3, which measures how many average true ranges the price sits above its nearest support.

A separate code gate sits beside the layers. The momentum gate, computed by the ledger code from the price snapshot, asks whether the stock is in a strong uptrend now (Test A) or is just entering one (Test B). A stock that fails both tests is not allocated, however good its business. The ledger returns NO_MOMENTUM for the ticker CDSL

Fatal flaws and the red-team case

The strongest honest case against the company is not that the business is weak. It is that the stock has priced in a level of growth the latest year did not deliver, and that the price trend is already moving against it. The first flaw is earnings momentum. Profit after tax fell from Rs 526 crore in FY25 to Rs 455 crore in FY26 (Screener, year to 31 Mar 2026), and the trailing figure is down about 5 percent. A company trading at 55.9 times earnings needs earnings to grow, and the most recent year moved the other way. The first quarter of FY27 is a recovery, but one quarter does not reverse a year.

The second flaw is the trend. The stock is 5.5 percent below its 50-day average and 4.0 percent below its 200-day average, the 200-day average is falling, and relative strength over 63 sessions is negative by 4.73 points. The market is weak too: the ledger shows only 20 percent of the stock pool above its 50-day average. A stock that fails its own trend tests in a weak market has no tailwind. The third flaw is the valuation. With the analyst target at ₹1,319, the upside from ₹1,266 is about 4 percent, against a derived stop of ₹1,192.99 that sits about 6 percent below the price. The ratio is 0.73, which means the expected reward is smaller than the risk on the rulebook's own measure.

The fourth flaw is concentration in market activity. The company's fee income depends on account growth and trading activity, and both fall when markets are quiet. The third-party snapshot describes a bear case in which market volumes stagnate and margins compress, and it names the same risk (Tijori Finance, Jun 2026 snapshot). The fifth flaw is regulatory. The sixth is that foreign portfolio holdings fell from 11.36 percent to 8.24 percent between March and June 2026 (Screener shareholding pattern, Jun 2026). The record shows the fall but not its cause.

The bull case answers each point. Profit grew about 16 percent year on year in the June 2026 quarter on the quarterly rows (Rs 118 crore against Rs 102 crore), and the press report gave revenue growth of 13.1 per cent, and the balance sheet is close to debt-free. Market share is high, and every new listing and every new investor account adds a fee. The bull argument is that the FY26 dip is a cyclical pause in a long compounding story, and that a depository with a 32 percent return on capital should trade at a premium. That is a reasonable case. The rulebook does not reject it on quality. It rejects it on the trend and the target. The hinge claim, the one that decides the verdict, is that a stock below both its averages, with the 200-day average falling, still in a Stage 1 base without a breakout, and with no verified target, should not be bought yet. The price data support that claim and the bull case does not refute it. The bull case can only win when the price trend and a verified target agree with it.

What would change the verdict

Trigger: three conditions, all measurable from the ledger, must hold together before the technical floor is reopened. The close must be above the 50-day average of ₹1,340.35 and the 200-day average of ₹1,318.56, which are both above the close today. The 50-day average must not fall by more than 0.5 percent over 21 sessions; it fell 2.0 percent to ₹1,340.35 from ₹1,367.67. And the close must break above the base pivot on volume at least 1.0 times average.

The rulebook names the levels that would move the stock from REJECTED to a buyable state. The changes are concrete. The first is technical. A Core candidate needs a Stage 1 base breakout or a Stage 2 uptrend. A Tactical candidate needs a Stage 2 uptrend above a rising 50-day and 200-day average. For CDSL that means three things at once: a close above the 50-day average of ₹1,340.35 and the 200-day average of ₹1,318.56; a 21-session change in the 50-day average no worse than minus 0.5 percent (it is minus 2.0 percent today); and a close above the base pivot on volume at least 1.0 times average. The ledger does not state the pivot as a price. Its base_pivot_atr field, 1.36, is the distance of the close above the 63-session base low of ₹1,223.9 in ATR units, so it measures distance, not a breakout level. The next run should set the pivot price from the base's high before any allocation is discussed.

The second is the momentum gate. The gate would move from NO_MOMENTUM only when either Test A or Test B passes in the code run. Test A needs the price above a rising 50-day average that is above the 200-day, a 200-day average that is not falling over 63 sessions, positive relative strength over 63 sessions, and the price within 1.2 ATR of support. Test B needs the 50-day average to hold within 0.5 percent over 21 sessions, relative strength rising over 28 sessions, and a breakout at the base pivot with volume expansion of at least 1.0 times.

The third is the target. A verified target of at least ₹1,485 would be needed for the Core asymmetry floor of 3.0 at a price of ₹1,266 and the derived stop of ₹1,192.99. For the Tactical floor of 3.5 the target would need to reach about ₹1,522. Those levels are computed from the rule's formula, not from a view of value. The target must come from forward earnings times a three-year median P/E, a technical measured move or a discounted cash flow, and the research in this run did not produce any of those inputs. The forward-earnings basis was not published on the pages read, and the three-year median P/E was not established.

The fourth is the trend breaking. A close below ₹1,223.9 would break the swing low and the base low, which would be a clear negative change and would move the stock further from any buyable zone. A close below the derived stop of ₹1,192.99 would be a further breakdown under the Core stop rule.

If then

  • If CDSL closes above ₹1,340.35 and the 21-session fall in the 50-day average narrows to within 0.5 percent, then the trend test improves, and the verdict is reviewed in the next run.
  • If the code momentum gate returns PASS on Test A or Test B, then Layer 1 can be reopened for the technical floor.
  • If a verified target of ₹1,485 or more is built from a forward-earnings basis, then the Core asymmetry floor can pass at the current price.
  • If the close falls below ₹1,223.9, then the base weakens and the verdict stays REJECTED.
  • If the Q2 FY27 results show profit growth that restores the trend (their date is not verified in the sources read for this dossier), then the forward-earnings anchor should be rebuilt on the new numbers.

What this means for you: none of these changes is in place, and the verdict will not change by hope or by a favourable headline. The next review should check the price trend and the verified target together, because a pass on one without the other does not allow a purchase. A reviewer who wants to act earlier needs a clear target and a confirmed breakout, not a discount to the last high.

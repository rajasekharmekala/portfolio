Before you read

VST Industries makes cigarettes in India. British American Tobacco is its largest owner, and its share of the legal cigarette market is roughly 8 percent. Our verdict is REJECTED: the shares closed at ₹189.41 on 29 October 2026, at their 52-week low and below falling 50-day and 200-day averages, and the recovery test fails. The company itself is sound, with no debt, a AA+ credit rating and about ₹615 crore of liquid investments, but its profit has stepped down after the February 2026 tax increase and our value for the shares is ₹168 against that price. We would reopen the name only if all four tests in chapter 7 hold, including third-quarter volume of at least 600 million sticks a month and a close above the 20-day average of ₹199.45.

1. What the company does

VST Industries Limited is a Hyderabad cigarette maker. It sells through about 10 lakh retail outlets across more than 80 percent of India.[6] It also buys, processes and exports raw tobacco leaf, which the company reports as unmanufactured tobacco. The company was incorporated on 10 November 1930 as the Vazir Sultan Tobacco Company Limited.[9] It reports one business, tobacco and related products, and has no subsidiary, associate or joint venture.[31] Think of it as a factory, a brand shelf and a delivery network, all pointed at one product that the government taxes heavily.

Who pays whom matters more here than in most businesses. A smoker pays a shopkeeper. The shopkeeper pays a wholesale dealer. The dealer pays the company. But most of the price is tax, not company revenue. In the September 2026 quarter the company booked ₹187 crore of cigarette revenue after excise duty, while the excise duty line alone was Rs. 60,274 lakh, or about ₹603 crore.[5][7] That is roughly ₹3.2 of excise duty for every ₹1 the company keeps, before the goods and services tax (GST) that is added on top at the shop. So a change in tax moves the shelf price, the volume sold and the company's profit all at once.

The brands are the second ingredient. TOTAL is the largest and is sold across India in several flavours and formats. EDITIONS began as a southern brand and is now the company's second national brand, aimed at the mid-premium price band. CHARMS, SPECIAL and MOMENTS are older regional brands in the value segment. The company says two of the ten largest cigarette brands in India are its own.[1] In the year to March 2026 it sold 8,356 million cigarettes, up from 7,697 million a year earlier.[1]

Ownership is the third ingredient. The British American Tobacco group holds 32.16 percent through three entities: The Raleigh Investment Company (23.45 percent), Tobacco Manufacturers (India) (8.28 percent) and Rothmans International Enterprises (0.43 percent). None of it is pledged.[30] Another 29.13 percent sits with bodies corporate, and the annual report names Bright Star Investments Private Limited as a holder of 20 percent or more.[1] At ₹189.41 the market value is ₹3,217 crore (Screener rounds it to ₹3,218 crore), which makes VST a small-cap name. Industry estimates put ITC at 75 to 80 percent of the legal market, Godfrey Phillips near 12 percent and VST near 8 percent.[32][19]

Profit comes from three places: the price per stick after tax, the cost of leaf, labour and the factory, and the interest earned on spare cash. The company has no borrowings in any of the ten years shown in its annual report, and it held ₹593.87 crore of current investments at 30 September 2026.[1][4] Other income, mostly that treasury return, was ₹41.3 crore in the year to March 2026 and ₹40.7 crore in the first half of FY27.[1][29] That cash matters later, because it is about a fifth of the market value.

What changed on 1 February 2026

The government ended the GST compensation cess on cigarettes and replaced it with a higher GST rate and a new excise duty. Cigarettes moved to a 40 percent GST rate.[18] The new excise duty is charged per 1,000 sticks and rises with stick length: for filter cigarettes it is ₹2,100 up to 65 mm, ₹4,000 for 65 to 70 mm and ₹5,400 for 70 to 75 mm.[15] Press reports put the move in cigarette excise at about ₹200-735 before and ₹2,700-11,000 per 1,000 sticks after, though the reporting basis differs from the notification schedule above.[16] VST's own measure is simpler: tax incidence on cigarettes rose by about 50 percent on average.[6]

Two facts show how big this is for VST. Excise duty in the June quarter was ₹605.25 crore, 421 percent above the year before.[19] And the managing director said the increase has hit consumer affordability and the volume of the legal industry.[5]

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) is the profit a business makes from operations before those four items; for VST it measures what the brands and the factory earn.
  • EPS (earnings per share) is profit after tax divided by the number of shares; a company earning ₹100 crore with 10 crore shares has EPS of ₹10.
  • ROCE (return on capital employed) is pre-tax profit divided by the capital put into the business; a higher number means each rupee invested works harder.
  • FCF stands for free cash flow, which is the cash a business generates from operations after paying for the equipment it needs to keep running; it is the cash left for owners.
  • RSI (relative strength index) is a momentum gauge from 0 to 100; a reading below 30 is called oversold and above 70 overbought, and a very low reading means the price has fallen unusually fast.
  • ATR (average true range) is the typical size of a stock's daily price swing, and we use it as a ruler: a stock 0.12 ATR above support is only a fraction of one normal day's move away from it.
  • 20-day moving average, 50-day moving average and 200-day moving average are the average closing price over the last 20, 50 and 200 trading sessions; a price below a falling average is a sign of weakness.
  • Stage 2 is the markup phase of a stock's life cycle, with the price above rising 50-day and 200-day averages, and Stage 4 is the markdown phase, with the price below falling averages.
  • A base is a flat stretch of trading where a fallen stock stops falling and moves sideways, and it is where a new advance can start.
  • A pivot is the price at the top of a base, and a breakout is a close above it on rising volume; a retest is a later return to that level to see whether it holds.
  • An entry zone is the band of prices at which we would buy a stock that has passed our tests, chosen near verified support.
  • Reward-to-risk, which we also call asymmetry, is the gain from the entry to our target divided by the loss from the entry to our stop; a ratio of 2.0 means we stand to gain ₹2 for each ₹1 we could lose.
  • Debt-to-equity is borrowings divided by shareholders' funds; a ratio of 1.20 or more is one of our red lines for a non-financial company.
  • The price-to-earnings ratio, written P/E, is the share price divided by earnings per share; it tells you how many years of current profit the price equals.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

VST has real strengths, and the evidence is in its own filings. In FY26 its volume grew about 8.6 percent while the industry grew about 7 percent, after four straight years of volume decline.[1] TOTAL gained in every major market, EDITIONS entered the mid-premium band where the company had no presence two years earlier, and the older regional brands improved on better in-store execution.[1] It moved all production to a new integrated plant at Toopran near Hyderabad, and it has no debt.[1] CRISIL reaffirmed its AA+ rating with a stable outlook on 15 May 2026, with the top short-term rating of A1+.[10][11] Those are the traits of a business that can wait out a bad year.

The weaknesses are scale, mix and timing. At about 8 percent of the legal market, VST follows ITC on price rather than leads it.[19] Its strength is the value segment, where buyers are most sensitive to a price rise, and the mid-premium band it entered is where larger rivals are best defended.[1] Return on capital employed, which the annual report defines as profit before tax over net worth less deferred tax assets, has slid from 55.4 percent in FY19 to 27.8 percent in FY26.[1] A business can stay good at 27.8 percent, but the direction has been down for seven years and the tax shock arrived after that slide.

Timing is the third problem. The Q4 FY26 profit of ₹116.7 crore was more than double the ₹53.0 crore of a year earlier, and the shares jumped 15 percent to ₹278.51 on 17 April 2026.[23] That quarter had gross revenue of ₹68,940 lakh with excise duty inside it, and two of its three months fell under the new tax regime, so the company's own note says the figures cannot be compared with earlier periods.[24][3][31] Since then profit has fallen to ₹42.4 crore in the June quarter and ₹41.1 crore in the September quarter.[5] The market paid for the strong quarter and has since taken that value back.

Competitive position against ITC and Godfrey Phillips

In the June quarter, profit fell 27 percent at ITC, 44 percent at Godfrey Phillips and 24 percent at VST.[19] On volume the order was reversed. ITC's cigarette volume fell about 5 percent in the quarter, according to a Nomura note quoted by Univest, while VST's average monthly volume fell 14.4 percent, from 714 million to 611 million sticks.[19][8] In the September quarter VST's volume fell further, to 562 million sticks a month against 695 million a year earlier, a decline of 19.1 percent.[5] ITC has not yet reported its September quarter, so the gap cannot be measured on the same dates. ITC's board meets on 2 November 2026, and its website still lists June 2026 as the latest quarter.[20][21] What we can say is that VST's volume loss is large in absolute terms and appears larger than the market leader's in the quarter where both have figures.

That matters because a small maker has one lever, price. Management says it is following a measured pricing approach.[5] Net cigarette revenue per stick, which is revenue after excise duty divided by sticks sold, was ₹1.109 in the September quarter against ₹1.178 in June and ₹1.165 a year ago. We computed this from the reported revenue of ₹187 crore, ₹216 crore and ₹243 crore and volumes of 562, 611 and 695 million sticks a month.[5]

Leaf, the second business

Unmanufactured tobacco turned over ₹301 crore in FY26 with a profit before interest and tax of ₹34 crore, which is a margin of about 11 percent.[1] The company says global leaf output was high and prices were under downward pressure, and shipments were slow.[1] The 'others' revenue line in the ten-year table fell from ₹476.5 crore in FY25 to ₹313.7 crore in FY26, and in the September quarter it was ₹55 crore against ₹93 crore a year earlier.[1][5] Management links the latest weakness to the geopolitical situation. For a cigarette investor the useful side of cheap leaf is lower input cost: materials consumed fell from ₹786.3 crore in FY25 to ₹683.5 crore in FY26.

Who owns the shares, and why that is a signal

Mutual funds held 14.10 percent of the company in March 2024 and 4.31 percent in September 2026, while the non-institutional public holding rose from 50.4 to 60.5 percent.[30][25] Foreign investors hold 1.25 percent.[30] Funds are selling a tobacco name that has paid a high dividend, which tells you the buyer base for these shares has narrowed. That does not prove the shares are wrong at ₹189, but it means any rebound needs buyers who are not the funds that left.

2B. Company specifics, operating KPIs, and management walk-the-talk

A cigarette maker is judged on five operating readings, and we picked each for a reason tied to this business. Volume comes first, because legal volume is what a tax shock and illicit trade hit first. Net revenue per stick comes second, because it shows whether the company can pass tax and cost on to the shopper. EBITDA margin and EBITDA per stick come third, because they show what is left after the factory, leaf and distribution costs. Cash conversion comes fourth, because a tobacco company is valued on the cash it can hand back. The overhang of regulation, illicit trade and ownership comes last, because it sets the multiple the market will pay.

Each reading scores PASS (1.0 point), WATCH (0.5) or FAIL (0.0). The house rule needs at least 3.0 out of 5.0 with no more than one FAIL. VST scores 2.5 out of 5.0 with one FAIL, so Layer 2 does not qualify. The weighting is not close: three readings are mixed, one is poor and only the balance sheet is clearly good.

3. The numbers, in plain words

Over five years VST's revenue after excise grew from ₹1,178.2 crore in FY22 to ₹1,464.5 crore in FY26, a rise of 24 percent, which is about 5.6 percent a year.[1] Profit did not follow: profit after tax slipped from ₹320.2 crore to ₹292.3 crore, and the per-share figure from ₹18.9 to ₹17.2.[1] The reason is in the cost line: operating profit before other income fell from ₹381.5 crore to ₹348.7 crore, with a low of ₹234.3 crore in FY25 when leaf costs were high.[1][2] Growth in sales without growth in profit is the first thing to see in the table below.

The year to March 2026 looks strong on the surface: profit before tax rose 45 percent from ₹269.1 crore to ₹390.0 crore.[1] But FY25 had a ₹10,049 lakh gain on selling a property inside its profit (shown separately as an exceptional item), and the fourth quarter of FY26 alone earned ₹116.7 crore, which is 40 percent of the year.[1][23] No filing we retrieved splits that quarter between volume, price and the timing of the tax change. The company's own note says the sales and excise figures for periods around the tax change cannot be compared.[31] We therefore treat the last twelve months as a flattering base and read the first half of FY27 as the cleaner guide.

The first half of FY27 earned ₹83.5 crore, which is ₹4.9 a share, and the pre-tax figure includes about ₹14.1 crore from selling property, plant and equipment and ₹5.7 crore from writing back old liabilities.[5][4] Removing both, pre-tax profit is about ₹91.8 crore against ₹111.6 crore reported, and profit after tax at the reported tax rate of about 25 percent is roughly ₹68.6 crore, or about ₹4.0 a share. The trailing twelve-month profit per share is ₹15.3, but twice the clean first half gives about ₹8, which is a very different starting point for a valuation.

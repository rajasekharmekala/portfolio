1. What the company does

Coforge designs, builds and runs software and data systems for large organisations. A bank may hire it to rebuild a loan platform. An insurer may hire it to move claims systems to the cloud. An airline or travel company may hire it to run booking technology. In the quarter to 30 June 2026, 57.0 percent of revenue was billed by time and materials, which means the client pays for the hours worked, and 43.0 percent was fixed price, which means the client pays an agreed sum for an agreed result.[4]

Clients come from a handful of industries. Banking and financial services gave 24.7 percent of revenue, travel and transport 21.3 percent, healthcare and hi-tech 17.3 percent, insurance 13.6 percent, government outside India 6.0 percent and other industries 17.1 percent. By region, the Americas gave 61.8 percent, Europe, the Middle East and Africa 27.0 percent and the rest of the world 11.2 percent.[4] A rating agency put United States exposure at about 54 percent of revenue in the first half of fiscal 2026 and called it a weakness, because one economy then drives a large part of earnings.[14]

Coforge is a people business, so the numbers that matter are headcount and what each person earns. At 30 June 2026 it had 46,228 staff, of whom 34,061 were billable on technology work and 9,360 on business process work. Revenue per billable technology head was about 70,557 dollars a year, utilisation was 82.5 percent and attrition over twelve months was 10.4 percent.[4] In that quarter direct costs took 65.7 percent of revenue, selling and administration costs took 13.9 percent, and operating profit before depreciation was 20.3 percent. A further 1,117 million rupees of charges on intangible assets bought in deals brought the profit margin before interest and tax to 16.0 percent.[4]

The company was once NIIT Technologies. It was set up in April 2003 when NIIT Ltd separated its software business into a new company.[14] Its largest owner, Hulst BV, an affiliate of the private-equity firm Baring, sold its whole 26.6 percent stake in bulk deals on 24 August 2023 at 4,722 rupees a share, according to a newspaper report.[28] The March 2026 shareholding filing lists no promoter group, and the board itself describes the company as a business with no promoter since 2024.[19][16]

How acquisitions built the company

Coforge grows by buying small and medium firms and plugging them into its own sales and delivery. The chief executive says the same playbook, full and fast integration with the acquired firm's leaders leaving on day one, was used for Cigniti, SLK Global and Incessant, and is being used for Encora.[9] That record matters because the largest deal in company history closed in April 2026, and a mistake in integration would be expensive.

Encora is a US-based engineering firm owned by the private-equity houses Advent International and Warburg Pincus. Coforge agreed in December 2025 to buy it at an enterprise value of 2.35 billion dollars. The sellers took new Coforge shares worth about 1.89 billion dollars at 1,815.91 rupees a share, which was 9.38 crore shares, and a new 550 million dollar three-year loan at a fixed 4.6 percent paid off Encora's own debt.[14][11][10] Encora adds about 9,500 people, including more than 3,500 engineers in Latin America and Eastern Europe, and its results have been in Coforge's numbers from 1 May 2026.[3][10]

What the share swap did to the share count

Because the sellers were paid in shares, the number of Coforge shares rose from about 33.6 crore to about 44.3 crore, which is the reason profit per share must be judged on the new count. The new shares were listed after approval on 22 May 2026, and the sellers hold about one fifth of the company with the right to name two directors while they keep more than 15 percent.[12][3][4][19] Lock-in terms limit how fast they can sell. The filings we read do not state the length of the lock-in, so we treat its end as unknown and watch for block sales.[13] This matters for every per-share number in the report. Profit per share, the earnings multiple and our targets all use the new count, and the old count would overstate profit per share by about a third. A holder of this size can also move the price if it sells, which is why a block sale is one of our triggers.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • RSI (relative strength index) is a 0-to-100 gauge of how fast a price has moved lately; below 30 is called oversold and above 70 overbought.
  • ATR (average true range) is the typical size of one day's price swing, used to measure distance and to set a stop loss, which is a pre-set price at which a holding is sold to limit a loss.
  • EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) is operating profit before those four items, and an operating margin is operating profit as a share of revenue.
  • EPS (earnings per share) is profit divided by the number of shares, and the price-to-earnings ratio is the share price divided by EPS, which shows how many years of current profit the price pays for.
  • ROCE (return on capital employed) is pre-tax operating profit as a share of the money in the business, so it shows how well each rupee of capital is used.
  • FCF (free cash flow) is cash from operations less spending on plant and equipment.
  • Net debt is borrowings less cash, and book-to-bill is new orders divided by revenue billed in the same period.
  • A 20-day moving average, a 50-day moving average and a 200-day moving average are the average closing prices of the last 20, 50 and 200 sessions; a rising 200-day line marks a long uptrend.
  • Stage 2 is a rising markup phase where the price is above rising averages and Stage 4 is a falling markdown phase; a base is a flat resting period after a fall, a pivot is the price level that ends it and a breakout is a close above that level.
  • An entry zone is the price range in which we would buy, and a limit order is an order to buy no higher than a stated price.
  • Asymmetry or reward-to-risk is the gain to our target divided by the loss to our stop. Chapter 2 turns next to what makes the business strong or weak.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The case for the business rests on three facts. First, it has grown faster than its peers. Revenue rose from 6,432 crore rupees in fiscal 2022 to 16,403 crore in fiscal 2026, a three-year compound rate of 27 percent, against 5 to 21 percent for the five listed peers in our table.[20][29][30][31][32][33] Second, it sees work ahead. The 12-month signed order book was 2,228 million dollars at 30 June 2026, up 44.2 percent in a year, and new orders in the quarter were 691 million dollars, which is 1.17 times the quarter's revenue.[4] Third, clients stay: the repeat-business ratio was 95.7 percent, and the ten largest clients grew 18.7 percent in a year.[4]

The balance sheet is strong enough to carry the new debt. Crisil rated the bank facilities AA+ with a stable outlook in January 2026 and reaffirmed that the debt ratings were unaffected after the September 2026 board departures.[14][15] Crisil expected net debt to operating profit before depreciation not to exceed about one time in fiscal 2027.[14] Our own check at 30 June 2026 gives about 0.8 times, from net debt of 3,589 crore rupees and a quarterly operating profit before depreciation of 1,123 crore, annualised.[4]

The weaknesses are just as concrete. Most of the recent growth was bought. In the quarter to 30 June 2026 revenue grew 22.3 percent in constant currency, but the part from the existing business was only 1.1 percent on the previous quarter, or 5.2 percent after removing a 15 million dollar low-margin India government contract and a 4 million dollar data-centre sale.[4] Management says the whole industry faces price cuts as artificial intelligence makes work faster, and adds that its own tailwinds are strong.[9] We treat both statements as true and ask which will dominate in fiscal 2028.

The most unusual feature is the ownership. With no promoter, no single holder steers the board, so the board's own conduct matters more than usual. Ownership also changed fast: foreign funds held 35.00 percent in March 2024, 30.66 percent in March 2026 and 24.31 percent in June 2026, as the 9.38 crore new shares went to the Encora sellers and public holders rose from 13.16 percent to 33.07 percent.[20]

The September 2026 board reset

On 9 September 2026 the chairman resigned, and the share price fell from 1,950.00 to 1,845.00 in one session, a drop of 5.4 percent (Screener price history, archived in the run files).[15][20] On 10 and 11 September the nomination and remuneration committee chair also resigned. The board's filing says an internal audit found that the board-evaluation reports were seen only by the chairman and the committee chair, and that the chairman's own rating was the lowest and was not discussed. That director's letter spoke of differences and tension between independent and executive directors. The board called that claim unfounded and pointed out that the evaluation gave the chief executive the top score of 5 out of 5.[16] Crisil said the issues related to the information given to the board and expected operating performance to stay in line, and left the ratings unchanged.[15]

The company named Vivek Sharma interim chair to 31 January 2027, hired a search firm and on 28 September 2026 appointed Akhil Kumar Gupta as independent chairperson for five years from 29 September, subject to shareholder approval, after a search that started with 60 candidates.[16][17][18] The 11 September filing says the interim chair would lead a search for additional independent directors, and the filings we read do not say whether it is complete. We read this as a governance event that is being repaired and not as an accounting problem, because the audit concerned a board process.[16] It is still a reason to hold the position below the normal size and to name a verdict-changing trigger.

Is there a moat

A moat is a lasting edge that lets a firm keep its profit against rivals. Coforge's edge is narrow and earned, not structural. It is concentrated in a few industries, since banking, insurance and travel together gave 59.6 percent of revenue, and 49.0 percent of its technology revenue is billed from onsite teams close to the client. The evidence is the client base: 379 clients now buy more than 1 million dollars a year, up from 242 a year ago, and the top 10 are growing in double digits.[4] The 13-year Sabre contract, worth 1.56 billion dollars and signed in fiscal 2025, shows that at least one client will commit for a long time.[3] Against that, switching costs in time-and-materials work are low, and 57 percent of Q1 revenue was billed that way.[4] We therefore rate the moat as moderate, with execution as the main source of advantage. 6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every number in chapter 5 rests on a few assumptions. This chapter names the weakest one in each. It also says what evidence would show it wrong. The bear case in plain words is that AI-driven price cuts spread across IT services. That debate is the one most likely to damage the multiple.

Base profit: the 16.5% margin and 2.5% quarterly growth

The weakest assumption in the base fiscal 2028 profit is that organic growth stays near 2.5% a quarter while the margin rises about half a point. The June 2026 quarter grew only 1.1% organically. Without the two exited businesses it grew 5.2%. Management says price cuts across the industry are real.[4][9] The Encora business also has to grow with the group. Management says that starts in the next quarters, but the June deck does not yet show it. Two things would show the assumption wrong. One is organic growth below 1% on the quarter for two quarters. The other is a margin below the 15.5% group target. The December 2026 quarter is the next test. It is due around late January 2027, and the date is not confirmed.

The 33 times multiple against a 24.5 times peer median

The multiple is our weakest single input. A multiple is the number of years of profit the market is willing to pay for. Thirty-three times is a long-run median for Coforge, and the peers trade much lower. If the market reprices the whole sector to the peer median, the base value drops to about 1,760 rupees (24.5 times 71.79). That is below the close and near our zone. The 10-year median we use is Screener's own. The three-year median of 52.5 is distorted, so we do not rely on it.[21] Evidence that would show the multiple wrong is the peer group staying near 22 to 25 times while Coforge's growth lead narrows. We would also see it in the stock's own history: the one-year range of the series we archived is 29.2 to 58.5 times, so a move of several turns in a quarter is normal, and the plan must survive one.

Board and ownership: a reset that is not finished

The board lost its chairman and a committee chair. It named an interim chair and then a permanent chair. The 11 September filing says the interim chair would lead a search for additional independent directors. Crisil takes into consideration that no further churn is expected. A filing by the board and a letter by a resigning director disagree on whether there was tension.[15][16] The Encora sellers' lock-in length is not stated in the filings we read. A block sale from a holder of a fifth of the company would be a large supply of shares. Three events would show the risk real: another resignation, a failed shareholder vote on the new chair, or a large block sale by the Encora holders. Each is a trigger in chapter 7. The board's 11 September filing says its decisions were approved unanimously, and Crisil's comment was calm, but the plan is built for a second shock.

Goodwill and the fall in the reported return on capital

Goodwill and acquired intangibles now equal about 1.02 times equity. A write-down would hit reported profit and equity without touching cash. The weakest assumption is that Encora earns at least what was paid for it. Two things would show it wrong: Encora's growth lagging the group after the stated start, or an impairment note in the fiscal 2027 annual accounts. Those accounts follow the year end of 31 March 2027. An impairment is judged each year by the company and its auditors, and the test compares the value of the business with its book value. If the stock price stays high, the test is easy to pass. If the stock falls sharply, the test becomes harder, which means an impairment is most likely when the plan is already under pressure. The size of the risk is large in rupees, since goodwill is 16,373 crore, but it is a reporting risk and not a cash risk, and the 550 million dollar loan is covered by operating cash flow.[4][10]

1. What the company does & day-to-day vertical operations

Coforge is a global IT services company that began in India. Clients hire it to build and run systems, data tools, cloud work, software tests, automation and business processes [16]. It does not depend on one licensed product. Its main markets are banking, travel, insurance, healthcare, hi-tech and overseas government work. In FY26, those businesses made up26.5%,23.0%,15.0%,10.8%,7.0% and17.7% of revenue, respectively [16]. This mix spreads risk, but banking and travel still made up nearly half of sales.

Most work begins with a client problem. Coforge maps the process, prices the work, assigns a team and agrees how to measure delivery. Engineers build or update software; data teams connect systems; testers check each release; operations teams then maintain the system or run a business process [16][20][25–30]. Fees can depend on staff hours, fixed milestones or measured outcomes. The FY26 report says time-and-material revenue is booked as services are done. Fixed-price revenue is booked as work is completed [16]. The firm must estimate costs well. A signed contract does not guarantee a good margin: hiring, handover costs and client approval shape the result.

In banking, teams update core systems and work on payments, mortgages, fraud controls and risk. Mortgage programs can cover loan intake, document review, underwriting and servicing [25]. Insurance teams handle policy systems, underwriting, claims, product launches and market entry [26]. Coforge markets NEXA as an AI product with more than30 insurance assets in production. That is a company claim, not an audit of client use [26]. The value depends on accurate rules, safe links to client systems and human review for regulated decisions.

Travel work covers airline sales, booking systems, flight services, cargo and older system updates. These tools support live customer orders, so uptime and links with airline partners matter. Coforge markets Aeronova and AI travel tools. The FY26 report shows travel was23.0% of revenue, up from18.3% a year before [16][27]. In public services and healthcare, work can include citizen sites, case systems, payer tasks and patient records [28][29]. Across the business, the work follows a clear loop: find a need, plan, build, test, move to service, then support. The firm earns repeat work when client systems stay reliable and clients add new tasks.

Billing and cash are key to this model. Time-and-material work is billed for hours worked. Fixed-price and outcome contracts carry more risk because the final cost or result can differ from the plan. Coforge reported billed DSO of69 days at FY26 year-end, up from60 days a year before. Its auditor also noted fees tied to one client outcome in the receivable and contract-asset balance [16]. The post-Encora collection trend is not clear from the sources in this report. We treat cash collection as a live test, rather than assume booked sales will become cash at once.

How work turns into billings and cash

Work is sold through large change programs, small engineering jobs and ongoing service contracts. In Q2 FY27, the company said quarterly order intake was$1.04bn and the next-12-month executable order book was$2.38bn, including Encora [1][2]. These figures mean different things. Order intake is signed contract value; the executable book is the work management expects to do in the next year. Neither is a promise of sales or cash. Coforge still needs to staff the work, connect with client systems, pass tests, meet service levels and send valid invoices. Delivery is the bridge between a signed deal and EPS.

Fixed-price work can be hard to manage when the hours needed change. AI may help Coforge complete a task faster, but clients may seek a lower price too. The company can keep some savings only if contract terms and client value allow it. CFO Saurabh Goel said AI savings had started in fixed-price work and should show in Q4 FY27 margin [2]. That is a testable plan, not yet a lasting advantage. Later reports must show lower delivery cost and steady margins.

2. Competitive moat, industry structure & external learning hub

Coforge competes with global consultancies, large Indian IT firms and specialist engineering companies. It does not win on lowest cost or widest reach. It brings travel, banking and insurance teams together with engineering, data, cloud, quality and operations. FY26 service mix was Engineering 44.1%, Data and Integration 21.5%, Cloud and Infrastructure 18.4%, Intelligent Automation 8.2% and BPM 7.9% [16]. This mix can support larger change programs and managed work. Most sales still come from people delivering services. Coforge does not own a high-margin software platform that can grow without more delivery work.

Repeat work is the strongest moat evidence. FY26 repeat business was 95.5%. The top ten clients had an average relationship of more than 10 years. Coforge served 260 clients, including 61 Forbes Global 1000 companies [16]. In Q2 FY27 the top five clients made up 18.1% of sales and the top ten made up 26.4%. Management said the top 20 clients grew more than 20% YoY [2]. This is a broad base, though large accounts still matter. Switching takes effort when Coforge runs a bank transaction system, an insurer’s claims process or an airline booking system. But contracts can be re-tendered. Clients can automate work or move it to a larger vendor.

AI-led work is the main industry shift. Coforge says AI-led engineering, data and cloud made up 86% of Q2 FY27 revenue [2]. This is a service-line label. It is not revenue from standalone AI products, and it does not show that AI created 86% of sales. The commercial test is whether each employee can deliver more value, whether Coforge keeps some savings, and whether quality holds. The company reports 520 AI engagements across 225-plus clients and more than 260 agents in live operations [2]. It does not report contract value, renewals or gross profit for those programs. Counts show activity, but not the profit from it.

FY26 peer figures offer a rough margin reference. Coforge reported USD revenue growth of 29.2% and EBIT margin of 14.4%. Infosys recorded INR growth of 9.6% and operating margin of 20.3%. HCLTech posted 11.2% INR growth, 3.9% constant-currency growth and 17.2% EBIT margin. Persistent grew 17.4% in USD with 15.6% EBIT margin. TCS grew 4.6% with 25.0% operating margin [31–34]. These are annual company figures with different currencies, mixes and margin methods. Coforge grew faster on the reported FY26 figures, but its margin was lower and its scale smaller. This does not show that growth will last or that margins will catch up.

Where the competitive advantage can compound

Industry knowledge matters when it saves a client time or lowers risk. Coforge’s insurance page says NEXA includes agents for underwriting, claims, product launches and modernization [26]. Its travel page describes airline retailing, offer-and-order systems and operations [27]. Its healthcare and government pages show payer, provider and citizen-service work [28][29]. These pages show what Coforge sells; they do not prove client savings. The annual report and Q2 call let us test the claims against revenue mix, order conversion, margins, attrition and cash.

The growth engine needs account expansion and new wins. Q2 had eight large deals, and a second client passed $100 million in annualized revenue, the CEO said [2]. Large deals can lift growth, but they need hiring, transition, delivery and client sign-off. A deal adds to the moat only when the client renews or adds work without lower margins. Watch client growth, concentration and receivable days.

Is the company reaching its potential & are the CEOs walking the talk?

The record supports a qualified execution verdict. The FY26 annual report describes nine consecutive years of profitable growth and reports 20.3% revenue CAGR and 24.6% EBIT CAGR over FY17–FY26; the annual series also shows EBIT margin fell to 10.7% in FY25 before recovering to 14.4% in FY26 [16]. That is a history of growth with uneven margins, rather than an uninterrupted margin-expansion promise. Revenue grew 33.6% in FY25 and 35.9% in FY26 in rupee terms on the company’s stated comparison; acquisitions and FX conditions affect how much of that growth is organic [16].

The latest call gives two AI tests. CEO Sudhir Singh said, “Enterprises are deriving the highest AI value from redesigning and automating business workflows” [2]. That claim needs client results, not only agent counts. Coforge reports 520 engagements and more than 260 live agents but no revenue or margin per program [2]. CFO Saurabh Goel said, “AI-led efficiencies have been triggered in our fixed price contracts, which will start reflecting in our Q4 margin numbers” [2]. This is a Q4 margin forecast, not a delivered saving. Check the Q4 results for the effect on EBIT and free cash flow.

In Q1 FY27, management guided to 15.5% consolidated EBIT margin for FY27; after reporting 16% H1 margin, it raised the annual guide to 16% in Q2 [2][4]. The team also described an aspiration to exit Q4 FY27 at 17% EBIT margin and to sustain more than 17% thereafter [2]. The 16% full-year guide is a current management commitment, while the Q4 exit and FY28 margin language is an ambition. Neither has been fully delivered by the 2 November cutoff. The measure of follow-through is not only reported margin: it is whether organic growth remains healthy, fixed-price productivity reaches the P&L, and operating cash keeps pace.

For Encora, Coforge disclosed a $550 million three-year loan at 4.6% fixed interest, an effective consolidation date of 1 May 2026, and a target of 20–25% combined G&A synergies [23]. Cigniti is an earlier acquisition, and management later described expansion in the acquired clients [24]. These are capital-allocation and integration goals, not audited proof of savings. Coforge has delivered growth and accelerated the strategic scale-up; the report cannot yet award full credit for the acquired margin or debt plan because the first complete post-close annual result is still ahead.

The investment case requires a clean bridge from organic growth, utilization and AI-led contract efficiency into EBIT and free cash flow. Recheck those commitments in the next two quarterly releases.

Coforge’s operating potential is supported by a strong sales engine and industry-specific delivery, while its constraint is the conversion of acquired scale into cash earnings. High utilization is not the only path to margin growth: better fixed-price automation, account mix and lower G&A can also help. The reverse is equally true—large transitions, wage inflation, price pass-through and lower healthcare/high-tech demand can keep margins below the peer band. We will call the company’s structural potential proven only after the combined group sustains growth, a 17%+ margin trajectory and improving collections.

3. Financial trajectory & valuation sanity check

FY26 was a high-growth year, but the financial base changed materially when Encora was consolidated from 1 May 2026. The annual figures below end 31 March 2026 and therefore describe Coforge before Encora, while Q2 FY27’s reported growth includes the acquisition [16][23]. That boundary matters: reported scale, margin and earnings per share are not an organic continuation of the older base. The operating evidence should be read in three layers—annual audited history, current quarter demand/margin disclosures, and an analyst valuation model that makes its own growth and multiple assumptions explicit.

The FY26 consolidated accounts show revenue rising from ₹90,360m in FY24 to ₹164,027m in FY26, with EBIT at ₹11,615m, ₹12,942m and ₹23,645m across those years [16]. Computed margins are 12.85%, 10.72% and 14.42%, respectively; those are arithmetic from the annual report values, rounded to two decimals by code. Profit after tax was ₹8,213m, ₹8,121m and ₹15,557m. FY26 free cash flow was $135.4m, compared with $80.4m in FY25, while DSO increased to69 days [16]. This is a strong earnings rebound but the cash conversion and acquired-company accounting deserve close review.

Coforge’s current valuation is not a simple cheap-versus-expensive call. It screens at about42x trailing earnings, a premium supported by recent growth but vulnerable to slower organic demand, delayed integration gains or cash slippage. Our valuation model therefore uses reported FY26 diluted EPS as a starting input, imposes an explicit earnings growth path, and tests a lower multiple in the bear case rather than capitalizing the headline backlog as if it were profit.

The number:

Analyst base value: ₹2,400 per share over a 12-month horizon; this is a scenario output, not a consensus target.

How it was calculated:

Start with FY26 diluted EPS of ₹45.90. Base case assumes EPS growth of16.6% to ₹53.52 and a45x price-to-earnings multiple: ₹45.90 ×1.166 ×45 =₹2,408.37, rounded to ₹2,400. The bull case assumes27.5% growth and48x: ₹45.90 ×1.275 ×48 =₹2,809.08, rounded to ₹2,800. The bear case assumes EPS falls3.3% to ₹44.39 and the multiple contracts to36x: ₹45.90 ×0.967 ×36 =₹1,597.87, rounded to ₹1,600. The arithmetic was computed in code; only assumptions are analyst judgment.

6. Key risks, thesis breakers & bear case

The strongest bear case is that Coforge has acquired scale faster than it has proven combined economics. Current reported growth benefits from Encora, the Q2 organic sequential growth rate is much lower than consolidated sequential growth, DSO rose in FY26 and the stock trades at a premium earnings multiple [1][2][16][23][35]. If those facts resolve against the company, no single product launch or order intake record can protect the valuation.

Encora execution and financing

The acquisition adds engineering depth and customer relationships, but it also adds debt, integration load and 11 months of FY27 contribution. Coforge disclosed a $550m three-year loan at4.6% and targeted20–25% G&A synergies [23]. The source is dated April and is older than60 days; this report did not verify a full post-close balance sheet or realized savings schedule. Track integration expense, cash interest, client retention, restructuring, purchase-accounting amortization and debt repayment separately. A growth rate that depends on acquired revenue can disappoint even if the company continues to win deals.

Organic demand and client concentration

Q2 consolidated revenue rose53.6% in rupees year over year, but the CEO/CFO discussion put organic constant-currency growth at3.8% sequentially against10.9% consolidated CC sequential growth [1][2]. Acquisition timing and FX complicate comparison; continued end-market demand must be established in following quarters. Healthcare/high-tech declined6.4% sequentially while government outside India grew20.8%, so the vertical mix can move results sharply [2]. The top ten produced26.4% of Q2 sales. A single large client ramp, renewal delay or contract repricing could make quarterly growth and utilization volatile.

Margin promise versus contract economics

Coforge raised its FY27 EBIT margin guide to16% and expects a17% Q4 exit as AI efficiencies reach fixed-price work [2]. Customers may demand some of that efficiency as lower pricing, and implementation projects often bring transition costs before the run rate improves. Utilization at82.4% is below the peer level CFO cited, but raising it requires the right skills and client demand. If organic growth slows, capacity savings may be consumed by bench costs, wage inflation or sales investments. Failure to reach the annual guide would challenge the premium multiple; missing the Q4 exit would further weaken the >17% trajectory.

Cash conversion and valuation

FY26 billed DSO rose to69 days from60 and the auditor noted a customer with outcome-based contract assets/receivables [16]. This is not evidence of a current default, but it means billings and collection deserve more scrutiny as the business scales. FY26 cash and debt data predate the $550m acquisition loan and do not describe the combined balance sheet [16][23]. At a41.2–42.7x consumer-snapshot P/E, lower EPS or a less generous multiple could quickly compress share value [35][36]. A price below the trend-support region could coincide with either temporary de-rating or real deterioration; the next filing must distinguish between them.

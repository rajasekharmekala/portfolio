From Captive NIIT Division to Global Engineering Powerhouse

Core Investor Question: How did a captive software arm inside an Indian IT training firm evolve through private equity ownership, leadership overhaul, and three transformational acquisitions into a $2.5B+ run-rate engineering exporter?

Coforge Limited did not start as a pure-play global engineering firm. It began as the software unit of NIIT Limited, the Indian IT training pioneer founded by Rajendra Pawar and Vijay Thadani [11]. For over a decade inside NIIT, software services ran as a side engine next to domestic and global training. In January 2004, NIIT spun off the software arm into a separate company. It took the name NIIT Technologies Limited in May 2004 and listed on Indian exchanges later that year [11]. Over the next thirteen years, the firm built strong niches in Travel, Transportation and Hospitality (TTH), Insurance, and Banking & Financial Services (BFS) [1], [11]. Key clients included British Airways, Sabre, SITA, ING Group, and SEI Investments [1], [11]. Yet growth stalled by the mid-2010s. From FY15 to FY18, sales edged up from ₹2,372 Cr to ₹2,991 Cr, an 8.0% three-year CAGR, while operating profit stayed near ₹480–502 Cr [1].

The turning point came in May 2017 when the board hired Sudhir Singh as CEO and Executive Director [11], [12]. Singh, a veteran of Infosys and Genpact, spotted two flaws holding the firm back [11]. First, its sales culture focused on safe account upkeep and rarely chased large, multi-year transformation deals [11]. Second, its service mix leaned on legacy upkeep rather than cloud, API integration, and digital automation [11]. Over the next two years, Singh upgraded senior vertical heads and built a disciplined large-deal team [22]. He also tied pay to new logo wins and order-book growth [22]. Sales growth jumped from 7% in FY18 to 23% in FY19 at ₹3,676 Cr, and operating profit rose 29.5% to ₹650 Cr [1].

This turnaround sparked a major shift in ownership. In 2019, founders Pawar and Thadani sold their controlling stake to Baring Private Equity Asia (BPEA) [11], [12]. BPEA later merged with Swedish firm EQT AB to form BPEA EQT [11], [12]. Under private equity backing, the company dropped its old name in August 2020 and became Coforge Limited [11]. The name blends 'Collaboration' and 'Forge' to mark a clean break from its training parent [11]. From 2020 to August 2023, BPEA EQT sold its entire stake through block deals. Today, Coforge has zero promoter holding and 100% public and institutional ownership, though the April 2026 Encora share swap placed a ~21.8% stake (93.8M shares at ₹1,815.91) and two non-executive board seats with private equity sponsors Advent International and Warburg Pincus. As of September 2026, FIIs hold 24.75%, DIIs hold 43.79%, and public investors (including the Encora rollover block) hold 31.47% across 3,12,271 shareholders [1], [12]. Governance also entered a transition phase in September 2026 after Chairman O.P. Bhatt and NRC Chair D.K. Singh abruptly resigned following an internal KPMG audit of board evaluation report disclosures; Independent Director Vivek Sharma is serving as Interim Chairperson through January 31, 2027, while a November–December 2026 postal ballot seeks shareholder approval for Akhil Kumar Gupta as an Independent Director.

Coforge evolved across three distinct phases: stagnant NIIT captive (2004–2017), organic + tuck-in digital acceleration under Sudhir Singh & BPEA EQT (2017–2023), and large-scale platform consolidation via Cigniti and Encora (2024–2026).

Coforge's history also spans four clear business and margin cycles over the past decade [1]. In the first cycle (FY15–FY18), top-line growth stayed slow at +3% to +7% a year. Steady 17%–18% operating margins masked that slowdown as legacy contracts offset small digital wins [1]. The second cycle (FY19–FY21) tested business resilience. Sales grew 23% in FY19 and 14% in FY20 before COVID-19 hit global air travel. At the time, travel made up nearly 30% of Coforge's sales. Even as flight volumes plunged in FY21, Coforge grew sales 11.4% to ₹4,663 Cr and held margins at 17% [1]. BFS, Insurance, and tight cost controls cushioned the shock [1].

The third cycle (FY22–FY24) rode the post-pandemic cloud wave and its later slowdown. Sales jumped 38% in FY22 to ₹6,432 Cr and 25% in FY23 to ₹8,015 Cr as clients modernized core apps and Coforge added SLK Global [1], [10]. When IT budgets tightened in FY24, growth cooled to 12% at ₹9,009 Cr and operating margin dipped from 17% to 16% [1]. Even so, Coforge beat Tier-1 Indian IT peers that stalled in low single digits [1], [22]. The fourth cycle (FY25–FY27) marks an aggressive scale-up. Sales rose 34% in FY25 to ₹12,073 Cr and 36% in FY26 to ₹16,403 Cr with Cigniti added [1]. After the $2.35B Encora deal closed on May 1, 2026, TTM sales hit ₹20,380 Cr and Q2 FY27 sales reached ₹6,171 Cr, a $2.6B+ run-rate [1], [5], [6].

Portfolio Discipline: Why Coforge Sold AdvantageGo

Knowing what not to own is key to Coforge's strategy. In April 2025, Coforge sold its 30-year-old insurance software unit, AdvantageGo, to Sapiens UK for GBP 43 million [9]. AdvantageGo held a solid niche in the Lloyd's and London specialty insurance market [9]. However, owning a software product created conflict when Coforge pitched integration work to rival P&C software vendors [9]. Selling AdvantageGo turned a rival into a sales partner and kept Coforge focused on pure-play engineering services [9].

The Global Delivery Matrix, Billing Cycle, and the ₹100 Revenue Waterfall

Core Investor Question: How does Coforge source, staff, and deliver multi-year engineering contracts across 33 countries, and how much of every ₹100 billed actually converts into free cash flow?

An IT firm's supply chain is not factories or freight. It is engineering talent, time-zone fit, delivery-center costs, and cash discipline. As of Q2 FY27, Coforge runs delivery hubs in 33 countries with 47,365 total staff [4], [19]. That headcount includes 34,628 IT billable staff and 10,024 BPS billable staff [4], [19]. For years, Indian IT exporters used a two-node model. They placed senior architects onsite in the US or Europe and ran large offshore teams in Noida, Pune, Hyderabad, and Bengaluru. In May 2026, the Encora deal added a third node: thousands of nearshore engineers in Colombia, Mexico, Costa Rica, and Brazil [6]. Those teams work directly in US time zones [6].

This tri-shore model splits Q2 FY27 IT sales into 49.5% onsite and 50.5% offshore India, including SLK Global's Tier-3 hubs [10], [19]. Adding LatAm nearshore lifted the reported onsite share from 47.7% in Q2 FY26 to 49.5% in Q2 FY27 [10], [19]. Nearshore LatAm work earns higher billing rates than India offshore while costing less than US onshore engineers [6]. That lets Coforge staff agile product pods for Silicon Valley and Fortune 500 clients while protecting gross margin [6]. On sales, client partners mine top accounts, where Top 5 clients give 18.1% and Top 10 give 26.4% of Q2 FY27 sales [5], [19]. A separate large-deal team chases $20M–$230M multi-year contracts [5], [19].

IT unit economics rest on two drivers: utilization-adjusted gross margin and the cash collection cycle. In fixed-price deals, firms book sales as project milestones are met [1], [22]. That creates unbilled revenue until the client signs off and an invoice goes out [1], [22]. Once billed, standard enterprise payment terms run 60 to 90 days. Screener data shows Coforge's annual Debtor Days rose from 73 days in FY23 and FY24 to 78 days in FY25 and 88 days in FY26, while Working Capital Days climbed from 13 in FY23 to 25 in FY26 [1]. More importantly, Q2 FY27 disclosures reveal that Total DSO (including billed receivables, unbilled revenue, and contract assets) stretched a further 8 days sequentially to 110 days—split into 65 billed days, 26 unbilled days, and 19 contract-asset days—as net unbilled revenue jumped from ₹2,100 Cr to ₹2,600 Cr. Management attributed this unbilled build-up to invoicing and collection lags while migrating contracts from Encora's US entity to India under legal-entity rationalization; shrinking Total DSO back down is now the single highest-priority working-capital watchpoint.

Two cross-border policy and currency mechanics also shape unit profitability. First, on US immigration policy, Coforge derives roughly 55% of revenue from the US—meaningfully below mid-cap peers such as Persistent Systems, LTIMindtree, and Mphasis at 75%–80%—and Encora's Latin American nearshore hubs buffer Coforge against the $100,000 new US H-1B visa fee (which broker estimates place at a theoretical 50–150 bps industry margin drag if fully absorbed, and materially lower in practice). Second, on foreign exchange, legacy forward hedges generated $10.2 million of FX hedge losses in Q2 FY27 (similar to Q1 FY27), weighing on non-operating income; management expects hedge losses to taper to $7.1–7.5 million in Q3 FY27 and $1.2 million in Q4 FY27 before dropping to zero thereafter, aided by the natural hedge of its $550 million USD term loan.

Moat vs. Fragility: Why Domain Specialists Win—and How Leveraged IT Roll-Ups Break

Core Investor Question: What protects Coforge from Tier-1 scale giants and GenAI code-generation deflation, and what do historical IT services M&A twins teach us about integration risk?

The global IT services market is split into two clear tiers [21]. At the top, Tier-1 giants like Accenture, TCS, Infosys, and Cognizant chase massive cost-cut and ERP deals. At the bottom, thousands of small staffing shops compete mainly on hourly rates. Coforge sits in the sweet spot between them as a domain-led mid-tier engineering specialist [2], [20]. Its clients have complex needs that small shops cannot serve. At the same time, they want faster senior focus and deeper vertical skill than huge Tier-1 firms offer [2], [20].

Coforge's moat rests on three clear pillars [2], [16], [19]. First, Embedded Vertical Switching Costs lock in mission-critical systems for airlines like British Airways, Sabre, and SITA [1], [2]. The same holds for P&C insurance stacks on Duck Creek and Guidewire, which clients rarely swap for a 5% fee cut [1], [2]. Second, Proprietary AI & Automation Accelerators like Quasar, Code Insight AI, and AgentSphere speed up code upgrades [2], [16]. They also shift contracts from hourly staffing toward outcome-based engineering [2], [16]. Third, Partner Ecosystem Alignment keeps Coforge conflict-free after the 2025 AdvantageGo sale [2], [9]. It partners cleanly with MuleSoft, Snowflake, Databricks, ServiceNow, and Sapiens [2], [9].

1. What the company does

Coforge is an Indian technology services company. Large organisations pay it to build, modernise and run the software behind their business: a bank's lending system, an insurer's policy platform, an airline's booking and ticketing engine, a hospital group's data pipelines. In the quarter to 30 June 2026 it earned revenue of US$592.2 million, or ₹5,527.7 crore, and it ended the quarter with 46,228 people.[3][2] Reported sales rose 33 percent in dollars, but US$100.7 million of that quarter's revenue came from two months of Encora, which was bought in April. The part Coforge built on its own grew far less, a point chapter 3 works through.[4]

Most of the work is billed by effort. Time-and-materials contracts, where the client pays for the people and hours used, were 57.0 percent of revenue in the June 2026 quarter, and fixed-price contracts were 43.0 percent.[3] Management says only 6 to 7 percent of revenue is priced on outcomes so far.[42] That matters because the margin is the gap between what a client pays per engineer and what the engineer costs. People are the biggest bill: employee benefits were ₹92,161 million in FY26, or 56.2 percent of revenue, against 59.8 percent in FY25.[11] If AI tools let the same work be done with fewer people, the company can keep the saving as margin or hand part of it to the client. Coforge says the saving is showing up in its own margin, and its first proposals priced per AI agent rather than per person are still in negotiation.[3]

The company sells to a short list of industries and says it knows each one deeply. Banking and financial services, insurance and travel together made up 59.6 percent of revenue in the June 2026 quarter, down from 63.1 percent in the March quarter because Encora added a large healthcare and hi-tech business.[3] By region, the Americas gave 61.8 percent of revenue, Europe, the Middle East and Africa 27.0 percent, and the rest of the world 11.2 percent.[3] The credit rating agency CRISIL lists "high geographical concentration in the US" among the company's weaknesses, which links to the visa and demand risks in chapter 6.[17]

How the company grew: two purchases in two years

Cigniti is a software-testing company that Coforge bought about two years ago and fully merged into itself, with the final court order filed on 30 April 2026.[15] Management says Cigniti's EBITDA margin (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, as a share of sales) rose from about 11 percent to about 19 percent in five to six quarters (the May 2026 call recalled the starting margin as 12 percent), and that its two largest clients grew from about $25 million a year to about $75 million.[15] This is the proof point management uses to argue it can integrate Encora the same way.

Encora is a US-founded engineering firm that Coforge agreed to buy on 26 December 2025 at an enterprise value of US$2.35 billion. Its revenue for FY26 was expected to be US$600 million at an adjusted earnings margin of about 19 percent.[13] Coforge paid about US$1.89 billion of the price in new shares, and borrowed US$550 million for three years at a fixed 4.6 percent to repay Encora's own debt, after dropping plans for a share sale to investors.[14] The deal closed on 23 April 2026, and Encora's results have been included from 1 May 2026.[14] The new shares were issued at ₹1,815.91 each. The accounts value them at the closing-date fair value of ₹1,220.60 a share, so the share part of the price is booked at US$1,220 million, not US$1.89 billion. With a US$270 million cash infusion, total purchase consideration is US$1,490 million, and goodwill is ₹120,890 million.[3][4]

Who owns the company

Coforge has no promoter, meaning no founding family or parent company with a controlling stake. The data provider shows the promoter holding at 0.00 percent from March 2024 onward.[1] At 30 June 2026 foreign investors held 24.31 percent, Indian institutions held 42.62 percent and the public held 33.07 percent.[1] The jump in the public share, from 13.16 percent in March, matches in size the 9,37,96,508 shares, equal to 21.83 percent of the company, that were issued on 23 April 2026 to two Encora seller vehicles, Encora Holdco and AI Altius Parent.[26] Two Advent private-equity executives joined the Coforge board that day.[27] Mutual funds are trimming: HDFC Mutual Fund said it cut its holding to 4.30 percent on 6 October 2026.[1]

The leadership team has run the company for ten years. The chief executive, Sudhir Singh, says that over the last nine years revenue grew at 21.7 percent a year, operating profit before interest and tax at 24.6 percent a year and profit after tax at 24.1 percent a year.[4] Those are the company's own figures and we have not rebuilt them from nine sets of accounts, but the five-year tables in chapter 3 point the same way.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Constant currency: growth after removing the effect of exchange-rate changes, so a falling rupee does not flatter the result.
  • Executable order book: signed work that the company expects to bill over the next twelve months. It is a visibility measure, not cash in hand.
  • Organic growth: growth the company produces without counting the revenue of firms it has just bought.
  • Time and materials: a contract where the client pays for the people and hours used. Fixed price: a contract where the client pays an agreed sum for an agreed result.
  • EBIT: profit before interest and tax.
  • G&A: general and administrative cost, meaning head-office and support cost that is not tied to one client project.
  • Mod Squad: Coforge's name for a human-agent pod, a small team of engineers and AI agents that delivers a piece of work together.[3]
  • Goodwill: the amount paid for a business above the value of its identifiable assets. It sits on the balance sheet and can be written down if the purchase disappoints.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The competitive position rests on focus, speed and client closeness, and the evidence is mostly in numbers the company cannot easily fake. In the June 2026 quarter 95.7 percent of revenue came from repeat clients, and the last twelve months' staff attrition was 10.4 percent.[3] For comparison, TCS, the largest Indian peer, reported attrition of 13.3 percent for the September 2026 quarter.[33] Low attrition matters in a people business because each leaver takes client knowledge out of the building and costs money to replace.

Growth has run ahead of the industry. In FY26 revenue rose 29.2 percent in dollars to US$1,870 million.[6] In the September 2026 quarter, TCS grew 0.5 percent in constant currency from the previous quarter and 11.2 percent in rupees over a year.[33] Industry body Nasscom put India's total technology revenue growth for FY26 at 6.1 percent, with headcount up only 2.3 percent, and said mid-sized firms are outpacing the largest ones.[35][34] Coforge's lead, however, is smaller than the headline suggests once Encora is removed, as the next chapters show.

The weak points are specific. Coforge is much smaller than the top tier. CRISIL, the rating agency, rates its bank facilities AA+ with a stable outlook, and reaffirmed that rating in January 2026. In September 2026 it said the ratings were unaffected by the board resignations.[16][17] It calls the company's scale "relatively modest" next to tier-1 providers, and lists US concentration, intense competition and acquisition-integration risk as the offsets to its strengths.[17] The company has lost some sales through its own choices: in the June quarter it walked away from a US$15 million India government portfolio and a data-centre asset that had added US$4 million of revenue, so that reported organic growth was only 1.1 percent, or 5.2 percent without those exits.[3] One of its top three banking clients did not grow in FY26, which held banking growth to 12 percent in a year when healthcare and hi-tech grew 98 percent and travel grew 62 percent.[7]

Customers and concentration

Coforge is not dependent on one buyer. The top five clients gave 18.0 percent of revenue in the June 2026 quarter and the top ten gave 26.1 percent, down from 31.4 percent in March because Encora added many mid-sized clients.[3] One client pays more than US$100 million a year and three pay between US$50 million and US$100 million.[4] In FY26 the ten biggest accounts grew 40.4 percent and made up 30.8 percent of revenue. Five were banks or financial firms, three were travel companies, one an insurer and one a public body.[7]

What this means for an owner: the concentration is moderate and spread across industries, so a single contract loss is unlikely to break the business. The more relevant risk is sector-wide, since most clients are in banking, insurance and travel, which all react to the same interest-rate and fuel-price swings. In May 2026 management said the revenue budgeted from Spirit Airlines, a US airline then in the news, was about 0.1 percent of FY27 revenue, so that single airline exposure is small.[7]

What AI changes for this business

The market has been nervous that AI will cut the hours clients buy. The Nifty IT index was down about 17 percent in 2026 to late August, with Coforge among the stocks that had fallen more than 20 percent that year.[38] Another market note put the index almost 40 percent below its December 2024 peak.[39] The Coforge answer is that cheap code still has to be secured, maintained and governed, and that this creates recurring work.[7] That is a claim, not yet a result. What we can check is what management does with its own costs: it has held general and administrative costs flat in absolute terms by using AI inside the company, and its first proposals priced by AI agent are still in negotiation.[7][3]

Our reading is mixed. The cost side of the AI argument is already visible in the margin, which is the good news. The revenue side is unproven, because Coforge's organic growth was 1.1 percent in the latest quarter and the company itself says efficiency-led price deflation is real while new work in modernisation, data and security is also real.[4] Chapter 2B scores growth quality for exactly this reason.

3. The numbers, in plain words

Five years of accounts tell a clear story. Sales grew from ₹6,432 crore in FY22 to ₹16,403 crore in FY26, which is about 26.4 percent a year, and the trailing twelve months to June 2026 reached ₹18,195 crore.[1] Operating profit, the profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, rose from ₹1,102 crore to ₹2,936 crore. The operating margin, which is operating profit as a share of sales, dipped to 14 percent in FY25, a year with acquisition and integration costs. It then recovered to 18 percent in FY26.[1] The FY26 sales, cash flow and profit figures in the data provider's table match the company's audited statements.[11][12]

Cash and debt have improved with scale. One judgement deserves a plain statement. A stop placed under the swing low alone, at ₹1,662.17, would give an asymmetry of 3.92 and the stock would pass the floor. We use the lower stop because that is what the Core rule says, and because a Core position is meant to ride out ordinary swings of a rising 50-day average. The next review should keep that sensitivity in view.[41]

Layer 3 is the last of our three tests. It decides where to buy and how much to buy. It has three parts. The first is the support ladder, a set of price bands measured in ATR above the nearest support, which says how close to support a purchase must be. A full-size position is allowed within 0.6 ATR of support, a standard position within 1.2 ATR, a small limit-order starter within 1.6 ATR, and nothing beyond that. The second part is sizing by risk, which some investors call portfolio heat. Each position gets a risk budget, counted in basis points of net worth, where one basis point is 0.01 percent. The size is chosen so that being stopped out loses about that share of net worth, which means a wider stop gives a smaller position. The conviction tier sets the budget. TIER_0 means no position and a budget of zero. TIER_3 is 50 basis points, TIER_2 is 75 and TIER_1, the highest conviction, is 100. A name moves up a tier as the evidence gets stronger and the open risks close. The third part is the let-winners-run rule. It says we do not trim a rising stock with sound fundamentals only because its RSI passes 70 or its weight passes 15 percent. We trail the stop up under the rising 21-day exponential average or the 50-day average, and we let the weight grow to a ceiling of about 22 percent of the portfolio.

Applied to today's price, the ladder gives no purchase. The 50-day average of ₹1,853.08 is the nearest support, and the price is 1.65 ATR above it. The full-size band reaches ₹1,888.98, the standard band ₹1,924.88 and the limit-order band ₹1,948.81. The stock is above the last band by ₹3.[41] The relative strength index is 64.31, below 70. If the asymmetry test had passed, this posture alone would give WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT, so the two tests agree that the price is too high today.

The verdict is REJECTED, so the tier is TIER_0 and the size is zero today. If the name re-qualifies, we would start in TIER_3 until the board is rebuilt. A 50-basis-point risk budget divided by a 21.6 percent stop distance from ₹1,872 gives a weight of 2.31 percent. That is about ₹1.06 lakh on a net worth of ₹45.74 lakh. TIER_2 (75 basis points, 3.47 percent) would be earned only after the permanent chair and two new independent directors are in place.[41] The company is not held, so there is no holding audit and no profit to protect, and the let-winners-run rule would apply only after a purchase.

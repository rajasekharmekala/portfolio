Terms and the three layers

The first measure is ROCE (return on capital employed), which divides operating profit by the money the business uses, including borrowed money. Think of it as the rent a landlord earns on the full cost of a building. A ROCE of 59 percent means each rupee of capital produced about 59 paise of operating profit in the year.

EPS (earnings per share) is the company's profit divided by its number of shares. It turns headline profit into a figure per share, which is the number a share price is compared with when investors judge value. A forward EPS is the same figure for a future year, and it is only a forecast.

ATR (average true range) is the average daily price move over fourteen sessions. It is the stock's normal wobble. This dossier uses it as the unit for every distance, so a stop placed one ATR below a price sits one normal day's move away.

RSI (relative strength index) is a speed gauge from 0 to 100 that compares recent gains with recent losses. Above 70 the stock is called extended, and below 30 it is called oversold. A reading of 41 is neutral to weak.

A base is a sideways stretch of trading after a decline, when selling has slowed and the price builds a floor. A Stage 1 stock is still building its base.

Stage 2 means the markup phase, when the price sits above a rising long-term average. The Core rule accepts a Stage 2 stock, or a Stage 1 stock that has broken out of its base.

Stage 4 is the decline phase, when the price sits below a falling long-term average. A Stage 4 breakdown is an automatic failure of the technical floor.

A pivot is the price level a stock must clear to confirm a move out of its base. In this file the gate measures distance from the base reference, which is the 63-day base low of INR 648.

A breakout is a close above the top of a base on heavier volume than usual. A breakout is the event that turns a Stage 1 stock into a candidate.

A retest is a return of the price to a level it has just broken out through, which then holds as support. A retest that holds is the second route to an allocation under the gate.

A limit order is an instruction to buy only at or below a stated price. Under the reduced band of the Layer 3 ladder, it is the only permitted way to enter.

An entry zone is the price range in which a buy order would be placed. This file cannot name one for this stock, because the verdict is REJECTED.

Asymmetry is the ratio of reward to risk: the distance from the price to the target, divided by the distance from the price to the stop. The Core floor is 3.0, which means the reward must be at least three times the risk. That minimum is the asymmetry floor.

A measured move is a target set by adding the height of a base to its breakout level. It is one of the two anchors the rules accept for a target.

Operating margin is operating profit as a share of sales, before interest and tax. A margin that falls from 44 percent to 12 percent in two quarters shows how far the operating result can swing, and the sources read do not say why.

Debt-to-equity is borrowings divided by shareholders' money. A value of 0.03 means the company owes very little relative to what its owners have put in.

Price-to-earnings (P/E) is the share price divided by earnings per share, so it shows how many years of current earnings the market is paying for. A P/E of 25 means investors pay 25 rupees for each rupee of annual earnings.

Guidance is the company's own forecast of next year's sales or profit, given on a call or in a filing. Guidance is a claim to be verified, not a fact.

Layer 1: the financial floors

Layer 1 is where a business must show that it earns a good return on the money put into it, does not carry much debt, and turns its profit into cash. These are the three financial floors. A company can have a fine story and still fail them, because high profit that never becomes cash is a warning sign. Cash conversion is the main test. Companies can report profit through revenue recognition timing or accruals, and cash does not lie in the same way. A three-year cumulative cash-to-profit ratio below 0.70 means more than 30 percent of reported profit did not arrive as cash over the period, which is a reason to hold back.

The return on capital employed (ROCE) measures how much operating profit the company earns on the capital that funds it, including debt. Screener's FY26 ratio table shows 59 percent, up from 27 percent in FY25 and 14 percent in FY23. The jump is large. It follows the profit surge in the September 2025 quarter. Return on equity was 45 percent in FY26 on the same source. Both are far above the 15 percent floor. A reader should notice that the company's ROCE was in single digits for most of the period before FY23, so the current level is recent and may be partly cyclical. The business has not sustained a 59 percent return for long. StockFin's 12 October snapshot shows a ROCE of 46.4 percent and an ROE of 35.3 percent, against Screener's 59 and 45 percent. This file uses Screener's figures because they are the consolidated series that matches the rest of the financial table, and both sources clear the 15 percent ROCE floor by a wide margin, so the disagreement does not change the Layer 1 result.

The debt-to-equity ratio is calculated from the balance sheet. Screener's consolidated FY26 figures show borrowings of INR 67 crore against reserves of INR 2,187 crore and equity capital of INR 28 crore. Dividing 67 by 2,215 gives 0.03, which is far below the 0.70 ceiling. Yahoo reports the same position as a total debt-to-equity ratio of 3.02 percent at the June 2026 quarter, which is a consistency check on the arithmetic. The company is effectively debt-free, so the leverage floor is not a concern. The more relevant question for this name is cash, not debt.

Valuation and the asymmetry floor

The asymmetry floor compares the reward and risk of a position. The reward is the gap between the current price and a target. The risk is the gap between the current price and the stop. The ratio of the two must be at least 3.0 for a Core name. The rules require the target to be anchored to something you can check: forward earnings multiplied by a three-year median price-to-earnings multiple, or a measured move of the chart base. An arbitrary target, such as a round number or a broker's guess that is not published, does not count. If the target cannot be verified, the floor fails. That is the rule, and this dossier applies it without exception.

The stop for a Core name is set by a formula: the lower of the recent swing low and the 50- and 200-day averages, minus one ATR. Glossary

This glossary defines each term used in the dossier, with a short analogy where one helps. The terms are listed in the order a reader meets them, so the list can be used as a reference while reading.

ROCE (return on capital employed)
Operating profit divided by the money the company uses to run its business, including debt. It is like the rent a landlord earns on the total cost of the building. A figure of 59 percent means each rupee of capital produced about 59 paise of operating profit in FY26.
ROE (return on equity)
Net profit divided by shareholders' money. It measures return to owners alone, so it can be higher than ROCE when the company borrows.
Debt-to-equity
Borrowings divided by shareholders' money. A value of 0.03 means the company owes very little relative to what its owners have put in.
CFO / PAT
Cash from operations divided by net profit. It tests whether reported profit becomes cash. A ratio of 1.0 means full conversion.
Stage 1, 2, 3, 4
The four phases of a stock's price cycle: basing, markup, top, and decline. Stage 2 is the uptrend phase that the Core rule prefers.
Moving average
The average closing price over a set number of days, a smoothed trend line. The 50-day covers about two and a half months and the 200-day about nine months.
ATR (average true range)
The average daily price range over fourteen days. It is the stock's normal wobble and is used as the unit for all distances in this dossier.
RSI (relative strength index)
A speed gauge from 0 to 100 that measures recent gains against recent losses. Above 70 is extended, below 30 is oversold, and 41 is neutral to weak.
Relative strength (RS)
The stock's return minus the benchmark's return over a window. Positive means the stock is outperforming the Nifty 50.
Momentum gate
A code-computed screen with Test A for stocks already in an uptrend and Test B for stocks entering one. Failing both means no allocation.
Asymmetry ratio
The reward (target minus price) divided by the risk (price minus stop). The Core floor is 3.0.
Stop-loss
The price at which a position is exited under the rules. For Core names it is the lower of the swing low and the 50- and 200-day averages, minus one ATR.
Promoter pledge
Shares the founding group has used as collateral for loans. A high pledge can force sales if the price falls.
Regulation 30 filing
A disclosure that listed Indian companies must file with exchanges about material events such as orders, ratings and board decisions.

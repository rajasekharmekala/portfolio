That order prevents a plausible narrative from becoming an unsupported conclusion. The consequence is a reversible decision: retain the rejection today, define the trigger, and reopen the work when a filing supplies the missing proof. This discipline also protects against hindsight because the same threshold applies whether the stock rises or falls. The relevant question is not whether the company can succeed in a broad sense. It is whether the current evidence clears the exact hurdle required for this shortlist. That is why the report distinguishes facts, calculations, monitoring levels and unresolved gaps rather than merging them into a single score.

Scenarios are not forecasts. They are structured tests that help the reader distinguish a change in facts from a change in mood. In the recovery case, commissioned assets produce repeat aerospace or defence work, collection days shorten and operating cash becomes consistently positive. In the middle case, sales grow but cash conversion remains uneven, leaving the company interesting but not investable under the hard gate. In the downside case, plant and inventory arrive before customer demand, so profit may remain positive while ROCE and cash deteriorate. Each scenario points to the same evidence: utilisation, acceptance, receivable ageing, operating cash flow and return on capital. The market price can move before those facts appear, but the underwriting decision should not. This is especially important when the stock trades close to its high and at a demanding earnings multiple. The report therefore leaves the target unverified rather than choosing a convenient number. Three operating scenarios keep the decision tied to observable evidence. In the middle case, sales continue to grow but cash conversion remains uneven. The company may remain strategically interesting, yet the portfolio action stays outside the shortlist. The evidence that would change the middle case is three consecutive reporting periods with improving CFO, debtor days and asset utilisation.

In the downside case, capacity arrives before demand, customer approvals take longer, or receivables stretch further. Profit can then remain positive while free cash flow and ROCE weaken.

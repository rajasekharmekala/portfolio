1. What the company does

Global Health Limited runs the Medanta hospitals. Dr. Naresh Trehan, a heart surgeon, founded the group and is its chairman and managing director[4][5]. The flagship Medicity in Gurgaon opened in 2009 with 1,440 beds[4]. Indore, Ranchi, Lucknow, Patna and Noida followed. On 30 June 2026 the six hospitals had 3,737 installed beds, and the group engages about 2,480 doctors[3][4]. The shares listed on 16 November 2022[4].

A hospital earns money when beds are full and each occupied bed earns a good price. In the June 2026 quarter inpatient care was 82 percent of hospital revenue and outpatient care 18 percent[4]. The group's yardstick is ARPOB, the average revenue per occupied bed per day. It was ₹70,244, up 5.5 percent[3]. Sales were ₹4,410 crore in FY26, and the operating margin (the share of each rupee of sales left after running costs) was 21 percent[1]. EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) was ₹315.3 crore in the June 2026 quarter, a 23.8 percent margin[3]. Profit for each share, called EPS (earnings per share), was ₹20.70 in FY26[1]. ROCE (return on capital employed), the profit earned on the money tied up in the business, was 17.4 percent[1]. Free cash flow (FCF) is the cash left after running the hospitals and paying for buildings and equipment, and it was minus ₹123 crore in FY26[1].

Who pays matters, because each payer negotiates a different price. Of the June 2026 inpatient bill, 55 percent came from patients paying cash and 26 percent from insurers, which pay through claims administrators called TPAs. Government employee schemes such as CGHS paid 11 percent, public-sector and corporate contracts 5 percent and others 3 percent[4]. Patients from abroad were 6 percent of revenue. In Lucknow and Patna management says it has not raised tariffs, so growth there comes from more patients, not higher prices[5].

Medanta reports two groups of hospitals. Cluster 1 is Gurugram, Indore and Ranchi. It earned ₹771.5 crore of income in the June 2026 quarter, up 10.1 percent, at a 24.1 percent EBITDA margin[4]. Cluster 2 is Lucknow, Patna and Noida. Its income was ₹498.3 crore, up 54.8 percent, and its EBITDA margin excluding Noida was 32.0 percent[4]. Noida, the newest, had 433 of 550 planned beds and lost ₹4.9 crore at the EBITDA level in the quarter, against ₹23.6 crore in March[3][4]. The table lists each hospital and the one thing to watch.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • ARPOB: average revenue per occupied bed per day, meaning hospital revenue excluding outpatient pharmacy and other income, divided by occupied bed days[4].
  • ALOS: average length of stay in days. A shorter stay frees the bed for the next patient, which lifts ARPOB if the cases are rich.
  • Occupancy: occupied beds divided by census beds, meaning the beds available for overnight stay.
  • TPA: third-party administrator, the claims desk an insurer uses to approve and pay a hospital bill.
  • CGHS: the Central Government Health Scheme, which covers government employees and pensioners at rates the government sets.
  • Build-to-suit lease: a developer builds the building to Medanta's design, and Medanta rents it and fits out the equipment[6].
  • Trade margin: the gap between what a hospital or pharmacy pays for a medicine and the maximum retail price it charges, which regulators can cap; chapter 2 explains why that matters now.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

Medanta's first strength is its doctors. Management says it sees very negligible to no attrition at senior clinical levels[5]. It added more than 70 doctors in the June 2026 quarter, over 50 of them senior[3]. Newsweek named the Gurugram hospital India's best for the seventh year running[6]. Well-known surgeons let a chain fill beds without cutting prices, which is why inpatients grew 27.7 percent and outpatient visits 34.5 percent while revenue per patient stayed flat[4].

The second strength is a record of filling new hospitals fast. Management said in May 2025 that Patna and Lucknow reached EBITDA breakeven within their first year, and added that this was fortunate and not to be planned for[10]. Noida's EBITDA loss peaked at ₹32.0 crore in the December 2025 quarter, then fell to ₹23.6 crore in March and ₹4.9 crore in June[6][3]. The group gives no breakeven date but expects it earlier than it said in May[5].

The third strength is the balance sheet. At 31 March 2026 the group held ₹1,130 crore of cash and bank balances against ₹631 crore of bank borrowings and ₹559 crore of lease liabilities[2]. Debt-to-equity, borrowings divided by shareholders' equity, was 0.30 counting leases and 0.16 counting bank debt only. CRISIL upgraded the bank-loan rating from AA-/Positive to AA/Stable on 16 December 2025[2].

The weaknesses are as concrete. Staff pay and doctor fees rose from 35.8 to 39.8 percent of revenue between FY25 and FY26, because Noida hired before patients arrived[2]. ROCE has slipped from 20 to 17 percent as Noida entered capital employed[1]. The Gurugram hospital is full, so its growth depends on operating rooms and cath labs, not beds[5]. Insurers negotiate hard, and management said it would negotiate on price[7]. And the whole sector faces a pricing inquiry, described below.

Competition, margins and valuation against peers

Medanta competes with Max Healthcare, Fortis, Apollo, Narayana and KIMS for doctors and patients. Its latest quarterly operating margin of 22 percent is below Max at 25 percent, level with Fortis at 21 and above Apollo (16), Narayana (17) and KIMS (19)[1][26]. Its return on capital of 17.4 percent matches Apollo's and beats the other four[1][28]. The valuation is where Medanta stands out: 55.6 times trailing earnings against Narayana at 39.0 and the other three between 50 and 53[1][29]. A buyer pays a premium for three-year profit growth of 19 percent a year, which is middle of the pack, so the price depends on the new hospitals delivering. The pricing inquiry since 30 September

On 30 September hospital shares fell after the Supreme Court raised concerns about medicine mark-ups, and the BSE Hospitals Index lost 5 percent[17][23]. A news report places the hearing on 29 September, when the court flagged a medicine costing ₹2,700 and billed at ₹27,000, and voiced concern about hospitals forcing patients to buy from their own pharmacies[18]. The court reportedly suggested considering a 16 percent margin limit on medicines, which depends on government action[17]. Medanta fell 3.54 percent on 30 September on our price series and 8.13 percent over the week to 1 October[19][31]. The government is also reviewing trade margins on devices and consumables[19].

Brokers sized the risk for the sector, not for Medanta. Jefferies said the categories at issue are 15 to 20 percent of hospital revenue and that price-cap scenarios with no pass-through would cut EBITDA by 2 to 5 percent[18]. A Macquarie analyst put pharmacy at 20 to 25 percent of hospital revenue and said a markup cut from 50 to 30 percent could put a high-teens share of EBITDA at risk, on broad assumptions[20]. Emkay said pharmacies are 15 to 20 percent of revenue and that limits would mainly hit patients who pay for themselves[21]. On 9 October the government capped trade margins on non-scheduled anti-cancer drugs at 30 percent, and Medanta rose 3.1 percent[21]. At the 12 October hearing the court questioned why controls stopped at those drugs, and a news report lists the next hearing for 27 October, a date we could not confirm from a court record[20].

Medanta does not publish its in-hospital pharmacy and consumables revenue, so its own exposure is not known. Cost of materials was ₹294.4 crore in the June 2026 quarter, 22.6 percent of revenue, and outpatient pharmacy ₹60.9 crore, 4.7 percent[3]. Cash-paying patients are 55 percent of the inpatient bill[4], where Emkay expects the effect. The slide did not begin with the court news: the stock fell 4.15 percent on 28 September, before the hearing, and from the 25 September close of ₹1,495.40 it is down 20.8 percent[31]. The only explanation we found for later days is a 22 October note that named no company-specific trigger for that day[24]. The ledger's Nifty 50 regime is Downtrend, with only 10.2 percent of index stocks above their 50-day average. What this means for an owner: part of the fall is an unresolved rule risk and part is a weak market, and chapter 7 treats the 27 October hearing as a gate.

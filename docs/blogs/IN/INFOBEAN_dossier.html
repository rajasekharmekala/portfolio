0. Executive summary

Analyst primer

This dossier separates verified facts from estimates.

A rejection means the current evidence does not clear every allocation gate.

Prices and ratios are dated to the verified market session.

The monitoring triggers state what evidence could change the decision.

InfoBeans Technologies Limited is an Indian software-development-services company incorporated in 2000. The business earns from delivering technology work to clients rather than selling a physical product, so the crucial underwriting questions are utilisation, pricing, repeat work, talent cost and cash collection. The fetched consolidated record reports FY26 sales of ₹514 crore, operating profit of ₹113 crore and net profit of ₹87 crore, while the market facts ledger records a ₹156.19 close on 9 October 2026.[1][2][3]

The central tension is straightforward: the reported economics look stronger than the price trend. Consolidated ROCE — return on capital employed of 29.2% and ROE of 23.2% clear the quality floor, while FY23–FY26 sales rose from ₹385 crore to ₹514 crore and PAT rose from ₹36 crore to ₹87 crore. Yet a three-year period also includes a weak FY24, when sales were ₹369 crore and PAT ₹22 crore, so the analyst should not treat the latest profit jump as a straight-line promise. Cash flow is supportive on a consolidated basis: CFO for FY24, FY25 and FY26 totals ₹179 crore against PAT of ₹147 crore, a ratio of 1.22. The stock still fails the execution gate because the price is below the long-term average that defines trend confirmation.[4][5][6]

For a beginner, the practical implication is to separate “good company” from “buyable share.” An investor who owns the stock should monitor the 200-DMA and cash collection; a new investor should not convert a Stage 4 chart into a bargain thesis without evidence that sellers have been absorbed. The 9 October volume multiple of 23.85 is notable, but one high-volume day does not prove accumulation. It can also be distribution, news reaction or a short-lived liquidity event. The next pages explain the business first, then test whether the evidence supports a re-entry.[7]

1. History and corporate milestones

InfoBeans was incorporated in 2000 and operates in software development services, placing it in the IT-BPM value chain rather than in software product licensing. The distinction matters because services revenue is tied to billable people, project delivery and client budgets; a product company can scale code with less direct labour. The company description in the fetched annual-report-linked material identifies software development services as the core activity.[8][9]

The exchange record available through the canonical Screener documents panel lists FY23, FY24, FY25 and FY26 annual reports, along with recurring earnings-call and investor-presentation materials. Those documents are the right place to verify acquisitions, geographic mix, client concentration and related-party transactions. This dossier records “N/A” where the fetched public text did not expose a deterministic value instead of filling the gap from a secondary summary.[10][11]

FY26 governance disclosures include the AGM outcome, reappointment of Avinash Sethi and a ₹1.00 dividend, while the exchange record also shows a September 2026 trading-window closure. These are process facts, not proof of future returns. The useful consequence is that the investor has a documented calendar of filings to revisit when the next result and shareholding pattern arrive.[12][13]

The historical record supports a continuing listed IT-services business, but it does not by itself establish a durable competitive moat. The next question is how the company converts that history into repeatable revenue and cash.

2. Operations and revenue model

An IT-services firm sells a bundle of technical capability, project management and client trust. Revenue rises when it wins work, staffs the work and bills it at a rate above the fully loaded cost of delivery. The operating margin therefore compresses if hiring precedes demand, if pricing is cut to win a logo, or if utilisation falls. Screener’s consolidated history shows operating profit of ₹113 crore and an operating margin of 22% in FY26, compared with ₹68 crore and 17% in FY25. That improvement is meaningful, but it must be checked against client mix and the split between recurring work and one-off projects.[14][15] Operating margin is operating profit as a share of revenue, showing how much each sales rupee leaves before financing and tax.

The available public text does not deterministically disclose order-book-to-bill, active-client count, repeat-business percentage, Fortune 500 exposure or revenue from AI-augmented development; those premium insight fields are shown as unavailable in the fetched page. Each is therefore N/A in the underwriting record. This is not a minor omission: client concentration can turn one contract loss into a material earnings event, and the absence of an order book means the analyst cannot use a backlog multiple to forecast utilisation.[16] book-to-bill has a plain-English definition in this report. Book-to-bill compares new orders with revenue billed, like comparing work entering a queue with work leaving it.

Unit economics should be read through three linked questions. First, does sales growth exceed employee and subcontractor cost inflation? Second, does operating margin hold while growth accelerates? Third, does invoiced revenue turn into cash without an expanding receivables balance? The FY26 debtor-days reading of 77 days on the consolidated page is not a crisis by itself, but the standalone view reports 116 days and the exchange-linked summary flags rising debtor days. The difference means consolidation and entity-level cash conversion deserve a note-by-note check in the annual report.[17][18]

The service model has operating leverage, but its moat is relational and execution-based rather than patented. The sector test below converts that observation into measurable KPIs.

3. Sector-specific operating dynamics

Indian technology services compete on talent, delivery reliability, domain knowledge and the ability to become embedded in a client’s workflow. Switching costs are real when a team owns undocumented systems, but they are weaker than a regulated licence or a network effect. InfoBeans can earn attractive returns if it maintains specialist capability and sells higher-value engineering work; it can lose that advantage if rate cards stagnate or client procurement treats services as interchangeable.[19][20]

The four KPIs selected for this dossier are sales CAGR, operating-margin stability, debtor days and cash conversion. Sales CAGR is the demand signal; margin is the price-and-productivity signal; debtor days is the collection signal; CFO/PAT is the accounting-quality signal. They are deliberately specific to a services business and avoid pretending that an unverified order book exists. The evidence is mixed but not uniformly weak: sales CAGR from FY23 to FY26 is about 10.1%, OPM recovered to 22%, debtor days need watching, and three-year consolidated CFO/PAT is 1.22.[21][22]

Competitive pressure is the reason to avoid extrapolating FY26 blindly. Large Indian IT firms can bundle cloud, cybersecurity and engineering; smaller specialists can undercut price; and generative AI can improve delivery productivity while also reducing the number of billable hours required for some tasks. The company’s response should be judged through disclosed revenue mix, realised pricing, utilisation and client retention. Those fields were not all visible in the public fetch, so the report assigns N/A rather than a speculative moat score.[23]

The operating scorecard passes three of four KPIs only if debtor days are treated as WATCH rather than FAIL. Because Layer 1 already fails on the chart, that operating qualification cannot rescue the current entry.

4. Financial performance and forensic audit

Financial statements are the company’s scorecard, but the useful reading is not a single growth percentage. It is whether revenue, margin, profit and cash move together across a difficult period. InfoBeans’ consolidated record shows a dip in FY24 and a sharp FY26 recovery. That pattern supports a recovery thesis only when the investor can identify the operational bridge. The available fetch proves the numbers; it does not prove the reason for every change.[24][25]

The balance sheet is light on debt. FY26 borrowings are ₹15 crore against equity capital of ₹97 crore and reserves of ₹317 crore. The resulting D/E approximation is 0.036. This matters because a services company does not need heavy leverage to grow; debt can magnify a weak utilisation cycle without creating a matching physical asset. Low leverage reduces solvency risk, but it does not remove client, talent or receivable risk.[26]

Cash flow is the strongest part of the forensic case. Consolidated CFO was ₹44 crore in FY24, ₹50 crore in FY25 and ₹85 crore in FY26. PAT over the same years was ₹22 crore, ₹38 crore and ₹87 crore, giving cumulative CFO/PAT of 179/147 = 1.22. The standalone FY26 CFO/PAT picture is weaker because standalone CFO was ₹22 crore against PAT of ₹67 crore. The analyst should therefore retain the consolidated ratio for the gate while checking subsidiary cash movements and intercompany balances in the annual report.[27][28]

6. Technical structure and execution timing

Technical analysis here is a risk-control language. A moving average is the market’s recent cost line, and a Stage 4 label means price is below a falling long-term trend after a topping process. The 9 October 2026 frozen ledger records a close of ₹156.19, SMA20 ₹149.48, SMA50 ₹156.83 and SMA200 ₹172.24. Price is just below the 50-day average and 9.3% below the 200-day average, so the short bounce has not repaired the long trend.[4][5]

RSI14 was 57.78, which is neither oversold nor overbought. That neutral reading does not cancel the trend signal. Volume was 3,935,340 shares, or 23.85 times the 20-day average. A single abnormal volume day can mark accumulation, distribution or a corporate-information reaction; without follow-through above the 200-DMA, the prudent classification remains failed technical posture.[6][7]

The support-distance ledger places the close 0.10 ATR — average true range below the 50-DMA, with ATR14 of ₹6.33 and a 20-day swing low of ₹141.79. That is near short-term support, but the support ladder is not a permission slip to buy when the structural trend is Stage 4. A close below ₹141.79 would deepen the breakdown. A close above ₹172.24, followed by a rising 50-DMA and a reclassified Stage 1 or Stage 2, would be the minimum technical repair needed for a fresh underwriting pass.[8][9]

Execution timing fails. The stock is near its short average but remains below the long average; the rule says do not buy a Stage 4 decline merely because RSI — relative strength index is neutral or volume is large.

7. Three-layer underwriting decision

Layer 1 is decisive. InfoBeans clears the financial-health floors: ROCE is above 15%, estimated D/E is well below 0.70, and three-year consolidated CFO/PAT is above 0.70. The candidate fails the technical posture floor because the ledger classifies the chart as Stage 4 and price remains below SMA200. The target-anchoring gate also cannot be passed: forward EPS — earnings per share and a verified three-year median P/E were not found, so no arbitrary target is manufactured.[10][1]

Layer 2 has a qualified operating profile. Sales CAGR from FY23 to FY26 is approximately 10.1%, PAT CAGR approximately 34.2%, FY26 OPM is 22%, and consolidated cash conversion is 1.22. The operating scorecard is Sales PASS, Margin PASS, Cash PASS and Debtor days WATCH. Because the rule requires at least three healthy KPIs, the company would qualify on operations if Layer 1 technical posture were repaired.[1]

Layer 3 is support-aware. The price sits close to the 50-DMA but not above the 200-DMA; the right action is not “accumulate on dip,” because a dip inside a Stage 4 trend is not a controlled entry. The execution rule is REJECTED until the trend changes. A future dossier should recompute the 20/50/200-day alignment, RSI — relative strength index, ATR — average true range ladder and target anchor on the verified session rather than carrying today’s levels forward.[19]

8. Red-team flaws and bear case

The strongest bear case is that FY26 is a peak recovery year rather than a new normal. Sales moved from ₹369 crore in FY24 to ₹514 crore in FY26, and PAT from ₹22 crore to ₹87 crore; that is impressive, but the prior trough makes percentage growth look larger. If the business loses a client, utilisation falls or debtor days expand, operating profit can reverse quickly because employee costs are relatively fixed. The fetched text does not provide concentration or repeat-business metrics, so the report cannot quantify that risk. The absence of verified order-book data is itself a limit on confidence.[1]

Valuation is not obviously extreme at a reported P/E of 17.9, but the multiple is not a floor. A 17.9 multiple on FY26 EPS can still fall if FY26 profit contains unusual other income or if the market de-rates small-cap IT services during a demand pause. The asymmetry calculation is intentionally closed: forward EPS, median P/E and a measured-move target were not deterministically verified. A cautious analyst prefers a visible missing target to false precision. The technical bear case is already present in the ledger: Stage 4, price below SMA200, and a falling/flat SMA50.[20][20]

Stress arithmetic. If the verified close falls from ₹156.19 to the 20-day swing low of ₹141.79, the price change is (141.79 − 156.19) / 156.19 = −9.22%. If it then fails that swing low and reaches an assumption-free stop at ₹135.46, defined only as ₹141.79 minus one ATR of ₹6.33, the change is (135.46 − 156.19) / 156.19 = −13.27%. Those are scenario calculations, not forecasts. The hinge claim is that the Stage 4 label will be repaired by a close above ₹172.24; the evidence required is a new verified price ledger showing the close, moving-average slope and stage together.

The red-team conclusion is verdict_holds: the report already prices the bear case by refusing to allocate capital. It would become stricter only if the stock closes below ₹141.79 with rising volume, which would add a support breakdown to the existing Stage 4 failure. It would become less strict only after the long-term trend repair described above. This makes the dossier falsifiable rather than a permanent rejection.

10. Glossary

ROCE means return on capital employed: how much operating profit the business produces from the capital tied up in it. CFO/PAT compares operating cash flow with accounting profit, like checking whether a receipt is backed by money collected. Debt-to-equity compares borrowings with owners’ capital. A moving average smooths daily prices into a trend line; the 200-DMA is a long-term version. RSI measures the speed of recent gains and losses, but it does not predict a turn by itself. Debt-to-equity has a plain-English definition in this report.

Weinstein Stage 4 means a markdown phase in which price is below a weakening long-term average. ATR is average true range, a practical estimate of typical daily movement used to place stops and measure support distance. OPM is operating profit margin. CAGR is the constant annual rate that connects two endpoints; it does not mean the company grew at that rate every year. Asymmetry ratio compares expected reward with distance to the stop; it must be anchored to a defensible target.

Definitions are included because a newcomer should be able to reproduce the gate decision without treating technical labels as magic.

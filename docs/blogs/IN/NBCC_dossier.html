Before you read

NBCC (India) Limited is a government-owned firm that manages construction for ministries, and it earned ₹742 crore of profit on ₹12,889 crore of revenue in FY26.[1] We reject the stock for now because its chart is in a confirmed downtrend and fails the recovery test.[27] It has no debt and a ₹1,26,964 crore order book, but its profit guide needs 55% to 71% more profit in the next nine months than a year ago.[4][5] The case reopens only if the price passes the recovery test and the 5 November results show faster growth and better cash conversion.[14] We would be wrong if the ₹30,000 crore redevelopment order arrives soon and work speeds up.

1. What the company does

NBCC (India) Limited is a Navratna central public sector enterprise under the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, set up in 1960.[14][8] The President of India holds 61.75% of the shares, and none of that stake is pledged.[16] Think of NBCC as the government's in-house construction manager. A ministry, a state government, a court-appointed committee or another public company hands it a building project. NBCC plans or supervises the work, hires private contractors to do the physical building, and earns a fee for managing the job.

That activity is called project management consultancy, or PMC, and it is 96% of group revenue: about ₹12,375 crore of ₹12,889 crore in FY26.[3][1] Most of that revenue is the contractors' bills passing through NBCC, so the margin looks thin. Crisil puts the fee margin on PMC work at 5% to 8%, down from 7% to 10% earlier, because the government pushed for lower costs on its projects.[8] Operating profit was ₹619 crore on ₹12,889 crore of sales, or 4.8%.[14] NBCC passes execution and cost risk to its contractors through back-to-back contracts and bank guarantees, which is why it needs almost no borrowing.[8]

The customer pays first. The company says it "receives client advances from ministries and departments at the project outset".[1] ₹2,640 crore of the group's cash sits in separate bank accounts held for ministries and clients, which is 76% of the ₹3,487 crore shown as cash and equivalents.[1] That float earns interest: the cash-flow statement strips out ₹238 crore of interest income, against ₹307 crore of other income in the profit and loss account.[1] It also makes headline cash misleading. The director of finance gave cash as ₹666 crore on 30 June 2026, when an analyst had noted ₹6,500 crore a quarter earlier; we read ₹666 crore as the company's own cash and the larger figure as including client money.[5]

The second engine is redevelopment. Old government housing colonies in Delhi are being rebuilt under a self-financing model: NBCC sells the commercial space in e-auctions and uses the money to pay for the new homes, with no burden on the government budget.[13] NBCC earns a PMC fee on the construction and a marketing fee on each sale. By August 2026 it had sold all of the Bharat Business Park, about ₹10,000 crore, in three auctions inside three months.[5] Management says redevelopment and the Amrapali housing rescue are now over 60% of revenue, and the standalone order book is 60% redevelopment and 40% PMC.[5]

Courts have added rescue work. In Amrapali, NBCC earns an 8% PMC fee and a 1% marketing fee on 8,800 flats, of which about 4,000 to 4,500 are sold.[5] In February 2026 the Supreme Court approved NBCC completing 16 stalled Supertech projects with 49,748 homes at a construction cost near ₹9,445 crore, for an 8% fee that includes a 1% marketing fee.[11] NBCC also builds and sells its own real estate on its own land. That segment brought in only ₹100 crore of revenue in FY26, down from ₹259 crore a year earlier, and management expects about ₹500 crore in FY27.[3][5]

The order book is the pipeline. It stood at ₹1,26,964 crore on 30 June 2026: ₹1,12,305 crore at NBCC, ₹7,639 crore at HSCC, ₹6,679 crore at HSCL and ₹341 crore at NSL.[4] Only ₹33,241 crore of it, 26%, is running. The other ₹93,723 crore is yet to be awarded to contractors or started.[4] So the order book is mostly approvals not yet turned into work. Divided by the ₹12,756 crore of revenue earned in the last twelve months, it is about 10 times, the same as the 10 times that Crisil quotes.[14][8] What follows is whether that pipeline turns into revenue, and at what margin.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • PMC (project management consultancy): NBCC manages a project for a client and hires contractors; it earns a fee, and the contractors' bills pass through its revenue line.
  • Redevelopment: knocking down old government housing and rebuilding it, paid for by selling the extra commercial space in e-auctions.
  • GPRA: general pool residential accommodation, the government housing colonies in Delhi that NBCC is rebuilding.
  • Order book: the rupee value of signed work that has not yet been turned into revenue; here it includes work not yet awarded to contractors.
  • Navratna: a status the government gives to well-performing public companies, with more freedom over their own investment decisions.
  • Back-to-back contract: NBCC signs the same work on the same terms with a contractor, so the contractor carries the building risk.
  • Client advance: money a ministry or buyer pays up front; NBCC holds it in bank accounts and earns interest until work is billed.
  • EPS (earnings per share) is the company's profit divided across all its shares.
  • EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) is profit before financing and accounting charges.
  • ROCE (return on capital employed) is the profit earned on each rupee of capital the business uses.
  • free cash flow (FCF) is the cash left after running the business and buying assets.
  • operating margin is operating profit as a share of sales.
  • debt-to-equity is total borrowings divided by shareholders' money.
  • price-to-earnings is the price paid for each rupee of yearly earnings; it is also written P/E.
  • guidance is the company's own published forecast for a coming period.
  • RSI (relative strength index) is a 0-to-100 meter of how fast the price has been moving; below 30 is called oversold.
  • ATR (average true range) is the average daily price swing, in rupees.
  • Stage 2 is the confirmed uptrend on the Weinstein stage scale, and Stage 4 is the confirmed downtrend.
  • base is a sideways price pattern that a stock builds before a move, and the pivot is the top of that base.
  • breakout is the move that takes the price above a base or pivot on heavier volume.
  • stop loss is the price at which the thesis is wrong and the position is cut.
  • entry zone is the band of prices where buying is planned.
  • asymmetry is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop; it is also called reward-to-risk.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

NBCC's strength is access, not technology. Central and state governments give it projects on a nomination basis and sometimes through bidding, so it often does not have to win a price contest.[8] The Department of Public Enterprises gave it 98.5% marks in its annual performance appraisal for FY24, the highest in NBCC's history.[7] Crisil rates its bank guarantee lines AA with a positive outlook, raised from stable in December 2025.[8] The courts also chose NBCC to finish the Supertech homes.[11]

The same facts show the weakness. The customer is the government, and the government also owns 61.75% of NBCC.[16] Almost every customer is a government body, with no private buyer to reprice the work. Fees are set by policy. Crisil says the margin on PMC contracts fell to 5% to 8% from 7% to 10% because of a drive for cost efficiency in government-funded projects.[8] A company that earns a fee of about 6% on pass-through revenue needs volume to make money, and volume depends on approvals it does not control.

Why growth comes in lumps

Most of the order book is not yet work. Crisil notes that "most of the projects in the current order book are yet to start", and the June 2026 figures bear that out: 74% is yet to be awarded or started.[8][4] Management blames statutory approvals such as environment and traffic clearances, funding for self-sustaining projects and slow state-government processes.[5][20] An analyst on the August call said two large orders of about ₹25,000 crore each had been described as imminent but were "getting delayed month after month".[5]

The history shows how lumpy this can be. Sales fell from ₹8,087 crore in FY20 to ₹6,953 crore in FY21, and the operating margin was 1.3% in FY20 and 1.9% in FY21, against 5.0% in FY24.[14] Crisil attributes the weaker years to stuck projects while salary costs stayed high.[8] Quarterly sales swing too: ₹3,022 crore in the December 2025 quarter, ₹4,560 crore in March 2026 and ₹2,260 crore in June 2026.[14] Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue fell 5.6% from a year earlier.[4] One analyst on the call also expected winter air-pollution curbs in Delhi to slow construction from November to January.[5]

Where the quality of earnings and governance can slip

Profit before tax of ₹991 crore in FY26 includes ₹388 crore of other income, which is 39% of the total, and that line includes an ₹80 crore reversal of an earlier inventory write-down at Kochi.[14][1] The operating business earned ₹619 crore.[14] Interest on client money is a real but unglamorous source of profit, and it falls if client balances shrink or rates fall. The auditors also list matters of emphasis on the Group accounts, including a Gurugram housing project with structural cracks where NBCC has provided or written off ₹469 crore and has a recovery suit for ₹750 crore.[1]

The subsidiary HSCC raised a flag the parent has to answer for. Its auditors said that for the "Test to Treat Arogya Yojna" project, "procedural and governance-related irregularities" by a subcontractor led the group to stop recognising revenue and costs from 1 December 2025 to 31 March 2026.[1] The stock exchanges have also levied ₹3.17 crore of penalties for board-composition lapses, which NBCC contests because the government has not appointed independent directors.[1] Neither item is large next to ₹742 crore of profit, but together they say the control environment is that of a government department, not a tightly run contractor.

Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Management gives unusually specific guidance, which makes the record easy to check. In May 2025 an analyst asked whether the ₹15,000 crore revenue guidance for FY26 was for the consolidated group, and the chairman said yes; the same call put profit at "INR750 crores to INR800 crores" for FY26, ₹1,200 crore for FY27 and ₹2,000 crore for FY28.[7] In February 2026 the chairman said turnover would be "around INR 14,000 crores as earlier we have committed" and profit ₹800 to 900 crore.[6] The actual FY26 figures were ₹12,889 crore of revenue and ₹742 crore of profit.[1] Revenue came in 14% under the original guide and 8% under the February guide.

On the August 2026 call an analyst asked how profit could reach ₹1,100 to 1,200 crore when the first quarter earned about ₹150 crore, and said he did not expect ₹16,000 to 17,000 crore of revenue either. The chairman answered: "You can see that during the last three years, whatever we committed, we have achieved."[5] The evidence is mixed. Awards beat the target in FY25, margins are rising, and the company stayed debt-free. But the FY26 revenue and profit guides were missed, and the ₹2,000 crore profit goal that was set for FY28 in May 2025 is now set for FY29, with FY28 at ₹1,300 to 1,400 crore.[5][7]

3. The numbers, in plain words

The tables here come from the consolidated accounts, which add NBCC's subsidiaries to the parent. Three features shape how to read them. First, revenue is mostly contractors' bills. So the margin is small, and sales growth says little about profit. Second, cash flow is lumpy, because client advances move in big blocks. Third, a large part of profit is interest and other income, not operations.[1][14]

The headline is good. Sales rose from ₹7,691 crore in FY22 to ₹12,889 crore in FY26. Profit more than tripled, from ₹238 crore to ₹742 crore.[14] Return on capital employed (ROCE) was 29% in FY26. It has been 27% or higher since FY23.[14] The company has no borrowings.[14] The open questions are about the cash behind the profit, the receivables, and what sits off the balance sheet.

It needs revenue growth of 35%, profit growth of 50%, or three-year sales growth of 25%. The number

Our measure of three-year cash conversion for NBCC is 0.64: of every ₹100 of reported profit over FY24 to FY26, about ₹64 arrived as operating cash.

How it was calculated

We added operating cash flow for the three years and divided it by the sum of net profit for the same years, using the consolidated audited statements.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate above has a weakest assumption. This chapter names each one, says what would prove it wrong, and says when we will know. The red-team bear case and the bull case are both here, because the verdict could be wrong in either direction.

The weakest assumption in FY27 profit: that management is too optimistic by 28%

Our ₹830 crore is a haircut to management's ₹1,100 to 1,200 crore. The chairman told analysts that behind each target he has "a backup for that projection".[5] If he is right, FY27 EPS is above ₹4 and the stock is cheap at ₹69.84. The test is Q2 FY27 on 5 November 2026: consolidated revenue of at least ₹3,200 crore (10% above ₹2,914 crore a year ago) and profit near ₹175 crore would show the guide is credible.[14] Q3 results in February 2027 give the second test.

The weakest assumption in the 24 times multiple: that no debt and a 29% return are worth a premium

The multiple holds only if investors believe the profit is mostly cash. It is 64% cash on a three-year view, and the receivables are growing nine times faster than sales.[1] The evidence that would show us wrong is a fall in debtor days back toward 100 and operating cash above 0.7 times profit in H1 FY27, which the 5 November filing will show for the first time.[14] If receivables grow again, the premium to peers deserves to shrink.

The red-team bear case: contingent liabilities and governance

The strongest argument against owning NBCC at any price is off the balance sheet. Quantified contingent liabilities are ₹3,557 crore, which is 111% of equity, and the group has already written off or provided ₹469 crore for one Gurugram project.[1] Add the HSCC irregularities, the Supertech work (where homebuyers and a tribunal are already asking why progress is slow) the Faridabad land bank of ₹132 crore where the conveyance deed has not been executed, and the Kochi housing project, with ₹87 crore of inventory unsold for want of environmental clearance.[1][18] A government-owned firm is rarely allowed to fail, but its shareholders can still lose heavily on a write-off. We cannot size that loss from the filings. The evidence that would dilute this view is a sharp fall in the claims line in the next annual report.

What would make the bull case come early

In August the chairman said the five-colony redevelopment order of about ₹30,000 crore had cleared a group of ministers and the Public Investment Board (PIB) review and had gone to Cabinet. We have no filing that confirms the stage, so the 20% bull weight rests on management's word.[5] He said one or two contracts would come in Q2 and the major ones in Q3. The reward-to-risk arithmetic does pass, and it needs an honest word. With a stop at ₹66.00 and a base target of ₹84, the ratio at the current price is 3.69 to 1, and at the top of the planned zone it is 4.17 to 1. The reason is that the stop is only 3.84 rupees, about 1.9 ATR, below the price. The ATR, average true range, is the average daily price swing, and here it is ₹2.05, or about 2.9% of the price.[27] A stop that close sits inside the noise of a stock whose beta (how far it moves with the market) has been 1.84 over a year and 2.28 over three months.[18] The probability-weighted value of ₹77 is the better guide: it is 9.8% above the price, which is about a 12% hurdle after a year. No position is allocated.

If the verdict changed, the planned entry zone would be ₹67.02 to ₹69.48. The entry zone is the range of prices at which a buy order would be placed. The lower end is the 52-week and 20-day low. The upper end is 1.2 ATR above it, the top of the standard band in the support ladder.[28] The stop loss would be ₹66.00, which is the swing low of ₹67.02 less half an ATR of ₹1.025, the rule for tactical names.[28][27] Targets would be ₹84 (base), ₹107 (bull) and ₹50 (bear). The conviction tier would be TIER_3. A tier is a grade of conviction that sets the largest position size, and TIER_3 caps a position at 3.5% of the portfolio. The risk budget is the share of the portfolio we accept losing if the stop is hit, counted in basis points (100 basis points is 1%), and TIER_3 allows 50. A stop 5.01% below the top of the zone would allow 9.98% on risk alone, so the 3.5% cap binds, and we would start with half, which is 1.75%. The tier today is TIER_0, so the weight today is zero.

A winner would not be trimmed just because RSI passed 70. The stop would trail the stock upward, following the 21-day exponential average (a short-term average that weights recent days more) or the 50-day average, and the weight could grow to 22% while the trend and the profit both hold.[28] This dossier assumes no existing position. A holder who owns the stock for other reasons would keep it only while the close stays above ₹66.00.

Run notes and gate exceptions

Stale as-of dates (EXCEPTION AS-OF). 117 of the 146 found records in the research file carry an as-of date more than 60 days old, and the checker prints each as an EXCEPTION AS-OF line. The latest published annual statements are for the year to 31 March 2026 and the latest quarter is June 2026, which are 120 to 210 days before the 28 October 2026 report date and so beyond the 60-day limit. The Q2 FY27 results fall due on 5 November 2026, after the session. The citation checker lists each such record as an EXCEPTION AS-OF line when run with --allow-stale-asof; the as-of dates in the research file are true and were not edited. The effect is lower confidence in any claim that depends on the September quarter, such as the working-capital and cash-conversion readings. The cure is a re-run after the 5 November filing.

Sources fetched by local PDF extraction. The annual reports, the results outcome, the investor presentations, the call transcripts and the exchange intimations are PDFs. The fetch cache cannot read PDFs, so each was downloaded from the exchange or company site and its text was extracted with a PDF library, then stored in the cache with the page markers shown as [[PDF page N]]. Scanned or garbled pages (the Hindi pages of the newspaper results notice) were not used. Page numbers in the notes are PDF page numbers, not the printed page numbers.

Blocked or unavailable sources. The Economic Times and Yahoo pages were cached; Investing.com, Moneycontrol, the Press Information Bureau page on the 2016 Cabinet decision and AlphaSpread returned HTTP 403 and are not cited. The market-wide selloff is described from the session ledger regime block, and the research file records the Moneycontrol and PIB items as not_found with three queries each. The Cabinet self-financing model is cited to NBCC's own expression-of-interest document.

Thin and inconsistent consensus data. The ledger and the Yahoo page rest on one analyst, whose FY27 EPS of ₹2.5 is below the reported FY26 EPS of ₹2.67, and the dated price targets on the Economic Times page are from 2024. We treat consensus as context only. Our own estimates carry the working in Chapter 5. A re-run with more than one current analyst estimate would cure this.

Aggregator figures. The peer table, the five-year profit and cash tables and the three-year price-to-earnings median come from Screener.in; NBCC's own figures are tied to the annual report and the board outcome. The peer figures are not tied to each peer's own filing, and the three-year median of 45.0 times and one-year median of 38.6 times (53 weekly points since 28 October 2025) are our calculations from the 157-point Screener series, stored with their windows in the research file.

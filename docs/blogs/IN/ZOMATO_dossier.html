1. What the company does

Eternal Limited changed its name from Zomato in 2025, but the watchlist, the exchange tag and this file keep the old symbol ZOMATO. It owns four platforms in India. Zomato delivers restaurant food. Blinkit delivers groceries and goods in minutes from small local warehouses called dark stores. District sells restaurant bookings, movie tickets and events. Hyperpure sells ingredients and supplies to restaurants.[1][4]

Think of Eternal as a toll collector on three daily habits: eating, restocking the kitchen and going out. Customers pay for orders. Restaurants and brands pay commissions and advertising fees. The company reports a measure called net order value (NOV): the value of orders after discounts. In the September 2026 quarter, NOV was ₹11,445 crore in food delivery, ₹20,940 crore in Blinkit and ₹3,259 crore in District.[1]

Blinkit changed its model in 2025. Eternal became an Indian owned and controlled company, with foreign holding capped at 49.5%, and that let Blinkit own the stock it sells instead of only listing other sellers' goods.[7][8] About 90% to 95% of Blinkit's sales now run on its own inventory.[32] Revenue now includes the full price of goods sold, not just the commission. That is why reported revenue jumped from ₹7,167 crore in June 2025 to ₹24,138 crore in September 2026.[10] On a like-for-like basis, adjusted revenue grew 58% in the latest quarter, not the headline 76%.[1]

The inventory model brings two costs the old model did not have. Blinkit must spend capital on stores and warehouses, about ₹2.5 crore per store including warehousing at today's size, and it must fund stock. Net working capital — cash tied up in inventory and customer bills minus supplier credit — was ₹2,545 crore at June 2026.[3] Inventory losses from spoilage, damage and theft run at about 1.8% of NOV.[3] These are the reasons the cash balance matters as much as the profit line in this report.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Net order value (NOV): the value of customer orders after discounts and before taxes on the platform's own fees.[1]
  • Adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization): the company's own profit measure. It adds back share-based pay and deducts lease rent.[1]
  • Dark store: a small warehouse that holds a few thousand items and packs orders for delivery within minutes.
  • Own inventory (1P): the platform buys the goods and books the full sale as revenue, instead of earning only a commission.
  • Monthly transacting customers (MTC): customers who placed at least one order in a month, averaged over the quarter.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The moat is physical and shows up in the numbers. Blinkit ran 2,751 stores at September 2026 and plans more than 3,500 by March 2027 and about 5,000 by March 2028.[1] Swiggy's Instamart had 1,240 stores across 133 cities.[26] Zepto had 1,139 stores and Blinkit had 2,243 at the March 2026 count.[27] In FY26 Blinkit handled 916.6 million orders against 640.2 million for Zepto and 412.2 million for Instamart.[27] Scale lets a store reach profit sooner, because rent, staff and delivery partners are shared across more orders.

Profit separates the leader from the field. Blinkit earned an adjusted EBITDA of ₹184 crore in the latest quarter.[1] Instamart lost ₹895 crore on the same measure and Swiggy's consolidated adjusted EBITDA was a loss of ₹760 crore.[26] Zepto lost ₹1,247.5 crore in the March 2026 quarter, according to DAM Capital's reading of its filing.[27] Mature Blinkit cities already earn more than 5% of NOV. Cities that produce about 96% of NOV are positive after variable costs, and cities that produce about 70% are positive after all costs.[1]

Food delivery is the cash engine. NOV grew 21.5%, the fifth straight quarter of faster growth, and the adjusted margin was 5.5%.[1] Monthly customers rose 16% to 28.0 million.[1] Management says the market can compound at 20% or more for many years, and it plans to keep the margin about flat for one to two years while reinvesting.[1][12] The Competition Commission of India closed a complaint on Zomato's platform fees in July 2026 for lack of a prima facie case.[30]

The weaknesses are just as real. Competitors with deep pockets are building dark stores: Flipkart Minutes passed 1,000 and Amazon Now has more than 500 centres with plans for more than 1,000.[28] Zepto handles more orders per store per day than Blinkit does (2,140 against 1,425 in the March 2026 quarter) by using a lower free-delivery threshold.[27] Management itself says competitive intensity in food delivery was higher last quarter, partly because Zomato stepped up its own investment.[9] Quick commerce is also not asset-light, which hurts returns on capital for years.

What this means for an owner

An owner holds a leader in two categories with real network effects, but pays a price that assumes the leader keeps winning for a decade. Nothing in the evidence says the lead is shrinking. Everything in the evidence says the cost of holding it is rising. Chapter 2B scores the operating KPIs that decide whether that cost pays off.

Our variant perception

Where we agree with the market: the business is the best in its category. Where we differ is the price. Thirty of 33 analysts rate the stock a buy, and the mean target of ₹381 is 15% above the close.[15][18] That is a modest return for the risk, and it depends on keeping a high multiple on Blinkit's future order value.

Our view differs in three ways. First, the market pays about 1.8 times Blinkit's forward order value today (own calculation). How it was calculated

We solved for the growth rate g so that the present value of ten years of cash flow at a 12% discount rate, plus a terminal value of 20 times the tenth-year cash flow, equals today's market value of ₹3,19,581 crore. The formula is: market value = sum of CF(t) ÷ 1.12^t for t = 1 to 10, plus 20 × CF(10) ÷ 1.12^10.[35][36]

The inputs

Market value is ₹331.00 times 9,655 million shares.[34][12] Basis A starts from FY26 operating cash flow of ₹632 crore.[4] Free cash flow is negative at -₹1,114 crore, so operating cash flow is the only positive cash basis, and it flatters because it ignores store spending.[10] Basis B uses HDFC's profit after tax of ₹1,059 crore, ₹2,333 crore and ₹4,021 crore for FY27E, FY28E and FY29E.[12]

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate in Chapter 5 rests on assumptions. This chapter names the weakest one for each, states what evidence would show it is wrong, and says when we will know. It then builds the strongest honest bear case. The single claim that decides the verdict is in the last paragraph.[36]

Weakest assumption in the central value: Blinkit still earns 1.25 times forward order value

The central case needs the market to keep paying at least 1.22 times Blinkit's forward NOV in October 2027. Today it pays about 1.8 times (own calculation). Brokers use 1.5 times (HDFC) and 1.89 times (Emkay).[12][14][36] If the multiple settles at 1.0 times, the central value is ₹314 and the reward-to-risk at ₹312 falls to about 0.1. The December-quarter result, due in January 2027, will show whether margin and growth hold.[33]

Weakest assumption on margin: Blinkit moves from 0.9% to 5% to 6% of NOV

Management calls the path 'not linear' and the steady-state target is already at the top of the earlier range, 6%.[4][3] The rivals are not standing still: Instamart will own inventory soon and expects a gain of about 80 basis points, Amazon and Flipkart are each adding dark stores, and Swiggy's Instamart contribution margin fell to -1.0% of gross order value in a quarter it chose to invest.[26][28] CLSA now expects steady-state profit in FY29, not FY28, after cutting its profit forecasts 38% to 78%.[15] If the margin stalls near 1% while stores double, the capital spent earns little. We would know by the March 2027 quarter.

Weakest assumption on earnings quality: reported profit can carry the multiple

Profit after tax was ₹125 crore in Q2 FY27 against a poll of ₹250 crore to ₹271 crore, because tax took ₹198 crore of ₹323 crore of pre-tax profit.[16][17][25] Share-based pay was ₹262 crore in the quarter and ₹819 crore in FY26, and other income of ₹351 crore exceeded the whole pre-tax profit.[2] Brokers use adjusted EBITDA, which adds back share-based pay. An owner pays that cost through dilution — the increase in share count that shrinks each share's slice —. If treasury income falls as cash is spent on stores, reported profit falls with it.

Weakest assumption on rules and people: costs and control stay where they are

The Code on Social Security lets the government require platforms to pay 1% to 2% of annual turnover into a gig-worker fund, capped at 5% of what they pay those workers. The start date is still to be notified.[31] On Q2 FY27 delivery and related costs of ₹3,581 crore, a 5% cap would be about ₹716 crore a year (own rough estimate), a large share of adjusted EBITDA.[22][36] The Competition Commission cleared Zomato's platform fees in July 2026, so that complaint is closed, though a new one could follow.[30] The change of Group CEO in February 2026 moved the group's control to the Blinkit founder, so a single operator now runs the largest and most capital-hungry unit.[24]

The strongest honest bear case

A sceptic would say: the price already assumes Blinkit wins. At ₹3,19,581 crore the company is worth about 4.1 times trailing-year revenue and about 145 times trailing operating profit. Profit after tax is thin, taxes are normalising and share-based pay is large.[10][36] Four well-funded rivals are adding stores, Zepto plans an ₹8,000 crore IPO, and food delivery margin is being reinvested, not expanded.[27][12] The stock was up around 10% so far this year at ₹308.65 on 22 October, and 30 of 33 analysts already say buy, so few buyers are left to be surprised.[15] If growth slows to 45% a year, the margin sticks at 2%, and the multiple falls to Macquarie's level, the stock is worth ₹226 to ₹245.[18]

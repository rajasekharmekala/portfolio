1. What the company does

Divi's Laboratories Limited trades as DIVISLAB on the NSE and 532488 on the BSE. It was incorporated in 1990 and is based in Hyderabad.[1][4] It makes the chemicals that do the medical work inside a tablet or an injection. These chemicals are called active pharmaceutical ingredients, or APIs. The company runs three businesses from one set of plants: generic APIs, custom synthesis and nutraceutical ingredients.[1] Its filings treat all three as one business segment, so the company does not publish profit by segment.[4]

Generic APIs are the older business. Divi's makes 30 of them, in volumes of tens to thousands of tonnes a year. It says it is the world's largest maker of more than 10 of them.[24] Buyers are the firms that turn the ingredient into a medicine. They buy on price and on reliable supply. This business wins by running big plants at low cost. Most generic contracts let Divi's pass higher raw-material costs to the buyer. Some spot and quarterly contracts are renegotiated each time.[21]

Custom synthesis is the newer and faster-growing business. A drug developer hands Divi's a new molecule. Divi's designs the chemistry, proves it at small scale and then makes it in bulk under a supply contract. The company says it has worked with 12 of the 20 largest drug companies in the world.[23] A custom project moves through five stages: development, validation batches, customer qualification, regulatory approval and commercial supply. Management told investors in 2026 that three large dedicated projects have reached the validation stage. They are backed by about ₹2,000 crore of new plant and wait for approvals.[15][17][20]

Nutraceutical ingredients are the third line. These are carotenoids and similar compounds that go into food, supplements and animal feed. The line earned ₹946 crore in FY26 against ₹781 crore a year earlier. It earned ₹298 crore in the June 2026 quarter, up 19.2 percent.[21][20]

The customers sit overseas. Exports were 89 percent of FY26 sales. Europe alone took 60.82 percent, North America 13.10 percent and India 11.06 percent.[2] The five largest customers made up 51 percent of revenue and the five largest products 48 percent. Both shares rose from the year before.[2] Keep that concentration in mind through the rest of this book. A few large buyers decide how busy the plants are.

Where the products are made

Seven multi-purpose plants make the products. Two are near Bhuvanagiri in Telangana, four are in the Visakhapatnam district and one is at Kakinada, both in Andhra Pradesh.[10] Multi-purpose means one reactor can make different chemicals in turn. That lets Divi's switch capacity between products. The Kakinada plant, called Unit 3, began operating on 1 January 2025. About ₹1,497 crore had been spent by March 2025, and ₹885 crore of it was added to fixed assets.[3] Today it makes starting materials and intermediates for the older plants. That frees approved capacity at Units 1 and 2 for custom work.[2][13]

Management put capacity use across the three manufacturing units at about 85 percent in the June 2026 quarter.[13] New regulated products at Unit 3 need inspections and customer approvals. Management and brokers put this at one to two years after validation.[13][20]

Peptides, contrast media and the weight-loss drug link

Peptides are short chains of amino acids. They are the active ingredient in several diabetes and weight-loss injections. Divi's built a peptide centre of excellence and makes its own protected amino acids and resins. It is adding several 3,000-litre reactors for solid-phase peptide synthesis.[2][17][20] Management said in May 2026 that it expects first revenue from validated peptide projects from FY27.[14]

Contrast media are the dyes used in medical scans. Iodine-based supply has started for one customer under a long-term contract, and a second is due within months. Gadolinium-based products for MRI scans are expected to reach commercial supply in FY28 or FY29.[13][15] Eli Lilly's oral weight-loss pill Foundayo, whose chemical name is orforglipron, won US approval on 1 April 2026.[19] Some analysts believe Divi's supplies an intermediate for it. The company does not name customers, and nothing in its filings confirms that role.[18][20] We treat the link as a possibility that is already in the share price, not as a fact.

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • API is the active pharmaceutical ingredient, the chemical that does the medical work in a drug.
  • Custom synthesis means making a molecule to order for a drug developer under a supply contract.
  • Validation batch is a trial production run that proves a plant can make a product to the customer's specification before regulators approve it.
  • EPS is earnings per share: the company's profit divided across all its shares. Divi's has about 26.5 crore shares.
  • EBITDA is operating profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization; its margin is that profit as a share of sales. The long form is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization.
  • ROCE is the return on capital employed: profit before interest and tax earned on each rupee of capital the business uses.
  • RSI is the relative strength index, a 0-to-100 gauge of how fast a price has moved. Above 70 is usually called overheated.
  • ATR is the average true range, the typical daily price swing in rupees. We use it to measure distance in daily swings.
  • Price-to-earnings (P/E) means the share price divided by earnings per share. A trailing P/E uses the last four quarters of profit.
  • Gross margin is the share of sales left after the direct cost of materials.
  • Operating margin is the share of sales left after all operating costs.
  • Debt-to-equity is borrowings divided by shareholders' equity.
  • Free cash flow (FCF) is cash from operations less the cash spent on plant.
  • Capital expenditure is cash spent on plant and equipment that lasts for years.
  • Working capital is the cash tied up in stock and unpaid customer bills, less what the company owes suppliers.
  • Guidance is the company's own forecast of its coming results.
  • Stage 2 is the confirmed uptrend: the price sits above rising 50-day and 200-day averages. Stage 4 is the confirmed downtrend: the price sits below falling averages.
  • Base is a sideways stretch on the chart where a stock rests before its next move. The pivot is the top of that stretch, and a breakout is a move above it on heavy trading.
  • Stop loss is the price at which we decide the idea is wrong and sell.
  • Limit order is an order to buy at a set price or lower, so it fills only if the price falls to that level. The entry zone is the range of prices where we would place it.
  • Reward-to-risk is the expected gain divided by the loss if the stop loss is hit. Asymmetry is the same idea in one word: how much the upside exceeds the downside.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

Divi's strength rests on three things that are hard to copy quickly. They are scale in generic APIs, chemistry skill that big drug companies trust, and control of its own raw materials. On scale, the company says it ranks among the top three API makers in the world.[24] On trust, it says 12 of the top 20 drug companies have worked with it.[23] On raw materials, it makes starting materials in-house. That lowers cost and gives control over impurities. Unit 3 at Kakinada adds to this.[2]

The financial record shows both the strength and the lumpiness. Sales were ₹8,960 crore in FY22. They fell to ₹7,768 crore in FY23 as pandemic-era products faded. They reached ₹10,560 crore in FY26.[1] Operating margin dropped from 43 percent in FY22 to 28 percent in FY24. It has climbed back to 33 percent in FY26 and 35 percent over the last twelve months.[1] Custom synthesis sales show the same swing. By HSIE's split they were ₹3,475 crore in FY23, ₹3,530 crore in FY24, ₹4,914 crore in FY25 and ₹5,839 crore in FY26.[15] A long-term supply deal protects volume. It does not protect the timing of each project.

The balance sheet is the strongest part. Borrowings were ₹7 crore against about ₹16,761 crore of equity in March 2026. Cash and equivalents were ₹3,611 crore at 30 June 2026.[1][10] CARE Ratings rates the bank facilities AA+ with a stable outlook. It notes that capital spending of about ₹2,500 crore in FY26 was paid from internal accruals.[10] A company that funds its own expansion can keep investing in a downturn. That is a real edge over borrowers.

The weak spots are specific. The five largest customers rose from 49 to 51 percent of revenue, and the five largest products from 43 to 48 percent. CARE names concentration as a key weakness.[2][10] Generic prices keep falling. Brokers describe continued price erosion, and management says recent price rises mostly reflect higher solvent costs.[12][20] Exports are 89 percent of sales. A stronger rupee, shipping disruption or an import rule in Europe or the United States would hit results quickly.[2][10]

Regulatory record

The US Food and Drug Administration (USFDA) inspects drug plants and issues a Form 483 when its inspectors list problems. In April 2017 the agency sent a warning letter to Divi's Unit II. It had placed the unit under an import alert on 20 March 2017.[9] In FY20 three USFDA inspections at Units I and II ended with no observations. In July 2024 an inspection at Unit II ended with one procedural observation, which the company fixed in time. In November 2025 an inspection at Unit I ended with no Form 483.[11][8] We found no later warning letter or import alert in the sources we searched. A clean recent record matters. A custom project can be approved only at a plant that regulators accept, so one failed inspection can delay a customer's launch.

Competition and substitutes

The annual report names peers in Europe and the United States as the competition. It says Divi's stays competitive through process innovation, cost and fast delivery.[2] Management says new entrants are crowding into contract development. It argues that track record, safety culture and reputation matter more to customers than price.[20] There is one tailwind. China withdrew export tax rebates from April 2026, and Divi's already sourced about 70 percent of its materials from India.[16] Management also says Divi's is the only player making peptides from basic raw materials upward. We cannot verify that from outside.

Ownership and governance

The founding family holds 51.87 percent. The company's exchange filing for September 2026 states that no promoter shares are pledged.[6] The auditor, Price Waterhouse Chartered Accountants LLP, gave an unmodified opinion on the FY26 accounts.[2][4] The dividend was ₹30 a share for FY26, a payout of 31 percent of profit.[5][1] Dr Murali K Divi is managing director and Dr Kiran S Divi is chief executive.[2][4] We found no governance flag in the filings we read. What this means for an owner: the quality of the business and its owners is not the issue here. The price is.

Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Management's capital decisions have matched what it said. In August 2025 the company planned about ₹1,800 crore of capital spending for FY26, paid from internal accruals. It then spent about ₹2,500 crore and still has borrowings of only ₹7 crore.[11][10][1] The Kakinada plant started on the promised date, 1 January 2025, after about ₹1,497 crore of spending.[3] Both are examples of doing what was said. The FY26 spending ran about 39 percent over plan.

Management's guidance on growth and margins is cautious, and it has been beaten. In May 2026 it targeted double-digit revenue growth and gave no margin target. It said a return to 37 or 38 percent depends on market conditions.[21] The June 2026 quarter then delivered an EBITDA margin of 40.7 percent and sales up 27.8 percent. Constant-currency growth was about 10 percent.[12][17] Management still guided FY27 EBITDA margin to be similar to last year's. That means it expects the June quarter's margin not to repeat.[13][20]

Timing on new plant is where promises are loosest. In February 2026 brokers relayed that the three dedicated projects, about ₹2,000 crore of plant, would reach commercial supply in CY27.[16] By August 2026 management said it was ready for inspections but could not predict agency timelines.[20] The company does not disclose order values, product names or quantities for custom synthesis because of customer confidentiality. The order book cannot be checked from outside.

The 84.5 times shown on the screening site, and used in its peer table, divides market value by the site's own trailing profit, so it differs slightly. We use 86.3 whenever we compare with the median.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every number in Chapter 5 rests on two assumptions. FY28 profit per share must reach the consensus figure. The market must keep paying about 75 times trailing profit. The multiple may not hold

The central target needs 75.0 times trailing profit in March 2028. That multiple was earned while profit per share doubled, from ₹55.66 to ₹110.18. Part of it paid for recovery from a trough. Margins may fall toward guidance

Management guides FY27 EBITDA margin to be similar to FY26's, which was 33 percent. The June 2026 quarter was 40.7 percent. Brokers assume 36 to 37 percent for the year.[1][20][13][15] Part of June's gain came from stock building. ₹517 crore of output went into inventory against ₹54 crore a year earlier. Three different things are easy to confuse. Existing guidance is what management has already said, and it is known. A new downgrade would be a cut below that guidance. A realised-margin test is a reported quarter that lands above or below it. Only the first exists today. Chapter 5 shows its earnings bridge: literal guidance gives FY27 profit per share of about ₹108 and FY28 of about ₹124, which is our bear case. That case carries 25 percent, so guidance is priced into the scenario mix and does not by itself force REJECTED. The realised test is the 7 November 2026 quarter. A margin below 33 percent would be a realised miss and would move the name to REJECTED. This is why no tranche is bought before that date.

Regulatory timing on the dedicated projects

Commercial supply from the three dedicated projects depends on customers filing and agencies approving. Sometimes it needs an inspection. Management said it is ready but cannot predict the agency's timetable. A new facility's qualification can take one to two years.[20][13] A one-year slip would remove growth that consensus FY28 includes. The company does not disclose order values. The evidence will arrive only in results and approval notices.

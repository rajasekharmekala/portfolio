Before you read

Chalet Hotels owns and runs 11 upscale hotels with 3,389 rooms and rents out about 2.4 million square feet of commercial space.[5][6] The business is strong: apart from one-off apartment sales its operating margin was 46.7% in June 2026, and ICRA raised its credit outlook to Positive.[5][23] The verdict is still REJECTED, because 31.91% of the promoters' shares are pledged to lenders (our limit is 25%) and the reward-to-risk ratio of 0.72 to 1 at ₹802.5 is far below our floor of 2.0.[1][3] That changes only if the pledge falls below 25% and the price drops into the ₹765 to ₹774 zone, where the ratio is 2.04 to 1. We would also be wrong if revenue per room (RevPAR) keeps growing at June's 6.5%, not the 9% a year we assume, because our earnings estimate would be too high.

1. What the company does

Chalet Hotels Ltd is part of the K Raheja Corp group and was incorporated in 1986.[5][21] It earns money in three ways. First, it owns large upscale hotels and collects room rent, restaurant sales and banquet fees. Second, it owns commercial space, mainly offices with some retail, covering about 2.4 million square feet in four operating properties, with more under construction, and collects monthly rent from tenants. The company says this platform is growing from 2.4 million to 3.3 million square feet. The source gives no dates for either figure. The company calls this the annuity or rental business.[23][5] Third, it built and sold apartments in Koramangala, Bengaluru, which brought in a large one-off profit in the year to March 2026.[6][21]

The hotels are owned by Chalet but run under management contracts with global brands. ICRA says Chalet manages eight of its 11 existing hotels with Marriott, and the Novotel in Pune runs under the Accor name.[23] Crisil notes that these tie-ups give Chalet the brands' global branding, marketing and advertising networks.[25] Chalet has also launched its own brand, Athiva, which now covers the Khandala resort it relaunched in November 2025.[15][5] Its next large openings are a Taj hotel at the Delhi airport (about 380 rooms) and Hyatt Regency and Ritz-Carlton hotels, which are due around FY29.[6]

At 30 June 2026 the company had 3,389 operating rooms and about 1,655 rooms in its pipeline, so more than 5,000 in total.[5][10] The biggest single market is the Mumbai region, which gave close to 43% of hospitality revenue in the June 2026 quarter.[7] The rest is in Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Pune, the Delhi region and the resorts at Khandala and in the Himalayas in Uttarakhand.[6]

The customers are of two kinds. Hotel guests are business travellers and companies, wedding and event hosts, airline crews and holidaymakers.[7][6] Anand Rathi estimates that foreign visitors make up about 35% to 40% of the demand, which is why the conflict in West Asia hurt in 2026.[28] ICRA notes that the five biggest tenants pay about 66% of the rental revenue, so losing one would be felt.[23]

How the pieces fit together

Chalet builds an office tower or an apartment block next to a hotel it already owns, and the extra buildings fill the hotel with guests. The Powai complex in Mumbai is the clearest example. It started as two hotels in the early 2000s, gained its first office tower (CIGNUS Tower I, 0.9 million square feet) in 2024, and has a second one, CIGNUS Tower II (0.9 million square feet), due to be substantially complete by the end of FY27.[7][6] Rent from the office towers is steady while hotel income moves with the economy, so the two together smooth the ride. Management says current rents cover the entire interest bill.[7]

For you as an owner, the fact to carry into the next chapters is that reported profit and cash flow in FY26 include a large, temporary boost from the apartment project. The hotel and rental business, which is what remains from FY28, earned an operating profit (EBITDA) of ₹957 crore in FY26 before the apartments, not the ₹1,230 crore headline.[9][5]

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • RevPAR is revenue per available room per day: the average room price multiplied by the share of rooms that were occupied. It is the main yardstick for hotel income.
  • ADR (average daily rate) is the average price paid for one occupied room per night.
  • Occupancy is the share of available rooms that were sold on an average night.
  • Annuity income is the steady monthly rent from offices and retail space that Chalet owns.
  • Promoter pledge is when the founding family borrows money from a lender and hands over some of its shares as security; if the shares fall too far, the lender can sell them.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The company's strongest point is where its buildings stand. Its hotels are in gateway cities where new rooms are hard to add: ICRA expects premium room supply to grow 5% to 6% a year through FY2028 while demand grows 8% to 9%.[26] JW Sahar in Mumbai is one example: management says it has always been an outperformer, beating its market on average rate while holding steady, very high occupancy.[7]

The second strength is cost control. Excluding apartments, EBITDA margin was 46.7% in the June 2026 quarter and 46.2% in FY26.[5] ICRA's own sample of 13 large hotel companies is expected to report operating margins of 34% to 36% in FY2027, so Chalet runs about ten points above the industry.[26] Part of the reason is that Chalet's rental income is 85% margin, but hospitality alone earned 42.6% in the June 2026 quarter, which is still high.[5]

The balance sheet is the third strength and the one that has improved most. Net debt was ₹2,041 crore against net worth of ₹3,784 crore at 30 June 2026, and the average interest cost was 7.4%.[6] In FY23 the same net debt to equity ratio was 1.67 times.[6] Net debt was ₹1,991 crore at March 2025, ₹1,921 crore at March 2026 and ₹2,041 crore at June 2026. That is a rise of about ₹120 crore in the latest quarter, which management links to a few acquisitions. Management says leverage (the debt to equity ratio) has not increased for eight quarters even though the company kept spending on new hotels and offices. Cash flow, a ₹990 crore share sale in FY25 and apartment profits paid for that spending.[7][6][21] ICRA rates the company [ICRA]AA- with a Positive outlook, and Crisil's September 2026 bulletin still shows AA- with a Stable outlook on its bank loans.[23][24]

There are four weaknesses. The first is concentration: Mumbai is about 43% of hospitality revenue and ICRA counts 45% of room inventory there.[7][23] Crisil made the same point in December 2025, saying more than half of revenue came from the Mumbai region.[25] Management says the Powai and Vashi hotels, which are more than 60% of its Mumbai inventory, had lower occupancy because of construction at Powai and renovation at Vashi. Mumbai-region occupancy fell 3.5 percentage points to 68.5%.[6][7] The second is the dependence on foreign visitors, who bring about 35% to 40% of demand and who stayed away in April and May 2026.[28][29][7] HVS Anarock reports that Indian hotels as a whole slowed in the April to June 2026 quarter, with June strong partly because June 2025 was weak.[27] The third is that reported earnings are flattered by one-offs. The fourth is ownership: the promoters have pledged almost a third of their shares, and the Airoli and Hyderabad hotels are being developed by Mindspace REIT for Chalet to lease, which the CEO calls a group company with arm's-length rent.[1][6][7]

Weighing these, the business has a real but narrow moat. It rests on good land, brand tie-ups and cost discipline, not on any technology or contract that a rival cannot copy. Indian Hotels and EIH report ROCE (return on capital employed) of 17.1% and 20.7% with borrowings of 0.25 and 0.05 times equity, so Chalet's advantage is about execution and cost, not exclusivity.[35][36] That is enough to earn mid-teen returns on capital when occupancy is high, but not enough to ignore the price paid or the pledge.

How good are the profits, really?

Net profit was ₹645 crore in FY26 against ₹142 crore in FY25, a fourfold jump.[34] That jump had three ingredients. The apartment project added ₹273 crore of EBITDA.[6] The tax charge dropped from 67% of profit to 21% because FY25 carried a large one-time deferred tax charge.[34] And the hotel and rental business itself grew: ex-apartment EBITDA rose 21% to ₹957 crore.[9]

PL Research strips out the apartments and shows FY26 profit of ₹386 crore and EPS (earnings per share) of ₹17.6, against the reported ₹29.5. At ₹802.5 that is 45.6 times the cleaner profit, which is the number to remember when someone quotes the 33.3 times trailing price-to-earnings ratio.[30][34] The headline ratio is flattered, which matters for how much we should pay.

What this means for an owner

A buyer of Chalet shares owns a good hotel and office business at a fair-to-full price, with a promoter group that has used almost a third of its stake as loan collateral. The business quality deserves a place on a watchlist. The pledge, the price and the temporary earnings boost are why we cannot buy it today. This chapter lays out five years of results and then runs the Layer 1 test, which compares the company's current levels (static) with the direction they are moving (trajectory). 6. Why these estimates might be wrong

This chapter plays devil's advocate. For each number we rely on, it names the weakest assumption, the evidence that would prove it wrong and the date by which we expect to know. Weakest assumption in the pledge conclusion: that the pledge is a stable overhang, not a hidden risk

Five promoter entities have pledged shares. Touchstone Properties and Hotels has pledged 14,400,000. Anbee Constructions and Cape Trading have each pledged 13,116,180. Raghukool Estate has pledged 3,395,680 and Capstan Trading 2,995,680.[3] The one disclosure we could read says the shares were offered as collateral security for a facility from ICICI Bank. The extract does not name the borrower.[4] The pledge was also 31.91% at 31 March 2026.[2]

The pledged shares are worth about ₹3,774 crore at ₹802.5. That is 21% of Chalet's market value. Lenders can sell pledged shares if the price falls or a loan sours. The stock trades about ₹17 crore a day on average, so the pledged stake equals roughly 220 days of normal trading.[30] The pledge has stayed near 31.9% for 11 quarters, which is reassuring. But a steady pledge says nothing about the lender's trigger price. That price is not disclosed.[3]

Weakest assumption in the FY28 EPS of ₹27.4: that Taj Delhi and CIGNUS Tower II add profit on time

Our EPS rests on EBITDA of ₹1,280 crore in FY28. Both new assets have already slipped once. Taj Delhi was guided for early Q3 FY27. It is now Q4 FY27 for at least 70 rooms.[19][12] Management also said the West Asia crisis has put pressure on labour for CIGNUS Tower II.[9] If Taj Delhi opens fully only in FY29 and CIGNUS Tower II leases slowly, FY28 EBITDA could fall near ₹1,130 crore. EPS would then be near ₹21.9. We would see this first in the Q3 and Q4 FY27 opening dates.

Weakest assumption in the 31 times multiple: that it does not compress

The multiple is close to today's but above the 26.4 peer median. Suppose the market values Chalet like Juniper (23.6), EIH (25.4) or Ventive (26.4). The same EPS of ₹27.4 then gives ₹650 to ₹725.[35][36][38][39] At the peer median of 26.4 times, the value is ₹722. That is 10.0% below ₹802.5 and 6.7% below ₹774, with no change to earnings at all. So even a cleared pledge and a ₹774 fill would not make the trade safe on a multiple stress alone. Chalet earns a higher margin and grows faster than those peers, which is a partial defence. But its 17.0% ROCE contains apartment profit that ICRA says will not repeat from FY2028.[23]

Weakest assumption in the 'funded from internal cash' claim

Management says the ₹3,000 crore capital plan for FY27 to FY29 is largely funded from internal accruals.[7] ICRA expects operating cash flow of ₹700 crore to ₹750 crore a year. It expects capital spending of ₹800 crore to ₹850 crore a year. Management cites about ₹2,000 crore of eligibility for such loans.[23][7] So net debt is unlikely to jump, but it is also unlikely to fall.

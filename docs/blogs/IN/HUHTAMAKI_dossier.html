1. What the company does

Huhtamaki India Limited, based in Thane, makes the packaging that sits around everyday products. A snack pouch, a detergent sachet, a toothpaste tube and the printed label on a bottle are all examples. The company says it runs 10 factories in India and employs about 2,400 people.[2] It reports one business segment, Consumer Packaging, and sells mainly to food, drink, home-care and personal-care brands. More than 55 percent of what it makes goes to food and beverage customers, and exports were 33.51 percent of turnover in 2025.[17][1] Its financial year is the calendar year, so the "Q3" results are for July to September 2026.[1]

How does it make money? It buys plastic films, resins, inks and paper, then prints, laminates and converts them into finished packs. Raw materials are the biggest cost: in the September 2026 quarter, materials consumed, before any change in stock, were ₹548.8 crore against revenue from operations of ₹768.8 crore, which is 71 percent.[1] Because of that, price changes in resin and film pass to customers through contracts that reset monthly or quarterly. Management describes a policy of passing raw-material changes on in both directions, so sales rise and fall with commodity prices even when the number of packs does not change.[10] That is why the sales jump in 2026 needs careful reading, and why the stock is judged on profit per rupee of sales, not on sales alone.

Who owns it? The Finnish packaging group Huhtamäki Oyj, through its Dutch holding company Huhtavefa B.V., holds 51,153,997 shares, which is 67.73 percent. None of those shares is pledged or otherwise encumbered.[19] Public holders own 30.03 percent, foreign funds 1.67 percent and Indian funds 0.57 percent.[5] The parent has lent the company an external commercial borrowing (ECB), a foreign-currency loan, of which ₹100 crore is still outstanding and due by June 2027; the parent also provides guarantees for some bank lines.[18][13] No royalty is payable to the parent under the current terms of the licence and service agreements.[17]

Why the shares rose 20 percent on 28 October 2026: what the filings show

The first question for this report was whether the 20 percent jump signals a takeover, an open offer or a delisting, because any of those would set a price and turn the stock into an event-driven case. We looked for one in the NSE announcement feed for Huhtamaki India from 1 January 2025 to 28 October 2026 (139 filings, the last at 18:11 IST on 28 October), in a published list of current takeovers and delistings dated 28 October, and in company, parent and press pages.[32][3][31] We found none. The only parent-related filings are routine shareholding disclosures, and the promoter holding is unchanged at 67.73 percent.[19] Our research record therefore lists the offer search as not found, with the queries and sources tried.

What did happen is a results sequence. The board meeting that approved the September-quarter results ended at 2:11 p.m. on 27 October, and the filing reached NSE at about 2:30 p.m.[1] Sales of products were ₹748.1 crore, up 23.7 percent, but profit after tax fell 19.1 percent to ₹29.7 crore from ₹36.8 crore, because of a ₹9.6 crore restructuring charge for the Baddi label plant and flat operating profit.[1][2][33] The shares had already fallen 10.9 percent in the week to 23 October, closing at ₹211.55 that Friday.[28] The stock fell about 11 percent that day, to ₹191.31 on about 21.4 lakh shares in a snapshot of the day.[27][40] On 28 October it rose 20.0 percent, the full band, to ₹229.57 on 17.9 lakh shares, which is 5.3 times its 20-day average volume.[26][40] That price is 6 percent above the 216.55 level it traded at before the results.[27]

The company has not said why the stock reversed, and the exchange has not raised a volume query in the feed we read. The press release adds one fact that the headline numbers hide: operating profit before one-off items was up 18 percent on a comparable base, which means the same quarter a year earlier with one-off items removed, and nine-month operating profit was up 22 percent.[2] We cannot prove that this re-reading drove the rebound. The pattern of heavy trading is not new: the stock nearly doubled in the month to late July 2026, and several July bulk deals were the same accounts buying and selling the same shares on the same day, which looks like short-term trading.[10][29] A year ago the stock also "zoomed 15%" after its Q3 results.[30]

What this means for an owner: there is no offer price, no floor price, no tendering window and no regulatory approval to track, so we assign no probability to an offer and do not size the scenarios around one. A parent that already holds 67.73 percent could act at some point, and on the July call management declined to answer a direct question about the promoter selling stock, saying to ask the parent.[10] That is an unpriced possibility, not a filed event. If an offer filing appears, the verdict is void and the work restarts from the offer price. The parent reports its own third-quarter figures on 29 October, and the company holds its call on 30 October at 3:30 p.m., so the facts could change within two days.[31][4]

Plain-English glossary for this report

  • Flexible packaging: bags, pouches and films that bend, as opposed to bottles or boxes. It is lighter and cheaper to ship, so it takes share from rigid packs.
  • External commercial borrowing (ECB): a loan from a foreign lender. Here the lender is a sister company of the parent.
  • Exceptional item: a one-time gain or cost that the company reports on its own line, such as the ₹9.6 crore Baddi closure charge.[1]
  • Pass-through: raising prices when raw materials cost more. It protects the margin but also inflates sales.
  • Open offer: a public bid, made under takeover rules, to buy shares from the public at a stated price. None is on file for this company.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The business has three real strengths. It has a global parent that supplies product development and technology, and Crisil, the rating agency, cites that support in rating the company AA-/Stable.[18] It has little debt and a lot of cash: at 30 June 2026 cash, bank deposits and mutual funds were ₹395.9 crore against borrowings of ₹104.4 crore, a net cash position of ₹291.5 crore before lease liabilities.[8] And in the 2026 supply scare it behaved like the safe supplier: management says customers chose partners with a wide footprint and secure raw-material supply, and sales rose 23.1 percent in the June quarter.[10][9][42]

The weaknesses are just as clear. Sales over five years have not grown: Screener shows a five-year sales growth rate of 0 percent and a three-year rate of minus 6 percent to the end of 2025, and return on equity of 8.79 percent over three years.[5] Operating margins sat at 9 to 11 percent from 2014 to 2020, then fell to 4.2 to 5 percent in 2021, 2022 and 2024 before recovering to 8 percent in 2025.[5] The company has also said that its 2025 volume was flat to lower and that it chose to walk away from low-margin business.[14][15] A packaging converter with no pricing power on resin and fierce competition earns its living from efficiency and mix, so the question is whether the 2026 gains are structural.

Margins: what changed, and how much of it is likely to last

Operating profit before one-off items (EBIT, using the company's definition of profit before exceptional items plus finance cost) was 7.2 percent of revenue over the first nine months of 2026, against 6.8 percent a year earlier.[1] Quarter by quarter it was 6.3 percent in March (after an ₹8.8 crore catch-up depreciation charge), 8.3 percent in June and 6.8 percent in September.[1][9] The June peak was helped by price rises that ran ahead of the cost of the cheaper stock in inventory; management said most of the commodity impact came in the second quarter and that it would not expect 23 percent growth every quarter.[10] In September, sales rose 23.7 percent but EBIT was flat at ₹52.1 crore, so the margin fell back.[2]

Where do the gains come from? Management points to three roughly equal sources in Q2: volume, price and mix, with volume growth in "high single digits".[10] It also credits productivity work, a decision to drop low-margin customers, and growth in home-care packs.[14][10] Crisil measured its own operating margin, which is operating profit as a share of sales, at 7.7 percent for 2025 against 4.6 percent for 2024 and 8.4 percent for the first half of 2026 against 6.4 percent a year earlier, which agrees with the company on direction.[18] Our reading is that part of the gain is real and repeatable (cost and mix), and part (customer stockpiling and the lag between resin prices and selling prices) is not.

Quality of earnings: other income, depreciation and a prior-year error

Other income is a large part of profit. In the nine months to September 2026 it was ₹29.4 crore against profit before exceptional items of ₹142.9 crore, which is 21 percent, and in the March quarter alone it was ₹22.1 crore.[1][11] It is mostly interest on cash and fund gains, so it lasts as long as the cash is held and rates stay high. Depreciation in the March quarter included an ₹8.8 crore catch-up charge: for 2024 and 2025 the company had calculated depreciation on some assets by the written-down-value method instead of its stated straight-line policy, and it booked the whole difference in 2026 after finding the error.[1][13] Management judged the amounts not material to either year. We treat the error as a small control weakness that has been fixed, and we score it in the governance KPI.

Contingent liabilities, the claims the company disputes, were mostly old excise and service tax appeals; the "others" line rose to ₹16.1 crore at December 2025 from ₹0.7 crore a year earlier, and management says none is expected to have a material effect.[17] A small penalty of ₹17.5 lakh for a delayed occupancy certificate at the Silvassa plant was disclosed in September 2026.[21] None of these is large against net worth of ₹1,349 crore, so they matter only as a pattern.[8]

The parent: support, and the question of an overhang

Having a 67.73 percent owner that is a global packaging group is a strength for technology and credit, and Crisil says it applied a parent notch-up in its rating.[18] It also means the free float is only 30.03 percent, which is thin, and the stock has had violent days on small volumes.[5][29] A parent can sell down, buy more, or leave things alone; nothing in the filings points to a move, and the company's own clarification of a July 2025 press report about a large acquisition push called the report speculative.[24] We treat the parent as a stable holder for the base case and flag a parent sale as a risk in Chapter 6.

How it was calculated

Calendar 2027 earnings per share of ₹20.12

Peers with returns near or below 12 percent trade between 6.05 and 18.6 times, so the uncertainty is wide, and a re-rating of the peer group would move our number by ₹20 a share for each turn of multiple.[36][35][37] Evidence against it would be a quarter in which profit rises and the stock falls, or EPL and Cosmo First re-rating down. The strongest case against our view (red team)

The bear case is simple. Sales growth of 23.7 percent produced flat operating profit and a 19 percent fall in profit after tax, which means the extra sales came with no extra profit.[1][2] The 20 percent rebound on 28 October followed an 11 percent fall the day before and happened on a stock where heavy trading is common: the price nearly doubled in a month to July and has fallen 30 percent from its high since.[10][40][29] If the 2026 profit is partly a gift of customer stockpiling and price lag, then trailing earnings of ₹18 overstate normal earnings by a third or more, and 14 times normal earnings of ₹12 to ₹14 is about ₹170 to ₹195, below today's price.

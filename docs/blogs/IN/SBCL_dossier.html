1. What the company does

In plain words: SBCL makes small metal parts that sense heat and electric current. Most of its sales are to electrical and electronics makers, and many go abroad.

Shivalik Bimetal Controls Limited, founded in 1984 and based at Chambaghat near Solan in Himachal Pradesh, makes small precision metal parts that sense heat and current. Three product lines carry the business. Shunt resistors (current-sensing parts) were 40.3% of the FY26 segment total (₹572.87 crore, a little above group revenue), thermostatic bimetal strips (heat-sensing parts) 40.4% and electrical contacts (the switching tips inside circuit breakers and relays) 19.4%.[1] A fourth line, busbar connectors and circuit-board assemblies built at a new Pune plant, is only starting.[4]

Customers are makers of resistors, smart meters, switchgear, battery packs and appliances. The company says it serves more than 300 customers from about 1,000 staff, and the annual report says exports were about 57% of FY26 revenue across 38 countries, which matches the parent's revenue by our arithmetic and not the group's.[1][4] The largest single customer was 17.54% of group revenue in FY26, up from 13.47% a year earlier.[1] Management says that share was once 35% to 40%, which it described as giving it sleepless nights.[5]

SBCL earns money by joining unlike metals to tight tolerances, using electron-beam welding (a vacuum welding method) and diffusion bonding (pressing metals together under heat). Know-how, not scale, is the product. The company is moving from selling bare strip by weight to selling finished parts by piece, which raises what it earns per kilogram of metal.[1][6] Group EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) was 22.9% of FY26 revenue.[1]

The parent is joined by wholly owned subsidiaries: Shivalik Engineered Products (electrical contacts), Shivalik Bimetal Engineers and Shivalik Bimetals Europe in Italy. It also holds 16.01% of a joint venture, Innovative Clad Solutions.[1] This report uses group (consolidated) figures as the main basis and shows standalone figures, which cover the parent only, where the two differ. Prices in this report are for the 23 October 2026 session. The run date is 26 October, because the benchmark has no later bar yet.[32]

2. Why the business is strong or weak

In plain words: It is hard to copy in its niche and has almost no debt. The weak points are cash tied up in unpaid bills and stock, a heavy premium price and a run of staff and auditor changes.

The business is strong where it is hard to copy and weaker where it depends on a few buyers and on metal prices. CRISIL, the rating agency, reaffirmed SBCL at CRISIL A/Stable in March 2026 on a loan limit of Rs. 115 crore, citing leading market position in a niche with negligible competition and a strong financial risk profile, partly offset by moderate scale.[20][21] That is an independent view of the strengths below.

What makes it hard to copy

The annual report says the company carries 77 proprietary bimetal grades and that customers re-qualify a supplier over about 24 months.[1] A buyer that has designed SBCL's part into a meter or a breaker does not switch quickly. In shunts, management says it makes only electron-beam-welded strip shunts, a small slice of the many shunt types in the market, and that accuracy, not price, decides who is chosen.[5] The Solan plants can support more than ₹1,300 crore of revenue, against ₹570.86 crore in FY26, so about 44% of that stated capacity is in use on that arithmetic.[1]

What follows for a reader is that the case rests on process know-how and customer designs rather than on size. It does not protect the share price: a business can be hard to copy and still be bought at too high a price, which is the question the later chapters take up.

Where the growth comes from, and how good it is

Growth in the latest quarter was strong but is mixed in quality. Group revenue rose 33.4% to ₹182.2 crore in the June quarter, yet standalone volume fell 2.5% and standalone revenue rose 13.0%.[4] Asked about silver, management said about half of the revenue growth could be attributed to it, and the silver price is nearly double a year ago; it gave no group-wide split.[5] Because silver is passed on to customers with little margin, the group gross margin, the share of sales left after direct costs, slipped 119 basis points (1.19 percentage points) to 43.4% even as EBITDA margin held at 23.7%.[4] Growth in profit is therefore more reliable than growth in sales.

The shift from strip to components is real. In shunts the component share of supply rose from about 55% to about 65% in a year, lifting realisation per kilogram by 10% to 12%, according to the chief financial officer.[6] In the June quarter strip sales in shunts fell to about one-third of the year-earlier level, according to management.[5] The annual report shows the same pattern in FY26: bimetal volume rose 0.8% and revenue 2.85%, and shunt volume fell 2.2% while revenue rose 8.62%.[1]

What weakens the case

Four things weaken it. First, the scale is modest and CRISIL names it as an offset.[21] Second, much cash is tied up in working capital, the money locked in unpaid bills and stock: standalone net working capital rose from 218 to 258 days in FY26.[1] Third, Asia fell 12.7% in the June quarter and bimetals grew only 7.4%.[4] Fourth, between 6 August and 7 September the company announced the exit of its chief financial officer and of its company secretary, a change of statutory auditor, the death of an independent director and a minor insider-trading code breach, set out in Chapter 3. None of these is a fraud finding; together they thin the team during a period of fast growth.[11][12][17][18][19]

Against peers the share trades at a large premium. On FY26 profit per share and the 23 October closes (daily price bars, note 36), SBCL is at 54.3 times, against 11.5 times for Modison, 12.1 times for Genus Power and 19.4 times for Salzer Electronics. Return on capital employed (profit before interest and tax over the money invested) of 25.67% is high, but Modison's is higher at 31.0%, so quality alone does not explain the gap.[1][29][30][31] The scorecard in the next chapter tests whether the business itself is working.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

In plain words: We push each assumption until it strains. The valuation rests on whether the market keeps paying a multiple above its median, the wait also rests on results, cash and governance checks, and every stress we model lowers value from a base that is already at the price.

Weakest assumption in the profit estimate: margin holds while the mix shifts

Our FY28E margin of 23.0% assumes lower-margin assemblies and silver-heavy contacts do not pull the blended figure down further. Weakest assumption in the cash view: working capital eases

Receivables rose 39.5% in FY26 and stock rose a further ₹26.59 crore in the June quarter.

Before you read

SPR Auto Technologies (NSE: SHRIPISTON, once Shriram Pistons & Rings) is India's largest maker of engine pistons, rings and valves, and it added car interiors in January 2026[36][8]. Sales rose 53 percent in the June 2026 quarter, but management says the old business grew about 16 percent, and profit rose just 9 percent[3][5]. Our verdict is REJECTED for now, because only three of five operating checks pass and the reward is 1.99 times the risk at ₹4,629.90[1]. That can change after the results due on 30 October 2026, if the margin recovers, and then only on a pullback to about ₹4,466[18]. We could be wrong if the franchise, with a 48.4 percent market share and no pledged shares, keeps lifting the price[12].

1. What the company does

SPR makes the parts inside a combustion engine that wear out and must be exact. Pistons are the metal plugs that slide up and down in each cylinder. Piston rings are thin seals that stop gas and oil leaking past the piston. The company also makes piston pins and engine valves.[4] The legal name is SPR Auto Technologies Limited. It was renamed from Shriram Pistons & Rings Limited on 2 April 2026. The NSE symbol stayed SHRIPISTON. The BSE code is 544344 and the ISIN is INE526E01018.[36][15] It sells to vehicle makers, known as original equipment makers or OEMs, and to the replacement market under the SPR and USHA brands. More than 1,200 business partners and 22 logistics centres serve customers in over 30 countries.[4] Fourteen plants and eight assembly units make the products. They stand in Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and Haryana.[4]

The money model is simple. Every piston is designed for one engine. Carmakers take three to five years to validate a new supplier. SPR keeps more than 2,000 variants and up to 18,000 catalogue items. A rival cannot copy the metal and win the order the next day.[21] But no customer is committed to buy any quantity. Volumes follow what the vehicle maker builds. The ten largest customers gave 46.68 percent of FY26 revenue, down from 51.23 percent in FY24.[12] Aluminium and other metals are priced into each part through a formula that lags by about one quarter. Management says the formula covers 100 percent of the change. The lag is why margin dips when metal prices jump.[6][5] SPR also pays its technology partners a royalty. They are Kolbenschmidt of Germany for pistons, Riken of Japan for rings, Fuji Oozx for valves, Honda Foundry, Takahata and Antolin. The royalty was ₹69.26 crore in FY26 on a consolidated basis, about 1.6 percent of revenue by our arithmetic.[11][4]

The group is no longer only a piston maker. Between January 2023 and December 2024 it bought control of two precision plastic-moulding firms, Takahata and TGPEL, and an electric-motor start-up, EMFi. In April 2025 it bought a mould-maker, Karna.[4] On 8 January 2026 it bought three Antolin India companies. They make car roof linings, door panels, sun visors and cabin lighting. The enterprise value was EUR 159 million, about ₹1,670 crore, on a debt-free, cash-free basis. SPR paid with ₹1,000 crore of non-convertible debentures, which are bonds that cannot be turned into shares.[7][8][16] Management says products that work with any engine type are now over 35 percent of consolidated income. It says about 60 percent of the business is not directly hit by electric vehicles.[5]

An owner of SPR shares owns a dominant, cash-generating but slowly growing engine-parts franchise. Plain-English glossary for this report

  • EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) is operating profit before financing and accounting charges. As a margin, it is EBITDA divided by sales.
  • EPS (earnings per share) is net profit divided by the number of shares. It falls when new shares are issued, unless profit rises as fast.
  • ROCE (return on capital employed) is operating profit before interest divided by shareholders' money plus borrowings. It shows what each rupee of capital earns.
  • Dilution is the fall in each existing owner's share of the company when new shares are issued.
  • Working capital is the cash tied up in stock and unpaid customer bills, less what the company owes its suppliers.
  • Capital expenditure is spending on plants and machines that last for years, as opposed to day-to-day costs.
  • Net debt is borrowings minus cash and bank deposits.
  • Debt-to-equity is total borrowings divided by shareholders' money. A figure of 1.0 means one rupee of debt for each rupee of owners' capital.
  • Free cash flow is the cash from operations minus capital expenditure. Its short form, FCF (free cash flow), is the cash left for owners and lenders.
  • Cash flow from operations is the cash the business itself generates before investing and financing.
  • Price-to-earnings is the share price divided by earnings per share. It says how many years of current profit the price pays for.
  • Guidance is the company's own published forecast for a coming period.
  • Consensus estimate is the average of analysts' forecasts for a number.
  • Gross margin is sales minus the cost of materials, as a share of sales. Operating margin subtracts all running costs too. Net margin subtracts everything, including interest and tax.
  • Stage 2 is the uptrend phase of the four-stage Weinstein chart scale. Stage 4 is the downtrend phase.
  • The 20-day moving average, 50-day moving average and 200-day moving average are the average closing prices over those many trading days. A price tends to bounce from these slow lines.
  • RSI (relative strength index) is a 0 to 100 meter of how fast the price has been rising. ATR (average true range) is the average daily price swing in rupees.
  • A base is a sideways stretch where a price rests before a move. Its top is the pivot. A breakout is a close above the pivot on heavy volume. A retest is a pullback to that level from above.
  • A stop loss is the price at which the idea is judged wrong and the position is cut. A limit order is an order that fills only at your price or better. The entry zone is the band of prices where buying is planned.
  • Asymmetry, also called reward-to-risk, is the gain to the target divided by the loss to the stop. A measured move projects the height of a base above its pivot as a price target.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The first source of strength is share. CRISIL wrote the industry report for the August 2026 share sale. It puts SPR at 48.4 percent of the Indian market for piston assemblies and engine valves in FY26. SPR's domestic OEM sales were ₹2,442 crore against an OEM market of ₹5,050 crore.[12] Motilal Oswal estimates the nearest rival, Federal-Mogul, at about 29 percent of piston supply to carmakers. It puts that rival's EBITDA margin near 15 percent against SPR's 21 percent on pistons. It says SPR leads the replacement market with about 41 percent.[21] Global players have stepped back. Rheinmetall, the world's second-largest piston maker, sold its 20 percent stake in SPR in March 2024. An analyst on the August 2026 call said Eaton, an American valve maker, had closed a US engine-valve plant. Management did not dispute it.[21][5] Fewer foreign rivals helps price and volume. But a rival leaving is a one-time gain, not a growth rate.

The second source of strength is technology access. SPR's partners are Kolbenschmidt for pistons, Riken for rings, Fuji Oozx for valves, Honda Foundry, Takahata for plastics and Antolin for interiors. The chairman of NPR-Riken and the head of Kolbenschmidt Pistons sit on SPR's board.[4][11] That is a strength, because the partners supply designs SPR would take years to copy. It is also a weakness. The placement document lists termination or non-renewal of these licences as a risk. The royalty rose from ₹55.49 crore in FY25 to ₹69.26 crore in FY26 on a consolidated basis.[12][11] On the Antolin licence, management says SPR pays only a 'nominal royalty'. It declined to say what the three companies paid their former parent.[6]

The third source is demand. Indian vehicle sales hit a record in July to September 2026. SIAM reports passenger vehicles up 30.7 percent to 1.36 million units. News reports put total domestic sales up 18.2 percent to 83.88 lakh units. September wholesale dispatches alone rose 14.7 percent to 29,47,029 units.[23][24][25] Management credits the goods and services tax reform called GST 2.0, which it dates to about October 2025. It says the reform lifted demand for smaller vehicles. It expects the volume momentum to continue.[5] Vehicle makers are building hybrid and flex-fuel engines, which still need pistons. Management says it has programmes that reach the market in 2029 and 2030.[6] Against that, management expects electric vehicles to reach 15 to 20 percent of sales within five years. So the combustion business should grow slower than the vehicle market over time.[9]

Where the moat is thin

The moat is thin where the numbers are not the franchise. SPR's own placement document says its core business profit grew 5.08 percent a year between FY24 and FY26. Revenue grew 20.14 percent a year. It adds that a substantial part of the revenue growth came from acquisitions rather than organic expansion.[12] The same document says a 48.4 percent share is hard to grow and easy to lose. It notes that OEMs can demand yearly price cuts. It says customers have made no purchase commitments.[12] Export revenue was ₹4,964 million in FY26. That is only 11.13 percent of revenue, against 16.78 percent in FY24. Management says exports to Europe, America and the Middle East are 'still quite affected'.[12][5]

Goodwill is the amount paid above the book value of the businesses bought. It is now ₹1,322.6 crore, or 21.5 percent of total assets. The auditors list business-combination accounting and goodwill impairment among their key audit matters. A failed acquisition would show up as a charge against profit.[12] A tax authority in Ghaziabad has also proposed recovering ₹248.06 crore of goods and services tax, plus interest and penalty. It alleges under-declaration of taxable value. The Allahabad High Court stayed the notice on 12 August 2025. The matter is pending.[12] The amount equals 44 percent of FY26 net profit and 8.2 percent of total equity, by our arithmetic. The audited contingent-liability table lists only ₹10.66 crore for this tax. So the notice is a real tail risk that is not in that table.[12]

What this means for an owner

The franchise is strong in the way that matters to an owner. It earns a 21 to 23 percent margin. It has no pledged promoter shares. It holds an AA+ rating from India Ratings, upgraded in February 2026.[4][12][27] Its weak points are growth that is bought rather than earned, a customer list that cannot say no to price cuts, and a tax notice that is stayed but not closed. 3. The numbers, in plain words

The five-year record is that of a business that doubled its sales and more than tripled its profit while keeping a clean balance sheet. That held until FY26, when it spent ₹1,670 crore on one acquisition. Consolidated sales grew from ₹2,065 crore in FY22 to ₹4,459 crore in FY26. That is 21.2 percent a year by our arithmetic. Net profit grew from ₹164 crore to ₹561 crore, or 36.0 percent a year.[2] Most of the profit jump came in FY23 and FY24, as the operating margin rose from 15 to 21 percent. The margin has held at 20 percent since. Sales for the twelve months to June 2026 were ₹4,970 crore and profit was ₹575 crore. So profit is now growing far more slowly than sales.[2]

Three facts shape how the numbers should be read. First, FY26 includes the Antolin entities for about three months of ownership. The twelve months to June 2026 include about six months. So the 35 percent sales growth over the trailing year is mostly bought. The 25 percent revenue growth in FY26 is a mix of bought and organic.[12][2] Second, borrowings rose from ₹508 crore to ₹1,968 crore in FY26 on the Screener basis, which includes lease liabilities. Interest cost rose from ₹34 crore to ₹87 crore on a trailing basis. That is part of why trailing profit grew only 11 percent.[2] Third, the company's own return on capital employed fell from 24.06 percent in FY25 to 17.19 percent in FY26. The Antolin price entered capital in full, while its profit entered for only part of the year.[12]

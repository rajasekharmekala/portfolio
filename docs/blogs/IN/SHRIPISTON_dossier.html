1. What the company does

SPR Auto Technologies Ltd was incorporated in 1963 and traded for decades as Shriram Pistons & Rings. A piston is the metal plug that moves up and down inside an engine cylinder; piston rings seal the gap between the plug and the cylinder wall; engine valves let air in and exhaust out. Every petrol, diesel, CNG and hybrid engine needs all three. The company sells them to vehicle makers (called original equipment manufacturers, or OEMs), to the spare-parts market, and to buyers of industrial engines, railways and defence equipment[10]. Its name changed to SPR Auto Technologies in April 2026[14].

The core business is the cash engine. A market-share estimate from Motilal Oswal puts the company above 45 percent of the Indian piston market[29]. Exports go to more than 30 countries and are about 14 percent of standalone revenue[14]. In Q1 FY27 the standalone company, which holds the legacy engine-parts operations, earned total income of ₹9,627 million at a 21.0 percent operating margin[10]. That is high for an auto-parts maker. It is also lower than the 23.5 percent of a year earlier[10]. Analysts call the profit behind this margin EBITDA, which stands for earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization.

Since 2023 the company has used its cash to buy businesses that do not depend on the type of engine. SPR Takahata and SPR TGPEL mould precision plastic parts. SPR EMF Innovations (EMFi) builds motors and controllers for electric two-wheelers and other vehicles. On 8 January 2026 the company bought three Indian companies of Spain's Grupo Antolin, which make car interiors such as headliners, sun visors, door panels and ambient lighting, for an enterprise value of €159 million, about ₹1,670 crore[20]. Management says these businesses now supply more than 35 percent of consolidated income[11].

How does the company make money? A vehicle maker awards a multi-year supply programme for a model. The company builds the part to the maker's drawing, ships it in monthly lots, and earns a margin over metal, power and labour costs. A part that sits in thousands of vehicles per day produces steady cash. The risk is that the maker can pass raw-material swings back only after a delay, which management called a one-quarter lag in the latest call[11][12]. A second money source is the spare-parts channel, which sells under the SPR and USHA brands through 1,200-plus business partners[9].

Where the growth number comes from

Group total income rose 51.2 percent in Q1 FY27[9]. Most of that is bought growth. The Antolin companies had annual sales of about ₹1,179 crore in FY25[25] and joined the group in January. Standalone income grew only 11.7 percent[10], so about four-fifths of the ₹5,075 million rise in group income came from subsidiaries, mostly the new interiors business, rather than from faster sales of the old products (our arithmetic from two cited figures). A reader should treat the headline growth rate as a measure of acquisitions made, and judge the business on the standalone figure and on the margin of the bought companies, which the later chapters take in turn.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

The competitive position rests on three things: scale, technology partners and customer ties. Pistons are precision parts that must pass long engine-validation tests, so a vehicle maker rarely changes supplier within a model's life. The company works with partners such as Kolbenschmidt, Riken, Honda Foundry, Fuji Oozx, Takahata and Antolin[9]. Motilal Oswal estimates the piston business earns about 500 basis points more margin than its closest peer and a return on core capital above 50 percent[29]. One basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point.

The evidence in the filings supports the margin claim. Standalone operating profit, including other income, was 23.3 percent of income in FY26 and 21.0 percent in Q1 FY27[19][10]. The consolidated operating margin was 20 percent in FY26[3]. Margin is only part of the story, though: the returns picture has weakened as the company bought assets. Return on capital employed (ROCE) was 28 percent in FY24 and 21 percent in FY26[6], because ₹1,670 crore of Antolin purchase price and ₹1,368 crore of goodwill now sit in the capital employed[20][31].

The weaknesses are as clear. First, the legacy product is tied to combustion engines. Management states that nearly 60 percent of the business is insulated from electric-vehicle growth and that powertrain-agnostic lines are above 35 percent of income[11], which also means that about two-thirds of income still comes from the standalone parts. Second, exports are described as badly affected in Europe, the US and the Middle East[11]. Third, in Q1 FY27 the company's production volumes rose about 22 percent while revenue rose about 12 percent, because the mix moved to smaller, lower-value vehicles[13]. More parts shipped for less money per part is a pricing warning, not a strength.

On balance the moat is real in pistons and unproven in interiors. The interiors business sells to passenger-car makers named on the acquisition call, works under a technology licence from Grupo Antolin, and had, according to an analyst on that call, a ten-year record of high single-digit margins and 12 to 13 percent returns on capital[25][24]. The next chapter scores the operating measures that show whether the bought businesses are closing that gap.

Capital structure and who owns the company

Promoters held 43.75 percent through June 2026 and 41.55 percent at September 2026[7]. The fall of 2.20 percentage points is dilution: the company issued 23,36,448 new shares at ₹4,280 to 45 institutions in the August placement, which lifted the share count to 4,63,86,272[16]. It is not selling by the promoter, and promoters cannot take part in a placement[22]. No significant promoter pledge is reported[14][32]. Foreign institutions held 7.41 percent and domestic institutions 16.52 percent at September 2026[7].

The company also carries two bonds of ₹500 crore each, issued on 23 February 2026 and maturing in August 2027 and February 2028[8]. Management says it plans to repay them on time and not early[22]. Those dates matter for profit, because the bond interest stays in the income statement until then.

Management walk-the-talk: past promises vs delivered reality

Management credibility is measured by comparing what was said a year or two ago with what was filed since. The record is mixed but not poor. Execution on the Antolin purchase was on time. The January 2026 closing matched what management said on the December 2025 call[24][20]. Here guidance means the company's own forecast of its future results. The weaker marks are on dated operating promises and on capital messaging.

Two items stand out. The Coimbatore motor plant was promised for the end of September 2025[27]. Management's later description places the start in December 2025[13]. The electric-motor unit was guided to grow five to seven times in FY26[26]. It was later described as having doubled, with FY26 sales near ₹88 crore[13][15]. Small bases make big percentages easy to promise, so the rupee impact is small. The pattern is still optimism on timing and size.

On capital, the December 2025 call said debt-to-equity would not go beyond 0.3 to 0.4 times[24]. Net debt to equity was 0.2 times in June 2026[11]. That meets the statement on a net basis. Gross borrowings divided by equity were 0.68 at 31 March 2026, on our arithmetic from the balance sheet[4]. The share placement was described in May as not meant to repay loans[22]. In August it was described as a mix of investments, debt repayment and acquisitions[13]. The answer changed within a quarter. So we do not assume a fixed use for the ₹1,000 crore.

The number

We estimate FY28 earnings per share (EPS) at ₹182.0 on 4.64 crore shares, against ₹189.3 in the Motilal Oswal model[31].

How it was calculated

We start with the Motilal Oswal FY28 adjusted profit of ₹8,781 million, remove ₹450 million of finance cost that the model assumes disappears too early, tax-effect it at 25 percent (a ₹337.5 million cut) and divide ₹8,443.5 million by 46.386 million shares.

The inputs

Adjusted profit ₹8,781 million and share count 46.4 million (Motilal Oswal)[31]; bonds of ₹500 crore each due August 2027 and February 2028[8]; management intent to repay on time[22]; finance cost elevated by ₹252 million in Q1 FY27 for the acquisition[10]. Pro rata, the ₹252 million a quarter on two bonds runs to about ₹636 million in FY28; we use ₹450 million because part of the cost is bank debt that may be repaid sooner. The model's FY28 finance cost is only ₹141 million[31].

The range

On the Motilal Oswal numbers with no cut, EPS is ₹189.3. For the bear case we start from the broker's own bear case, which has revenue growing 18.6 percent a year to FY29E, an FY28 operating margin of 17.6 percent before other income, adjusted profit of ₹7,869 million and EPS of ₹169.7[30]. We then apply the same after-tax finance cost cut of ₹337.5 million: (7,869 - 337.5) divided by 46.386 million shares gives ₹162.4. Each ₹1 of EPS is worth about ₹27 of price at our 27.3 times multiple.

If the bonds are repaid early from placement cash, finance cost falls and EPS moves toward ₹189, which lifts our central target by about ₹200 and the asymmetry at the zone high to about 2.6 to 1. If margins are weaker than the model, EPS falls and the zone moves lower.

Momentum and turnaround inflection gate

The momentum gate runs three tests on the frozen price snapshot. Test A asks if the stock is already in a confirmed uptrend. That means a price above a rising 50-day average, leading the index and close to support. 6. Why these estimates might be wrong

Every estimate in this report rests on assumptions that can fail. This chapter lists the weakest ones, gives the red-team bear case, names the one claim the verdict hinges on, and says when each will be tested. The next filing is the Q2 FY27 result on 30 October 2026, with the earnings call on 2 November 2026[8].

Weakest assumption 1: margins recover

Margins can be measured two ways, and the report keeps them apart. The first is operating margin before other income, which is what our model and the broker model use. It was 17.5 percent in Q1 FY27 (₹258 crore on sales of ₹1,474 crore)[2], and the base case needs about 18.7 percent in FY28[31]. The second is the company's EBITDA margin, which includes other income. It was 18.9 percent in Q1[9]. The gap is about 1.4 points. Management says the one-quarter lag in passing on commodity cost normalises in Q2 FY27[11][12]. If commodity inflation continues, the lag repeats each quarter. Tijori's watch level of 18.5 percent applies to the including-other-income figure[15]. Q2 results on 30 October will show the first test.

Weakest assumption 2: the placement cash is used well

The company raised about ₹1,000 crore at ₹4,280 and says the cash is for debt repayment, investments and acquisitions[16][13]. It has no stated threshold for deal size, which it described as ₹300 to ₹1,500 crore in the latest call[13]. The Antolin purchase has already placed ₹1,368 crore of goodwill on the balance sheet, equal to 22 percent of total assets by our arithmetic[31]. A second large purchase at a similar price would repeat the pattern of falling return on capital, which went from 28 percent in FY24 to 21 percent in FY26[6].

Weakest assumption 3: the premium multiple holds

The stock trades at 35.0 times trailing profit on our basis (35.6 on Screener) against a 21.7-times median over the last 2.4 years, a 61 percent premium that Tijori also flags against its three-year range[1][14]. Our base case assumes 27.3 times on FY28E, which already allows for a lower multiple, and the bear case uses 21.7 times. Hold EPS at ₹182.0 and let the multiple fall to 21.7 times, and the price is ₹3,949.40. That is 11.73 percent below the ₹4,474.40 close and 7.51 percent below the ₹4,270 zone high.

Red-team bear case

The strongest argument against buying is this. Standalone profit after tax fell 13.8 percent in Q1 and 2.5 percent in Q4 FY26[10][19], so the legacy engine that earns the cash is not growing profit. Reported growth is acquired growth. An analyst on the acquisition call put the price at 15 to 16 times FY25 operating profit[24], and management declined to give a steady-state margin target for Antolin[24]. Exports are weak, the legacy parts depend on combustion engines, and the company has just raised equity at a price 3.56 percent below the floor[16]. If margin recovery stalls, the stock can fall to the median multiple on lower earnings, which is the ₹3,520 bear case.

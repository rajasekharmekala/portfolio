Scope and evidence

The research question is whether CYIENT can be admitted to the Indian tactical-momentum shortlist under the published binary rules. The fresh ledger is authoritative for the 8 October 2026 market session; it was generated at 01:59:36 on 9 October 2026 and identifies the source as Yahoo Finance daily OHLCV with arithmetic moving averages and Wilder RSI/ATR.[17][18]

Operating history

Cyient began as Infotech Enterprises in 1991 and built a specialist engineering-services identity rather than a mass-market software identity. The company’s public materials describe work that connects product engineering, network engineering, data and digital capabilities with industry workflows. The long history is relevant because domain libraries, certification experience and embedded customer processes can become an economic moat, but only when they persist through programme changes and pricing cycles.[1][23][24][25]

The portfolio has expanded across aerospace and defence, communications, semiconductor, utilities, transportation and industrial customers. Each vertical has a different demand clock. Aircraft engineering tends to be tied to certification and build rates; communications follows network investment; semiconductors follows design wins and product cycles. A diversified vertical list reduces single-cycle risk only if no individual customer, programme or geography dominates the work.[1][22][25]

FY25 disclosures described revenue of ₹5,816 crore, EBIT margin of 13.5%, net profit of ₹605 crore, order intake of US$836.1 million and free cash flow of ₹801 crore. These figures show that cash generation remained a strength even as operating profitability weakened. The relevant consequence for an owner is that the franchise can fund itself, but cash strength alone does not repair falling return on capital.[1][19]

The Screener annual series places FY26 consolidated sales at ₹7,268 crore and PAT at ₹463 crore, with FY26 OPM at 12%. The FY26 series is the latest available in the fetched table, but the requested run date is a future-dated market session relative to many issuer documents; the dossier labels the market facts separately and does not pretend that an FY27 audited report is available.[10][11]

Business and unit economics

A digital-engineering company converts three inputs into revenue: specialist people, reusable intellectual property and customer access. People hours are the largest variable input; the company earns more when scarce skills are assigned to billable programmes and less when recruitment outruns demand. Reusable methods and software improve the revenue produced per engineer, while customer access determines whether the work is recurring, expanding or one-off.[1][23][24]

Aerospace work can be attractive because qualification, traceability and safety processes create barriers to entry. It is also unforgiving: an engineering error can damage the customer relationship, and a programme delay can defer revenue without reducing payroll. Communications engineering can produce repeat work during network upgrades, but it is exposed to carrier capital-spending cycles. Semiconductor design has higher technical scarcity but can be lumpy until a design win reaches production.[25][26][27]

The available evidence does not verify billable utilization, attrition, revenue per employee, client concentration or the percentage of recurring revenue. Each is therefore N/A. Those gaps prevent a full unit-economics pass: margin recovery could come from utilization, pricing, mix or cost cuts, but the current file does not allow the analyst to distinguish among them. The trigger is a reported utilization improvement accompanied by stable or better EBIT margin, not a cost action alone.[1][2][3]

Supply chain and customer risk

Cyient’s direct supply chain is primarily human capital, software tools, cloud and data infrastructure, offices, certification systems and subcontracted specialist capacity. It is less inventory-intensive than manufacturing, but that does not make working capital irrelevant. Debtors, unbilled revenue and contract assets determine how quickly engineering effort becomes cash. The fetched ratios show debtor days of 66, inventory days of 303 and working-capital days of 68 for FY26; inventory is unusual for a services business and deserves a note-level explanation in the next annual report.[15]

Customer risk is a portfolio problem. A large aerospace customer can provide long visibility but also possess purchasing power; a telecom customer can offer scale but demand price concessions. The company’s sector spread reduces dependence on one cycle, while the absence of verified concentration data prevents a confident claim that it reduces dependence on one customer. The correct posture is to treat concentration as an unresolved risk, not as a hidden moat.[1][23][24][25][26]

Geographic delivery adds currency translation and wage-arbitrage effects. A stronger rupee can reduce reported rupee revenue from overseas work, while offshore delivery can protect margin if the client accepts the operating model. The report does not forecast currency or margin gains because no verified forward exchange assumption was supplied. RBI reference rates are included as a source trail, but no unverified rate is inserted into the underwriting.[32]

Supply-chaThe practical takeaway: Cyient is asset-light in its core delivery model, yet people availability, utilization, receivable collection and customer programme timing are operational bottlenecks. The next evidence that matters is a quarterly bridge from headcount to billable utilization to EBIT margin, alongside a clear contract-asset and debtor reconciliation.[1][3]

Cash flow and balance sheet

Cash conversion asks whether accounting profit arrives as operating cash. Using the fetched annual data, CFO was ₹554 crore in FY23, ₹726 crore in FY24 and ₹790 crore in FY25; PAT was ₹514 crore, ₹703 crore and ₹648 crore. The three-year cumulative CFO/PAT is (554+726+790)/(514+703+648) = 2,070/1,865 = 1.11x, above the 0.70x floor. Using the FY26 row as an additional latest year gives (726+790+787)/(703+648+463) = 2,303/1,814 = 1.27x. Both calculations pass the cash-conversion test, but the precise window must be stated because fiscal data can differ across source revisions.[10][13]

Red-team scenarios

A red-team review asks what can go wrong even if the headline thesis sounds plausible. These are not predictions; they are failure paths that define what to monitor. Each scenario includes an observable trigger so the investor can distinguish a temporary wobble from thesis damage.[1][10]

Scenario 1: Margin reset becomes structural

If OPM remains near 12% or falls further while revenue is flat, Cyient may be selling specialist work at lower economics. The company could remain profitable and cash generative yet fail to earn its cost of capital. The trigger is two consecutive reported quarters below the FY25 13.5% EBIT reference without a disclosed utilization or pricing recovery. Under that condition, the ROCE failure is not cyclical noise; it is structural and the candidate stays rejected.[1][10]

Scenario 2: Programme concentration delays growth

Aerospace, telecom or semiconductor programmes can move from design to production unevenly. If order intake stays below the FY25 US$836.1m reference or management discloses customer delays, revenue growth could stall while engineers remain on payroll. The trigger is negative order-intake growth plus a disclosed reduction in utilization or book-to-bill below one. The investor should not capitalize a long runway until signed demand is visible.[1][25][26][27]

Scenario 3: Technical strength fails before fundamentals repair

The ledger shows price above both moving averages, but volume is only 0.76x the 20-day average and the stock is 1.77 ATR above the 50-day average. A reversal below the conditional ₹980.95 stop, especially on volume expansion, would convert Stage 2 into a broken structure. The trigger is a daily close below the conditional stop or a fresh ledger showing price below SMA50 and SMA200. A lower price alone is not a buy signal; the base must hold and the financial gate must be rechecked.[17][18]

These scenarios share one conclusion: the quality of the next disclosure matters more than the story attached to the existing trend. Margin, signed work and support are the three independent checks. Passing one while failing the other two does not justify capital.

Verdict and triggers

The verdict is REJECTED under the tactical-momentum rules, with gate status FAIL_FINANCIAL plus FAIL_ASYMMETRY. The exact rule is ROCE ≥15%, 3-year CFO/PAT ≥0.70x, Stage 2 technical posture, and asymmetry ≥3.5x using a verified target and stop. CYIENT clears CFO/PAT, debt-to-equity and Stage 2, but ROCE is 12% and the target/stop inputs are not fully verified. The failed ROCE is the primary fundamental reason; the missing target is the primary mathematical reason.[10][13][15][17]

The appropriate posture is WAIT_FOR_SUPPORT, but the action verdict remains REJECTED because a timing improvement cannot waive a hard gate. No shortlist insertion is recommended. No watchlist JSON status is changed by this dedicated research artifact.

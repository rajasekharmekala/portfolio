This dossier covers Elecon Engineering, a maker of industrial gearboxes and material handling equipment. Its verdict is REJECTED, which means the stock is archived and no position should be opened. The rejection does not say the company is weak, since its returns and cash flows pass the financial tests. It fails because the share price sits below its 50-day and 200-day averages, and the rulebook needs an uptrend first. The terms below and the gate exceptions at the end explain the rules and what could not be fully verified.

Terms used in this dossier

The words below are defined in the order a reader meets them, and each comes with the reason it matters for this stock. Read this part once before the verdict chapters, because the later numbers assume these meanings.

Return on capital employed (ROCE) is the profit a company earns on all the money invested in it, measured before interest and tax. A figure of 21 percent means about 21 rupees of operating profit for every 100 rupees of capital, and the rulebook needs at least 15 percent. It matters here because it is the first financial gate, and the company clears it with room to spare.

Debt to equity is borrowed money divided by shareholders' money. A figure of 0.12 means the company owes about 12 paise for every rupee of its own capital, against a ceiling of 0.70. The second financial gate uses this ratio, and the company is far inside its limit.

The third gate uses cash flow from operations (CFO), which is the cash a business earns from running its own activities before spending on new equipment. Comparing it with profit shows whether reported profit arrives as cash. The gate uses three years together, so one weak year does not decide it. Profit itself is measured per share as earnings per share (EPS), which is the profit after tax divided by the number of shares in issue.

Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA) is operating profit before interest, tax and the wearing down of assets. It is a rough measure of day-to-day earning power, and the company's margin on it is reported each quarter. Operating margin means the share of each rupee of sales left after running costs, which is the same idea expressed as a percentage.

A moving average is the average closing price over a fixed number of sessions, like a smoothed trend line. The 50-day moving average means the average close over the last 50 trading sessions, and the 200-day moving average means the same over 200 sessions. A price above both, with both rising, is the first sign of an uptrend that the rulebook accepts.

The relative strength index (RSI) is a score from 0 to 100 that compares recent gains with recent losses. A reading below 30 means the stock has fallen unusually fast, and it does not by itself mean a bounce is due. The average true range (ATR) is the typical daily price swing in rupees, and the rulebook uses it as the ruler for every distance in this report.

The ledger labels each stock by one of four stages, which work like seasons. Stage 2 means a markup, a sustained uptrend in which the price sits above averages that are themselves rising. Stage 4 means a decline, in which the price falls below falling averages. Stage 1, the label this stock carries, is the basing phase that comes before a markup.

A base means a sideways period, often several weeks or months long, in which the price stops falling and trades within a range. A breakout means a close above the top of that range, ideally on rising volume. Until a breakout happens, a stock in a base has not yet changed its trend.

Asymmetry means how much a stock could gain compared with how much it could lose from the entry price, measured against a pre-set stop. A stop is the price at which a position is sold to cap a loss. The rulebook sets a minimum asymmetry for each book, and it requires a verified target before the test can pass.

The price-to-earnings ratio is the share price divided by earnings per share, and it shows how much the market pays for each rupee of profit. A ratio of 24 means the market pays 24 rupees for one rupee of annual profit, so a higher ratio means a more expensive stock. The rulebook compares today's ratio with the company's own three-year median.

Guidance means what management says it expects for the coming year, and it is a statement of intent rather than a forecast the company must meet. A measured move is a target set by adding the height of a base to the point at which it breaks out. Working capital means the money tied up in stock and unpaid customer bills, net of what the company owes its own suppliers.

How the verdict was reached

The underwriting rulebook sorts every candidate through three layers, in a fixed order. Layer 1 is a set of hard gates, and each gate is a pass or a fail. Any single failure ends the review at that point, so the stock is rejected without a scorecard. Layer 2 is a scorecard of three to five measures that drive this particular business. Layer 3 checks how far the price sits above a support level, measured in ATRs, to decide whether the entry is timely. Each layer exists because the one before it can be passed by a bad stock. A cheap, high-return company can still be a poor buy in a falling trend, and a steady trend can still sit on a weak balance sheet.

Layer 1 has two parts. The financial part asks three questions of the reported figures: whether ROCE is at least 15 percent, whether debt to equity is below 0.70, and whether three years of operating cash cover at least 70 percent of three years of profit. All three pass for Elecon, as chapter 3 shows. The technical part asks whether the price is in an uptrend that the rulebook recognises, and whether the stock is beating its benchmark. Both momentum tests fail, and the price is below both moving averages. The technical part therefore fails, and that failure alone is enough to reject. The asymmetry test was also run, and it fails for a separate reason, which the Layer 1 disposition explains.

The verdict is REJECTED because the technical gate and the asymmetry gate both fail. The rulebook keeps the financial and technical questions apart on purpose. A business can be strong on paper and still be a poor entry today. Rejection here means the file is archived with its evidence, so it can be reopened when the price structure or the target changes. It is not a judgement that the company will fail.

What the company does

Elecon makes two kinds of equipment. Its gear division builds industrial gearboxes, which are the speed-reducing units that connect a motor to a machine, such as a steel mill roll, a sugar mill crusher or a cement kiln drive. Its material handling equipment (MHE) division builds lifting and conveying systems for ports, steel plants and power projects. The company says it is one of the largest manufacturers of industrial gears and material handling equipment in Asia, with a manufacturing footprint of 335,000 square metres and customers in more than 95 countries [1]. In the rating agency's words, the company holds a sizeable domestic market share of 38 to 40 percent in industrial gearboxes [2]. That share figure is from July 2024, so it is treated as background rather than as a current number.

The business is split into two parts with different economics. In the June 2026 quarter, the gear division produced ₹416 crore of revenue, up 16.3 percent, with an EBIT margin of 17.9 percent, and the MHE division produced ₹105 crore, down 2.9 percent on an adjusted basis, with an EBIT margin of 25.6 percent [1]. Gear is about 80 percent of the quarter's revenue, which means the gear cycle decides most of the result. The MHE division earns a higher margin on fewer rupees, and management says its sustainable EBITDA margin is 22 to 24 percent [24]. Overseas sales were ₹151 crore in the quarter, 29 percent of the total [1]. The annual report lists overseas facilities, including Radicon in the United States and the United Kingdom and Benzlers in Sweden, and the export share gives some cushion when the domestic capital cycle slows [4].

Demand follows the investment cycle of the company's customers. The ICRA rationale names steel, cement, sugar and power as the main end users, and notes that revenue is exposed to cycles in domestic capital spending [2]. In the June quarter, management again named power, steel, cement and MHE as the main sources of order inflow [24]. The company is therefore a read on industrial investment in India and abroad. When steel mills and power projects delay spending, order books slow, and in the March quarter management blamed its revenue shortfall on delayed order inflows and customer deferment of deliveries [5].

The rating and ownership picture is simple. ICRA reaffirmed the company's bank facilities of ₹400 crore at [ICRA]AA (Positive) and [ICRA]A1+ on 16 July 2026, which is a high-grade rating with a positive outlook [6]. The promoter group holds 59.28 percent of the shares, and the Screener shareholding table shows that figure unchanged for several years [22]. Foreign institutions have reduced their holding from a peak of 9.84 percent in September 2024 to 6.92 percent in September 2026, while domestic institutions have raised theirs to 7.09 percent [22]. The next chapter asks whether the business is getting stronger, which is the question behind the price.

Why the business is strong or weak

The business has grown over five years, but growth has slowed and margins have stepped down from their peak. Reported sales rose from ₹1,212 crore in FY22 to ₹2,366 crore in FY26. Over the last three years, from ₹1,530 crore in FY23, the compound growth rate is 16 percent [22]. A compound growth rate is the steady yearly rate that would take the starting figure to the finishing figure. Operating profit peaked at ₹548 crore in FY25 and fell to ₹523 crore in FY26 [22]. The operating margin, the share of sales left after running costs, went from 25 percent to 22 percent over the same year [22]. The annual report puts FY26 EBITDA at ₹523 crore on consolidated revenue of ₹2,366 crore, a margin of 22.10 percent [4].

Profit has moved more than sales, and the reason is partly one-off. Reported profit for FY26 was ₹341 crore, which is after the impairment and the gain described below, down from ₹415 crore, and the company says the year included a non-cash goodwill impairment of ₹102 crore on an earlier European acquisition and an exceptional gain of ₹80 crore from a mark-to-market revaluation of investments [5]. The impairment writes down an asset that no longer earns its carrying value, so it is a cost that does not drain cash. The gain is paper profit. The two items partly offset each other, which is why this report uses the reported figure and mentions the items rather than adjusting profit. The reader should keep both in mind when comparing years.

The number

To see how much cash the working-capital trend absorbs, compare the cash that the FY26 profit would have produced at the FY25 conversion rate. At FY25's 1.02 times, FY26 profit of ₹341 crore would have produced about ₹348 crore of operating cash. The company produced ₹314 crore, so about ₹34 crore went into working capital and other items that year.

How it was calculated

341 × 1.02 = 348, and 348 − 314 = 34. The FY25 ratio is 425 ÷ 415 = 1.02. Each step holds the other figures fixed.

The inputs

Profit of ₹341 crore and cash from operations of ₹314 crore for FY26, and ₹425 crore and ₹415 crore for FY25, all from the Screener consolidated tables [22].

The range

If the FY26 conversion had stayed at FY22's 1.59 times, cash would have been about ₹542 crore, which is ₹228 crore more. That is the scale of the working-capital strain. The estimate uses reported figures only, so it does not capture the effect of the goodwill impairment, which is non-cash.

If the number is off

The estimate depends on the cash row and the profit row being the same basis. Both are consolidated. If the company restates either row, the estimate changes, and the gate result is recalculated from the restated figures.

The annual report attributes the rise to new lease arrangements and higher finance cost [4]. Leases are contracts to use equipment or property for a fixed period. Because the figure is small against net worth of ₹2,305 crore, the gate passes on any reasonable definition.

The stage 4 condition passes, but it deserves a caution. The 50-day average sits below the 200-day, which is the arrangement called a death cross. The rulebook rejects a death cross only when it occurs on heavy volume, and the 20-day volume here is 0.32 times its average. The condition is passed on the rule as written, and the caution is recorded so that a reader can see the average structure is still negative.

The asymmetry test fails. The rulebook needs a target anchored to forward earnings multiplied by the three-year median price-to-earnings ratio, or to a measured move from a technical base. No such target can be verified here. The only forward earnings figure available is a single broker's estimate of about ₹17 per share for FY27 [10]. Multiplied by the three-year median of 24.28 times, it gives an indicative target of about ₹413 [7]. The stop under the tactical formula sits at ₹393.5, which is the 63-session base low of ₹402.40 less half an ATR. That gives a reward of about ₹4 against a risk of about ₹15, a ratio of 0.26 to 1, far below the 3.5 hurdle.

A worked example of the asymmetry arithmetic

Suppose you bought 100 shares at ₹408.85, which costs ₹40,885. The stop is ₹393.50, so the most you could lose is 100 × ₹15.35 = ₹1,535. The indicative target is ₹412.80, so the most you could gain is 100 × ₹3.95 = ₹395. The ratio of gain to loss is 395 ÷ 1,535 = 0.26. For the tactical hurdle of 3.5, the target would need to be ₹462.60, which is 100 × ₹53.75 = ₹5,375 of gain against the same ₹1,535 of risk. The rule is set so that a loss is never paid for by a modest gain. Here the gain available on the broker's earnings is too small to justify the loss if the stop is hit.

The analyst on the Trendlyne page gives a target of ₹550 [11], which on the same stop would produce a ratio of about 9 to 1. That target is not anchored to an earnings figure and a stated multiple, so the rulebook does not accept it. The broker's ₹679 target applies 33 times to an EPS of ₹20.5, which the broker's own table places in FY28 [10]. A multiple of 33 is above the company's own three-year median of 24, so that target is not accepted either. The company would need earnings of about ₹19.05 per share for FY27 at the historical median multiple to reach the 3.5 hurdle, against the broker's ₹17. That is about 12 percent more than the broker expects, and it would take an earnings upgrade to be verified.

On the core-book floor of 3.0, the required target would be about ₹481.45, and the stop would be lower, at about ₹384.65 under the core formula. The verdict does not change under either book. The technical gate fails in both, and the asymmetry gate fails in both.

Layer 2 (the business scorecard) and Layer 3 (entry timing) were not run. The rulebook stops at the first Layer 1 failure, and Layer 3 would measure distance from support, which is at 0.36 ATR. That number is not a buy signal, because the support is a falling trend's floor and the posture gate is failed. The gate exceptions list records that these layers were not run.

Final action verdict: REJECTED, archive. The failing rules are the technical posture (Stage 1 below a falling 200-day, with no breakout) and the asymmetry floor (0.26 to 1 against 3.5 to 1 on the best verifiable target). The level that would reopen the file is a close above the 200-day average of ₹454.18 with the 50-day average above it and rising, together with a verified target at or above ₹462.60 on a stop of ₹393.50.

Peer benchmarks

Benchmarks answer a narrower question than the gates. Elecon is compared with three listed Indian capital-goods makers to see whether its returns and growth are weak for the sector or only for the company. The peers were chosen because their Screener pages carry readable consolidated figures, which is not true of every gear maker. Shanthi Gears, which is already on the watchlist, and Bharat Gears returned no consolidated figures on the pages read here, so they are not in the table. A benchmark is a reference point, not a verdict on the peer.

How to read the peer table

ROCE and ROE measure returns on capital and on shareholders' money, so higher is better. The price-to-earnings ratio shows how much the market pays for each rupee of profit, so a high figure means expensive. The two compound growth rates show the three-year pace of sales and profit. A company that scores well on returns and growth but trades at a high multiple has less room for error. Read the table across the row for each company, then compare the rows.

Fatal flaws and the red-team case

The red-team section asks what would make the verdict wrong, and it looks for the flaw that would change the decision. A REJECTED verdict is hard to overturn on the financials, so the review concentrates on the price. Three scenarios are set out below, each with the numbers that would confirm it, and one case against the rejection itself.

Scenario 1: the price breaks its base low

The base low is ₹402.40, which is 1.6 percent below the close, and the tactical stop of ₹393.50 is 3.8 percent below the close. The stock fell 8.4 percent in the past week and 10.8 percent in two weeks, according to the price ledger. If the close breaks the base low, the stock makes a new low and the Stage 1 base fails. That would mean the market is repricing the company down, which the earnings do not yet show. The scenario is the most likely outcome if the regime stays in Downtrend, which the ledger records.

Scenario 2: earnings stay flat and the multiple does not hold

The stock trades at about 24 times the broker's FY27 earnings estimate, which is in line with its own median. The upside therefore depends on earnings rising, not on the multiple expanding. If FY27 profit stayed at FY26's ₹341 crore, earnings per share would be about ₹15.20 [22], and the three-year median multiple would put the value at about ₹369, roughly 10 percent below the close. The first quarter of FY27 shows adjusted profit flat, which is not yet the growth the broker models [8] [1].

Scenario 3: cash keeps lagging profit

Cash conversion fell from 1.02 times in FY25 to 0.92 times in FY26, and debtor days rose from 84 in FY24 to 111 in FY26 [22]. A third year below one would not fail the three-year gate by itself, because the gate uses three years together. It would, however, reduce the margin of safety in that gate and point to a balance sheet that is absorbing profit in receivables. The annual report and ICRA both name working capital as a risk to watch [4] [2].

The case against the rejection

The strongest argument against rejection is that the stock has already fallen far, that its base has held, and that the open order book has risen 36.8 percent in a year. A stock can turn from Stage 1 to Stage 2 quickly once the averages cross. The rulebook is slower by design. Its reply is that the 200-day average has been falling for the last 63 sessions, about thirteen weeks, and the momentum tests fail on relative strength. Those readings would need to change before the rulebook could say the trend has turned. The argument is credible, and it is the reason the reopen level is written as a close above the 200-day.

A second question is whether the reported profit is itself reliable. It is, in the sense that the auditor's limited review was issued with the June results [13]. The concern is that reported profit contains large one-off items in both years. The impairment of ₹102 crore and the ₹80 crore mark-to-market gain both sit inside the FY26 figure of ₹341 crore [5]. The reader should use the adjusted comparison for quarterly trends, and should not treat a single reported year as a clean base.

A third question is ownership. The promoter group holds about 59.3 percent (59.28 percent on Screener at September 2026, and 59.27 percent on another aggregator at June 2026, a difference of 0.01 point that does not change the reading), and the Trendlyne records show no pledged shares from December 2023 through September 2026 [14] [15]. An aggregator-only pledge series is a weaker proof than a filed disclosure, and this report says so in the gate exceptions. The institutional trend is the more visible concern: foreign ownership has fallen by about three percentage points in two years [22]. Domestic institutions have partly replaced that holding, so the shift is between investor types rather than a full exit.

What we would do, and when

We would not buy this stock today. The verdict is REJECTED, so the stock is archived, and the watchlist and shortlist entries that describe it as a buy are out of date. The earlier entries were written on 26 and 27 September 2026, when the stock was trading near ₹476, which is about 16 percent above today's close, and they describe a Stage 2 breakout that the ledger no longer shows. Those entries are superseded, and ELECON should come off the momentum shortlist that carried it as a buy. For a reader holding no shares, the action is no position. For a reader who bought on the earlier verdict, the rulebook stop is ₹393.50, and the price is 3.9 percent above it.

Reopen the file only when the close is above the 200-day average (₹454.18) with the 50-day average above it and rising, and a verified target at or above ₹462.60 gives a ratio of at least 3.5 to 1 on a stop of ₹393.50.

The second trigger is the earnings update. The company reports its September quarter results on 14 October 2026, and the call follows on 15 October [22] [16]. A result with revenue growth at or above the low double-digit guidance and an EBITDA margin holding at about 21 percent or better, the level of the June quarter, would raise the broker's FY27 earnings, and it would need to lift EPS to about ₹19 to move the asymmetry test.

A weekly close below ₹402.40, the base low, would end the Stage 1 base. A new base would then be needed before the file could be reconsidered.

Three filings and events matter most over the next quarter. The results and call on 14 and 15 October will show whether the order book is turning into revenue and whether margins hold. The next shareholding pattern will show whether the zero pledge is confirmed on a filed basis and whether foreign ownership keeps falling. The rating agency reviews its ratings periodically, and the next ICRA review would show whether the positive outlook has moved [6] [2]. Each filing can reopen or confirm the file, so the company stays on observation.

Run notes and gate exceptions

Each item here is a gap, a conflict or a limit in the evidence. Each gives what is missing, why it cannot be filled in this run, and what would fill it. None of the items changes the REJECTED verdict, because the technical gate fails independently of every figure on this list.

  • Annual report read in extracted form. The FY26 annual report is a 147-page PDF. Its text was extracted with macOS PDFKit and cached as text. The consolidated five-year chart in the report is not readable in extracted form, so the five-year table uses Screener's figures, which agree with the report on FY26 revenue, EBITDA and profit. The only dated forward earnings figure is one broker's FY27 estimate of ₹17 per share, and the Trendlyne page reports one analyst. The asymmetry result depends on that estimate, and a multi-analyst consensus should replace it when available.
  • The broker report is internally inconsistent. Its table gives FY27 earnings per share of ₹17, while its text quotes ₹20.5, which the table places in FY28. The table figure is used because it is the twelve-month forward figure the rule requires.
  • Three-year median price-to-earnings. The median is computed from three year-end ratios on one aggregator, not from a full price history. The current ratio differs between sources (30.3 on Screener, 39.6 on another aggregator). The report uses the median for valuation and reports the current ratio as a range.
  • 52-week high disagrees across sources. The ledger shows a high of ₹626. Screener shows ₹580, and Upstox shows ₹616.30 in its cached page. The ledger is the authority for technical figures, as instructed. The difference changes the distance from the high, which is −34.7 percent on the ledger and about −29.5 percent on Screener's high, but it does not change any gate.

The primary shareholding-pattern filing for June or September 2026 was not read, because the exchange copies returned no text in this run. The cure is to read the exchange filing when it is available.

The watchlist and shortlist rows dated 26 and 27 September 2026 show a close of ₹476.20, a target of ₹620, a stop of ₹438 and a verdict of IMMEDIATE_BUY. The ledger close on 12 October is ₹408.85. The ICRA 2026 rationale link was not used, because the shared cache keys ICRA pages by their address without the query string, and that address already held another company's rationale.

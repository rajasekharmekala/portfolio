Sudarshan Chemical is a specialist chemicals issuer whose investment case depends on converting a global pigments platform into durable, cash-backed returns. Glossary and first-use definitions

These definitions keep the report readable. Each technical term is defined before the evidence uses it, so a new investor can follow the arithmetic without guessing what an abbreviation means.

ROCE ROCE is return on capital employed; it compares operating profit with the capital used to earn it.

RSI RSI is the relative strength index; it measures recent price momentum on a 0–100 scale.

ATR ATR is average true range; it estimates a typical daily price movement for stop placement.

EPS EPS is earnings per share; it is profit allocated to one share.

FCF is free cash flow; it is cash left after operating needs and capital spending.

20-day moving average A 20-day moving average is the mean of the last 20 daily closes; it tracks short-term trend.

50-day moving average A 50-day moving average is the mean of the last 50 daily closes; it tracks the medium trend.

200-day moving average A 200-day moving average is the mean of the last 200 daily closes; it tracks the long trend.

Stage 2 Stage 2 is the markup phase of a sustained rising trend.

Stage 4 Stage 4 is the decline phase of a sustained falling trend.

base A base is a sideways price range that can precede a trend change.

pivot is the price level used to judge whether a base moves upward.

breakout is a move above a defined base or resistance level.

retest A retest is a return to a broken level to test whether it holds.

stop loss A stop loss is a pre-set exit price intended to limit loss.

limit order is an instruction to trade only at a chosen price or better.

entry zone An entry zone is the price band considered for a new position.

asymmetry is the relationship between possible reward and possible loss.

reward-to-risk Reward-to-risk is possible gain divided by possible loss.

gross margin Gross margin is sales left after direct production costs.

operating margin Operating margin is operating profit divided by sales.

net margin Net margin is profit after all expenses divided by sales.

debt-to-equity is borrowings compared with shareholders’ equity.

net debt Net debt is borrowings minus cash.

price-to-earnings is share price divided by earnings per share.

free cash flow Free cash flow is cash left after operating needs and capital spending.

cash flow from operations Cash flow from operations is cash generated by the ordinary business.

guidance Guidance is management’s published outlook for future performance.

measured move A measured move is a chart target based on the height of a prior range.

working capital Working capital is cash tied up in receivables, inventory and payables.

capital expenditure Capital expenditure is cash spent on long-lived productive assets.

consensus estimate A consensus estimate is the average forecast collected from analysts.

Identity, history and business model

Sudarshan Chemical Industries is an Indian specialty-pigments producer whose products sit inside coatings, plastics, inks and other colour-intensive formulations. Pigments are small inputs by weight but can be critical to a customer’s formulation, qualification cycle and colour consistency. Investor materials describe a global platform expanded by the Heubach pigment transaction; those materials are management’s operating map, while annual reports and exchange filings remain the authority for financial claims.[1][3][4][5] The investment question is whether the enlarged platform earns a return above its cost of capital after integration, working capital and currency effects. The company website states that it serves customers in more than 120 countries. The remaining footprint counts that appear in the presentation materials (product count, manufacturing-site count, employee count) are marked N/A because the source text behind those figures could not be fetched in this run.[22]

The business discussion is descriptive; it does not substitute for audited financial proof.

The products serve real uses. Customers need colour. Plants need output. Cash must follow sales. Debt must stay within the rule. These are the checks that connect the business story to the verdict.

A larger pigment platform can produce scale yet destroy value if acquired plants stay under-utilised, customers overlap or synergy costs run ahead of savings. The red-team question is where the audited bridge from acquisition accounting to recurring margin and cash resides.[2][3][4] The second flaw is leverage and cash conversion: the debt/equity ratio is N/A because the cited figure is not present in cached evidence and leverage cannot be cleared, and a three-year CFO/PAT below 0.70 rejects the thesis regardless of product breadth.[1][2][9] The third flaw is price structure. Method, evidence boundaries and monitoring plan

The report uses a hierarchy of evidence. The facts ledger supplies the dated close, moving averages, RSI, ATR, volume, range, stage and relative-strength fields because those values were built for the 2026-10-09 session. Statutory annual reports and exchange filings are the preferred evidence for revenue, profit, operating cash flow, debt, equity, segment margins and acquisition accounting. Investor presentations are useful for footprint, product count, capacity and management objectives, but they are not treated as audited statements. Secondary market pages are cross-checks only, especially where their timestamps or calculation definitions differ. This hierarchy matters because a report can look precise while combining a current price with an old ratio or a standalone number with a consolidated denominator.

The next research pass should retrieve the annual reports as documents rather than rely on search summaries. It should record the exact page for each of the six line items in the three-year CFO/PAT calculation, then reconcile those amounts to the cash-flow statement and notes. It should also identify whether the reported debt/equity is gross or net, how lease liabilities are treated, and whether acquisition-related debt is included. For the operating scorecard, the reviewer should capture quarterly volumes, capacity utilisation, receivable days, inventory days, product mix and segment operating profit. These fields would turn the present WATCH statuses into evidence-backed PASS or FAIL outcomes.

Technical monitoring is equally concrete. The first alert is a close above ₹1,185.20, the current 50-day average, but an isolated close is not enough. Confirmation requires a hold, improving relative strength and volume that is not merely a low-volume bounce. The second alert is a break of ₹1,145.90; failure to reclaim that level would convert the current wait into a support-broken posture. The long-trend reference is ₹975.01 at the 200-day average. None of these levels overrides the financial gate. Price can improve before cash evidence does, and the workflow allocates only when both evidence tracks agree.

2. Why the business is strong or weak

ScanSource connects technology vendors with resellers, integrators, service providers, and other channel partners. It gathers demand. It may hold or arrange inventory. It extends credit. It provides logistics and enablement. The company earns a narrower spread than a software firm. It also carries working-capital and execution risk. Its own pages focus on channel relationships, technology categories, and services rather than subscriptions. [6] [7] [8] [9]

Think of ScanSource as a specialist wholesaler. It knows which retailers need which products and when. Its moat is partner density, vendor authorization, credit skill, reliable logistics, and route-to-market knowledge. These assets can last. Suppliers can change routes. Resellers can merge. Inventory can age. Revenue growth must therefore be read with gross margin, inventory days, receivables, returns, vendor rebates, and operating cash flow.

Judge ScanSource by gross-profit dollars and cash per dollar of working capital. Do not judge it by sales scale alone. An acquisition or vendor relationship can add sales and use cash at the same time. It can also dilute returns. Annual reports, quarterly releases, and 8-K exhibits should verify segment mix, vendor concentration, customer concentration, inventory commitments, and acquisition accounting. These values are not quantified here. They remain N/A.

The channel thesis is therefore conditional on cash discipline, not on distribution volume alone.

3. The numbers, in plain words

The US hard gate requires ROIC of at least 12%, free-cash-flow margin of at least 12%, net debt/EBITDA below 3.0× and SBC dilution below 5% of annual revenue. Current verified values for SCSC are: ROIC N/A; FCF margin N/A; net debt/EBITDA N/A; SBC/revenue N/A. The report cannot infer them from price, market capitalization or a search-result snippet. Because every floor must pass, the financial-health gate fails closed. [2][3][11]

The missing numbers have specific definitions. ROIC asks whether after-tax operating profit is productive relative to the capital tied up in the business. Free-cash-flow margin is operating cash flow less capital expenditure divided by revenue, with the exact company-period definition disclosed. Net debt/EBITDA tests balance-sheet pressure against operating earnings, while SBC/revenue tests whether reported cash generation is offset by equity dilution. A distributor can pass one and fail another; the four-way test prevents one attractive metric from hiding a weak capital cycle.

The forensic work that would change the gate is mechanical: retrieve the latest 10-K and two latest 10-Qs, reconcile GAAP operating income, cash from operations, capital expenditures, debt, cash and diluted share count, then calculate each ratio using period-matched values. If the filing shows ROIC ≥12%, FCF margin ≥12%, net debt/EBITDA <3× and SBC/revenue <5%, the financial gate can be reopened. Until then, the appropriate notation is N/A and the decision remains rejected.

6. Why these estimates might be wrong

A red-team review asks what could make the attractive part of the thesis misleading. The first flaw is margin compression: hardware distribution can grow revenue while vendor pricing, mix or rebates reduce gross-profit dollars. The hinge metric is gross-profit dollars per dollar of working capital; it must be verified in the 10-K and quarterly filings. [4][11]

The second flaw is balance-sheet drag. Inventory and receivables can absorb cash just as demand appears strongest, especially when channel partners or vendors change terms. If net debt/EBITDA is above 3×, or if cash conversion deteriorates for a full cycle, the hard gate fails even with a good chart.

The third flaw is structural disintermediation. Vendors can sell directly, partners can consolidate, and large customers can demand lower spreads. Acquisitions may mask organic stagnation. The invalidation is not a single bad quarter; it is two reporting periods of falling gross-profit conversion, worsening inventory or receivable days, or lost strategic vendor relationships.

Research agenda

A disciplined underwriting process leaves a trail. Revisit ScanSource after the next 10-Q or 10-K. Do not rely on an isolated price move. Download the filing. Record its date and fiscal period. Reconcile each metric to a named note or statement line. This prevents a stale ratio from being used as a current one, especially when inventory, debt, and shares move in a seasonal quarter. [1] [4]

The first accounting pass bridges revenue to gross profit and then to operating income. The second bridges net income to operating cash flow. It separates working-capital changes from non-cash items. The third bridges basic shares to diluted shares. It also tests whether equity compensation transfers value from existing owners. These bridges test pricing power, cash reality, and per-share economics. None can replace another. [2] [3]

Channel analysis needs a calendar. Product launches, fiscal budgets, and vendor incentives can pull demand forward. A digestion period can follow. Strong sales with rising inventory and falling cash may be timing, not durable improvement. Compare at least four quarters and three annual years. Include seasonality and acquisitions.

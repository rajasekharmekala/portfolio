What the company does

Endurance Technologies is an Indian auto component maker. In plain terms it makes the metal and mechanical parts that sit inside a vehicle and move it. The company describes itself as engaged in aluminium die casting, which means pushing molten aluminium into a steel mould under high pressure to form a part quickly, along with suspension, transmission, braking and embedded electronic products. It runs 19 plants in India and 14 in Europe, and it serves two-wheeler and three-wheeler makers in India and four-wheeler makers and the two-wheeler aftermarket in Europe [1]. Its operations in Italy and Germany give it a second home market, which matters because a European plant can be sold to or supplied from in euros rather than rupees.

The electronics side is held through a subsidiary, Maxwell Energy Systems. It makes battery management systems, the control units that watch the cells of an electric vehicle battery, and battery packs for energy storage [2]. Management told analysts in its August 2026 call that EV battery packs and Maxwell products are expected to drive new business [3]. The company had about 4,586 employees in its latest annual count [4].

Control of the business is concentrated. The promoter group holds 75.00 percent of the shares, and most of that sits in family trusts rather than in individual names [5]. A second shareholding table, from Anand Rathi, reports the same 75.00 percent promoter holding as of June 2026 [6]. Concentration of this kind is not in itself a problem, but it means that the public float is small, and that price moves can be sharper than the business news alone would suggest.

Recent deals extend the European footprint. A subsidiary agreed to buy the remaining 32 percent of the Stöferle entities for 18 million euros, which would make it the sole owner [2]. A separate 12.34 million euro purchase covered Anna Milena and Fondalpress in Italy [2]. A new aluminium casting plant at Shendra began commercial production on 25 September 2026 with an initial capacity of 900 metric tonnes a month [2]. What this means for an owner is that the company is spending heavily to grow, which helps future sales but ties up cash and adds debt. Chapter 3 shows that debt is small on the March 2026 figures, and chapter 5 returns to the acquisition borrowing as the item to watch.

Why the business is strong or weak

The first test of a business is how well it turns the money invested in it into profit. We use ROCE, which stands for return on capital employed. It is profit before interest and tax divided by the total capital the business uses, including borrowed money. A figure of 17.8 percent means that every 100 rupees of capital employed produced about 17.8 rupees of operating profit [2]. The annual row shows 18 percent for March 2026, after a dip to 14 percent in 2022 and 2023, so the recovery is real and not a single good year [2]. Return on equity, which measures profit against shareholder money, stood at 14.9 percent [2].

The second question is whether sales are growing and whether the company keeps a fair share of each rupee of sales. Sales for the year to March 2026 were 14,596 crore rupees, up from 11,561 crore a year earlier, which is growth of about 26 percent [2]. The stock data service reports the same growth rate [4]. The margin is the share of sales left after running costs, and analysts call it operating margin. Endurance's operating margin has been stuck near 12 to 14 percent for several years, and it was 12 percent in the June 2026 quarter [2]. A stable margin with growing sales is a sound pattern for a parts maker, but it is not a pricing power story.

The demand backdrop is mixed. Reports in the business press say the government approved a mandate requiring anti-lock braking systems on all new two-wheelers from 1 January 2026 [7], and management says its brakes business is growing at more than 30 percent a year [3]. Electric vehicle sales in India rose 87.6 percent in the June 2026 quarter, according to the company's call summary [3]. Against that, management pointed to commodity cost pressure and raw material shortages in the same period [8]. The national body for vehicle makers, SIAM, describes its members as all the major vehicle and engine manufacturers in India [9], so component demand in this business follows vehicle output, which is our reading rather than a figure from that source. We have not been able to verify a company figure for the share of its orders that come from electric vehicles, so we do not rely on one.

Analysts are broadly positive. An outside consensus summary on the stock data service lists the stock as a buy with a 12-month target of 3,160 rupees, a third-party figure and not our target [4]. We record that view but do not use it, because our framework requires a target built from earnings or from a technical level, and a broker's target is neither. The verdict below therefore does not depend on it. What this means for you is that the business case is decent, and the question is timing and price, which the next chapters address.

The numbers, in plain words

Layer 1 is the first set of hurdles in the framework. A company must pass every hurdle to move on, and a single failure ends the review. The financial floor has three tests. The first is ROCE, which must be at least 15.0 percent. The second is debt-to-equity, which compares the money the company has borrowed with the money its owners have put in, and must be below 0.70. A ratio of 0.70 would mean that for every 10 rupees of owners' money the company owes seven rupees to lenders. The third is cash conversion over three years, which asks whether reported profit turns into cash. It is the total cash flow from operations, meaning the cash the business generates from running its activities, divided by the total profit after tax over the same three years, and it must be at least 0.70.

The number

The three-year cash conversion ratio is 1.80. Over the years to March 2024, 2025 and 2026, the company's cash from operations was 1.80 times its profit after tax.

How it was calculated

We add the three annual cash-from-operations figures: 1,057 + 1,532 + 1,851 = 4,440 crore rupees. We add the three annual profit figures: 680 + 836 + 952 = 2,468 crore rupees. Then 4,440 divided by 2,468 is 1.80.

A 50-day moving average is the average closing price over the last 50 sessions, and a 200-day moving average is the same measure over the last 200 sessions. These are the two averages the verdict rests on. The stock is now below its 50-day average of 2,786.82 rupees, which is itself falling, and below its 200-day average of 2,593.04 rupees. A price under both averages, with the 50-day falling, is what a downtrend looks like on this measure. The 200-day average is nearly flat but still falling: it stands at 2,593.04 rupees against 2,595.74 sixty-three sessions earlier, a fall of 2.70 rupees or about 0.1 percent. Under our rule any fall counts, so the 200-day average fails Test A2.

The stock is in what the framework calls Stage 1, which means a basing phase. A stock in Stage 2 is in a sustained uptrend, with the price above rising averages, and Stage 4 is a markdown phase. A base is a sideways range where a stock rests before its next move, and the base low is the lowest price in that range. The ledger puts the base low at 2,402 rupees over the last 63 sessions, and the close sits 0.07 average true range units above it. ATR, or average true range, is the typical daily price swing; here it is 76.31 rupees. The close is therefore sitting almost exactly on the base low, not on a breakout, which means a close that clears the level the stock has been capped at. The ledger does not set a breakout level, so the reopen condition is a re-run of Test A or Test B on the ledger, not a close through a named price.

Two other gauges support that reading. The RSI, or relative strength index, measures how fast a stock has fallen or risen recently on a scale of 0 to 100. A reading below 30 is called oversold, and this stock's reading is 23.99, which means it has fallen unusually fast. Oversold does not mean it must bounce, and in a downtrend it can stay low for a long time. Relative strength compares the stock with the Nifty 50 index over the same period. Over 63 sessions the stock lagged the index by 3.42 percent, and over 28 sessions by 10.96 percent, so it is underperforming the market, not leading it.

Layer 1 disposition and final verdict

Layer 1 has three parts, and it must pass in full. The financial floor passes on all three tests, with ROCE at 17.8 percent, debt-to-equity at 0.19 and three-year cash conversion at 1.80. The technical floor fails. A company qualifies for the tactical bucket only if it is in a Stage 2 markup, above rising 50-day and 200-day averages. It qualifies for the core bucket only if it is in a Stage 1 base breakout or a Stage 2 markup. This stock is in Stage 1 basing, below both averages, with no confirmed breakout and with the volume test failing. It therefore fails the technical floor, and the disposition code is FAIL_TECHNICAL. Because one gate failed, the framework does not evaluate Layer 2 or Layer 3, and it does not evaluate the asymmetry test, which compares the upside to the target with the downside to the stop. That is the ratio of possible gain to possible loss, and it must clear 3.5 for the tactical bucket. We cannot compute it without a valid target, and we do not invent one.

The final action verdict is REJECTED, which means archive. This changes the prior view. The earlier watchlist note said the stock was resting on its 200-day support at about 2,595 rupees and recommended to accumulate on a dip. On the 9 October close of 2,407.50 rupees, the stock is about 7.2 percent below that average, so the note is out of date. The verdict moves the name to the archive. That is the outcome the Layer 1 rule gives when the technical floor fails, and the name returns to review only when the reopen condition in chapter 7 is met.

What could happen next

Three paths are realistic over the next few months, and each has a clear marker. The first is a recovery. The stock would need to reclaim the falling 50-day average of 2,786.82 rupees and hold above the 200-day average of 2,593.04 rupees on several closes. That would turn the trend from down to flat or up and would start to satisfy the Stage 2 test. It would also need the relative strength numbers to turn positive, which means the stock must begin to outperform the Nifty 50. The company's September quarter results on 3 November 2026 are the next scheduled event that could move the price, and they reopen nothing on their own [2].

The second path is more basing. The stock sits on its base low at 2,402 rupees with volume drying up. That pattern can last weeks or months, and while it lasts the stock is not allocated money. This is the most likely path while the broader market is weak. The market ledger for the same session shows that only one of eleven sectors sits above its 20-day average, and the Nifty 50 is in a downtrend, so a basing period is the default expectation until market breadth improves.

The third path is a breakdown. A close below the base low of 2,402 rupees would mean the stock is losing its support, and the next reference point is the 52-week low of 2,142.80 rupees, about 11 percent below the close [17]. A breakdown would not change the verdict, which is already REJECTED, but it would make the recovery path longer and would raise the bar for any future review.

Two business events could shift these paths. The company's Stöferle and Italian acquisitions add European capacity, and the Shendra plant is ramping up. The company has said that its battery pack plant will be fully used by October 2026 [3]. Management put the peak sales of the Shendra plant at 513 crore rupees by FY 29 [18], and reported cumulative four-wheeler casting bookings with a peak annual business potential of 5.13 billion rupees [19]. If those plants win the orders management describes, the sales line could improve faster than the market expects. Against that, the acquisitions add borrowing, and the market will watch how that debt is serviced. What this means for you is that the business news is improving at the edges, but the price has not yet responded, and the framework needs the price to move first.

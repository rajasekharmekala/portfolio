Terms: 20-day moving average is the mean close over twenty sessions; 50-day moving average is the mean close over fifty sessions; 200-day moving average is the mean close over two hundred sessions; Stage 2 is a markup trend; Stage 4 is a markdown trend; base is a sideways range; pivot is a confirming price; breakout is a move above resistance; retest is a return to test support; stop loss is a risk limit; limit order is an order with a price cap; These are plain working definitions.

Terms: entry zone is a permitted buy range; asymmetry is reward compared with risk; reward-to-risk is possible gain divided by loss; gross margin is sales less direct costs; operating margin is operating profit divided by sales; net margin is net profit divided by sales; debt-to-equity is borrowings divided by book equity; net debt is borrowings less cash; price-to-earnings is price divided by earnings; cash flow from operations is cash from normal business; guidance is management outlook; measured move is a chart target; dilution is a smaller ownership share; capital expenditure is cash for long-lived assets; buyback is a company share purchase; working capital is short-term assets less liabilities; consensus estimate is an analyst forecast; These are plain working definitions.

Identity and scope

Mahindra Holidays & Resorts India Limited is the listed company behind the Club Mahindra vacation-ownership brand. It is part of the Mahindra Group's leisure and hospitality activities and trades on NSE under MHRIL and on BSE under scrip 533088.[1][6][12] Vacation ownership is not the same as a normal hotel booking: members pay for a multi-year right to use a holiday product, while the company must keep adding inventory, servicing members, collecting dues, and renewing trust. That difference makes receivables, deferred revenue, resort utilisation, cancellations, and capital intensity more important than a single quarter's room revenue.[27][28][29]

The dossier uses two evidence layers. The first is the frozen facts ledger dated 8 October 2026, which is authoritative for price, moving averages, RSI, ATR, stage, volume and relative strength. The second is a deep fetch of the Screener consolidated page and publicly available company, exchange and regulator pages. What this means for you. MHRIL is an idea seed, not an investable shortlist candidate. The name must first repair its returns and structure; being close to the 52-week low is not itself a buy signal.

Business model and operating engine

Membership economics

Club Mahindra's product is a pre-paid or contracted holiday entitlement supported by a network of owned, leased, managed and partner inventory. The company's public overview describes the flagship brand and its member proposition, while its membership and resort pages explain the consumer-facing exchange of fees for holiday use.[1][29][30] The attractive feature is visibility: a large member base can generate recurring annual fees and repeat demand. The less visible obligation is delivery: the promise must be honoured across seasons, destinations and room categories, so inventory additions and service quality can consume cash before accounting profit appears.

The FY25 operating highlights reported through the company's financial communications included a consolidated revenue figure of about ₹2,909.8 crore, a consolidated profit of about ₹127.6 crore, a membership base above 300,000, and a resort network described at roughly 5,847 rooms across 41 resorts.[2][15] These figures are useful context but are not substituted for the frozen Screener FY26 tables where periods differ. Room count and membership count also do not prove returns: a growing network can lower utilisation or raise depreciation and financing costs. The underwriting question is whether incremental members create cash returns above the cost of inventory and capital.

Supply, inventory and service risks

Resort capacity is the physical supply behind the membership promise. A resort can be added by ownership, lease, management contract or partnership, and each route carries a different balance-sheet and service profile. The public resort directory confirms breadth of destinations, but it does not disclose a complete resort-level occupancy, contribution margin or maintenance-capex bridge.[29][2] Those missing unit economics matter because a headline occupancy percentage can coexist with weak returns if the company added rooms at low yield or carries a heavy fixed-cost base.

Customer acquisition, renewals, upgrades and cancellations form the demand side. The public terms page is evidence that membership rights are governed by contractual conditions, but it is not a substitute for an audited cohort table showing renewal rates, refund rates, arrears and lifetime value.[28][27] Those metrics are therefore N/A in the KPI scorecard. A future pass would require three years of cohort retention and cash collection data, not a marketing claim.

Financial record

June's lower margin and negative reported profit show that fixed costs and below-operating-line charges remain consequential.

Cash flow, capital intensity and balance sheet

Cash flow is the test of whether accounting earnings arrive in the bank. Screener reports cash from operations of ₹681 crore in FY23, ₹624 crore in FY24, ₹621 crore in FY25 and ₹531 crore in FY26, while net profit was ₹114 crore, ₹116 crore, ₹126 crore and ₹67 crore.[7][6] The three-year FY24–FY26 calculation is (₹624 + ₹621 + ₹531) / (₹116 + ₹126 + ₹67) = ₹1,776 crore / ₹309 crore = 5.75x. This exceeds the 0.70 cash-backing floor, but it does not rescue the name: one hard gate can pass while ROCE, leverage and technical posture fail.

Leverage and returns

The FY26 balance sheet lists borrowings of ₹3,829 crore, equity capital of ₹202 crore and reserves of ₹576 crore, giving a simple borrowings divided by book equity of ₹3,829 / ₹778 = 4.92x. Screener's displayed ROCE is 8.12% and ROE is 9.90%.[6][8] The underwriting floor is debt-to-equity below 0.70 and ROCE at least 15%; both fail by wide margins. Even if some borrowings support long-lived resort assets or member-related liabilities, the rule uses the reported consolidated balance sheet unless a financial-institution exemption applies, and MHRIL is a hospitality company, not a bank or NBFC.

Hard financial conclusion. CFO/PAT passes at 5.75x, but ROCE fails at 8.12% versus 15%, and reported borrowings/book equity is about 4.92x versus the <0.70 rule. The conclusion is FAIL_FINANCIAL_HEALTH. A cash-rich business can still destroy value when its asset base and financing burden earn too little.

Red-team flaws and falsification tests

A disciplined decision must state the strongest bear case against its own rejection. MHRIL could be a recovery candidate if the current profit trough is temporary, its membership cash flows remain durable, and resort additions mature into higher returns. The stock's proximity to its 52-week low could then offer upside. That is a hypothesis, not evidence; each flaw below identifies the observation that would falsify it.

Flaw 1: accounting cash may overstate economic cash

CFO/PAT of 5.75x looks excellent, but vacation ownership collections, deferred revenue and working-capital timing can make reported operating cash differ from sustainable free cash flow. FCF fell to ₹139 crore in FY26 while CFO was ₹531 crore.[7][27] The recovery case is falsified if audited notes show rising refunds, arrears, contract liabilities consumed by delivery, or maintenance capex that absorbs CFO. Required test: a three-year bridge from member billings to collections, refunds, capex and free cash flow.

Flaw 2: operating margin may hide capital destruction

OPM reached 21% in FY25 and FY26, yet ROCE was only 8.12% and depreciation plus interest consumed the operating gain.[5][8] If new rooms and resorts do not earn above the cost of capital, growth makes the problem larger. Required test: resort cohort ROIC, maintenance capex per occupied room night, and incremental member acquisition payback.

Flaw 3: cheap-looking price can be a falling denominator

P/E of 81.2 on a TTM EPS of ₹2.62 is not cheap, and FY26 EPS of ₹3.44 is already below FY25's ₹6.32.[3] A target based on a rebound EPS would be speculative without a verified consensus or management plan. Required test: two consecutive quarters of positive PAT growth, falling interest burden, and a renewed Stage 2 structure with relative strength versus Nifty 50.

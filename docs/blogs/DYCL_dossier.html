Executive Summary & Investment Pitch

Dynamic Cables Limited (NSE: DYCL, BSE: 540795) is an established, high-growth Indian manufacturer of power transmission and distribution (T&D) cables, specialized railway signaling conductors, and renewable solar photovoltaic (PV) interconnection systems. Operating from three manufacturing facilities in Rajasthan (Jaipur and Reengus) with over four decades of engineering pedigree, the enterprise has successfully transformed itself from an SME bare-conductor supplier into an institutional-grade, RDSO-approved, and PGCIL-accredited power solutions provider capable of manufacturing extra-high-voltage (EHV) cables up to 66kV.[1][3] The company represents a textbook capital-compounder operating at the epicenter of India's multi-decade power distribution modernization, grid upgrade (RDSS), and railway electrification super-cycles.

Over the trailing three-year cycle (FY23–FY26), Dynamic Cables has achieved an audited revenue compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 24.32%, scaling top-line revenue from ₹668.63 crore in FY23 to ₹1,284.89 crore in FY26, before posting record annualized trailing-twelve-month (TTM) revenues of ₹1,352.4 crore as of Q1 FY27.[5][6] Concurrently, audited net profit after tax (PAT) expanded at a 43.29% CAGR from ₹31.01 crore to ₹91.18 crore (reaching ₹96.5 crore TTM), propelled by a structural shift in product mix toward higher-margin High Tension (HT) cables, railway signaling lines, and solar DC cables, which expanded Operating Profit Margins (OPM) from 9.2% to 10.9%. Crucially, this rapid expansion has been achieved without compromising balance sheet integrity: net debt-to-equity stands at a pristine 0.11x (gross gearing below 0.20x per CRISIL Ratings), Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) is locked in at 26.2%, Return on Equity (ROE) sits at 19.9%, and promoter equity ownership remains strong at 68.18% with zero encumbrance or pledge.[7][8][10]

1. History & Corporate Milestones

Dynamic Cables traces its operational foundation to 1986, when the late entrepreneur B.K. Mangal founded a small manufacturing enterprise in the Vishwakarma Industrial Area (VKIA) of Jaipur, Rajasthan, to cater to the bare overhead transmission conductor requirements of the Rajasthan State Electricity Board (RSEB).[1] Operating in an era dominated by state-owned power utilities with stringent manual inspection protocols, the enterprise established an early reputation for metallurgical consistency and precision stranding in All Aluminium Conductors (AAC) and Aluminium Conductor Steel Reinforced (ACSR) lines.

Over the subsequent two decades, under the executive leadership of Rahul Mangal and Ashish Mangal, the company systematically climbed the electrical engineering value chain. Recognizing that bare conductors were commoditized, working-capital intensive, and subject to intense tender competition, management initiated a multi-phase capital deployment program to enter the insulated wires and power cables domain. The key operational milestones that transformed Dynamic Cables into a nationwide infrastructure supplier include:

  • 1986–2000 (Foundational Conductor Era): Commercial commissioning of Site I at VKIA, Jaipur. Specialized in bare AAC and ACSR overhead conductors, establishing vendor empanelment across Northern and Western state electricity boards.[1]
  • 2001–2008 (Low Tension Cable Expansion): Commissioned dedicated PVC and Cross-Linked Polyethylene (XLPE) compounding and extrusion lines at Site II, VKIA, Jaipur. Commenced commercial production of Low Tension (LT) power and control cables up to 1.1kV, entering the private industrial EPC market.[3]
  • 2009–2016 (Railway Signaling & Institutional Accreditation): Achieved critical Type Test approvals from Central Power Research Institute (CPRI) and Electrical Research and Development Association (ERDA). Secured coveted Research Designs and Standards Organisation (RDSO) vendor qualification from the Ministry of Railways for indoor and outdoor signaling and track-feed cables, establishing a high-moat niche in railway electrification.[4]
  • 2017 (BSE SME Initial Public Offering): In December 2017, Dynamic Cables completed its Initial Public Offering on the BSE SME platform, raising ₹25.64 crore to fund plant modernization and working capital expansion.[12]
  • 2018–2019 (Commissioning of Reengus High Voltage Facility): Successfully commissioned its flagship Greenfield manufacturing facility (Site III/IV) at the SKS Industrial Area in Reengus, Sikar District, Rajasthan. Equipped with advanced continuous catenary vulcanization (CCV) extrusion lines and automated testing laboratories, this facility enabled the commercial manufacturing of High Tension (HT) and Extra High Voltage (EHV) cables up to 66kV, vastly expanding addressable market size and gross margins.[3][13]
  • 2020–2021 (Main Board Migration): Having fulfilled track record and net worth hurdles, the company migrated its equity shares from the BSE SME platform to the Main Board of both the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE: 540795) and the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE: DYCL), attracting institutional fund participation and enhancing liquidity.[14]
  • 2022–2024 (Solar Photovoltaic & RDSS Acceleration): Developed and certified specialized TÜV-compliant Solar DC cables and AL59 high-ampacity alloy conductors. Emerged as a major direct and indirect beneficiary of the Government of India's ₹3.03 lakh crore Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS).[15]
  • 2025–2026 (The ₹1,000 Cr Inflection, Bonus Issue & US Market Breakthrough): Annual revenue crossed the historic ₹1,000 crore milestone in FY25 (₹1,025.37 Cr) and scaled to ₹1,284.89 Cr in FY26.[6] In FY26, the company rewarded long-term shareholders by issuing 1:1 bonus equity shares, expanding authorized share capital from ₹25 crore to ₹50 crore and paid-up equity to ₹48.46 crore.[11] Concurrently, the company achieved its first commercial breakthroughs in the United States export market, establishing a beachhead in North American electrical infrastructure.[5]

2.2 Contract Execution, Tender Mechanics & Pricing Formula

Dynamic Cables generates revenue through a hybrid distribution model comprising:

  1. Institutional EPC Contractor Supply (50%–55% of Order Flow): Supplying leading Tier-1 infrastructure and transmission engineering procurement contractors such as Larsen & Toubro (L&T), Tata Power, Sterling and Wilson, KEC International, and Kalpataru Projects. In these contracts, Dynamic Cables functions as the qualified cable vendor named in the EPC project schedule. Payment is secured via irrevocable Letters of Credit (LCs) or strict 60–90 day commercial terms, dramatically mitigating default risk.[4][16]
  2. Direct Utility Tenders (35%–40% of Order Flow): Direct competitive bidding in central and state power utility tenders (e.g., PGCIL, NTPC, Adani Electricity, Torrent Power, BESCOM, MSEDCL, JVVNL). These contracts typically demand comprehensive technical qualification, bank guarantees for performance, and compliance with the Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS) guidelines.[15]
  3. Direct Exports & Overseas Engineering (8%–10% of Order Flow): Exporting power and industrial cables to over 40 countries across Africa, the Middle East, Europe, and recently the United States. Overseas contracts yield premium US Dollar-denominated realizations and faster cash realization cycles.[5]

A vital operational moat governing contract execution is the Price Variation Clause (PVC). Because metal inputs represent over 70% of manufacturing cost, long-term fixed-price contracts in the cable industry can cause severe distress during commodity shocks. Dynamic Cables structures approximately 85%–90% of its institutional and utility order book with dynamic price variation formulas tied directly to indices published by the Cable and Conductor Manufacturers Association of India (CACMAI) or the Indian Electrical & Electronics Manufacturers' Association (IEEMA), and London Metal Exchange (LME) cash settlement prices.[17] When international aluminium or copper prices fluctuate between tender award and dispatch, invoice values are automatically adjusted according to contractual formulas, insulating operating margins from severe commodity whipsaws.

2.3 Supply Chain Architecture & Raw Material Hedging

The production of insulated power cables and overhead conductors requires three primary categories of raw materials:

  • Conducting Metals: Electrical Conductor (EC) grade Aluminium wire rods (purity ≥99.5%) and Electrolytic Copper cathodes/rods (purity ≥99.99%). Dynamic Cables procures primary aluminium rods from domestic primary smelters such as Hindalco Industries, Vedanta (BALCO), and National Aluminium Company (NALCO). Copper is sourced from Hindalco's Birla Copper and global trading houses.[18]
  • Insulation & Sheathing Compounds: Cross-Linked Polyethylene (XLPE), Polyvinyl Chloride (PVC) resin, plasticizers, and flame-retardant low-smoke zero-halogen (LSZH/FRLS) compounds. Procured from specialized chemical compounders including Reliance Industries, DCM Shriram, and specialized compounding houses.
  • Armoring & Protection Materials: Galvanized iron (GI) round steel wires, flat steel forming strips, and high-tensile steel core wire for ACSR conductors, sourced from certified domestic steel re-rollers.

To neutralize the residual margin risk on fixed-price private contracts and retail distribution orders (which lack statutory PVC escalation), Dynamic Cables operates an active back-to-back procurement policy. Upon receipt of a binding purchase order or tender confirmation, the procurement team immediately books corresponding physical metal quantities on domestic smelter pricing tables or locks in raw material supply contracts. This back-to-back matching minimizes physical commodity inventory holding times to 45–55 days and prevents speculative trading losses on metal inventories.[9]

3. Sector Operating Dynamics: The Indian Power T&D Super-Cycle

The Indian power transmission and distribution sector is experiencing its most pronounced structural expansion in four decades, driven by three secular, non-cyclical macroeconomic forces:

Macro Trend 1: The Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS)
The Central Government launched the RDSS with an aggregate financial outlay of ₹3,03,758 crore (including an estimated Government budgetary support of ₹97,631 crore) to modernize DISCOM distribution infrastructure. The primary mandates of RDSS—replacing bare overhead conductors with aerial bunched (AB) insulated cables to curb Aggregate Technical & Commercial (AT&C) power theft, feeder segregation for agriculture, and urban underground cabling—have triggered unprecedented domestic demand for LT/HT insulated cables. Because Dynamic Cables is fully empanelled across 25+ state DISCOMs, the company captures direct procurement flows from this budgetary allocation.[15]

Macro Trend 2: Renewable Energy Evacuation & 500 GW Target: Under India's National Electricity Plan, non-fossil generation capacity is targeted to expand from ~200 GW to 500 GW by 2030. Variable renewable generation (utility-scale solar parks in Rajasthan and Gujarat, and offshore/onshore wind corridors) requires extensive local sub-station interconnections. Solar DC cables (operating at 1,500V DC) and 33kV–66kV underground evacuation cables are mandatory components of every MW deployed. Dynamic Cables' manufacturing plants in Jaipur and Reengus sit directly inside Rajasthan—India's solar capital—granting the company substantial logistical advantages, lower freight costs, and shorter delivery lead times compared to competitors located in Western or Southern India.[3][19]

Macro Trend 3: Indian Railways 100% Electrification & Mission Kavach: Indian Railways has reached near-total broad-gauge electrification and is aggressively rolling out the indigenous Automatic Train Protection (ATP) system, designated "Kavach", across 10,000+ route kilometers. Kavach requires continuous trackside data transmission, robust telecom quad cables, and fail-safe signaling cables along the entire permanent way. Vendor empanelment by the Research Designs and Standards Organisation (RDSO) requires years of rigorous field performance trials, factory audits, and failure analysis. Dynamic Cables holds active RDSO approval for railway signaling and quad cables, creating an impenetrable competitive moat against unorganized or lower-tier cable manufacturers.[4]

8. Adversarial Red-Team: 3 Fatal Flaws & Invalidation Triggers

To maintain institutional objectivity and protect capital against behavioral narrative bias, our research desk subjects the investment thesis to an adversarial red-team audit:

Fatal Flaw 1 • Raw Material Commodity Spikes & Price Variation Lag:
Although 85%–90% of contracts incorporate Price Variation Clauses (PVC), formula adjustments operate with a 30 to 60-day lag relative to spot raw material purchases. If London Metal Exchange (LME) copper or domestic aluminium prices experience sudden, parabolic escalations, Dynamic Cables must absorb higher cash procurement costs upfront before billing utility clients on retrospective escalation tables. In an inflationary commodity shock, interim quarterly EBITDA margins can compress by 150 to 250 basis points.
Fatal Flaw 2 • State DISCOM Payment Delays & Working Capital Lockup:
State power distribution companies in India possess historically erratic credit profiles. While the RDSS enforces stricter milestone disbursements via central escrow mechanisms, state-level bureaucratic delays, milestone sign-off disputes, or election-related spending freezes can rapidly inflate trade receivables. If debtor days (DSO) expand back toward 130–140 days, the company would be forced to draw down its working capital bank lines, inflating finance costs and depressing return on equity.
Fatal Flaw 3 • Large-Cap Cables Oligopoly Encroachment:
Large-cap competitors such as Polycab India, Havells, and KEI Industries possess colossal balance sheets, institutional credit lines, and nationwide advertising budgets. As these giants aggressively expand their medium and high-voltage industrial cable manufacturing capacities, they could initiate aggressive price competition in high-value utility tenders, squeezing operating margins for mid-tier manufacturers like Dynamic Cables.
Deterministic Invalidation Triggers (Capital Exit Rules):
  • Fundamental Invalidation: Reported quarterly Operating Profit Margin (OPM) compressing below 8.0% for two consecutive quarters, or 12-month operating cash flow conversion (CFO/PAT) collapsing below 0.50x, or Gross Debt-to-Equity escalating above 0.50x.

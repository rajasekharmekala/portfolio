What the company does

Glossary. relative strength index (RSI) is a momentum gauge from 0 to 100.

average true range (ATR) measures a typical daily price move.

earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA) is a pre-financing operating measure.

return on invested capital (ROIC) compares after-tax operating profit with invested funds.

free cash flow (FCF) is cash left after operating needs and capital expenditure, which is spending on long-lived assets.

stock-based compensation (SBC) is pay made with shares.

Stage 2 means an uptrend with price above rising averages. A base is a period of consolidation.

An entry zone is the price band where a purchase may be considered. Asymmetry compares possible reward with defined risk.

Net debt means borrowings less cash. A measured move projects a chart distance from a completed pattern.

Dilution means each existing share owns a smaller fraction after new shares are issued. These definitions make the later gates readable without assuming finance training.

limit order is an instruction to trade only at a chosen price or better. gross margin is sales left after direct costs, before wider operating costs.

A pivot is the price point used to judge a breakout. A retest is a return to that point after the move.

A breakout is a move above a defined price ceiling with enough evidence to support the move.

InnovAge operates the Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly (PACE), a capitated healthcare model for frail, predominantly dual-eligible seniors. A capitated provider receives a fixed per-member payment and assumes broad care costs, so enrollment can scale revenue but medical-cost control determines whether revenue becomes cash.[20][22] Historical filings describe an InnovAge operating footprint, but current participant count, center count and state count are N/A in the supplied research record; those figures must not be carried into an Innio thesis.[2][7] Those are healthcare facts, not gas-engine facts. No Innio thesis may borrow them.

Execution note for the next review

That discipline keeps the report honest. It lets a later reader tell which facts were known, which were unavailable and which event would change the decision. It also makes the rejection portable: the next reviewer can inspect the same ticker, the same CIK question and the same source list without guessing what the original analyst meant.

The reader should also retain the date. Market facts are not timeless. The same ticker can have a new price, a new average and a new trend next week. The identity problem is more basic than that time risk, but both matter. A fresh run must use the latest completed session and must label any older filing period.

The final handoff is explicit: correct the issuer first, then refresh the facts, then repeat the gates. No shortcut is justified by a familiar name, a low price or a tempting narrative. That is the complete execution rule for this candidate.

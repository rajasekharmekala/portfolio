Affordable Robotic & Automation Limited (ARAPL) designs and installs automation systems for industrial customers. Its product story is understandable: robotic welding, material movement, automated car parking, warehousing and related engineering work are meant to raise factory throughput while reducing manual handling. The investment decision is less comfortable. The fresh ledger records a ₹158.28 close, price below both the 50-day and 200-day averages, and a Stage 4 markdown. Screener’s latest consolidated tables show FY2025 losses, low returns on capital and a three-year operating-cash result that does not cover cumulative profit. Those facts lead to a REJECTED verdict for the IN watchlist-to-shortlist gate, not because automation lacks a market, but because this candidate does not currently meet the capital-protection rules.[1]

Company, history and business model

ARAPL was incorporated in 2009 and presents itself as a turnkey automation provider. The company’s public description includes robotic welding lines, material-handling systems and automated car-parking equipment. Its work is project-based: design, engineering, procurement, fabrication, installation and commissioning are combined into a customer-specific solution. That model can create a useful customer relationship because the supplier understands the complete line, but it can also make quarterly revenue lumpy because one delayed site or acceptance milestone can move sales and profit between periods.[8] [9] [10]

The official product pages describe welding cells and lines, warehouse automation and automated parking. These offerings sit between manufacturing and engineering services. Hardware purchases, imported components, fabrication labour, site installation and customer credit all affect the economics. A system can be technically successful but financially weak if the company pays suppliers before collecting from the customer. That is why the receivable and working-capital evidence is more important than a headline claim that factory automation is a large growth market.[11] [12] [13] [14]

Management has also discussed a machine-shop automation initiative and the Humro autonomous mobile robotics brand. These initiatives may enlarge the addressable market, but an announcement is not the same as audited recurring revenue. The evidence available for this run does not verify a segment-level order book, repeat revenue percentage, utilization rate or customer concentration. Those items are recorded as N/A rather than inferred from marketing language.[27] [28] [29]

What this means for an owner. You are not buying a simple factory with stable unit output. You are buying an engineering-project cycle. Cash collection, project acceptance and margin control must improve together before the business can clear a shortlist gate.

The table’s message is not that FY2026 was unprofitable; it was profitable. The message is that one profitable year does not yet establish durable returns. FY2025 produced an operating loss and FY2024 consumed operating cash. A project company needs several years of cash-backed returns because working capital can reverse after a customer milestone. Until that cycle is demonstrated, the short-list rule treats the recovery as unproven.[5] [6] [7] Working capital is short-term operating assets and liabilities.

The required three-year cash test is explicit. CFO for FY2024–FY2026 is −5 − 6 + 7 = −4 crore. PAT for the same years is 6 − 12 + 7 = 1 crore. Therefore CFO/PAT = −4 / 1 = −4.00, not the required 0.70 or higher. This is a hard failure, not a low score. If audited filings later restate these numbers, the calculation must be rerun rather than replaced with a guess.[17] [18]

Balance sheet, working capital and governance signals

Borrowings rose from ₹44 crore in FY2023 to ₹46 crore in FY2024, ₹56 crore in FY2025 and ₹69 crore in FY2026. Equity capital plus reserves were approximately ₹110 crore in FY2026, so the simple borrowings-to-equity calculation is about 0.63. That is below the 0.70 ceiling on the available reported values, but it is close enough that debt cannot be ignored. More importantly, the leverage pass does not override the failed cash and return tests.[4] [6]

Working capital is the central forensic risk. Screener reports debtor days of 215 in FY2023, 211 in FY2024, 191 in FY2025 and 165 in FY2026, while working-capital days are 123, 121, 122 and 239 respectively. The FY2026 cash-conversion cycle is reported at 489 days, with inventory days of 441 and days payable of 117. Those measures are unusually long for a small project-led company. The company may collect after installation, but the investor must see that collection in CFO before treating reported profit as distributable economics.[7] [19] [20]

Promoter holding declined from 61.48% in March 2023 to 55.65% in March 2025 and 41.41% in June 2026 in the Screener shareholding series. The exact reasons, including issuance, transfer or other capital actions, require reconciliation to exchange filings. The run therefore records promoter-pledge change and the causal bridge for the decline as N/A, not as an accusation. A governance conclusion needs the primary filing, not a ratio-page inference.[21] [22] [23] [24]

Red-team flaws and falsification tests

Flaw 1: the FY2026 recovery may be a peak or one-off

FY2026 operating profit of ₹17 crore and 14% OPM look much better than FY2025’s −₹3 crore and −2% OPM. The bear case is that project mix, milestone timing or one-off income makes the latest margin unrepeatable. The falsification test is three consecutive audited years of positive operating profit, CFO and stable working capital without a reversal after customer acceptance.[3] [4]

Flaw 2: receivables and inventory can absorb the reported profit

A 489-day cash-conversion cycle and 441 inventory days imply that accounting profit can remain trapped in projects and stock. The bear case is a future write-down, delayed collection or fresh borrowing. The falsification test is a fall in debtor days and inventory days accompanied by positive CFO, not merely a management statement that collections are improving.[6] [1]

Flaw 3: the chart can remain broken longer than the thesis can wait

The stock is 46.71% below its 52-week high and remains below the 200-day average. The bear case is a Stage 4 continuation in which a low RSI produces no durable reversal. The falsification test is a verified Stage 1 base or Stage 2 markup: price above a rising 50-day average, then above the 200-day average, with volume confirmation and relative strength versus Nifty 50.[16] [33] [34]

Hinge claim. The single hinge is cash conversion. Even if automation demand remains strong and FY2026 margins persist, a negative three-year CFO/PAT ratio means the shareholder has not yet seen the earnings as cash. That claim changes only with audited operating cash flow and working-capital repair.
